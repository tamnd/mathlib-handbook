# C*-algebras

A C*-algebra is a complex Banach algebra with an involution that satisfies $\|a^* a\| = \|a\|^2$. The algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space and the algebra of continuous functions on a compact space are the two basic examples, and the theorems of this chapter say that each C*-algebra is made from these. Section 1 defines C*-rings and C*-algebras, gives the examples, proves that the norm of a self-adjoint element is its spectral radius, and adds a unit with the unitization and the multiplier algebra. Section 2 constructs the continuous functional calculus, which defines $f(a)$ for a normal element $a$ and a continuous function $f$ on its spectrum. Section 3 uses the calculus to study the order on a C*-algebra, the norm and the continuity of $f(a)$, the theorem of Fuglede, Putnam and Rosenblum and the absolute value of an operator. Section 4 defines Hilbert C*-modules and the C*-algebra of matrices over a C*-algebra. Section 5 proves the commutative theorem of Gelfand and Naimark, constructs the GNS representation of a positive functional and studies positive and completely positive maps. Section 6 constructs the approximate unit of a C*-algebra, characterizes projections and studies the unitary group.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 and 2, and the spectrum and the Gelfand transform of Chapter 9. Sections 1, 3 and 4 also use the Hilbert spaces of Chapter 6, Sections 1 and 3 use the adjoint of Chapter 7, and Section 5 uses Chapter 4 for the completion of the GNS space. Each section from Section 2 uses the sections before it.
