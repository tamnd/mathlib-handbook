# Duality

The dual space of a vector space $V$ is the space of the linear functions $V \to K$. Each linear map $V \to W$ gives a map of the duals in the opposite direction, and in coordinates this map is the transpose matrix. This chapter develops the duality for vector spaces and for free modules. Section 1 defines the dual space and the dual basis, proves that a space of finite dimension and its dual have the same dimension, and proves that a space of finite dimension is canonically isomorphic to its double dual. Section 2 defines the annihilator of a subspace and the dual map, proves the formula for the dimension of the annihilator, identifies the dual map with the transpose, and gives a pointer to the inner product spaces. Section 3 describes the matrix of a bilinear or sesquilinear form in a pair of bases, the change of basis, and the criterion that a form is nondegenerate if and only if the determinant of its matrix is not zero. Section 4 studies the Cartan matrices of the simple Lie algebras, the generalized and finite Cartan matrices, and the symmetrizable matrices.

The chapter needs the bases and the dimension of Chapter 2 and the matrices of Chapter 3.
