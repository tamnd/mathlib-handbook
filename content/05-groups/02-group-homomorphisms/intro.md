# Group homomorphisms

A homomorphism is a map that respects the product. Section 1 defines the bundled type `G →* H`, proves that a homomorphism preserves the unit, inverses and powers, and studies composites and isomorphisms `G ≃* H`. Section 2 covers the invariants of an isomorphism, the product of groups and the Chinese remainder theorem for cyclic groups, and the automorphism group, with conjugation and $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}/n) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$. Section 3 describes the homomorphisms as objects: the classes that define operations on bundled maps by evaluation, the commutative group of homomorphisms into a commutative group, the ring of endomorphisms of an abelian group, isomorphisms between types of homomorphisms, and the graph of a homomorphism. Section 4 treats the monoid of self-maps of a type and the group of its permutations, the automorphism group of a magma, transpositions and translations, and the action of homomorphisms on units. Section 5 gives the pointwise group structures on a product of a family of groups, the homomorphisms into and out of such a product, its units, and the additive group of finitely supported functions. Section 6 treats additive characters, maps that shift by a constant, the translation of functions and the forward difference operator.

The chapter needs Chapter 1. The kernel and the image of a homomorphism are subgroups, the topic of Chapter 3, and the first isomorphism theorem in Chapter 5 describes the image.
