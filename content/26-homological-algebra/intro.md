# Homological algebra

This volume teaches homological algebra and its form in Mathlib. Homological algebra measures how far a functor is from exact. A short exact sequence of modules does not stay exact under the functor $\operatorname{Hom}(A, -)$ or under the tensor product with $A$, and the derived functors $\operatorname{Ext}$ and $\operatorname{Tor}$ record the failure. The same machinery computes the cohomology of groups, of sheaves and of spaces. The volume starts from short complexes and ends with the cohomology of groups. It proves the main theorems on paper and shows the Mathlib names for each step.

Chapter 1 treats short complexes, their homology, exactness and the snake lemma. Mathlib builds all of homology on the short complex $X_1 \to X_2 \to X_3$, so this chapter is the base of the volume. Chapter 2 defines complexes of each shape, their homology and quasi-isomorphisms, and it proves the long exact homology sequence of a short exact sequence of complexes. Chapter 3 gives chain homotopies, the mapping cone and the homotopy category, and it shows that the homotopy category is triangulated. Chapter 4 constructs projective and injective resolutions, the left and right derived functors, and the functors $\operatorname{Ext}$ and $\operatorname{Tor}$, with the computation of $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z})$.

Chapter 5 constructs the derived category of an abelian category as a localization at the quasi-isomorphisms, and it defines the Ext groups as morphisms in the derived category. Chapter 6 treats graded objects, double complexes, total complexes and spectral sequences. Chapter 7 studies the concrete categories of algebra: monoids, groups, rings, modules, algebras, coalgebras and Hopf algebras. Chapter 8 defines the homology and cohomology of a group, computes them in low degrees and for finite cyclic groups, and gives the long exact sequences and the lemma of Shapiro.

The volume needs Volume 24 in full and Chapters 2, 4 and 5 of Volume 25 for abelian, localized and triangulated categories. The examples use groups from Volume 5, rings from Volume 6, and modules and tensor products from Volumes 7 and 8. Chapters 1 to 4 come in order. Chapter 5 uses Chapters 3 and 4, and Chapter 6 uses Chapter 2. Chapter 7 needs only Volume 24, and Chapter 8 uses Chapters 4 and 7. The level is that of a graduate course. The references are Weibel, *An Introduction to Homological Algebra*, Cartan and Eilenberg, *Homological Algebra*, and Brown, *Cohomology of Groups*.

The conventions are those of Volumes 24 and 25. Mathlib writes `f ≫ g` for the composite $g \circ f$ of the textbook. A chain complex in `ChainComplex V ℕ` has its differential from degree $n+1$ to degree $n$, and a cochain complex in `CochainComplex V ℤ` has its differential from degree $n$ to degree $n+1$. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
