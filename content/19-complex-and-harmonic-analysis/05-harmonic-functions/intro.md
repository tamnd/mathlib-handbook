# Harmonic functions

A real function $u$ on an open subset of $\mathbb{C} = \mathbb{R}^2$ is harmonic when it is $C^2$ and $\Delta u = u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$. The real part of a holomorphic function is harmonic, by the Cauchy-Riemann equations of Chapter 2. On a disc the converse holds, and this link lets complex analysis prove the main facts about harmonic functions in the plane. The chapter also proves two growth theorems for holomorphic functions on unbounded sets, which are the maximum principle of Chapter 3 under weaker hypotheses.

Section 1 defines harmonic functions in the form of Mathlib, proves that a harmonic function on a disc is the real part of a holomorphic function, and deduces that it is analytic, satisfies the mean value property and, if bounded on the plane, is constant. Section 2 proves the Poisson integral formula, which recovers a harmonic or holomorphic function on a disc from its values on the boundary circle, proves the Harnack bounds for the Poisson kernel and the Borel-Caratheodory inequality. Section 3 proves the Phragmen-Lindelof principle in a strip and deduces Hadamard's three lines theorem, a convexity statement for the logarithm of the maximum of $|f|$ on vertical lines.

The chapter needs the Cauchy integral formula of Chapter 2, the maximum modulus principle of Chapter 3 and the circle average from Chapter 4, Section 2. It uses the Laplacian on a finite dimensional inner product space of Volume 16 and the interval integral of Volume 18. Vertical lines return in Chapter 8, where the Mellin transform and its inverse are integrals along them.
