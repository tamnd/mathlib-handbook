# Number fields and rings of integers

A number field is a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$. Its ring of integers plays the role that $\mathbb{Z}$ plays for $\mathbb{Q}$. Volume 6 showed that $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ has unique factorization and that $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ does not. This chapter explains the difference. Section 1 defines number fields and rings of integers, computes the ring of integers of a quadratic field, and introduces the norm and the discriminant. Section 2 defines the class group, which measures the failure of unique factorization, states with the bound of Minkowski that it is finite, and computes the class number of $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-5})$. It then studies the group of units: it states Dirichlet's unit theorem in the form of Mathlib and connects it with Pell's equation. Section 3 describes the canonical embedding of a number field into the mixed space $\mathbb{R}^{r_1} \times \mathbb{C}^{r_2}$, the convex bodies of Minkowski's theorem and the fundamental cone of the units. Section 4 treats the complex embeddings, ramification at the infinite places, totally real and totally complex fields, and CM fields with their units. Section 5 studies the cyclotomic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$: their ring of integers, discriminant, Galois group and the factorization of primes, with the field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)$ as a worked case. Section 6 completes a number field at its finite and infinite places, proves the product formula, and builds the adele ring, the ideles and the idele class group. Section 7 studies how a prime ideal splits in an extension: the Galois action on the primes above it, the decomposition and inertia fields, the Kummer-Dedekind theorem, and the link between ramification and the discriminant. Section 8 defines the heights of elements and of points of projective space for fields with a product formula, proves the Northcott property for number fields, and applies it to the points of elliptic curves and to the Mahler measure of polynomials. Section 9 gives the theory of cyclotomic extensions over a general base: the class of these extensions, the power bases of $\zeta$ and $\zeta - 1$, their norms and discriminants, the Galois group and the cyclotomic character.

The chapter needs the Gaussian integers and the factorization theory of Volume 6, Chapter 6, the bases of Volume 7, Chapter 2, the modules over a PID of Volume 7, Chapter 7, and Pell's equation from Chapter 4. Some facts on Dedekind domains and field extensions are stated without proof.
