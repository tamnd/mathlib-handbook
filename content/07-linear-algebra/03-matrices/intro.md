# Matrices

A matrix is a table of scalars, and it is the form in which linear maps compute. This chapter builds the bridge between the two. Section 1 defines matrices, their sum, product, transpose and trace, and proves the laws of the matrix algebra. Section 2 attaches to each linear map its matrix in a pair of bases, proves that the product of matrices is the composition of maps, and derives the formula for a change of basis. Section 3 defines the rank of a matrix, proves that the row rank equals the column rank, and computes ranks by row reduction. Section 4 surveys the general matrix API, with dependent matrices, partial permutation matrices and reflections. Section 5 treats the matrix units and the block matrices. Section 6 gives the notation for concrete matrices, the rows and columns, the reindexing and the dot product. Section 7 treats the conjugate transpose and the symmetric and diagonal matrices. Section 8 defines the Kronecker, Hadamard and circulant matrices. Section 9 describes the ideals of matrix rings and the actions of matrices on vectors.

The chapter needs the bases and the dimension of Chapter 2 and the finite sums of Volume 3. Mathlib defines a matrix as a function of two indices, so many proofs are computations with sums. Chapter 4 defines the determinant of a square matrix, and Chapter 5 studies its characteristic polynomial.
