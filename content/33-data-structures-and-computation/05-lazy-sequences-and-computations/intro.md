# Lazy sequences and computations

A stream never ends, and a list always ends. This chapter studies types whose elements may stop or may run forever, and the proof methods for such types. The first section defines `Stream'.Seq α`, a stream of optional values that stays at `none` after the first `none`. It covers the constructors, destructuring, corecursion, append, map and join, the conversions from and to lists, and the proof of equalities by bisimulation. The examples include the sequence `[1, 2, 3]`, the sequence of natural numbers, and the fact that an infinite sequence absorbs any sequence appended to it. The second section defines `Computation α`, a sequence of steps that may end with a value, and the weak sequences `Stream'.WSeq`, which mix values with silent steps. It proves that a computation has at most one result, studies `think`, `bind` and the equivalence that ignores the number of steps, and runs several computations in parallel. It closes with `Semiquot`, a set of possible values with one value inside.

The chapter needs the streams and the bisimulation method of Chapter 4, the options and lists of Volume 1 and Chapter 1 of this volume, and the induction on natural numbers. Some results use the monad notation, which the reader can take on trust until Chapter 6.

Chapter 6 makes the monad structure of `Computation` and of `Option` precise and studies functors and traversals in general. Chapter 7 explains sequences and streams from a single point of view: they are M types, the coinductive fixed points of polynomial functors, and bisimulation is the proof principle of every M type.
