## Finitely generated groups and subgroups of finite index

### Overview

A group is *finitely generated* if a finite subset generates it. Finite groups are finitely generated, and so are $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}^n$ and the free group on a finite set. This section gives the library of finitely generated groups, their rank, and their subgroups of finite index. The main link to the index of Section 1 is the lemma of Schreier: a subgroup of finite index in a finitely generated group is finitely generated, and its rank is at most the index times the rank of the group.

The section also treats some classes of groups that are defined with subgroups of finite index. A group is *residually finite* if each element other than $1$ is outside some normal subgroup of finite index. A group is *virtually cyclic* if it has a cyclic subgroup of finite index. Two smaller modules complete the section: a descent theorem that proves finite generation from a height function, and the fact that in a finite monoid a left inverse is a right inverse.

### The library around it

#### Finite generation

The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Finiteness` has the basic class `IsMulFG M`, with the additive form `IsAddFG M`. It says that there is a finite set $S$ such that the subsemigroup closure of $S$ is all of $M$. For monoids and groups the library also has the names `Monoid.FG`, `Submonoid.FG`, `Group.FG` and `Subgroup.FG`, with the additive forms `AddGroup.FG` and `AddSubgroup.FG`. These names are abbreviations for `IsMulFG` of the type or of the subtype, so a proof can move between them by unfolding. The characterizations state finite generation with the closure that fits the structure.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Group.fg_iff` | $G$ is FG if and only if $G = \langle S \rangle$ for a finite set $S$ |
| `Group.isMulFG_iff` | `IsMulFG G` if and only if `Subgroup.closure S = ⊤` for a `Finset` $S$ |
| `Subgroup.fg_iff` | a subgroup $H$ is FG if and only if $H = \langle S \rangle$ for a finite set $S$ |
| `Group.fg_iff_monoid_fg` | a group is FG as a group if and only if it is FG as a monoid |
| `Group.fg_iff_subgroup_fg` | the subtype of $H$ is FG as a group if and only if $H$ is FG as a subgroup |
| `Group.fg_iff_exists_freeGroup_hom_surjective_finite` | $G$ is FG if and only if a free group on a finite type maps onto $G$ |

The group and monoid closures give the same notion for a group, because the inverses of the generators are in the monoid closure of the generators and their inverses. The last line of the table connects finite generation to free groups, which Chapter 10 describes.

Finite generation goes to images. If $f : G \to G'$ is a surjective homomorphism and $G = \langle S \rangle$, then $G' = f(G) = \langle f(S) \rangle$, and $f(S)$ is finite. This is `Group.fg_of_surjective`, with the monoid form `Monoid.fg_of_surjective`. So a quotient of a finitely generated group is finitely generated, and the instance `QuotientGroup.fg` gives this for $G / N$. A finite group or monoid is finitely generated by the instances `instIsMulFGOfFinite` and `Monoid.fg_of_finite`: take $S = G$. For subgroups, `Subgroup.FG.sup`, `Subgroup.FG.iSup`, `Subgroup.FG.prod` and `Subgroup.FG.pi` give the join of two subgroups, a finite join, a product and a finite product. A subgroup of a finitely generated group need not be finitely generated: the free group on two letters contains a free group on infinitely many letters. Schreier's lemma below says that the subgroups of finite index are finitely generated.

The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Generators` gives a structure `Group.Generators G ι` for a family of generators indexed by $\iota$. It has the family `val : ι → G` and the field `closure_eq_top`. A generating set gives a family by `Group.Generators.ofSet`, and `Group.Generators.lift` is the homomorphism `FreeGroup ι →* G` that sends the letter $i$ to the generator $i$. This homomorphism is surjective (`Group.Generators.lift_surjective`). The extension principle `Group.Generators.hom_ext` says that two homomorphisms $\varphi, \psi : G \to H$ that agree on each generator are equal. Proof: the set $\{g \mid \varphi(g) = \psi(g)\}$ is a subgroup that contains the generators, so it contains their closure, which is $G$. A family of generators goes along a surjection by `Group.Generators.map`. Finite generation is the existence of a family indexed by `Fin n`, by `Group.fg_iff_nonempty_finite_generators`.

#### The rank

The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Rank` defines `Group.rank G` for a finitely generated group $G$: the least cardinality of a finite generating set. The definition takes the instance `[Group.FG G]` as an argument. The two basic lemmas are `Group.rank_spec`, which gives a generating `Finset` whose card is the rank, and `Group.rank_le`, which says that the rank is at most the card of each generating `Finset`. Each other lemma follows from these two.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Group.rank_le_of_surjective` | $\operatorname{rank} G' \le \operatorname{rank} G$ for a surjection $G \to G'$ |
| `Group.rank_range_le` | $\operatorname{rank} f(G) \le \operatorname{rank} G$ |
| `Group.rank_congr` | isomorphic groups have the same rank |
| `Group.rank_eq_zero_iff` | $\operatorname{rank} G = 0$ if and only if $G$ is trivial |
| `Subgroup.rank_closure_finset_le_card` | $\operatorname{rank} \langle S \rangle \le |S|$ for a `Finset` $S$ |

The first line has a short proof: the image of a generating set of size $\operatorname{rank} G$ generates $G'$, and its size is at most $\operatorname{rank} G$. The additive form `AddGroup.rank` gives the rank of an additive group, for example $\operatorname{rank} \mathbb{Z}^n = n$, but the library does not compute this value.

#### Schreier's lemma

Let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$ and let $R$ be a right transversal of $H$ that contains $1$. Each $g \in G$ is $h r$ with a unique $h \in H$ and $r \in R$, and the map $g \mapsto r$ is `Subgroup.IsComplement.toRightFun`. Write $\bar g$ for this $r$. The lemma of Schreier says: if $G = \langle S \rangle$, then

$$H = \langle\, r s\, \overline{r s}^{-1} \mid r \in R,\ s \in S \,\rangle.$$

This is `Subgroup.closure_mul_image_eq`. Each element $r s \overline{r s}^{-1}$ is in $H$ by the definition of $\bar{\cdot}$. Proof sketch for the other inclusion: let $K$ be the subgroup on the right. The set $K R$ contains $1$ and is closed under right multiplication by each $s \in S$, because $k r s = k (r s \overline{r s}^{-1}) \overline{r s}$. A similar computation gives closure under $s^{-1}$. So $K R$ is all of $G$, and an element $h \in H$ is $k r$ with $k \in K$, so $r = k^{-1} h \in H \cap R = \{1\}$ and $h = k$.

When $H$ has finite index, $R$ is finite with $|R| = [G : H]$. So there are at most $[G : H]\,|S|$ Schreier generators. The library states this as `Subgroup.exists_finset_card_le_mul` and draws two results.

1. `Subgroup.fg_of_index_ne_zero`: if $G$ is finitely generated and $[G : H]$ is finite, then $H$ is finitely generated. This is an instance with the hypotheses `[Group.FG G]` and `[H.FiniteIndex]`.
2. `Subgroup.rank_le_index_mul_rank`: $\operatorname{rank} H \le [G : H] \cdot \operatorname{rank} G$. Proof: apply the bound to a generating set of size $\operatorname{rank} G$, then use `Group.rank_le`.

The same module proves a theorem of Schur. If the set of commutators of $G$ is finite, then the commutator subgroup is finite. The statement `Subgroup.card_commutator_le_of_finite_commutatorSet` bounds the order of `commutator G` by `Subgroup.cardCommutatorBound` applied to the number of commutators. A step of the proof is that the center has finite index, and `Subgroup.card_commutator_dvd_index_center_pow` gives a bound in terms of that index. For a finitely generated commutative group, `card_dvd_exponent_pow_rank` says that $|G|$ divides $\exp(G)^{\operatorname{rank} G}$.

#### A descent theorem

The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Descent` gives a criterion for finite generation in the form of the proof of the theorem of Mordell and Weil. Let $h : G \to \mathbb{R}$ be a height function with the Northcott property: for each bound $B$, the set $\{x \mid h(x) \le B\}$ is finite. The class `Northcott h` states this. Assume that a homomorphism $f$ has an image of finite index with a finite set $s$ of coset representatives, that $h(x) \le a\, h(g x) + c$ for each $g \in s$, that $b\, h(x) - c \le h(f(x))$, and that $0 \le a < b$. Then `Group.fg_of_descent` says that $G$ is finitely generated. Idea of the proof: write $x = g f(y)$ with $g \in s$. Then $h(y)$ is less than $h(x)$ when $h(x)$ is large. So repeated division reduces each element to a product of elements of $s$ and elements of small height, and there are only finitely many of them. For a commutative group, `CommGroup.fg_of_descent` takes $f = x \mapsto x^n$, and the hypothesis is that `(powMonoidHom n).range` has finite index. The lemma `CommGroup.finite_torsion_of_descent` says that under the same conditions the elements of finite order form a finite set.

#### Finite monoids are Dedekind-finite

A monoid is *Dedekind-finite* if $a b = 1$ implies $b a = 1$. The class `IsDedekindFiniteMonoid` states this. The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.DedekindFinite` has one instance, `instIsDedekindFiniteMonoidOfFinite`: a finite monoid is Dedekind-finite. Proof: let $a b = 1$. Then $x \mapsto b x$ is injective, because $b x = b y$ gives $x = a b x = a b y = y$. An injective map of a finite set to itself is surjective, so $b c = 1$ for some $c$. Then $a = a (b c) = (a b) c = c$, so $b a = 1$.

#### Normal subgroups of finite index

The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.FiniteIndexNormalSubgroup` bundles a subgroup with the two properties *normal* and *of finite index*. The type is `FiniteIndexNormalSubgroup G`. The map `FiniteIndexNormalSubgroup.toSubgroup` forgets the properties, it is injective (`FiniteIndexNormalSubgroup.toSubgroup_injective`), and the type has a `SetLike` instance, so membership is the membership of the subgroup. A subgroup with the instances `[H.Normal]` and `[H.FiniteIndex]` gives a term by `FiniteIndexNormalSubgroup.ofSubgroup`. The type is a lattice (`FiniteIndexNormalSubgroup.instLattice`), because the intersection and the join of two normal subgroups of finite index are again normal of finite index. The preimage along a homomorphism is `FiniteIndexNormalSubgroup.comap`, and it respects the identity and composition. Each subgroup of finite index contains a normal subgroup of finite index, its normal core (`Subgroup.finiteIndex_normalCore`), so the two kinds of subgroups give the same topology on $G$.

#### Residually finite groups

The class `Group.ResiduallyFinite G` says that the intersection of all normal subgroups of finite index is trivial. The field is `Group.ResiduallyFinite.iInf_eq_bot`. The characterizations state the property element by element.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Group.residuallyFinite_iff_exists_finiteIndexNormalSubgroup` | each $g \ne 1$ is outside some $N$ in `FiniteIndexNormalSubgroup G` |
| `Group.residuallyFinite_iff_exists_finiteIndex` | each $g \ne 1$ is outside some subgroup of finite index |
| `Group.residuallyFinite_of_forall_exists_finite_monoidHom` | if each $g \ne 1$ has $f(g) \ne 1$ for a homomorphism $f$ to a finite group, then $G$ is residually finite |

The second line has a short proof. One direction is clear, because a normal subgroup of finite index is a subgroup of finite index. For the other direction, let $g \notin H$ with $[G : H]$ finite. The normal core of $H$ is contained in $H$, so $g$ is outside it, and the core is normal of finite index. The instances give the closure properties: finite groups are residually finite (`Group.instResiduallyFiniteOfFinite`), and so are subgroups (`Group.instResiduallyFiniteSubtypeMemSubgroup`) and products (`Group.instResiduallyFiniteProd`).

#### Virtually cyclic groups

The class `Group.IsVirtuallyCyclic G` says that $G$ has a cyclic subgroup of finite index (`Group.isVirtuallyCyclic_iff`). Finite groups and cyclic groups are virtually cyclic, by `Group.instIsVirtuallyCyclicOfFinite` and `Group.instIsVirtuallyCyclicOfIsCyclic`. The infinite dihedral group is virtually cyclic and not cyclic. The class goes to subgroups and quotients by instances, and along injective and surjective homomorphisms by `Group.IsVirtuallyCyclic.of_injective` and `Group.IsVirtuallyCyclic.of_surjective`. The lemma `Group.IsVirtuallyCyclic.exists_isCyclic_and_finiteIndex_and_normal` gives a cyclic subgroup of finite index that is also normal. Proof idea: the normal core of a cyclic subgroup of finite index is a subgroup of a cyclic group, so it is cyclic, and it has finite index. The module also proves that a virtually cyclic group is virtually nilpotent, and Chapter 12 describes nilpotent groups.

#### Common errors

1. `Group.rank G` takes `[Group.FG G]` as an instance argument. For a subgroup, Lean must find `Group.FG H` for the subtype; for a subgroup of finite index the instance `Subgroup.fg_of_index_ne_zero` supplies it, but only when `H.FiniteIndex` is an instance in the context.
2. `Group.FG` and `Subgroup.FG` are abbreviations for `IsMulFG`. A lemma about `IsMulFG` applies to them, but `simp` and `rw` see the abbreviation only after it unfolds, so a proof sometimes needs `Group.isMulFG_iff` or `Group.fg_iff` first.
3. `Subgroup.closure_mul_image_eq` needs a right transversal $R$ with $1 \in R$ in the form `Subgroup.IsComplement H R`. A left transversal gives the wrong form of the generators.

### Exercises

1. (A) Use `Group.fg_of_surjective` to prove that a quotient of a finitely generated group is finitely generated.
2. (A) Prove that a finite monoid in which each element has a left inverse is a group, with `instIsDedekindFiniteMonoidOfFinite`.
3. (B) Let $G$ be generated by $a$ and $b$, and let $H$ be the kernel of the map $G \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ that sends $a$ and $b$ to $1$. Write the Schreier generators of $H$ for the transversal $\{1, a\}$.
4. (B) Prove that a finitely generated group has only finitely many subgroups of a given finite index $n$.
5. (B) Prove that $\mathbb{Z}$ is residually finite with `Group.residuallyFinite_iff_exists_finiteIndex`.
6. (C) Prove that a finitely generated residually finite group $G$ is Hopfian: each surjective homomorphism $G \to G$ is injective.
