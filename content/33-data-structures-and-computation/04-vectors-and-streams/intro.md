# Vectors and streams

A list may have any length. This chapter studies two types that fix the length: a vector has a length that its type records, and a stream has no end. The first section defines `List.Vector α n` as the subtype of lists of length $n$. It covers the constructors and the access functions, the induction principles from the front and from the back, the maps with an accumulator that run from right to left, membership, `zipWith`, and the equivalence of vectors with the functions `Fin n → α`. It closes with `Vector3`, which defines a vector as a function on `Fin2 n`. The second section defines a stream `Stream' α` as a function `ℕ → α`. It covers head, tail, cons, drop, map, zip, iterate and corecursion, and it proves equalities of streams by bisimulation. The examples are the stream of natural numbers, the even and odd parts of a stream and their interleave, and the take theorem, which compares two streams by their finite prefixes.

The chapter needs lists and their operations from Chapter 1 of this volume, subtypes and the extensionality of functions from Volume 1, and equivalences of types. The second section uses no result on vectors.

The two types show two ways to make the length precise. A vector is a list with a proof about it, and a stream is a function. The next chapter joins the two ideas: a sequence that may stop is a stream of optional values, and a computation that may not stop is a stream that may never give a result. Chapter 6 studies the class `Traversable`, of which vectors are an instance, and Chapter 7 builds infinite trees, of which streams are the simplest case.
