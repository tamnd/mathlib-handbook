## The uniform structure of a topological group

### Overview

In a topological group, $x$ and $y$ are close when $yx^{-1}$ lies in a small neighbourhood of $1$. This section turns that idea into a uniform structure in the sense of Volume 13, Chapter 7. It proves that the uniform structure gives back the topology of the group, that continuous homomorphisms are uniformly continuous, and that in a commutative group the operations are uniformly continuous. A group that is not commutative has a right and a left uniformity, which can differ. As an application, a discrete subgroup of a Hausdorff group is closed.

### Definitions

**Definition (Right and left uniformity).** Let $G$ be a topological group. For a neighbourhood $V$ of $1$ let
$$R_V = \{(x, y) \in G \times G : yx^{-1} \in V\}, \qquad L_V = \{(x, y) \in G \times G : x^{-1}y \in V\}.$$
The right uniformity of $G$ is the filter on $G \times G$ generated by the sets $R_V$. The left uniformity is generated by the sets $L_V$.

**Definition (Uniform group).** A uniform group is a group $G$ with a uniform structure such that the map $G \times G \to G$, $(x, y) \mapsto xy^{-1}$, is uniformly continuous.

For an additive group the two uniformities agree: $R_V = L_V = \{(x, y) : y - x \in V\}$. So a commutative topological group has one natural uniform structure.

### Theorems and proofs

**Theorem (The right uniformity).** Let $G$ be a topological group. The sets $R_V$ form a base of a uniform structure on $G$, and the topology of this uniform structure is the topology of $G$. For each $a \in G$ the right translation $x \mapsto xa$ is uniformly continuous.

*Proof.* Recall the axioms of a base of a uniformity (Volume 13, Chapter 7): each set contains the diagonal; the intersection of two contains a third; for each set $R$ there is $R'$ with $(R')^{-1} \subseteq R$; and for each $R$ there is $R'$ with $R' \circ R' \subseteq R$. Each $R_V$ contains $(x, x)$, since $xx^{-1} = 1 \in V$. Also $R_V \cap R_W = R_{V \cap W}$. If $(x, y) \in R_{V^{-1}}$ then $yx^{-1} \in V^{-1}$, so $xy^{-1} = (yx^{-1})^{-1} \in V$ and $(y, x) \in R_V$. For the composition, choose $W$ with $WW \subseteq V$ (Chapter 1, Section 2). If $(x, y) \in R_W$ and $(y, z) \in R_W$, then $zx^{-1} = (zy^{-1})(yx^{-1}) \in WW \subseteq V$, so $(x, z) \in R_V$. For the topology, the ball of $R_V$ at $x$ is $\{y : yx^{-1} \in V\} = Vx$. The sets $Vx$, for $V$ a neighbourhood of $1$, are the neighbourhoods of $x$ by homogeneity (Chapter 1, Section 1). Finally $(ya)(xa)^{-1} = yaa^{-1}x^{-1} = yx^{-1}$, so the right translation maps $R_V$ into $R_V$. $\square$

In the right uniformity, a filter $\mathcal{F}$ on $G$ is Cauchy if and only if for each neighbourhood $V$ of $1$ there is $A \in \mathcal{F}$ with $AA^{-1} \subseteq V$. A sequence $(x_n)$ is Cauchy if and only if $x_m x_n^{-1} \to 1$ as $m, n \to \infty$.

**Theorem (Commutative groups).** Let $G$ be a commutative topological group with its uniform structure. Then $G$ is a uniform group.

*Proof.* Write $G$ additively. Let $V$ be a neighbourhood of $0$ and choose $W$ with $W + W \subseteq V$ and $W = -W$. Let $(x, x')$ and $(y, y')$ be in $R_W$, that is, $x' - x \in W$ and $y' - y \in W$. Then $(x' - y') - (x - y) = (x' - x) - (y' - y) \in W - W = W + W \subseteq V$. So the map $(x, y) \mapsto x - y$ maps the entourage $R_W \times R_W$ of $G \times G$ into $R_V$, and it is uniformly continuous. $\square$

**Theorem (Homomorphisms).** Let $f : G \to H$ be a continuous homomorphism of topological groups. Then $f$ is uniformly continuous for the right uniformities, and also for the left uniformities.

*Proof.* Let $V$ be a neighbourhood of $1$ in $H$. Then $U = f^{-1}(V)$ is a neighbourhood of $1$ in $G$. If $(x, y) \in R_U$, then $f(y)f(x)^{-1} = f(yx^{-1}) \in V$, so $(f(x), f(y)) \in R_V$. The same computation with $x^{-1}y$ works for the left uniformities. $\square$

By Chapter 1, Section 2, continuity at $1$ is enough. In Mathlib the results are `uniformContinuous_monoidHom_of_continuous` and `MonoidHom.uniformContinuous_of_continuousAt_one`, stated for uniform groups.

**Theorem (Discrete subgroups are closed).** Let $G$ be a Hausdorff topological group and $H \le G$ a subgroup that is discrete in the subspace topology. Then $H$ is closed.

*Proof.* Since $H$ is discrete, there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $1$ with $U \cap H = \{1\}$. Choose a symmetric neighbourhood $V$ of $1$ with $VV \subseteq U$. Let $x \in \overline{H}$. If $h_1 = v_1 x$ and $h_2 = v_2 x$ are in $H \cap Vx$, then $h_1h_2^{-1} = v_1 v_2^{-1} \in VV \subseteq U$ and $h_1 h_2^{-1} \in H$, so $h_1 = h_2$. The set $Vx$ is a neighbourhood of $x$, so it meets $H$, and $H \cap Vx = \{h\}$ for one point $h$. Each neighbourhood $N$ of $x$ contains the neighbourhood $N \cap Vx$, which meets $H$ only in $h$. So $h$ is in each neighbourhood of $x$. Since $G$ is Hausdorff, $x = h \in H$. $\square$

In Mathlib the theorem is the instance `Subgroup.isClosed_of_discreteTopology`, which needs only `T1Space G`. The module `Mathlib.Topology.Algebra.IsUniformGroup.DiscreteSubgroup` adds that commensurable subgroups are discrete together (`Subgroup.Commensurable.discreteTopology_iff`).

### Examples

**Example (The real line).** For $(\mathbb{R}, +)$ and $V = (-\varepsilon, \varepsilon)$, $R_V = \{(x, y) : |y - x| < \varepsilon\}$. This is the uniformity of the metric $|x - y|$. So the Cauchy sequences of the group $\mathbb{R}$ are the usual ones, and $\mathbb{R}$ is complete. The same holds for $\mathbb{R}^n$ and for each normed space.

**Example (Two groups on one space).** The group $(\mathbb{R}_{>0}, \cdot)$ is homeomorphic to $(\mathbb{R}, +)$ by $\log$, a continuous isomorphism with continuous inverse. Its uniformity is therefore given by $|\log y - \log x| < \varepsilon$, that is, $y/x \in (e^{-\varepsilon}, e^{\varepsilon})$. The sequence $x_n = 1/n$ is not Cauchy: $x_m x_n^{-1} = n/m$ does not tend to $1$. But it is Cauchy for the metric $|x - y|$ of the subspace $\mathbb{R}_{>0} \subseteq \mathbb{R}$. So the uniform structure depends on the group operation, not only on the topology. For the group $(\mathbb{R}_{>0}, \cdot)$, the space is complete; for the metric $|x - y|$ it is not.

**Counterexample (The left and right uniformities differ).** Let $G = \{(a, b) : a > 0, b \in \mathbb{R}\}$ with the topology of $\mathbb{R}^2$ and the product $(a, b)(c, d) = (ac, ad + b)$. This is the group of the maps $t \mapsto at + b$ under composition. The unit is $(1, 0)$, and $(a, b)^{-1} = (a^{-1}, -a^{-1}b)$. The product and the inverse are given by continuous formulas, so $G$ is a topological group. Let $x_n = (1/n, 0)$ and $y_n = (1/n, 1/n)$. Then
$$y_n x_n^{-1} = (1/n, 1/n)(n, 0) = (1, 1/n) \to (1, 0), \qquad x_n^{-1} y_n = (n, 0)(1/n, 1/n) = (1, 1).$$
So for each $V$, $(x_n, y_n) \in R_V$ for large $n$, but $(x_n, y_n) \notin L_V$ for a neighbourhood $V$ of $(1, 0)$ that misses $(1, 1)$. The two uniformities differ. With the right uniformity $G$ is not a uniform group. The inversion $\iota$ satisfies $\iota(y)\iota(x)^{-1} = y^{-1}x = (x^{-1}y)^{-1}$, so it maps $R_V$ onto $L_{V^{-1}}$. Since the uniformities differ, $\iota$ is not uniformly continuous for the right uniformity.

**Remark (Common errors).** A learner often writes the entourages as $\{(x, y) : x^{-1}y \in V\}$ and calls them the right uniformity. Conventions differ between books. Mathlib's `rightUniformSpace` uses $yx^{-1}$, for which the right translations are uniformly continuous. A second error is to apply a lemma about uniform groups to a group that is not commutative with its right uniformity; the counterexample shows that it is not a uniform group. A third error, in Lean, is to expect an instance `UniformSpace G` from `IsTopologicalAddGroup G`. Mathlib provides none; a proof installs `IsTopologicalAddGroup.rightUniformSpace G` itself.

### The library around it

Products and subgroups of uniform groups are uniform groups: `Prod.instIsUniformGroup`, `Pi.instIsUniformGroup`, `Subgroup.isUniformGroup`, and `IsUniformInducing.isUniformGroup` for the structure induced by a homomorphism. The right translations are uniform embeddings, `isUniformEmbedding_translate_mul`, and `uniformity_translate_mul` states the invariance of the uniformity. Products of Cauchy sequences are Cauchy, `CauchySeq.mul`. If $\mathcal{N}(1)$ has a countable basis, so does the uniformity, `IsUniformGroup.uniformity_countably_generated`. A quotient of a complete first countable group by a normal subgroup is complete, `QuotientGroup.completeSpace_right`. A compact Hausdorff group is a uniform group, `IsUniformGroup.of_compactSpace`. For actions, `UniformContinuousConstSMul` says that each map $x \mapsto cx$ is uniformly continuous, with `UniformContinuous.const_smul`.

The module `Mathlib.Topology.Algebra.Equicontinuity` reduces the equicontinuity of a family of homomorphisms to the identity element. Let $F$ be a family of group homomorphisms from a topological group $G$ to a uniform group $M$. If $F$ is equicontinuous at $1$, then $F$ is equicontinuous (`equicontinuous_of_equicontinuousAt_one`), and uniformly equicontinuous when $G$ is a uniform group (`uniformEquicontinuous_of_equicontinuousAt_one`). The proof is the argument for one homomorphism, applied to the whole family: $f(y)^{-1} f(x) = f(y^{-1}x)$ for each $f \in F$. The additive forms, such as `equicontinuous_of_equicontinuousAt_zero`, have the same proofs.

The module `Mathlib.Topology.Algebra.MetricSpace.Lipschitz` collects facts on Lipschitz maps between metric spaces. A Lipschitz map sends Cauchy sequences to Cauchy sequences (`LipschitzWith.cauchySeq_comp`, and `LipschitzOnWith.cauchySeq_comp` on a set). A map with a bound $d(f(y), f(x)) \le K d(y, x)$ near $x$ is continuous at $x$ (`continuousAt_of_locally_lipschitz`). A map that is locally Lipschitz on a compact set is Lipschitz on it (`LocallyLipschitzOn.exists_lipschitzOnWith_of_compact`).

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that in a topological group the sets $R_V$ and $L_V$ give the same topology on $G$, even when the uniformities differ.
2. (A) Let $G$ be a commutative topological group. Show that a sequence $(x_n)$ is Cauchy if and only if $x_{n+k} - x_n \to 0$ uniformly in $k \ge 0$.
3. (A) Show that the inversion is a uniform isomorphism from $G$ with the right uniformity to $G$ with the left uniformity.
4. (C) Show that the left and right uniformities agree if $G$ has a basis of neighbourhoods $V$ of $1$ with $xVx^{-1} = V$ for all $x$. Apply this to the group $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$ and decide whether it has such a basis. *Hint:* use the conjugates of a diagonal matrix.
5. (B) Show that $\mathbb{Z}^n$ is a discrete closed subgroup of $\mathbb{R}^n$, and that $\{m + n\sqrt 2 : m, n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$ that is not discrete.
6. (B) Let $f : G \to H$ be a continuous homomorphism of commutative topological groups. Show that $f$ maps Cauchy filters to Cauchy filters.
7. (B) In the group of the counterexample, show that $N = \{(1, b) : b \in \mathbb{R}\}$ is a closed normal subgroup isomorphic to $(\mathbb{R}, +)$, and that the left and right uniformities of $G$ induce the same uniformity on $N$.

### Notes and history

The uniform structure of a topological group is one of the examples from which Weil developed uniform spaces, in his monograph of 1937 on spaces with a uniform structure. The left and right uniformities appear in Chapter III of Bourbaki's "Topologie générale". A theorem of Birkhoff and Kakutani, from 1936, states that a Hausdorff topological group is metrizable if and only if $1$ has a countable basis of neighbourhoods.
