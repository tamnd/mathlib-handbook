# Finite fields

The finite fields are the one family of fields that the theory of this volume describes completely. Each finite field has $p^n$ elements for a prime $p$ and some $n \geq 1$. For each such number there is a field of this size, and it is unique up to isomorphism. Its group of units is cyclic, its subfields correspond to the divisors of $n$, and its Galois group over $\mathbb{F}_p$ is cyclic of order $n$, generated by the Frobenius map $x \mapsto x^p$. So the fundamental theorem of Chapter 5 takes its simplest form here.

Section 1 studies one finite field at a time: its size, the identity $x^q = x$, the factorization of $X^q - X$, the sums of powers, the squares, and the theorem of Chevalley and Warning on the number of solutions of a system of polynomial equations. Section 2 constructs the field with $p^n$ elements as a splitting field, proves its uniqueness, computes its Galois group and its subfields, counts the irreducible polynomials of each degree, and works out the field $\mathbb{F}_8$. It ends with the theorem of Wedderburn that a finite division ring is commutative.

The chapter needs the characteristic and the Frobenius map (Volume 6, Chapter 4), the theorem that a finite subgroup of the units of a field is cyclic (Volume 6, Chapter 5), splitting fields (Chapter 3), perfect fields (Chapter 4) and the fundamental theorem (Chapter 5).
