## Quaternion algebras

### Overview

The Hamilton quaternions are the real vector space with basis $1, i, j, k$ and the products $i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1$. They form a division ring that is not commutative. Mathlib builds them as a special case of a more general family. For a commutative ring $R$ and constants $c_1, c_2, c_3 \in R$, the quaternion algebra `QuaternionAlgebra R c₁ c₂ c₃` is the free $R$-module with basis $1, i, j, k$ and the relations

$$i^2 = c_1 + c_2 i, \qquad j^2 = c_3, \qquad ij = k, \qquad ji = c_2 j - k.$$

The Hamilton quaternions over $R$ are `Quaternion R`, the case $c_1 = -1$, $c_2 = 0$, $c_3 = -1$.

Section 1 uses `Quaternion R` as an example of an algebra that is not commutative, and Section 2 uses its conjugation as an example of a star ring. This section describes the full API: the components and the product, the conjugation, the norm, the inverse over an ordered field, and the universal property in the module `Mathlib.Algebra.QuaternionBasis`. The construction is a noncommutative relative of the quadratic algebras of Section 4: the subalgebra generated by $i$ is a copy of the quadratic algebra with $\omega^2 = c_1 + c_2\omega$.

### The library around it

#### The construction and the notation

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Quaternion` defines `QuaternionAlgebra R c₁ c₂ c₃` as a structure with four fields `QuaternionAlgebra.re`, `QuaternionAlgebra.imI`, `QuaternionAlgebra.imJ` and `QuaternionAlgebra.imK`. The element with fields $a_1, a_2, a_3, a_4$ stands for $a_1 + a_2 i + a_3 j + a_4 k$. After `open Quaternion`, the notation `ℍ[R,c₁,c₂,c₃]` stands for the general algebra, `ℍ[R,c₁,c₂]` for the case $c_2 = 0$ with the constants renamed, and `ℍ[R]` for `Quaternion R`. A reader must check which constants each lemma uses: most of the results with a norm are only for `ℍ[R]`.

The product is the formula `QuaternionAlgebra.mk_mul_mk`. It follows from the relations above by bilinearity, and the component lemmas `QuaternionAlgebra.re_mul`, `QuaternionAlgebra.imI_mul`, `QuaternionAlgebra.imJ_mul` and `QuaternionAlgebra.imK_mul` give each field of a product. The instance `QuaternionAlgebra.instRing` makes the type a ring, and `QuaternionAlgebra.instAlgebra` makes it an $S$-algebra for each $S$ that acts on $R$.

The embedding of $R$ is the coercion `QuaternionAlgebra.coe`, the element $r + 0i + 0j + 0k$. Its image is central (`QuaternionAlgebra.coe_commutes`), and a product with a coercion is a scalar multiple (`QuaternionAlgebra.coe_mul_eq_smul`). The simp normal form writes the scalars as coercions.

The pure part `QuaternionAlgebra.im` sets the real field to $0$ and keeps the others. So each $a$ is $\mathrm{re}(a) + \mathrm{im}(a)$ (`QuaternionAlgebra.re_add_im`), and $\mathrm{im}$ is idempotent (`QuaternionAlgebra.im_idem`). The linear structure has the usual names.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `QuaternionAlgebra.basisOneIJK` | the basis $1, i, j, k$ indexed by `Fin 4` |
| `QuaternionAlgebra.equivTuple` | the bijection with `Fin 4 → R` |
| `QuaternionAlgebra.finrank_eq_four` | the rank over $R$ is $4$, when $R$ has the strong rank condition |
| `Cardinal.mk_quaternion` | the cardinality of `ℍ[R]` is $\#R^4$ |

For $c_2 = 0$, the map `QuaternionAlgebra.swapEquiv` is an isomorphism from `ℍ[R,c₁,0,c₃]` to `ℍ[R,c₃,0,c₁]`. It sends $i$ to $j$ and $j$ to $i$, so it sends $k = ij$ to $ji = -k$. The relations of the target hold for the images: $j^2 = c_3$, $i^2 = c_1$, and $j$ and $i$ anticommute, as the new $i$ and $j$ must when $c_2 = 0$.

#### Conjugation

The conjugate of $a_1 + a_2 i + a_3 j + a_4 k$ is

$$(a_1 + c_2 a_2) - a_2 i - a_3 j - a_4 k,$$

by `QuaternionAlgebra.star_mk`. For $c_2 = 0$ this is the familiar sign change of the pure part. The extra term makes $\bar i = c_2 - i$, the other root of $X^2 - c_2 X - c_1$, as for the quadratic algebras of Section 4. The instance `QuaternionAlgebra.instStarRing` makes the algebra a star ring, so $\overline{ab} = \bar b \bar a$. The order of the factors changes, and so the conjugation is an algebra isomorphism to the opposite algebra, `QuaternionAlgebra.starAe`.

The two key identities say that $a + \bar a$ and $a \bar a$ are scalars:

$$a + \bar a = 2\,\mathrm{re}(a) + c_2\, a_2, \qquad a \bar a = \mathrm{re}(a \bar a).$$

The first is `QuaternionAlgebra.self_add_star`, and it follows at once from `QuaternionAlgebra.star_mk`. The second is `QuaternionAlgebra.mul_star_eq_coe`. Its proof computes the three pure fields of $a \bar a$ with the product formula and finds $0$ in each. The companion `QuaternionAlgebra.star_mul_eq_coe` treats $\bar a a$. Each element commutes with its conjugate, because $\bar a = (a + \bar a) - a$ is a scalar minus $a$. This is the instance `QuaternionAlgebra.instIsStarNormal`, so each quaternion is normal in the sense of Section 2.

#### The norm of a Hamilton quaternion

For `ℍ[R]`, the namespace `Quaternion` gives the norm `Quaternion.normSq`, a monoid homomorphism with zero from `ℍ[R]` to $R$. Its definition is $N(a) = \mathrm{re}(a \bar a)$ (`Quaternion.normSq_def`), and the computation gives the sum of four squares:

$$N(a_1 + a_2 i + a_3 j + a_4 k) = a_1^2 + a_2^2 + a_3^2 + a_4^2,$$

which is `Quaternion.normSq_def'`. The multiplicativity $N(ab) = N(a)N(b)$ follows from $a \bar a = N(a)$: $ab\,\overline{ab} = a (b \bar b) \bar a = N(b)\, a \bar a = N(a)N(b)$, since $N(b)$ is central. Over $\mathbb{Z}$, this gives the four-square identity: a product of two sums of four squares is a sum of four squares. The lemmas `Quaternion.self_mul_star` and `Quaternion.star_mul_self` say $a \bar a = \bar a a = N(a)$, and `Quaternion.normSq_add` expands $N(a + b)$.

The pure part behaves like a square root of a negative number. For a pure quaternion $v$, $v^2 = -N(v)$ (`Quaternion.im_sq`). With an ordered ring of scalars, a quaternion $a$ satisfies $a^2 = -N(a)$ if and only if $\mathrm{re}(a) = 0$ (`Quaternion.sq_eq_neg_normSq`). The proof writes $a = r + v$ with $v$ pure. Then $a^2 = r^2 - N(v) + 2rv$, and $-N(a) = -r^2 - N(v)$. The real parts agree only when $2r^2 = 0$, so $r = 0$.

#### Division over an ordered field

Over a linearly ordered commutative ring, the norm is a sum of squares, so $N(a) \geq 0$ (`Quaternion.normSq_nonneg`), and $N(a) = 0$ only for $a = 0$ (`Quaternion.normSq_eq_zero`). The proof: each square is nonnegative, so a zero sum forces each square to be zero. Then `ℍ[R]` has no zero divisors (`Quaternion.instNoZeroDivisors`), because $ab = 0$ gives $N(a)N(b) = 0$, and it is a domain (`Quaternion.instIsDomain`).

Over a field, the inverse is defined by $a^{-1} = N(a)^{-1} \bar a$ (`Quaternion.inv_def`). Over a linearly ordered field, this is a true inverse for $a \neq 0$, since $a \cdot N(a)^{-1} \bar a = N(a)^{-1} a \bar a = 1$. So `ℍ[R]` is a group with zero (`Quaternion.instGroupWithZero`) and a division ring (`Quaternion.instDivisionRing`). The norm respects the inverse (`Quaternion.normSq_inv`). The ordered field is necessary for this proof: over $\mathbb{C}$, the element $1 + \sqrt{-1}\, i$ has norm $1 + (\sqrt{-1})^2 = 0$, and the algebra is isomorphic to the $2 \times 2$ matrices.

#### Bases and the universal property

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.QuaternionBasis` describes the algebra maps out of a quaternion algebra. A structure `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis A c₁ c₂ c₃` in an $R$-algebra $A$ is a triple $i, j, k$ of elements of $A$ that satisfy the four relations. Its fields are the elements `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.i`, `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.j` and `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.k`, with the relations `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.i_mul_i`, `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.j_mul_j`, `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.i_mul_j` and `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.j_mul_i`. The element $k$ is fixed by $i$ and $j$, so a basis is fixed by $i$ and $j$ (`QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.ext`). The standard basis of the algebra itself is `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.self`.

The other products follow from the four relations. They are listed here.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.i_mul_k` | $ik = c_1 j + c_2 k$ |
| `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.k_mul_i` | $ki = -c_1 j$ |
| `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.j_mul_k` | $jk = c_2 c_3 - c_3 i$ |
| `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.k_mul_j` | $kj = c_3 i$ |
| `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.k_mul_k` | $k^2 = -c_1 c_3$ |

For example, when $c_2 = 0$ the relation $ji = -k$ gives $k^2 = (ij)(ij) = i(ji)j = -i(ij)j = -i^2 j^2 = -c_1 c_3$. For Hamilton's constants this is $k^2 = -1$. The general case has extra terms in $c_2$ that cancel.

A basis $q$ in $A$ gives the map $a_1 + a_2 i + a_3 j + a_4 k \mapsto a_1 + a_2 q_i + a_3 q_j + a_4 q_k$ (`QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.lift`), and the relations make it an algebra map (`QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.liftHom`). Its range is the subalgebra generated by $q_i$, $q_j$ and $q_k$ (`QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.range_liftHom`). Conversely, an algebra map $f$ sends the standard basis to a basis of $A$, and `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis.compHom` composes a basis with an algebra map. Together these give the universal property `QuaternionAlgebra.lift`:

$$\mathrm{Basis}(A, c_1, c_2, c_3) \simeq \big(\mathbb{H}[R, c_1, c_2, c_3] \to_R A\big).$$

So two algebra maps out of a quaternion algebra are equal when they agree at $i$ and $j$. This is the extensionality lemma `QuaternionAlgebra.hom_ext`, and `Quaternion.hom_ext` is its form for `ℍ[R]`. A typical use builds the map $\mathbb{H} \to M_2(\mathbb{C})$ by giving two complex matrices $I$ and $J$ with $I^2 = J^2 = -1$ and $JI = -IJ$.

#### Common errors

1. The general relation is $i^2 = c_1 + c_2 i$, and the conjugate has the term $c_2 a_2$ in its real part. A computation that drops $c_2$ is only correct for `ℍ[R,c₁,c₃]` with $c_2 = 0$.
2. The norm `Quaternion.normSq` and the division ring structure exist only for the Hamilton quaternions `ℍ[R]`. The division ring needs a linearly ordered field, not only a field: over $\mathbb{C}$ the quaternions have zero divisors.
3. The conjugation reverses products. So `star` on `ℍ[R]` is not an algebra map to `ℍ[R]`, and the library gives the conjugation as `QuaternionAlgebra.starAe`, into the opposite algebra.

### Exercises

1. (A) Use `QuaternionAlgebra.mk_mul_mk` to check $ij = k$, $jk = i$ and $ki = j$ in `ℍ[ℝ]`.
2. (A) Prove that $N(1 + i + j + k) = 4$ with `Quaternion.normSq_def'`, and compute $(1 + i + j + k)^{-1}$ with `Quaternion.inv_def`.
3. (B) Show that each pure quaternion $v$ with $N(v) = 1$ satisfies $v^2 = -1$, and deduce that $x^2 = -1$ has infinitely many solutions in `ℍ[ℝ]`.
4. (B) Show that the center of `ℍ[ℝ]` is the image of `QuaternionAlgebra.coe`.
5. (B) Give a `QuaternionAlgebra.Basis` in the $2 \times 2$ complex matrices for Hamilton's constants, and use `QuaternionAlgebra.lift` to get an injective algebra map from `ℍ[ℝ]`.
6. (C) Use `Quaternion.normSq` on quaternions with integer coefficients to show that the set of sums of four integer squares is closed under multiplication.
