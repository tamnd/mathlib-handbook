# Preadditive, additive and linear categories

Volume 24 ended with the definitions of preadditive, additive and abelian categories. This chapter studies the first two of these in more depth, as a base for the rest of the volume. Section 1 treats finite biproducts. It proves that one equation, the total identity, detects a biproduct, that morphisms between biproducts are matrices, and that Gaussian elimination works in every preadditive category. It shows that binary biproducts alone fix the addition of morphisms, by the Eckmann-Hilton argument, and it builds the category of matrices `Mat_ C`, the free category with finite biproducts on $C$. Section 2 adds scalars from a ring and gives linear categories and linear functors, and the Yoneda embedding of a preadditive category into functors with values in abelian groups. It then treats idempotents, idempotent complete categories and the Karoubi envelope, which adds the images of all idempotents. Section 3 shows that the envelope keeps finite biproducts, that categories of functors, of chain complexes and of simplicial objects are idempotent complete when their target is, and that functors extend from a category to its envelope. Section 4 is a guide to the rest of the preadditive API: the tests for monomorphisms and epimorphisms by zero, the preadditive structure on opposites, functor categories, comma categories, categories of algebras and on Ind objects, the uniqueness of group objects in an additive category, and the right Freyd category, a quotient of the arrow category by homotopy that adds cokernels.

The chapter needs Volume 24: categories and functors from Chapters 1 and 2, the Yoneda lemma from Chapter 3, limits from Chapter 4, and the preadditive categories and binary biproducts of Chapter 8. The examples use rings from Volume 6 and modules from Volume 7.

Chapter 2 uses the matrix calculus in each diagram chase in an abelian category, and the fact that an abelian category is idempotent complete. The additive functors of Section 1 become the exact functors of Chapter 2. The shifts and triangles of Chapter 5 live in additive categories, and the Karoubi envelope appears again in the homological algebra of Volume 26.
