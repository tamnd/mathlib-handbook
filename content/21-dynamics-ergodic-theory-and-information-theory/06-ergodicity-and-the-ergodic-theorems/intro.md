# Ergodicity and the ergodic theorems

This chapter studies the maps that cannot be split into two invariant pieces of positive measure, and proves that for such maps the time average of an observable along almost every orbit equals its space average. Ergodicity is the hypothesis that turns the recurrence of Chapter 5 into a statement about frequencies, and the ergodic theorems are the first limit theorems of the subject.

Section 1 defines pre-ergodic, ergodic and quasi ergodic maps, proves that ergodicity is the same as the triviality of invariant functions and that ergodic measures are the extreme invariant probabilities, and proves with Fourier series that irrational rotations and the maps $x \mapsto nx$ of the circle are ergodic. Section 2 extends the definition to actions of monoids and groups, proves that a set almost invariant under a dense set of elements is trivial, and deduces the ergodicity of translations with dense powers and of endomorphisms of compact groups. Section 3 defines Birkhoff sums and averages, proves the maximal inequality, the mean ergodic theorem of von Neumann and the pointwise theorem of Birkhoff, and constructs invariant measures for continuous maps of compact spaces.

The chapter needs Volume 18 for measures and integrals, Volume 19 for Fourier series, Volume 16 for Hilbert spaces and weak topologies, Volume 14 for topological groups, and Volume 20 for probability measures. It uses the measure preserving maps of Chapter 5. In Mathlib ergodicity is the structure `Ergodic`, group actions use the class `ErgodicSMul`, and the averages are `birkhoffSum` and `birkhoffAverage`. The pointwise theorem itself has no declaration in this edition.
