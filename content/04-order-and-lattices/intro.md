# Order and lattices

This volume teaches the order theory that the other volumes of the handbook use. Order theory studies a relation $\le$ and the structures that it gives: bounds, suprema and infima, monotone maps, lattices and fixed points. Most of the results are short, but they occur everywhere. The supremum of a set of reals, the closure of a set in a topological space, the subgroup generated by a set and the limit of a sequence all have a form in order theory. Mathlib states each of them once, in the most general order where it holds, and then uses the general form in each special case. So a reader who knows the order theory of this volume can read many proofs of the other volumes with less effort.

The volume has eight chapters.

1. **Orders.** Preorders, partial orders and linear orders, with the classes `Preorder`, `PartialOrder` and `LinearOrder`. Upper and lower bounds, least upper bounds, maximal and greatest elements, and the order dual. Well-founded orders, induction on a well-founded order, chains and Zorn's lemma.
2. **Monotone functions and order maps.** The predicates `Monotone`, `Antitone` and `StrictMono`, and the bundled maps `α →o β`, `α ↪o β` and `α ≃o β`.
3. **Lattices.** Lattices as orders and as algebras, bounded lattices, distributive lattices, complements and Boolean algebras. Order ideals and order filters, and the filters of sets that the topology volumes use.
4. **Complete lattices and fixed points.** The supremum and the infimum of an arbitrary set, the theorem of Knaster and Tarski, its use in the theorem of Cantor, Schröder and Bernstein, and the fixed point theorem of Kleene.
5. **Conditionally complete lattices.** The supremum of a nonempty set that is bounded above, the junk values that Mathlib gives in the other cases, and the completeness of the real numbers in the form of nested intervals.
6. **Galois connections.** Pairs of adjoint monotone maps, the image and the preimage, the floor function, Galois insertions and closure operators.
7. **Intervals.** The nine kinds of intervals of an order, order-connected sets and the finite intervals of `ℕ` and `ℤ`.
8. **Ordered groups and the absolute value.** Groups and monoids with an order that addition keeps, the absolute value, the triangle inequality and the positive and negative parts.

The chapters are in an order that a first reading can follow. Chapters 1 and 2 are the base of all the others. Chapter 3 needs Chapters 1 and 2. Chapters 4, 5 and 6 need Chapter 3, and they can be read in any order. Chapters 7 and 8 need only Chapters 1 and 2, with the lattice operations of Chapter 3 at a few places. The section on filters in Chapter 3 uses Zorn's lemma from Chapter 1.

The reader needs Volume 1, for the notation of sets, functions, classes and instances, and the elementary facts on the numbers of Volume 2. No earlier knowledge of order theory is necessary. Each section gives the definitions in mathematical form, proves the theorems in full and computes examples, and then shows the form of the definitions and theorems in Mathlib. The analysis and the topology volumes use the supremum, the intervals, the absolute value and the filters of this volume. The algebra volumes use the lattices of subobjects and the Galois connections of this volume.

The standard references for the subject are the book of Davey and Priestley, *Introduction to Lattices and Order*, and the book of Birkhoff, *Lattice Theory*. The book *Mathematics in Lean* has a short chapter on orders that is a good companion to Chapters 1 to 3.
