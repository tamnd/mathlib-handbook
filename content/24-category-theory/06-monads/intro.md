# Monads

A monad on a category $C$ is an endofunctor $T$ with a unit $\mathrm{id} \Rightarrow T$ and a multiplication $TT \Rightarrow T$ that satisfy the laws of a monoid. Every adjunction gives a monad, and every monad comes from an adjunction. The algebras of a monad describe algebraic structure in the language of categories: the algebras of the free group monad on sets are the groups, those of the list monad are the monoids, and those of the power set monad are the complete join semilattices.

Section 1 defines monads, their morphisms and their Eilenberg-Moore algebras. It builds the monad of an adjunction, the free algebra adjunction and the comparison functor, and it explains how Lean's own class `Monad` gives a monad on the category of types. Section 2 builds the Kleisli category, shows that the Kleisli and the Eilenberg-Moore adjunctions are the initial and the terminal adjunctions with a given monad, proves that the forgetful functor from algebras creates limits, and proves Beck's monadicity theorem. The examples show that groups are monadic over sets and that topological spaces are not.

The chapter needs functors and natural transformations from Chapter 2, limits and coequalizers from Chapter 4 and adjunctions with the triangle identities from Chapter 5. The examples use groups (Volume 5) and complete lattices (Volume 4, Chapter 4). Chapter 7 shows that a monad is a monoid object in the monoidal category of endofunctors. Volume 25 continues the study of categories with abelian and triangulated categories and sheaves. The comonad of an adjunction gives the standard resolutions of homological algebra, the subject of Volume 26.
