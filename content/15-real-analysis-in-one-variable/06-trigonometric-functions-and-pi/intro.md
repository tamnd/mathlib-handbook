# Trigonometric functions and pi

The sine and the cosine are the second family of special functions of the volume, after the exponential and the logarithm. This chapter defines them from the exponential series, as Mathlib does, and proves all their properties from that definition. No geometry is assumed: the angle, the circle and the number $\pi$ come out of the analysis.

Section 1 defines $\sin$ and $\cos$ as the real parts of the complex functions built from $\exp$, derives the Pythagorean identity and the addition formulas, and computes the derivatives. It then defines $\pi$ as twice the unique zero of the cosine in $[1, 2]$, with the estimates $\cos 1 > 0$ and $\cos 2 < 0$ from the series, and proves the periodicity of $\sin$ and $\cos$, the description of their zeros and the limit $\sin x / x \to 1$. Section 2 defines the tangent and the inverse functions $\arcsin$, $\arccos$ and $\arctan$ with their derivatives. It computes $3.14 < \pi < 3.15$ from nested square roots, as Mathlib proves `Real.pi_gt_d2`, proves the series of Leibniz for $\pi/4$ and the inequality of Jordan. Section 3 proves that $e$ and $\pi$ are irrational, the second by the method of Niven, and describes the topology of the irrational numbers. Section 4 defines the type `Real.Angle` of angles as $\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}$, with its sine, cosine and tangent, the representative `Real.Angle.toReal` in $(-\pi, \pi]$ and the sign of an angle. Section 5 computes the derivatives and the order properties of $\sinh$ and $\cosh$, and defines $\operatorname{arsinh}$, $\operatorname{arcosh}$ and $\operatorname{artanh}$ by logarithms, with their inverse identities and derivatives. Section 6 gives the power series of $\sin$, $\cos$, $\sinh$ and $\cosh$, the function $\operatorname{sinc}$, Euler's product for $\sin \pi z$ and the partial fraction expansion of $\pi \cot \pi z$.

The chapter needs the series of Chapter 1, the intermediate value theorem of Chapter 2, the derivative rules, the mean value theorem and the inverse function rule of Chapter 3, and the exponential series of Chapter 5. Sections 1 to 5 use no integral: each proof that is usually written with an integral is written there with the mean value theorem. Section 6 uses the complex sine and cotangent, because the sine product and the cotangent series are statements about complex functions. Chapter 7 uses $\pi$ in Stirling's formula. The full theory of the complex trigonometric functions belongs to Volume 19, and the integral proofs to Volume 16.

In Mathlib the definitions of `Real.sin`, `Real.cos` and `Real.tan` and their algebraic identities sit in `Mathlib.Analysis.Complex.Trigonometric`. The facts that need $\pi$, derivatives or inequalities sit in the files `Mathlib.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.Trigonometric.*` and `Mathlib.Analysis.Real.Pi.*`, which are the subject of this chapter.
