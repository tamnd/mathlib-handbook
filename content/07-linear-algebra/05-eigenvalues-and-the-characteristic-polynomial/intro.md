# Eigenvalues and the characteristic polynomial

An eigenvector of a linear map is a nonzero vector that the map only stretches. The scalar of the stretch is an eigenvalue. In a basis of eigenvectors, the matrix of the map is diagonal, and its powers and its polynomials are easy to compute. This chapter finds the eigenvalues as the roots of a polynomial and studies the polynomials that a map satisfies. Section 1 defines the eigenvalues and the eigenspaces, proves that eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are independent, and defines the characteristic polynomial $\det(X I - A)$. Section 2 proves the theorem of Cayley and Hamilton, compares the characteristic polynomial with the minimal polynomial, and states the decomposition of Jordan and Chevalley as Mathlib has it. Section 3 has two applications of the characteristic polynomial: families of endomorphisms that depend linearly on a parameter, with their nil rank, and the linear recurrences, whose geometric solutions come from the roots of a characteristic polynomial. Section 4 treats the trace of an endomorphism and of its restrictions, and gives more results on characteristic polynomials: the change of rings, the theorem of Cayley and Hamilton for finitely generated modules, the discriminant and the Hessenberg form. Section 5 studies the Hermitian and the positive definite matrices, the Schur complement and the disks of Gershgorin. Section 6 studies the annihilating polynomials of an endomorphism and the semisimple endomorphisms.

The chapter needs the determinant of Chapter 4 over the ring of polynomials, and the polynomials of Volume 6.
