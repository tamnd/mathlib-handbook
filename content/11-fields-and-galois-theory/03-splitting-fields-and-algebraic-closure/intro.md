# Splitting fields and algebraic closure

A polynomial over $K$ need not have a root in $K$. This chapter builds fields in which it has all its roots. Galois theory needs such a field for each polynomial: the Galois group of $f$ acts on the roots of $f$, so the roots must exist somewhere first.

Section 1 constructs the splitting field of one polynomial. The construction of Kronecker adjoins one root of an irreducible factor as the quotient $K[X]/(g)$. Repeated, it gives a field of degree at most $n!$ in which $f$ is a product of linear factors and which the roots generate. The main theorem says that the splitting field is unique up to isomorphism, and it proves more: an isomorphism of the base fields extends to the splitting fields. Chapter 5 uses this theorem to characterize normal extensions.

Section 2 treats all polynomials at once. An algebraically closed field is one in which each nonconstant polynomial has a root. An algebraic closure of $K$ is an algebraic extension that is algebraically closed. The section proves that it exists, with the construction of Artin by one large polynomial ring and a maximal ideal, and that it is unique up to isomorphism. The section also states the classification of algebraically closed fields by the characteristic and the transcendence degree.

The chapter needs Chapter 2: minimal polynomials, the structure of $K(\alpha)$ and the theorem on embeddings of $K(\alpha)$. It also uses maximal ideals and the lemma of Zorn from Volume 6, Chapter 2. Chapter 4 studies when the roots in a splitting field are distinct, and Chapter 7 builds the finite fields as splitting fields of $X^{p^n} - X$.
