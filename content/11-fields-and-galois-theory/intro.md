# Fields and Galois theory

This volume teaches the theory of field extensions $L/K$ and its form in Mathlib. It measures an extension by its degree, the dimension of $L$ over $K$, and describes each element of $L$ by its minimal polynomial over $K$. The main theorem is the fundamental theorem of Galois theory. For a finite Galois extension $L/K$ with group $G$, it gives a bijection between the fields between $K$ and $L$ and the subgroups of $G$. The volume ends with two classical uses of the theorem: the structure of the finite fields, and the theorem of Abel and Ruffini that the general equation of degree five has no solution by radicals.

Each main theorem has a complete proof on paper, and each section computes examples, mostly over $\mathbb{Q}$ and over the finite fields $\mathbb{F}_p$. Three examples come back through the volume: the field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 2, \sqrt 3)$; the splitting field of $X^3 - 2$, whose Galois group is the symmetric group $S_3$; and the field $\mathbb{F}_8$ with eight elements.

Chapter 1 defines field extensions, intermediate fields and the degree, and proves the tower law. Chapter 2 defines algebraic and transcendental elements and the minimal polynomial, and computes norms and traces. Chapter 3 constructs splitting fields and the algebraic closure. Chapter 4 studies separability, perfect fields and the primitive element theorem. Chapter 5 proves the theorem of Artin and the fundamental theorem. Chapter 6 computes the Galois groups of polynomials, with the discriminant as a tool. Chapter 7 classifies the finite fields and their extensions. Chapter 8 studies roots of unity, cyclotomic and Kummer extensions, and solvability by radicals as far as Mathlib has it.

The volume needs Volume 5 for groups, group actions, quotient groups and the symmetric groups, Volume 6 for rings, ideals, fields, the characteristic and the polynomial ring, and Volume 7 for vector spaces and their dimension. Volume 9 treats Gauss's lemma and the irreducibility criteria in full generality; this volume proves the special cases that it needs. Volume 12 on algebraic number theory builds on Chapters 2, 5 and 8.

In Mathlib an extension of fields is two types `K` and `L` with the instances `[Field K] [Field L] [Algebra K L]`. The intermediate fields form the complete lattice `IntermediateField K L`. The degree is `Module.finrank K L`, the minimal polynomial is `minpoly K x`, and a Galois extension is the class `IsGalois K L`. Each chapter explains these choices and their junk values, for example `minpoly K x = 0` when `x` is not algebraic and that `Module.finrank K L = 0` when the extension is infinite. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the Mathlib boxes give the names and the signatures of the library.
