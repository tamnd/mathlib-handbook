## The projective spectrum and affine space

### Overview

Affine schemes come from rings. Projective schemes come from graded rings. Classical projective space $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ is the set of lines through the origin in $k^{n+1}$, and a point is a ratio $[a_0 : \dots : a_n]$. A polynomial does not give a function on such ratios, but a quotient $g/h$ of two homogeneous polynomials of the same degree does. The construction $\operatorname{Proj}$ turns this observation into a scheme: its points are homogeneous primes, and its functions are fractions of degree $0$.

This section defines $\operatorname{Proj} A$ for a graded ring $A = \bigoplus_{n \geq 0} A_n$, shows that it is covered by the affine schemes $\operatorname{Spec} A_{(f)}$, and constructs its morphisms to $\operatorname{Spec} A_0$ and its functoriality. It ends with affine space $\mathbb{A}^n_S$ over an arbitrary scheme $S$.

### Definitions

A *graded ring* is a ring $A$ with additive subgroups $A_n$, $n \geq 0$, such that $A = \bigoplus_n A_n$, $1 \in A_0$ and $A_m A_n \subseteq A_{m+n}$. An element of some $A_n$ is *homogeneous* of degree $n$. An ideal is *homogeneous* when it is generated by homogeneous elements, or equivalently when it contains the homogeneous components of each of its elements. The *irrelevant ideal* is $A_+ = \bigoplus_{n > 0} A_n$. The main example is $k[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ graded by total degree, with $A_+ = (x_0, \dots, x_n)$.

**Definition (Projective spectrum).** The *projective spectrum* $\operatorname{Proj} A$ is the set of homogeneous prime ideals $\mathfrak p$ of $A$ with $A_+ \not\subseteq \mathfrak p$. For a homogeneous ideal $I$, let $V_+(I)$ be the set of $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Proj} A$ with $I \subseteq \mathfrak p$. The sets $V_+(I)$ are the closed sets of the *Zariski topology*. For a homogeneous $f$, the *basic open* $D_+(f)$ is the set of $\mathfrak p$ with $f \notin \mathfrak p$.

The primes that contain $A_+$ are left out on purpose. For $k[x_0, \dots, x_n]$ the ideal $A_+$ corresponds to the origin of $k^{n+1}$, which is not a line.

As for $\operatorname{Spec}$, the sets $V_+(I)$ satisfy $V_+(I) \cup V_+(J) = V_+(IJ)$ and $\bigcap_\lambda V_+(I_\lambda) = V_+(\sum_\lambda I_\lambda)$, so they form a topology. Also $D_+(f) \cap D_+(g) = D_+(fg)$ because the points are prime, and the sets $D_+(f)$ form a basis.

For a homogeneous $f$ of degree $d > 0$, let $A_f$ be the localization, graded by $\deg(a/f^n) = \deg a - nd$. Its degree $0$ part is the ring
$$A_{(f)} = \{ a/f^n : n \geq 0,\ a \in A_{nd} \}.$$
For $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Proj} A$, let $A_{(\mathfrak p)}$ be the ring of fractions $a/s$ with $a, s \in A_i$ for one $i$ and $s \notin \mathfrak p$. It is a local ring.

**Definition (Structure sheaf of Proj).** For an open $U \subseteq \operatorname{Proj} A$, a section of $\mathcal{O}(U)$ is a function $s$ that sends each $\mathfrak p \in U$ to $s(\mathfrak p) \in A_{(\mathfrak p)}$, and is *locally a fraction*: each point of $U$ has an open neighbourhood $V \subseteq U$ and elements $a, h \in A_i$ for one $i$, with $h \notin \mathfrak q$ and $s(\mathfrak q) = a/h$ for all $\mathfrak q \in V$.

This is the definition of the structure sheaf of $\operatorname{Spec}$ with fractions of degree $0$ in place of all fractions. The condition is local, so $\mathcal{O}$ is a sheaf, and its stalk at $\mathfrak p$ is $A_{(\mathfrak p)}$ by the argument for $\operatorname{Spec}$.

**Definition (Affine space).** Let $S$ be a scheme and $n$ an index set. *Affine space* over $S$ is the fiber product
$$\mathbb{A}^n_S = S \times_{\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}} \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[x_i : i \in n],$$
with its projection to $S$. The *coordinate* $x_i$ is the pullback of $x_i$ to $\Gamma(\mathbb{A}^n_S, \mathbb{A}^n_S)$.

The scheme $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$ is terminal, so this fiber product is a product. Its definition works over every base, not only over a field.

### Theorems and proofs

**Lemma (The basic opens cover Proj).** Let $\{f_\lambda\}$ be homogeneous elements of positive degree whose ideal contains $A_+$. Then the sets $D_+(f_\lambda)$ cover $\operatorname{Proj} A$.

*Proof.* Let $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Proj} A$ lie in no $D_+(f_\lambda)$. Then each $f_\lambda$ lies in $\mathfrak p$, so $A_+ \subseteq (f_\lambda)_\lambda \subseteq \mathfrak p$. This contradicts the definition of $\operatorname{Proj} A$. $\square$

**Theorem (The affine pieces).** Let $f \in A_d$ with $d > 0$. The map
$$\psi : D_+(f) \to \operatorname{Spec} A_{(f)}, \qquad \psi(\mathfrak p) = \mathfrak p A_f \cap A_{(f)}$$
is a homeomorphism. Its inverse sends $\mathfrak q$ to the ideal $\varphi(\mathfrak q)$ generated by the homogeneous $a \in A_i$ with $a^d/f^i \in \mathfrak q$.

*Proof.* *Step 1: $\psi$ is well defined.* Since $f \notin \mathfrak p$, the ideal $\mathfrak p A_f$ is prime, and its contraction to the subring $A_{(f)}$ is prime.

*Step 2: a key equivalence.* Let $\mathfrak p \in D_+(f)$ and $a \in A_i$. Then $a \in \mathfrak p$ if and only if $a^d / f^i \in \psi(\mathfrak p)$. One direction is clear. Conversely, if $a^d/f^i = x/f^n$ with $x \in \mathfrak p$, then $f^N (f^n a^d - f^i x) = 0$ for some $N$, so $f^{N+n} a^d \in \mathfrak p$. As $f \notin \mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak p$ is prime, $a \in \mathfrak p$.

*Step 3: $\varphi(\mathfrak q)$ is in $D_+(f)$.* Let $P_i = \{ a \in A_i : a^d/f^i \in \mathfrak q \}$. For $a, b \in P_i$, expand $(a + b)^{2d}/f^{2i}$. Each term $\binom{2d}{k} a^k b^{2d - k}/f^{2i}$ has $k \geq d$ or $2d - k \geq d$, so it is a product of $a^d/f^i$ or $b^d/f^i$ with an element of $A_{(f)}$, and it lies in $\mathfrak q$. So $((a+b)^d/f^i)^2 \in \mathfrak q$, and $a + b \in P_i$ because $\mathfrak q$ is prime. For $c \in A_j$, $(ca)^d/f^{i+j} = (c^d/f^j)(a^d/f^i) \in \mathfrak q$. So $\varphi(\mathfrak q) = \bigoplus_i P_i$ is a homogeneous ideal. For homogeneous $a \in A_i$, $b \in A_j$ with $ab \in \varphi(\mathfrak q)$, the product $(a^d/f^i)(b^d/f^j)$ lies in $\mathfrak q$, so $a$ or $b$ lies in $\varphi(\mathfrak q)$. For a homogeneous ideal this test on homogeneous elements implies primality. Finally $f^d/f^d = 1 \notin \mathfrak q$, so $f \notin \varphi(\mathfrak q)$, and in particular $A_+ \not\subseteq \varphi(\mathfrak q)$.

*Step 4: the maps are inverse.* By Step 2, $\varphi(\psi(\mathfrak p))$ and $\mathfrak p$ have the same homogeneous elements, so they are equal. Let $b/f^j \in A_{(f)}$ with $b \in A_{jd}$, and put $\mathfrak p = \varphi(\mathfrak q)$. Then $b/f^j \in \mathfrak q$ if and only if $b^d/f^{jd} \in \mathfrak q$, since $\mathfrak q$ is prime, if and only if $b \in \mathfrak p$. By Step 2 applied to $\mathfrak p$, this holds if and only if $b^d/f^{jd} \in \psi(\mathfrak p)$, that is, if and only if $b/f^j \in \psi(\mathfrak p)$. So $\psi(\varphi(\mathfrak q)) = \mathfrak q$.

*Step 5: continuity.* For $g \in A_e$, Step 2 gives $\psi(D_+(f) \cap D_+(g)) = D(g^d/f^e)$. The sets $D_+(fg)$ form a basis of $D_+(f)$ and the sets $D(b/f^j)$ form a basis of $\operatorname{Spec} A_{(f)}$, and since $D(b/f^j) = D(b^d/f^{jd})$ both bases correspond under $\psi$. So $\psi$ is a homeomorphism. $\square$

**Theorem (Proj is a scheme).** The ringed space $(\operatorname{Proj} A, \mathcal{O})$ is a scheme. For $f \in A_d$ with $d > 0$, the open subscheme $D_+(f)$ is isomorphic to $\operatorname{Spec} A_{(f)}$, and $\mathcal{O}(D_+(f)) \cong A_{(f)}$.

*Proof.* Let $\mathfrak q = \psi(\mathfrak p)$. A degree $0$ fraction $a/h$ with $h \notin \mathfrak p$ equals $(a h^{d-1}/f^i)/(h^d/f^i)$ in $A_{(f)}$, where $a, h \in A_i$. The denominator $h^d/f^i$ lies outside $\mathfrak q$ by Step 2. This gives an isomorphism $A_{(\mathfrak p)} \cong (A_{(f)})_{\mathfrak q}$, and so sends locally fractional functions on $D_+(f)$ to locally fractional functions on $\operatorname{Spec} A_{(f)}$. Both sheaves are defined by the same local condition, so $\psi$ extends to an isomorphism of ringed spaces. By the lemma, these affine opens cover $\operatorname{Proj} A$. $\square$

**Proposition (The map to Spec of the degree 0 part).** There is a morphism $\operatorname{Proj} A \to \operatorname{Spec} A_0$, given on each $D_+(f)$ by the inclusion $A_0 \to A_{(f)}$, $a \mapsto a/1$. It is separated.

*Proof.* The maps $A_0 \to A_{(f)}$ agree on the overlaps $D_+(fg)$, so they glue. For separatedness use the criterion: a morphism is separated when the target is affine and the source has an affine cover $\{U_\lambda\}$ with each $U_\lambda \cap U_\mu$ affine and $\Gamma(U_\lambda) \otimes \Gamma(U_\mu) \to \Gamma(U_\lambda \cap U_\mu)$ surjective; then the diagonal is a closed immersion on each $U_\lambda \times U_\mu$. Take $f \in A_d$, $g \in A_e$, with $d, e > 0$. The intersection $D_+(f) \cap D_+(g) = D_+(fg)$ is affine. Let $c/(fg)^n \in A_{(fg)}$ with $c \in A_{n(d+e)}$. Then
$$\frac{c}{(fg)^n} = \frac{c\, g^{n(d-1)}}{f^{n(e+1)}} \cdot \frac{f^{ne}}{g^{nd}}.$$
The first factor lies in $A_{(f)}$, since $\deg(c\, g^{n(d-1)}) = n(d+e) + ne(d-1) = n(e+1)d$. The second factor lies in $A_{(g)}$, since $\deg f^{ne} = nde$. Multiply out to check the identity: the numerator is $c\, f^{ne} g^{n(d-1)}$ and the denominator is $f^{n(e+1)} g^{nd}$, and the quotient is $c / (f^n g^n)$. So $A_{(f)} \otimes_{A_0} A_{(g)} \to A_{(fg)}$ is surjective. $\square$

**Remark (Properness).** When $A$ is a finitely generated $A_0$-algebra, the morphism $\operatorname{Proj} A \to \operatorname{Spec} A_0$ is of finite type and universally closed, and so proper. Finitely many homogeneous generators $f_1, \dots, f_r$ of positive degree give a finite cover by $D_+(f_i)$, and each $A_{(f_i)}$ is of finite type over $A_0$. Universal closedness is proved with the existence part of the valuative criterion, which belongs with the morphisms of the next chapter.

**Proposition (Functoriality).** Let $\varphi : A \to B$ be a map of graded rings, with $\varphi(A_n) \subseteq B_n$, such that $B_+$ is contained in the ideal $\varphi(A_+) B$. Then $\mathfrak q \mapsto \varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$ is a continuous map $\operatorname{Proj} B \to \operatorname{Proj} A$, and it underlies a morphism of schemes. The construction sends composites to composites in the reverse order.

*Proof.* The inverse image of a homogeneous prime under a graded map is a homogeneous prime. If $A_+ \subseteq \varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$, then $\varphi(A_+) B \subseteq \mathfrak q$, so $B_+ \subseteq \mathfrak q$, which is false. So the map lands in $\operatorname{Proj} A$. The inverse image of $D_+(f)$ is $D_+(\varphi(f))$, so the map is continuous. On these opens the ring maps $A_{(f)} \to B_{(\varphi(f))}$, $a/f^n \mapsto \varphi(a)/\varphi(f)^n$, are compatible and glue. The inverse image of a composite is the composite of inverse images in the reverse order. $\square$

**Theorem (Universal property of affine space).** Let $X$ be a scheme over $S$. The map that sends an $S$-morphism $g : X \to \mathbb{A}^n_S$ to the family $(g^* x_i)_{i \in n}$ is a bijection
$$\operatorname{Hom}_S(X, \mathbb{A}^n_S) \cong \Gamma(X, X)^n.$$
If $S = \operatorname{Spec} R$, then $\mathbb{A}^n_S \cong \operatorname{Spec} R[x_i : i \in n]$.

*Proof.* An $S$-morphism to the fiber product is a pair of a morphism $X \to S$, which is the structure map, and a morphism $X \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[x_i]$; no compatibility is needed over the terminal object $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$. By the adjunction between $\Gamma$ and $\operatorname{Spec}$, morphisms $X \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[x_i]$ correspond to ring maps $\mathbb{Z}[x_i] \to \Gamma(X, X)$. A ring map out of a polynomial ring over $\mathbb{Z}$ is the same as the family of images of the $x_i$. For the second claim, $\operatorname{Spec}$ sends tensor products to fiber products, so
$$\operatorname{Spec} R \times_{\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}} \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[x_i] \cong \operatorname{Spec}(R \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}[x_i]) \cong \operatorname{Spec} R[x_i]. \quad \square$$

The universal property also gives the maps between affine spaces: a morphism $S' \to S$ induces $\mathbb{A}^n_{S'} \to \mathbb{A}^n_S$, and the square with the projections is a pullback.

### Examples

**Example (The projective line).** Let $A = k[x, y]$ with the total degree. By the lemma, $\mathbb{P}^1_k = \operatorname{Proj} A$ is covered by $D_+(x)$ and $D_+(y)$. Here $A_{(x)}$ consists of $a/x^n$ with $a$ homogeneous of degree $n$, so $A_{(x)} = k[y/x]$, a polynomial ring in $u = y/x$. In the same way $A_{(y)} = k[v]$ with $v = x/y$. On $D_+(xy)$ the ring is $A_{(xy)} = k[u, u^{-1}]$, with $v = u^{-1}$. So $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ is two affine lines glued along $\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}$ by $v = 1/u$. A global function is a pair $p(u) \in k[u]$, $q(v) \in k[v]$ with $p(u) = q(u^{-1})$ in $k[u, u^{-1}]$. Comparing exponents, $p$ and $q$ are the same constant. So $\Gamma(\mathbb{P}^1_k, \mathcal{O}) = k$, and $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ is not affine: an affine scheme with global ring $k$ is one point.

**Example (The points of the projective line).** Let $k$ be algebraically closed. A homogeneous prime of $k[x, y]$ other than $(0)$ and $(x, y)$ contains a homogeneous irreducible polynomial. Over $k$ a homogeneous polynomial in two variables factors into linear forms, since $g(x, y) = y^n g(x/y, 1)$ up to a power of $y$. So the points of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ are the generic point $(0)$ and the closed points $(bx - ay)$, one for each ratio $[a : b]$. Under $D_+(x) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k[u]$, the point $(bx - ay)$ with $a \neq 0$ goes to $(u - b/a)$, by Step 2 of the theorem.

**Example (A single variable).** Let $A = k[x]$ with $\deg x = 1$. Then $A_+ = (x)$, and the only homogeneous prime that does not contain $x$ is $(0)$. So $\operatorname{Proj} k[x] = D_+(x) \cong \operatorname{Spec} k[x]_{(x)} = \operatorname{Spec} k$, one point. This is $\mathbb{P}^0_k$.

**Example (Affine space over a ring).** Let $S = \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$ and $n = \{1, 2\}$. The theorem gives $\mathbb{A}^2_{\mathbb{Z}} = \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[x_1, x_2]$. A point of $\mathbb{A}^2_{\mathbb{Z}}$ with values in a ring $R$, that is a morphism $\operatorname{Spec} R \to \mathbb{A}^2_{\mathbb{Z}}$, is a pair $(r_1, r_2) \in R^2$. For a general $S$, the fiber of $\mathbb{A}^n_S \to S$ over a point $s$ is $\mathbb{A}^n_{\kappa(s)} = \operatorname{Spec} \kappa(s)[x_i]$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\operatorname{Proj} k[x, y, z]$ is covered by three opens, each isomorphic to $\mathbb{A}^2_k$. *Hint:* Compute $A_{(x)} = k[y/x, z/x]$.
2. (A) Show that $V_+(I) = \emptyset$ for each homogeneous ideal $I$ with $A_+ \subseteq \sqrt{I}$. *Hint:* A prime that contains $I$ contains $\sqrt{I}$.
3. (B) Let $A = k[x, y]$ with $\deg x = 1$ and $\deg y = 2$. Show that $A_{(x)} = k[y/x^2]$ and $A_{(y)} = k[x^2/y]$, and that $\operatorname{Proj} A \cong \mathbb{P}^1_k$. *Hint:* Compare with the subring $k[x^2, y]$, graded by half the degree.
4. (B) Let $\varphi : k[x, y, z] \to k[x, y]$ send $z$ to $0$. Show that $\varphi$ satisfies the condition of the functoriality proposition, and that the image of $\mathbb{P}^1_k \to \mathbb{P}^2_k$ is $V_+(z)$. *Hint:* $\varphi(A_+) B = (x, y)$, and $\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$ contains $z$.
5. (B) Use the universal property to describe the morphisms $\mathbb{A}^1_S \to \mathbb{A}^1_S$ over $S$. *Hint:* They correspond to elements of $\Gamma(\mathbb{A}^1_S, \mathbb{A}^1_S)$.
6. (C) Show that $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ is separated by computing the map $k[u] \otimes_k k[v] \to k[u, u^{-1}]$. *Hint:* The image contains $u$ and $v = u^{-1}$.
7. (C) Let $A$ be a graded ring and $d > 0$. Let $A^{(d)} = \bigoplus_n A_{nd}$, graded by $n$. Show that $D_+(f) \subseteq \operatorname{Proj} A$ and $D_+(f) \subseteq \operatorname{Proj} A^{(d)}$ have the same ring for $f \in A_d$. *Hint:* Both rings are $A_{(f)}$.

### Notes and history

Grothendieck defined $\operatorname{Proj}$ of a graded ring in EGA II (1961), and Hartshorne (1977) treats it in Section II.2. Serre (FAC, 1955) had studied projective varieties through graded modules. The Stacks project proves properness of $\operatorname{Proj}$ over $A_0$ with the valuative criterion, and the Mathlib proof follows the same path.
