# Homotopy and the fundamental group

This chapter builds the homotopy theory of spaces from continuous maps and the unit interval. Section 1 defines homotopies of maps, homotopies relative to a subset, homotopy equivalences and contractible spaces. It constructs the straight-line homotopy in a convex set, treats products of homotopies and local contractibility, and shows that topological groups and loop spaces are H-spaces. Section 2 builds the fundamental groupoid from paths up to homotopy with fixed end points, proves the groupoid laws, and defines the fundamental group as the automorphism group of a point. It proves homotopy invariance, computes the groupoid of a product and of a point, and studies simply connected spaces. Section 3 defines the higher homotopy groups through maps of cubes that send the boundary to the base point, builds the group law, and proves by the Eckmann-Hilton argument that the groups are abelian from dimension 2 on.

The chapter needs topological spaces, continuity, products and the subspace topology from Volume 13, with compactness and connectedness for some proofs. It uses topological groups from Volume 14 for H-spaces and convex sets from Volume 22 for the straight-line homotopy. It uses groups from Volume 5, and categories, functors, groupoids and equivalences of categories from Volume 24. Every proof is given in full on paper, and each section names the Mathlib declarations that state the results.

Chapter 2 applies the fundamental group to covering spaces and computes the fundamental group of the circle as the integers. The singular simplicial set of Chapter 5 is linked to this chapter through the homotopies and the path components of Section 2. The spheres and disks of Section 3 are the cells of the cell complexes in Chapter 6, and the homotopy categories of Chapter 6 generalize the homotopy relation of Section 1.
