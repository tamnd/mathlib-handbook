# Expectation, variance and moments

The expectation of a random variable is its integral, so Volume 18, Measure and integration, supplies all the general theorems of this chapter: linearity, monotonicity and the convergence theorems. The chapter shows what these theorems give in probability, and adds the results that need independence. It also gives the three inequalities that bound the probability of a large deviation: Markov's inequality from the mean, Chebyshev's inequality from the variance, and the Chernoff bound from the moment generating function. The limit theorems of Chapter 5 use all three.

Section 1 defines the expectation, gives the tail formula, Markov's and Jensen's inequalities, and proves that the expectation of a product of independent variables is the product of the expectations. Its examples compute expectations with indicators: the fixed points of a random permutation and the coupon collector. Section 2 defines the variance and the covariance, proves that the variance of a sum of pairwise independent variables is the sum of the variances, proves Chebyshev's inequality, and computes the variance $np(1-p)$ of a binomial variable. Section 3 defines the moments and the moment generating function, proves that the derivatives of the generating function at $0$ are the moments and that the generating function of a sum of independent variables is a product, and proves the Chernoff bound and Hoeffding's inequality, with an exponential bound for coin tosses.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 and 2 and Volume 18. In Mathlib, the expectation is the Bochner integral `∫ ω, X ω ∂μ`, and a variable that is not integrable has the junk value `0`. The variance, the moments and the generating function inherit this convention, and the text points out where it matters.
