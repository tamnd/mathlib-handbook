## σ-algebras and Borel sets

### Overview

Chapter 3 shows that no translation invariant, countably additive measure on all subsets of $\mathbb{R}$ gives $[0, 1]$ the length $1$. So measure theory fixes in advance a family of sets that receive a size, closed under complements and countable unions. Such a family is a σ-algebra.

This section defines σ-algebras, the σ-algebra generated by a family of sets, and the order on the σ-algebras of a type. It defines the Borel σ-algebra of a topological space, the smallest σ-algebra that contains the open sets, and proves that the Borel sets of $\mathbb{R}$ are generated by the open intervals with rational end points and by the rays $(-\infty, q)$ with $q \in \mathbb{Q}$. Later proofs check a property of all Borel sets on intervals only.

### Definitions

**Definition (σ-algebra).** Let $X$ be a set. A *σ-algebra* on $X$ is a family $\Sigma$ of subsets of $X$ such that $\emptyset \in \Sigma$, $A^c = X \setminus A \in \Sigma$ for each $A \in \Sigma$, and $\bigcup_{n} A_n \in \Sigma$ for each sequence $(A_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ in $\Sigma$. The pair $(X, \Sigma)$ is a *measurable space*, and the members of $\Sigma$ are the *measurable sets*.

The power set $\mathcal{P}(X)$ and the family $\{\emptyset, X\}$ are σ-algebras, the largest and the smallest. The word "countable" matters: the family of finite and cofinite subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ is closed under complements and finite unions, but the union of the finite sets $\{0\}, \{2\}, \{4\}, \dots$ is the set of even numbers, which is neither finite nor cofinite.

**Definition (generated σ-algebra).** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a family of subsets of $X$. The σ-algebra $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$ *generated* by $\mathcal{C}$ is the intersection of all σ-algebras on $X$ that contain $\mathcal{C}$.

**Definition (order on σ-algebras).** For σ-algebras $\Sigma_1, \Sigma_2$ on $X$ write $\Sigma_1 \le \Sigma_2$ if $\Sigma_1 \subseteq \Sigma_2$. The *infimum* of a family $(\Sigma_i)$ is the intersection $\bigcap_i \Sigma_i$, and the *supremum* is $\sigma(\bigcup_i \Sigma_i)$.

**Definition (Borel σ-algebra).** Let $X$ be a topological space. The *Borel σ-algebra* $\mathcal{B}(X)$ is the σ-algebra generated by the open sets of $X$. Its members are the *Borel sets*.

Closed sets, countable intersections of open sets ($G_\delta$ sets) and countable unions of closed sets ($F_\sigma$ sets) are Borel.

**Definition (countably generated).** A measurable space $(X, \Sigma)$ is *countably generated* if $\Sigma = \sigma(\mathcal{C})$ for a countable family $\mathcal{C}$.

### Theorems and proofs

**Proposition (closure properties).** Let $\Sigma$ be a σ-algebra on $X$. Then $X \in \Sigma$, and $\Sigma$ is closed under finite unions, countable intersections and differences.

*Proof.* $X = \emptyset^c$. For $A, B \in \Sigma$, the sequence $A, B, \emptyset, \emptyset, \dots$ has the union $A \cup B$, and the same padding gives each finite union. For a sequence $(A_n)$ in $\Sigma$, De Morgan's law gives $\bigcap_n A_n = \big(\bigcup_n A_n^c\big)^c$, which is in $\Sigma$. Finally $A \setminus B = A \cap B^c$. $\square$

**Proposition (the generated σ-algebra).** Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a family of subsets of $X$. Then $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$ is a σ-algebra that contains $\mathcal{C}$, and for each σ-algebra $\Sigma$, $\sigma(\mathcal{C}) \subseteq \Sigma$ if and only if $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \Sigma$.

*Proof.* The power set is a σ-algebra that contains $\mathcal{C}$, so the intersection is over a nonempty family. Each σ-algebra of the family contains $\emptyset$, and is closed under complements and countable unions, so the intersection has the same properties. It contains $\mathcal{C}$ because each member of the family does. If $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \Sigma$, then $\Sigma$ is one of the intersected σ-algebras, so $\sigma(\mathcal{C}) \subseteq \Sigma$. The converse follows from $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \sigma(\mathcal{C})$. $\square$

The same argument shows that an intersection of any family of σ-algebras is a σ-algebra. So the σ-algebras on $X$ form a complete lattice (Volume 4, Chapter 4). A union of two σ-algebras need not be one, so the supremum is generated.

**Theorem (generators of the Borel sets of $\mathbb{R}$).** Let $\mathcal{C}_1 = \{(a, b) : a, b \in \mathbb{Q},\ a < b\}$ and $\mathcal{C}_2 = \{(-\infty, q) : q \in \mathbb{Q}\}$. Then $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}) = \sigma(\mathcal{C}_1) = \sigma(\mathcal{C}_2)$.

*Proof.* Each member of $\mathcal{C}_1$ and of $\mathcal{C}_2$ is open, so $\sigma(\mathcal{C}_1) \subseteq \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ and $\sigma(\mathcal{C}_2) \subseteq \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ by the proposition on generated σ-algebras.

Let $U$ be open. For $x \in U$ choose $\varepsilon > 0$ with $(x - \varepsilon, x + \varepsilon) \subseteq U$, and rationals $a \in (x - \varepsilon, x)$ and $b \in (x, x + \varepsilon)$. Then $x \in (a, b) \subseteq U$. So $U$ is the union of the members of $\mathcal{C}_1$ that lie in $U$. The family $\mathcal{C}_1$ is countable, because $\mathbb{Q} \times \mathbb{Q}$ is countable (Volume 3, Chapter 8). So $U$ is a countable union of members of $\mathcal{C}_1$, and $U \in \sigma(\mathcal{C}_1)$. Thus $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}) \subseteq \sigma(\mathcal{C}_1)$.

It remains to show $\mathcal{C}_1 \subseteq \sigma(\mathcal{C}_2)$, because then $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}) = \sigma(\mathcal{C}_1) \subseteq \sigma(\mathcal{C}_2)$. Let $a < b$ be rational. The numbers $a + 1/n$ are rational, and $(-\infty, a] = \bigcap_{n \ge 1} (-\infty, a + 1/n)$. So $(-\infty, a] \in \sigma(\mathcal{C}_2)$, and $(a, b) = (-\infty, b) \cap (-\infty, a]^c \in \sigma(\mathcal{C}_2)$. $\square$

**Proposition (second countable spaces).** If the topology of $X$ has a countable basis $\mathcal{U}$, then $\mathcal{B}(X) = \sigma(\mathcal{U})$. So $\mathcal{B}(X)$ is countably generated.

*Proof.* The members of $\mathcal{U}$ are open, so $\sigma(\mathcal{U}) \subseteq \mathcal{B}(X)$. Each open set $V$ is the union of the members of $\mathcal{U}$ contained in $V$, which is a countable union, so $V \in \sigma(\mathcal{U})$ and $\mathcal{B}(X) \subseteq \sigma(\mathcal{U})$. $\square$

### Examples

**Example (a finite partition).** Let $X = \{1, \dots, 6\}$ and $\mathcal{C} = \{\{1, 2\}, \{3\}, \{4, 5, 6\}\}$, a partition of $X$. The unions of blocks form a σ-algebra: the complement of a union of blocks is the union of the other blocks, and a union of unions of blocks is a union of blocks. Each σ-algebra that contains the blocks contains their unions. So $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$ is the family of the $2^3 = 8$ unions of blocks: $\emptyset$, the three blocks, the three unions of two blocks, and $X$. The set $\{1\}$ is not in it.

**Counterexample (the generators must generate the topology).** On $\mathbb{R}$ let $\Sigma$ be the family of the sets $A$ such that $A$ or $A^c$ is countable. It is a σ-algebra: it contains $\emptyset$, it is closed under complements by its symmetric form, and for a sequence $(A_n)$ in $\Sigma$, either each $A_n$ is countable and so is the union, or some $A_k^c$ is countable and $\big(\bigcup_n A_n\big)^c \subseteq A_k^c$ is countable. Each singleton is in $\Sigma$, and each σ-algebra that contains the singletons contains the countable sets and their complements, so $\Sigma = \sigma(\{\{x\} : x \in \mathbb{R}\})$. The interval $(0, 1)$ and its complement are uncountable, so $(0, 1) \notin \Sigma$. Thus the singletons are Borel sets, but they generate a σ-algebra strictly smaller than $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$. In the theorem above the intervals generate the Borel sets because they form a basis of the topology.

### The library around it

The lemmas on measurable sets have the names `MeasurableSet.<operation>`: `MeasurableSet.empty`, `.univ`, `.compl`, `.union`, `.inter`, `.diff`, `.symmDiff`, `.iUnion`, `.iInter`, `.biUnion`, and `Set.Countable.measurableSet`, `Set.Finite.measurableSet` for countable sets. The last two need the class `MeasurableSingletonClass α`, which says that each singleton is measurable. A Borel space with a Hausdorff topology is an instance. The tactic `measurability` combines these lemmas.

The Borel σ-algebra satisfies `borel_eq_generateFrom_isClosed` and `borel_eq_top_of_countable` (in a countable $T_0$ space each set is Borel).

Common errors of a learner:

- Writing `MeasurableSet s` when two σ-algebras are present, and getting the one found by instance search instead of the intended one.
- Assuming that `borel ℝ` is the instance on `ℝ`. The instance is `Real.measurableSpace`, and `BorelSpace ℝ` says that it equals `borel ℝ`.
- Proving that a family generates the Borel sets by showing only that its members are open.

### Exercises

1. (A) Find the σ-algebra on $\{1, 2, 3\}$ generated by $\{\{1\}, \{1, 2\}\}$.
2. (A) Let $\Sigma$ be a σ-algebra on $X$ and $Y \subseteq X$. Show that $\{A \cap Y : A \in \Sigma\}$ is a σ-algebra on $Y$.
3. (A) Show that $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ is generated by the closed intervals $[a, b]$ with $a < b$.
4. (B) Show that $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$ is generated by the intervals $(a, b]$ with $a < b$ rational. Hint: write $(a, b)$ as a countable union of such intervals.
5. (B) Show that $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^2)$ is generated by the products $(a, b) \times (c, d)$ with rational end points. Hint: they form a countable basis.
6. (B) Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a family of subsets of $X$. Show that $\sigma(\mathcal{C})$ is the union of the σ-algebras $\sigma(\mathcal{C}_0)$ over the countable subfamilies $\mathcal{C}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{C}$. Hint: show that this union is a σ-algebra.
7. (C) Show that an infinite σ-algebra $\Sigma$ is uncountable. Hint: find a sequence of nonempty, pairwise disjoint sets in $\Sigma$, and consider the unions of its subsequences.

### Notes and history

Borel introduced the sets that carry his name in his *Leçons sur la théorie des fonctions* of 1898, as the sets obtained from intervals by complements and countable unions, and asked for a measure on them. Lebesgue's thesis of 1902 constructed this measure and extended it to a larger σ-algebra, the Lebesgue measurable sets of Chapter 3. The Mathlib file `Mathlib.MeasureTheory.MeasurableSpace.Defs` has the authors Johannes Hölzl and Mario Carneiro, and `Mathlib.MeasureTheory.Constructions.BorelSpace.Basic` has the authors Johannes Hölzl and Yury Kudryashov.
