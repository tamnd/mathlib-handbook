# Manifolds and bundles

This volume teaches the theory of smooth manifolds and of fiber and vector bundles, together with their form in Mathlib. A manifold is a space that looks like a model space near each of its points. The model is a normed space, or a half-space when the manifold has a boundary, and the charts glue the local pictures together. Differential calculus moves from the normed spaces of Volume 17 to the manifold through the charts: a map is smooth if it is smooth in charts, and its derivative at a point is the derivative in charts. Each main theorem has a complete proof on paper: the independence of smoothness from the chart, the chain rule for `mfderiv`, the smoothness of the tangent map, the construction of a fiber bundle from its transition functions, the existence of smooth partitions of unity, the local injectivity of an immersion, the embedding of a compact manifold in a Euclidean space, and the bracket of two vector fields.

Mathlib builds manifolds in layers, and the volume follows the layers. A charted space is a topological space with an atlas of partial homeomorphisms to a model space. The atlas is data, and it carries no condition on the changes of charts. A structure groupoid is a family of partial homeomorphisms of the model space that is closed under composition, inverse and restriction. A charted space has a groupoid structure if all its changes of charts are in the groupoid. A model with corners embeds the model space into a normed space, so that the changes of charts can be differentiated. A manifold of class $C^n$ is a charted space whose changes of charts are in the groupoid of $C^n$ maps of the model with corners. Each layer is a class in Mathlib, and each theorem asks only for the layers that it uses.

The chapters are in the order of a course.

- Chapter 1 defines charted spaces, the atlas and the preferred chart `chartAt`, products of charted spaces, structure groupoids, the maximal atlas, and the local invariant properties that let one property of maps in the model become a property of maps between charted spaces.
- Chapter 2 defines models with corners, the groupoid `contDiffGroupoid`, the class `IsManifold`, the extended charts `extChartAt`, and gives the examples: normed spaces, open subsets, products, the half-space and the quadrant, the interval $[0, 1]$, the sphere and the group of units of a Banach algebra. It defines the interior and the boundary of a manifold.
- Chapter 3 defines smooth maps between manifolds with `ContMDiff`, proves that the definition does not depend on the charts, studies the operations on smooth maps and the maps into normed spaces, and defines diffeomorphisms and local diffeomorphisms.
- Chapter 4 defines the tangent space, the derivative `mfderiv` and the tangent map, proves the chain rule, and computes the derivative of the charts, of maps between normed spaces and of the elementary maps.
- Chapter 5 defines the total space of a bundle, trivializations, fiber bundles and their construction from transition functions, topological vector bundles and the constructions on them, and smooth vector bundles. The tangent bundle is the main example, and the smoothness of the tangent map closes the chapter.
- Chapter 6 constructs smooth bump functions on a manifold and smooth partitions of unity subordinate to an open cover, and applies them.
- Chapter 7 defines immersions and submersions by their local normal forms, proves their basic properties, defines smooth embeddings, and proves the embedding theorem of Whitney for compact manifolds in the form that Mathlib has.
- Chapter 8 studies smooth sections and vector fields, the Lie bracket of vector fields, integral curves and their existence and uniqueness, and Lie groups with their Lie algebra of left invariant derivations.

The volume needs Volume 13 for topological spaces, Hausdorff spaces, compactness and connectedness, Volume 16 for normed spaces, finite dimensional spaces and continuous linear maps, and Volume 17 for the Fréchet derivative, the classes $C^n$ with `ContDiff`, the inverse function theorem and the existence of solutions of differential equations. Volume 8 gives the multilinear and alternating maps that appear in the differential forms and in the bundles of alternating maps. Volume 7 gives the linear algebra of the fibers, and Volume 5 the groups that act. Two notions of topology that Volume 13 does not treat are explained where they first appear: partial homeomorphisms in Chapter 1 and paracompact spaces in Chapter 6.

Mathlib states the theory in more generality than a first course. The model space can be an infinite dimensional normed space, the scalar field can be $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$, and the manifold can have a boundary or corners. The order of smoothness $n$ takes values in `WithTop ℕ∞`, so that one definition covers $C^k$, $C^\infty$ and analytic manifolds. The tangent space at a point is by definition the model vector space $E$, and the tangent bundle is a sigma type over the manifold. A map has a derivative `mfderiv` at every point, which is $0$ where the map is not differentiable. The text names these conventions where they matter, and says where the library stops: the index of this edition has no general theorem on regular values or on the existence of embedded submanifolds, and its theory of differential forms lives on normed spaces. The library also has diffeological spaces, Riemannian metrics on bundles, covariant derivatives, smooth sheaves, bordism groups and a category of manifolds. The volume mentions them where they meet its subject and does not treat them in full. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
