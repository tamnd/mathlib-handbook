## The topology of ℝ≥0, ℝ≥0∞ and the extended reals

### Overview

Four ordered types extend or restrict $\mathbb{R}$ in Mathlib. The type `ℝ≥0` is the set of nonnegative reals. The type `ℝ≥0∞` adds a top element $\infty$ to `ℝ≥0`. The type `EReal` adds $-\infty$ and $+\infty$ to $\mathbb{R}$. The type `ℕ∞` adds $\infty$ to $\mathbb{N}$. Each is a linear order, and each carries the order topology. This section describes these topologies and the continuity of the arithmetic operations, which is not automatic: the conventions that make the operations total, such as $0 \cdot \infty = 0$ and $\infty - \infty = 0$, break continuity at a few points.

The section proves that `ℝ≥0` is an open part of `ℝ≥0∞`, that addition is continuous and multiplication is continuous away from $(0, \infty)$ and $(\infty, 0)$, that `toReal` is continuous at finite points, and that addition on `EReal` is continuous away from $(\pm\infty, \mp\infty)$.

### Definitions

The type `ℝ≥0`, written `NNReal`, is the subtype $\{x \in \mathbb{R} : x \ge 0\}$ with the subspace topology. Since the set $[0, \infty)$ is an interval of $\mathbb{R}$, this topology is also the order topology of `ℝ≥0`. The type `ℝ≥0∞`, written `ENNReal`, is `WithTop ℝ≥0`: an element is either the image $\uparrow r$ of some $r \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ or the top element $\infty$. It carries the order topology. A basis of neighbourhoods of $\infty$ is given by the rays $(a, \infty]$ with $a < \infty$, and a basis of neighbourhoods of a finite point $\uparrow r$ is given by the intervals around it.

The maps between these types follow the conventions of Volume 4. The map `ENNReal.toNNReal` sends $\uparrow r$ to $r$ and $\infty$ to $0$. The map `ENNReal.toReal` is its composition with the inclusion in $\mathbb{R}$. The map `ENNReal.ofReal` sends $x \in \mathbb{R}$ to $\uparrow \max(x, 0)$. The arithmetic of `ℝ≥0∞` satisfies $a + \infty = \infty$, $a \cdot \infty = \infty$ for $a \ne 0$, $0 \cdot \infty = 0$, $\infty - \infty = 0$ and $1 / 0 = \infty$.

The type `EReal` is `WithBot (WithTop ℝ)` with the order topology. Its addition satisfies $(+\infty) + (-\infty) = -\infty$, a convention that the statement below avoids. The type `ℕ∞`, written `ENat`, is `WithTop ℕ` with the order topology. Each natural number is an isolated point, and the neighbourhoods of $\infty$ are the sets that contain a ray $(n, \infty]$.

### Theorems and proofs

**Theorem (The finite part).** The set $\{a \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^\infty : a \ne \infty\}$ is open, and the inclusion $r \mapsto \uparrow r$ is a homeomorphism from `ℝ≥0` onto it.

*Proof.* The set is the open ray $[0, \infty)$, so it is open. The inclusion is a bijection of `ℝ≥0` onto this ray that preserves the order. An interval $(l, u)$ of `ℝ≥0` corresponds to the interval $(\uparrow l, \uparrow u)$ of `ℝ≥0∞`, and a ray $[0, u)$ to the ray $[0, \uparrow u)$. Since the ray $[0, \infty)$ is an interval of `ℝ≥0∞`, the subspace topology on it is generated by these intervals and rays. So the bijection carries the basis of one topology to a basis of the other, and it is a homeomorphism. $\square$

::: mathlib
`ENNReal.neTopHomeomorphNNReal` · `Mathlib.Topology.Instances.ENNReal.Lemmas`

The homeomorphism `{a | a ≠ ⊤} ≃ₜ ℝ≥0`, given by `toNNReal`. The variant `ENNReal.ltTopHomeomorphNNReal` uses the set `{a | a < ⊤}`. The lemma `ENNReal.nhds_coe` states that the neighbourhoods of `↑r` are the images of the neighbourhoods of `r`, and `ENNReal.nhds_top` gives the neighbourhoods of `⊤` as the filter generated by the rays `Ioi a` with `a ≠ ⊤`.

<?decl ENNReal.neTopHomeomorphNNReal?>

So a sequence $a_n$ in `ℝ≥0∞` tends to $\infty$ exactly when for every $r \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ eventually $a_n > r$ (`ENNReal.tendsto_nhds_top_iff_nnreal`). For example $n \to \infty$, and $1/n \to 0$.

**Theorem (Addition).** Addition on `ℝ≥0∞` is continuous.

*Proof.* Let $(a, b)$ be a pair. If both are finite, the previous theorem reduces the question to continuity of addition on `ℝ≥0`, which follows from $\mathbb{R}$. Suppose $a = \infty$, so $a + b = \infty$, and let $(c, \infty]$ be a basic neighbourhood of $\infty$. The set $(c, \infty]$ is a neighbourhood of $a$. For $x \in (c, \infty]$ and any $y$, $x + y \ge x > c$. So the sum maps the neighbourhood $(c, \infty] \times \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^\infty$ of $(a, b)$ into $(c, \infty]$. The case $b = \infty$ is symmetric. $\square$

**Theorem (Multiplication).** Multiplication on `ℝ≥0∞` is continuous at $(a, b)$ unless $(a, b) = (0, \infty)$ or $(a, b) = (\infty, 0)$. It is not continuous at these two pairs.

*Proof.* If both are finite, the previous theorem reduces to $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$. Suppose $a = \infty$ and $b \ne 0$, so $ab = \infty$. Choose $b'$ finite with $0 < b' < b$. Given $c$ finite, choose $x_0$ finite with $x_0 b' > c$. For $x > x_0$ and $y > b'$, $xy \ge x_0 b' > c$. The set $(x_0, \infty] \times (b', \infty]$ is a neighbourhood of $(a, b)$ that maps into $(c, \infty]$. The case $b = \infty$, $a \ne 0$ is symmetric. At $(0, \infty)$ the pairs $(1/n, \infty)$ tend to $(0, \infty)$, and their products are all $\infty$, while $0 \cdot \infty = 0$. So the products do not tend to the product of the limit. The pair $(\infty, 0)$ is symmetric. $\square$

**Theorem (The real part).** The map `toReal` is continuous at every $a \ne \infty$ and is not continuous at $\infty$. The map `ofReal` is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$.

*Proof.* On the open set $\{a \ne \infty\}$ the map `toNNReal` is the inverse of the homeomorphism of the first theorem, so it is continuous there, and `toReal` is its composition with the continuous inclusion of `ℝ≥0` in $\mathbb{R}$. At $\infty$, the sequence $n$ tends to $\infty$ while $\mathrm{toReal}(n) = n$ does not tend to $\mathrm{toReal}(\infty) = 0$. The map `ofReal` is the composition of $x \mapsto \max(x, 0)$, from $\mathbb{R}$ to `ℝ≥0`, which is continuous, with the continuous inclusion of `ℝ≥0` in `ℝ≥0∞`. $\square$

**Theorem (Addition on EReal).** Addition on `EReal` is continuous at $(a, b)$ unless $\{a, b\} = \{+\infty, -\infty\}$.

*Proof.* At a pair of finite numbers, the inclusion $\mathbb{R} \to \mathrm{EReal}$ is an open embedding, and the question reduces to $\mathbb{R}$. If $a = +\infty$ and $b \ne -\infty$, choose a real $m < b$. For $x > c - m$ and $y > m$, $x + y > c$, so the sum maps a neighbourhood of $(a, b)$ into $(c, +\infty]$. The cases with $-\infty$ are symmetric. At $(+\infty, -\infty)$ the pairs $(n + t, -n)$ tend to $(+\infty, -\infty)$ for every real $t$, and their sums are $t$. So no value at this pair makes addition continuous. $\square$

**Theorem (Sums in ℝ≥0).** A family $f : I \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ has the sum $r$ in `ℝ≥0` exactly when the family of reals $\uparrow f_i$ has the sum $\uparrow r$ in $\mathbb{R}$. It is summable in `ℝ≥0` exactly when it is summable in $\mathbb{R}$, and then $\uparrow \sum' f = \sum' \uparrow f$.

*Proof.* The inclusion of `ℝ≥0` in $\mathbb{R}$ is an embedding and an additive map, so it carries the finite sums of $f$ to the finite sums of $\uparrow f$. A function into a subspace tends to a point of the subspace exactly when its composition with the inclusion does. This gives the first claim. If $\uparrow f$ has a sum $a$ in $\mathbb{R}$, then $a \ge 0$ since the finite sums are nonnegative and $[0, \infty)$ is closed, so $a = \uparrow r$ for some $r$, and the first claim applies. The formula for `tsum` follows, also when $f$ is not summable, since both sides are $0$. $\square$

### Examples

**Example (Truncated subtraction).** The map $x \mapsto \infty - x$ on `ℝ≥0∞` takes the value $\infty$ at every finite $x$ and the value $0$ at $\infty$. Since $n \to \infty$ and $\infty - n = \infty$ does not tend to $0$, the map is not continuous at $\infty$. The map $x \mapsto a - x$ is continuous for every finite $a$ (`ENNReal.continuous_sub_left`), since it is then the composition of continuous maps on $[0, a]$ and constant $0$ beyond.

**Example (The extended naturals).** In `ℕ∞` the neighbourhoods of a natural number $n$ include $\{n\}$ (`ENat.nhds_natCast`), so a sequence converges to $n$ exactly when it is eventually equal to $n$. The sequence $n$ tends to $\infty$ (`ENat.tendsto_natCast_nhds_top`). The map `ℕ∞ → ℝ≥0∞` is a closed embedding (`ENat.isClosedEmbedding_toENNReal`), with image the closed set $\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$.

**Example (Compactness).** The map $x \mapsto x/(1 + x)$, with $\infty \mapsto 1$, is an order isomorphism from `ℝ≥0∞` onto $[0, 1]$. An order isomorphism between two linear orders is a homeomorphism of their order topologies, and the order topology of $[0, 1]$ is its subspace topology from $\mathbb{R}$. So `ℝ≥0∞` is homeomorphic to $[0, 1]$, and it is compact and metrizable. Every sequence in `ℝ≥0∞` has a convergent subsequence, which is one reason that limits and sums in measure theory are taken there.

**Remark (Common errors).** The convention $0 \cdot \infty = 0$ is the right one for measure theory, where the integral of $0$ over a set of infinite measure is $0$. Its price is the side conditions of `ENNReal.tendsto_mul`. A proof that $a_n b_n \to ab$ from $a_n \to a$ and $b_n \to b$ must check that the limits are not $0$ and $\infty$. A second error is to apply `toReal` to a limit: from $a_n \to \infty$ nothing follows on $\mathrm{toReal}(a_n)$, since $\mathrm{toReal}(\infty) = 0$ and `toReal` is not continuous there.

### The library around it

The module `Mathlib.Topology.Instances.ENNReal.Lemmas` is the main reference for limits in `ℝ≥0∞`. It also has the arithmetic of limits, such as `ENNReal.Tendsto.mul`, and results on `liminf` and `limsup`. The module `Mathlib.Topology.Instances.NNReal.Lemmas` transfers limits and sums between `ℝ≥0` and $\mathbb{R}$. The module `Mathlib.Topology.Instances.EReal.Lemmas` treats `EReal`. The module `Mathlib.Topology.Instances.ENat` gives `ℕ∞` the structure of a topological semiring, and `Mathlib.Topology.Instances.ENNReal.ENatENNReal` relates it to `ℝ≥0∞`.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $2^{-n} \to 0$ and $2^n \to \infty$ in `ℝ≥0∞`.
2. (A) Find sequences $a_n \to 0$ and $b_n \to \infty$ in `ℝ≥0∞` with $a_n b_n \to 5$.
3. (A) Show that the map $x \mapsto 1/x$ on `ℝ≥0∞`, with $1/0 = \infty$ and $1/\infty = 0$, is continuous. *Hint:* it reverses the order and is a bijection.
4. (B) Show that `ENNReal.ofReal` is not injective and that its restriction to $[0, \infty)$ is a homeomorphism onto $\{a \ne \infty\}$.
5. (B) Show that the map $x \mapsto 2x$ on `ℝ≥0∞` is continuous, and that $x \mapsto \infty \cdot x$ is not continuous at $0$.
6. (B) Let $f : I \to \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$. Show that $\sum' \uparrow f$ in $\mathbb{R}$ is never negative, with no hypothesis on $f$.
7. (C) Show that no value of $(+\infty) + (-\infty)$ in `EReal` makes addition continuous at $(+\infty, -\infty)$, and that the same holds for $0 \cdot \infty$ in `ℝ≥0∞`.

### Notes and history

The extended real line goes back to the use of $\pm\infty$ as limits in the analysis of the nineteenth century, and its topology as the two-point compactification of $\mathbb{R}$ is standard in measure theory. Mathlib chose total operations with fixed conventions at the critical points, so that expressions such as $\mu(A) \cdot \infty$ always have a value, and moved the conditions into the hypotheses of the continuity lemmas.
