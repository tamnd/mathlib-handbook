# Pigeonhole and Ramsey theory

Ramsey theory says that complete disorder is impossible. Each colouring of a large enough structure with finitely many colours contains a large part in one colour. The simplest case is the pigeonhole principle: if more than $kn$ objects go into $n$ boxes, some box gets more than $k$ objects. Each theorem of this chapter applies the pigeonhole principle again and again, in an induction that makes the structures very large.

Section 1 starts from the pigeonhole principle of Volume 3. It proves the theorem of Erdős and Szekeres on monotone subsequences, the theorem of Ramsey for graphs with the bound $R(s, t) \le \binom{s + t - 2}{s - 1}$, the value $R(3, 3) = 6$, and the lower bound $R(k, k) > 2^{k/2}$ of Erdős by counting. It ends with the infinite theorem of Ramsey and the compactness principle of Rado, which connects the infinite and the finite theorems. The index of this edition has no declaration for the theorem of Ramsey for graphs, so this section proves it on paper and shows the parts that Mathlib has: the colourings of the edges of a graph and the compactness principle.

Section 2 colours points instead of pairs. It proves the Hales-Jewett theorem on monochromatic combinatorial lines in the cube $\alpha^n$ in full, by induction on the size of the alphabet with colour-focused families of lines, as in Mathlib. It deduces the theorem of van der Waerden on monochromatic arithmetic progressions, and its form for homothetic copies of a finite set in a commutative monoid. It then proves the theorem of Hindman on finite sums. The proof finds an idempotent ultrafilter with the lemma of Ellis and Numakura on compact semigroups.

The chapter needs Chapter 1 for graphs, and Volume 3 for the pigeonhole principle and the binomial coefficients. Section 2 uses ultrafilters and the Stone-Čech compactification of Volume 13 for the theorem of Hindman. Chapter 6 returns to arithmetic progressions with density instead of colourings: the theorem of Roth is a density form of the case of length $3$ of the theorem of van der Waerden.

In Mathlib, a colouring is a function to a type of colours, often a finite type. The pigeonhole principle has many forms in `Mathlib.Combinatorics.Pigeonhole`, for finsets and for finite types, with strict and non-strict inequalities, and with weights. The names are long because they record the form: `Finset.exists_lt_card_fiber_of_mul_lt_card_of_maps_to` is the form with finsets, the strict inequality and a map to a finset of colours.
