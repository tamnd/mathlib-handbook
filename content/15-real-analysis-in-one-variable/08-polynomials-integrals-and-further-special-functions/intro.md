# Polynomials, integrals and further special functions

The earlier chapters build the elementary functions and prove their main properties. This chapter collects the parts of the library that use these functions as tools: the analysis of polynomials, the integrability of the elementary functions, a set of auxiliary functions with one purpose each, and the elementary functions applied to the elements of a C⋆-algebra. The sections are independent of each other, and a reader can take them in any order.

Section 1 studies real and complex polynomials as functions: their growth at infinity, the sign near and beyond the roots, the bound of Rolle on the number of real roots, the Cauchy index, the Cauchy bound on the roots, the norm on the circle and the Mahler measure. Section 2 links the Chebyshev polynomials to the cosine by $T_n(\cos\theta) = \cos n\theta$, and derives their roots and extrema, the extremal property of the leading coefficient, the orthogonality for the weight $1/\sqrt{1 - x^2}$ and the quadrature rule of Chebyshev and Gauss. Section 3 decides when powers, logarithms, the exponential and $1/x$ are integrable, and proves the layer-cake formula, Frullani's integral and the change to polar coordinates. Section 4 treats the sigmoid function, the smooth step built from $e^{-1/x}$, the cut-off $x e^{-\varepsilon x^2}$, the Japanese bracket, the Bernstein polynomials with the Weierstrass approximation theorem, the asymptotics of binomial coefficients and the arithmetic-geometric mean. Section 5 defines the absolute value, the positive and negative parts, the exponential and the logarithm of an element of a C⋆-algebra by the continuous functional calculus. Section 6 does the same for real powers and the square root, and proves that $a \mapsto a^p$ is operator monotone and operator concave for $0 \le p \le 1$.

The chapter uses the derivatives of Chapter 3, the exponential, the logarithm and the powers of Chapter 5, the trigonometric functions of Chapter 6 and the asymptotic notation of Chapter 7. Sections 2 and 3 need the measure and the integral of Volume 18. Sections 5 and 6 need the C⋆-algebras and the continuous functional calculus of Volume 16.
