# Probability spaces and random variables

This chapter sets up the objects of the volume. A probability space is a measure space of total mass $1$, an event is a measurable set, a random variable is a measurable map, and the law of a random variable is its image measure. Each later chapter uses these four notions, and each statement about probabilities is a statement about measures.

Section 1 states Kolmogorov's axioms, derives the first rules of computation such as the union bound and inclusion-exclusion, and builds the uniform probability on a finite set. It defines the conditional probability $P(B \mid A)$ and proves the law of total probability and Bayes' formula, with the birthday problem and a medical test as computed examples. Section 2 defines random variables and their laws, proves the transfer formula that computes probabilities and integrals through the law, and studies the distribution function of a real variable. Its main theorem says that the distribution function determines the law. It ends with the existence of a variable with a given law.

The chapter needs Volume 18, Measure and integration, for σ-algebras, measures, the image measure, the π-λ theorem and the monotone convergence theorem, and Volume 3 for finite sums and cardinalities. In Mathlib, a probability space is a measurable space with a measure `μ` and the class `IsProbabilityMeasure μ`, the conditional probability is the measure `μ[|s]`, and the law of `X` is `μ.map X`, packaged in the predicate `HasLaw`. Chapter 2 adds independence, Chapter 3 the expectation, and Chapter 4 the standard laws.
