# Recursive functions

This chapter builds the theory of computable functions from the natural numbers up. Section 1 defines encodable and primcodable types, the pairing function on $\mathbb{N}$ and the primitive recursive functions, first on $\mathbb{N}$ and then on each primcodable type. It proves that the usual operations on numbers and lists are primitive recursive, and that the Ackermann function is computable but grows faster than each primitive recursive function. Section 2 adds unbounded search and defines the partial recursive and the computable functions. It numbers the programs by codes, proves that evaluation of codes is partial recursive, and proves the smn theorem and the recursion theorems. Gödel's beta function closes the section. Section 3 proves the undecidability of the halting problem and Rice's theorem, defines recursively enumerable predicates and proves Post's theorem. It then compares problems by many-one and one-one reductions, defines oracle computation, and orders the Turing degrees.

The chapter needs propositional logic and decidable propositions from Volume 1, the natural numbers, divisibility and the Chinese remainder theorem from Volume 2, lists and countable types from Volume 3, and partial orders from Volume 4. Partial functions in Mathlib are functions to `Part`, and the chapter uses them throughout. No earlier chapter of this volume is needed.

Chapter 7 gives the second classical model of computation, the Turing machine, and proves that each partial recursive function is computed by a Turing machine. It compiles the partial recursive functions of this chapter into machines, and it measures computation by the number of steps of a machine. Chapter 8 turns from what is computable to how fast it is, with the running time of divide-and-conquer algorithms, and Volume 33 on data structures and computation uses the same ideas for concrete programs.
