# Abelian categories

This chapter develops the theory of abelian categories from the definition of Volume 24 to the embedding theorem. Section 1 proves the structure results. Each morphism factors as an epimorphism followed by a monomorphism, the coimage and the image are isomorphic, and abelian categories have finite limits and colimits, with pullbacks of epimorphisms epi. The section also shows that the comparison of coimage and image characterizes abelian categories, that the addition of morphisms follows from the other axioms, and that the opposite of an abelian category is abelian. Section 2 makes diagram chases work without elements, by refinements and by pseudoelements. It proves the four and five lemmas and works out the snake lemma for $\mathbb{Z}$-modules. Section 3 treats left and right exact functors, projective and injective objects, the Grothendieck axioms AB3 to AB5 and Grothendieck abelian categories, and states the Freyd-Mitchell embedding theorem, which Mathlib proves. Section 4 goes through the library on Grothendieck abelian categories: the examples, filtered colimits of subobjects, the proof that such a category has enough injectives, and the Gabriel-Popescu theorem. Section 5 treats Serre classes and the abelian Serre quotient, torsion theories with Dickson's theorem, and preradicals and radicals.

The chapter needs Chapter 1, mainly the matrix calculus and the total identity of biproducts. From Volume 24 it needs limits from Chapter 4, adjunctions from Chapter 5 and the definitions of Chapter 8. Modules from Volume 7 give the examples.

The chapter is the base for homological algebra. Volume 26 builds complexes, homology and derived functors on the exact functors, the projectives and the injectives of Section 3, and it proves the general snake lemma. In this volume, Chapter 3 studies generators and simple objects of abelian categories, Chapter 5 compares triangulated categories with abelian ones, and Chapter 6 shows that sheaves of abelian groups form an abelian category.
