# Derivatives

This chapter defines the derivative of a real function of one real variable and proves the theorems that connect the derivative with the values of the function. Section 1 gives the derivative as the limit of the difference quotients, proves that a differentiable function is continuous, and proves the rules for sums, products, quotients, compositions, powers and inverse functions. It also explains the two forms of the derivative in Mathlib: the predicate `HasDerivAt f f' x` and the function `deriv f`, with its junk value $0$ at a point where $f$ is not differentiable. Section 2 proves the theorems of Fermat and Rolle, the mean value theorems of Lagrange and Cauchy, the test for monotonicity by the sign of $f'$, and the theorem of Darboux that a derivative has the intermediate value property. Section 3 uses the mean value theorem of Cauchy to prove the rule of l'Hôpital for limits of the form $0/0$, at a point and at infinity, and shows by counterexamples that each of its hypotheses is necessary. Section 4 collects the derivatives of special maps: affine and linear maps, polynomials, integer powers with their iterated derivatives, translations and dilations of the variable, maps into products, the star operation, and the support of a derivative. Section 5 treats the slope function `dslope`, the logarithmic derivative $f'/f$, Fermat's theorem for directional derivatives, absolutely monotone functions, and the theorem of Lebesgue that monotone functions and functions of bounded variation are differentiable almost everywhere.

The chapter needs the limits and the continuity of Chapters 1 and 2, in particular the extreme value theorem for the proof of Rolle's theorem, and the filters of Volume 13 for the Mathlib statements. Chapter 4 uses the mean value theorems to prove Taylor's theorem and the tests for extrema with higher derivatives. Chapter 5 uses the derivative to construct the exponential and the logarithm and to define the real powers, and Chapter 6 does the same for the trigonometric functions. Several examples of this chapter use $\exp$, $\log$ and $\sin$ with a forward pointer to those chapters. Chapter 7 gives the asymptotic notation that organizes the limits of Section 3. Volume 17 treats the derivative of a map between normed spaces, of which the one-variable derivative is a special case.
