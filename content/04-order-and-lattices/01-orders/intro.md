# Preorders, partial orders and linear orders

The relation `≤` is the base of each chapter of this volume. This chapter gives the axioms of an order and the first tools to work with them. The first section defines preorders, partial orders and linear orders, the strict relation `<`, and the standard ways to build an order: products, the lexicographic order, the order pulled back along a function, and the quotient that turns a preorder into a partial order. The second section gives the language of bounds: upper bounds, greatest elements, least upper bounds and maximal elements, and the dual order `OrderDual α` that turns each theorem about upper bounds into a theorem about lower bounds. The third section treats well-founded relations, which are the general form of induction. The fourth section proves Zorn's lemma from the axiom of choice, through the Bourbaki-Witt fixed point theorem, and gives two applications in algebra. The section on covering relations gives the API of `a ⋖ b`, the graded orders and the height of chains. The section on directed sets treats directed and cofinal sets, antichains, and the direct limit of a directed system of types. The section on well-founded sets gives well-foundedness on a subset, well-quasi-orders with Dickson's and Higman's lemmas, the chain conditions and Kőnig's lemma. The section on series treats finite chains $a_0 < \cdots < a_n$ as sequences, the Krull dimension and the height of an element, and the Jordan-Hölder theorem. The section on relation classes gives the unbundled classes such as `IsPartialOrder α r` and `IsWellOrder α r` for a relation that is not the order of the type, with the pullback of a relation along a function. The section on minimal and maximal elements gives the API of `Minimal P x` and `Maximal P x`, with their existence in well-founded orders and in finite sets. The section on order instances on standard types collects the orders on lifted and small types, on propositions, on finitely supported functions, on unordered pairs, on quotients and on restrictions to an initial interval. The section on lexicographic orders treats the lexicographic order on functions, products, dependent pairs and sums, and the order types that compare linear orders by embeddings. The section on adjoining a bottom or a top element gives the API of `WithBot α`, `WithTop α` and `WithBotTop ι`, the types behind the degree of a polynomial and the extended numbers.

The chapter needs Volume 1: sets as predicates, functions, classes and instances, and the classical logic of Mathlib. It needs no algebra, except in the two applications of Zorn's lemma, which use the definitions of an ideal and of a linearly independent set.

The next chapter studies the maps that keep an order. The chapter on lattices turns the least upper bound of two elements into an operation `⊔`, and the chapter on complete lattices does the same for each set. The chapter on conditionally complete lattices uses the bounds of the second section for the supremum of a set of real numbers.
