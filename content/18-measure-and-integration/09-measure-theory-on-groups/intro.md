# Measure theory on groups

This chapter studies measures on groups and on spaces with a group action. Chapter 3 constructed Haar measure and proved its uniqueness. This chapter uses the two facts: it integrates against invariant measures, cuts a space into the translates of one piece, and convolves functions and measures.

Section 1 defines invariant measures for an action and proves that integrals do not change under translation. It combines invariance with the theorem of Fubini to prove that two left invariant measures differ by a constant, and it defines the modular character of a locally compact group. Section 2 defines fundamental domains of an action up to null sets. It decomposes an integral into a sum over the translates of a fundamental domain and proves the lemma of Blichfeldt and the theorem of Minkowski on lattice points in convex sets. It ends with Følner sequences and the invariant means that they give. Section 3 studies how Haar measure scales under automorphisms and linear maps, and how it passes to quotients and to extensions of groups. Section 4 defines the convolution of functions and of measures, gives conditions for its existence and proves its algebraic rules, its continuity and the convergence of approximate identities.

The chapter needs the measures of Chapter 2, Haar measure from Chapter 3, the integrals of Chapters 4 and 5 and the product measures of Chapter 6. Section 4 also uses the spaces $L^p$ of Chapter 7 and the interval integral of Chapter 8. The Fourier transform of Volume 19 and the ergodic theory of Volume 21 use the results of this chapter.
