# Arithmetic functions and Möbius inversion

An arithmetic function is a function on the positive integers. The number of divisors, the sum of divisors, Euler's totient and the Möbius function are examples. This chapter puts all of them into one algebraic structure. Section 1 defines the Dirichlet convolution $(f * g)(n) = \sum_{ab = n} f(a) g(b)$ and proves that it makes the arithmetic functions with values in a commutative ring $R$ into a commutative ring. It defines the multiplicative functions and deduces the formulas for the number and the sum of the divisors. Section 2 defines the Möbius function $\mu$, proves that $\mu$ is the inverse of the constant function $1$ under convolution, and deduces the Möbius inversion formula. It applies the formula to Euler's totient and to the von Mangoldt function $\Lambda$.

The chapter needs Chapter 1 for the divisors of a number and for the valuations, Volume 2, Chapter 6 for Euler's totient, and the finite sums of Volume 3, Chapter 6.

The ring of arithmetic functions is the algebraic side of the theory of Dirichlet series. Chapter 6 attaches to an arithmetic function $f$ the L-series $\sum f(n) n^{-s}$, and the convolution of functions becomes the product of series. Chapter 7 uses $\Lambda$ and the identity $\Lambda * 1 = \log$ in the Chebyshev estimates and in the prime number theorem.
