# Rings and ring homomorphisms

This chapter gives the axioms of a ring in the form that Mathlib uses, as a chain of classes from a semiring through a ring to a commutative ring. Section 1 proves the first identities from the axioms: $0 a = 0$, the rule of signs, the commutativity of the addition, and the binomial theorem in a commutative ring. It gives the first examples, among them $\mathbb{Z}/n$, the matrix rings, the products and function rings, and the Boolean rings. Section 2 defines ring homomorphisms and isomorphisms, shows that $\mathbb{Z}$ has exactly one homomorphism to each ring, and describes the homomorphisms out of $\mathbb{Z}/n$ and $\mathbb{Q}$. Section 3 defines subrings, the subring generated by a set, the range of a homomorphism and the center, with the Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ as the main example. Sections 4 to 7 describe the library around these definitions. Section 4 collects the identities of commuting elements, geometric sums, idempotents and divisibility. Section 5 shows how to build a ring structure from minimal axioms or carry it along a map or an equivalence. Section 6 treats group actions on rings, the group of ring automorphisms and the centroid. Section 7 treats the product of additive subgroups, the action of a group on subrings and opposite subrings.

The chapter needs monoids, groups, group homomorphisms and subgroups from Chapters 1 to 3 of Volume 5. A ring is an abelian group under the addition and a monoid under the multiplication, so each lemma of those chapters applies to it.

Chapter 2 uses the homomorphisms and the subrings of this chapter to define ideals, quotient rings and the isomorphism theorems. The examples $\mathbb{Z}/n$, $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ and the matrix rings come back in all later chapters.
