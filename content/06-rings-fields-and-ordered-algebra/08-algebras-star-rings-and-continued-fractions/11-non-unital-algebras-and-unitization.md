## Non-unital algebras and the unitization

### Overview

Many algebras of analysis have no unit. The continuous functions on $\mathbb{R}$ that vanish at infinity, the compact operators on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and the functions in $L^1(\mathbb{R})$ with convolution are examples. Mathlib treats such an algebra as a `NonUnitalSemiring` or `NonUnitalRing` $A$ with a module structure over a commutative ring $R$ and the two compatibility classes `IsScalarTower R A A` and `SMulCommClass R A A`. These say $(r a) b = r(ab)$ and $a(rb) = r(ab)$.

This section gives the homomorphisms and the subalgebras of non-unital algebras, and then the unitization $R \oplus A$, which adds a unit in the cheapest way. The unitization has a universal property: a non-unital map from $A$ to a unital algebra $C$ extends in exactly one way to a unital map from $R \oplus A$. So a question about a non-unital algebra can often move to a unital one. The section needs the algebras and subalgebras of Section 1 and the products of Section 8.

### The library around it

#### Non-unital homomorphisms

A non-unital algebra homomorphism `A →ₙₐ[R] B` is a map that is additive, multiplicative and $R$-linear, with no condition on a unit. The structure is `NonUnitalAlgHom`, and it is defined more generally over a monoid homomorphism $\phi : R \to S$ as a semilinear map, with the notation `A →ₛₙₐ[φ] B`. The class `NonUnitalAlgHomClass` lets lemmas apply to every bundled type of such maps; the instance `AlgHom.instNonUnitalAlgHomClassOfAlgHomClass` says that every unital algebra map is one. The forgetful maps `NonUnitalAlgHom.toMulHom` and `NonUnitalAlgHom.toDistribMulActionHom` are injective.

The basic constructions are the same as for unital maps. `NonUnitalAlgHom.id` and `NonUnitalAlgHom.comp` give the category. The zero map is a non-unital algebra map, by `NonUnitalAlgHom.instZero`; it is not a unital map unless the target is the zero ring. For products, `NonUnitalAlgHom.fst` and `NonUnitalAlgHom.snd` are the projections, and `NonUnitalAlgHom.prodEquiv` is the universal property $(A \to B) \times (A \to C) \simeq (A \to B \times C)$, with the same proof as for unital maps. Here the inclusions `NonUnitalAlgHom.inl` and `NonUnitalAlgHom.inr`, $a \mapsto (a, 0)$ and $b \mapsto (0, b)$, are also maps, because no unit must go to a unit. `NonUnitalAlgHom.inverse` builds the inverse map from a two-sided inverse function, and `NonUnitalAlgHom.restrictScalars` works in a tower.

#### Non-unital subalgebras

A non-unital subalgebra of $A$ is a subset closed under addition, multiplication and the scalar action, with no condition on $1$. The structure is `NonUnitalSubalgebra R A`. It is a complete lattice, and the generated subalgebra is `NonUnitalAlgebra.adjoin R s`. The Galois connection `NonUnitalAlgebra.gc` says $\operatorname{adjoin}(s) \leq S \iff s \subseteq S$; the lemmas `NonUnitalAlgebra.adjoin_le` and `NonUnitalAlgebra.subset_adjoin` are the two halves. The induction principle `NonUnitalAlgebra.adjoin_induction` proves a property of each element of the adjoin from the generators, sums, products and scalar multiples.

The theorem `NonUnitalAlgebra.adjoin_eq_span` describes the adjoin as a module: it is the span of the multiplicative closure of $s$, `Submodule.span R (Subsemigroup.closure s)`. Proof: the span of a set closed under products is closed under products, by bilinearity, so it is a non-unital subalgebra that contains $s$. Each non-unital subalgebra that contains $s$ contains the products of elements of $s$ and so their span. The unital adjoin of Section 1 is the span of the products and $1$, so the difference is exactly the unit.

The centralizer `NonUnitalSubalgebra.centralizer R s` is the set of elements that commute with each element of $s$, and the center `NonUnitalSubalgebra.center R A` is the centralizer of the whole algebra. The theorem `NonUnitalAlgebra.adjoin_le_centralizer_centralizer` says that the subalgebra generated by $s$ lies in its double centralizer. Proof: the double centralizer is a non-unital subalgebra, and each $x \in s$ commutes with each element of the centralizer of $s$, so $s$ lies in it. The consequence is `NonUnitalAlgebra.instIsMulCommutative_adjoin`: a commutative set generates a commutative subalgebra, by `NonUnitalAlgebra.commute_of_mem_adjoin_of_forall_mem_commute`.

The rest of the module repeats the API of `Subalgebra`. A map $f$ has the range `NonUnitalAlgHom.range f` and the equalizer `NonUnitalAlgHom.equalizer f g`. There are `NonUnitalSubalgebra.map` and `NonUnitalSubalgebra.comap`, inclusions `NonUnitalSubalgebra.inclusion`, products `NonUnitalSubalgebra.prod` with `NonUnitalSubalgebra.center_prod`, and directed unions with `NonUnitalSubalgebra.iSupLift`. The functions `nonUnitalSubalgebraOfNonUnitalSubring` and `nonUnitalSubalgebraOfNonUnitalSubsemiring` view a non-unital subring as a $\mathbb{Z}$-subalgebra or $\mathbb{N}$-subalgebra.

#### The unitization

Let $A$ be a non-unital $R$-algebra. The unitization `Unitization R A` is the module $R \times A$ with the product
$$(r, a)(s, b) = (rs, \ r b + s a + ab).$$
The unit is $(1, 0)$. The inclusions are `Unitization.inl` for $r \mapsto (r, 0)$ and `Unitization.inr` for $a \mapsto (0, a)$, and `Unitization.inr` is a coercion, written `↑a`. The underlying pair is `Unitization.toProd`, and the lemmas `Unitization.fst_mul` and `Unitization.snd_mul` state the product formula. The induction principle `Unitization.ind` says that each element is `inl r + ↑a`.

The multiplication rules for the two kinds of elements follow from the formula.

| Lean name | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| `Unitization.inl_mul_inl` | $(r, 0)(s, 0) = (rs, 0)$ |
| `Unitization.inl_mul_inr` | $(r, 0)(0, a) = (0, r a)$ |
| `Unitization.inr_mul_inl` | $(0, a)(r, 0) = (0, r a)$ |
| `Unitization.inr_mul` | $(0, a)(0, b) = (0, ab)$ |
| `Unitization.inr_injective` | $a \mapsto (0, a)$ is injective |

The ring axioms hold. Associativity uses both compatibility classes, since the expansion of $((r,a)(s,b))(t,c)$ has the terms $t(rb)$, $(rb)c$ and $(ab)c$, and these must match the terms of the other product. The instances `Unitization.instRing`, `Unitization.instCommRing` and `Unitization.instAlgebra` make it a ring and an $R$-algebra. The structure map is the first inclusion: `Unitization.algebraMap_eq_inl` says `algebraMap R (Unitization R A)` is `Unitization.inl`, by definition of the algebra structure. The map `Unitization.fstHom` is the algebra map to $R$, and its kernel is the image of $A$. `Unitization.inrNonUnitalAlgHom` is the inclusion of $A$ as a non-unital map.

#### The universal property

The equivalence `Unitization.lift` identifies the non-unital maps $A \to C$ into a unital $R$-algebra $C$ with the unital maps $R \oplus A \to C$. A non-unital map $f$ goes to
$$\tilde f(r, a) = \operatorname{algebraMap}(r) + f(a).$$
This map is multiplicative: $\tilde f((r,a)(s,b)) = rs + f(rb + sa + ab) = rs + r f(b) + s f(a) + f(a) f(b) = (r + f(a))(s + f(b))$, where $r$ stands for its image in $C$. The inverse direction restricts a unital map to $A$. The two directions are inverse because a unital map is fixed by its values on $A$, as `Unitization.algHom_ext` says. The map `NonUnitalAlgHom.toAlgHom` is the forward direction as a function.

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Algebra.Subalgebra.Unitization` describes the range. The theorem `Unitization.lift_range` says that the range of $\tilde f$ is the subalgebra generated by the range of $f$: $\operatorname{Algebra.adjoin}(f(A))$. Proof: the range of $\tilde f$ is a subalgebra that contains $f(A)$, so it contains the adjoin. Each value $r + f(a)$ is in the adjoin, since the adjoin contains the scalars. For a non-unital subalgebra $s$ of a unital algebra $A$, the map `NonUnitalSubalgebra.unitization s` is the lift of the inclusion, with range `Algebra.adjoin R s` by `NonUnitalSubalgebra.unitization_range`. When $1 \notin s$ and $R$ is a field, this map is injective, so `NonUnitalSubalgebra.unitizationAlgEquiv` is an isomorphism $R \oplus s \cong \operatorname{Algebra.adjoin}(s)$. Proof of injectivity: if $r + a = 0$ with $r \neq 0$, then $1 = -r^{-1} a \in s$, which is false. So $r = 0$ and then $a = 0$. The theorem `AlgHomClass.unitization_injective` states this for any algebra map on the unitization that fixes $s$.

#### Star structures

When $A$ has a star operation compatible with $R$, the unitization is a star algebra with $(r, a)^* = (\bar r, a^*)$, by `Unitization.instStarRing`. The lift has a star version `Unitization.starLift`, and `Unitization.starMap` applies a star map on $A$ to the unitization. The lemma `Unitization.isStarNormal_iff_snd` says that an element is normal exactly when its $A$-part is normal, since the $R$-part commutes with everything. The lemma `Unitization.isSelfAdjoint_iff_snd` says the same for self-adjoint elements when the star on $R$ is trivial. Volume 16 uses this unitization for non-unital C*-algebras.

#### Common errors

1. `Unitization R A` needs both `IsScalarTower R A A` and `SMulCommClass R A A` for its ring structure. A non-unital algebra without these instances gives no ring.
2. The unitization of an algebra that already has a unit is not the same algebra: $R \oplus R$ is $R \times R$ by $(r, a) \mapsto (r, r + a)$, not $R$. The unit of $A$ is not the unit of `Unitization R A`.
3. `NonUnitalSubalgebra.unitizationAlgEquiv` needs $1 \notin s$ and a field $R$. For a subalgebra that contains $1$ the map has a kernel, the span of $(1, -1)$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute $(2, a)(3, b)$ in `Unitization ℤ A` with `Unitization.fst_mul` and `Unitization.snd_mul`.
2. (A) Show that the image of `Unitization.inr` is an ideal of the unitization.
3. (B) Prove that the map $R \oplus R \to R \times R$, $(r, a) \mapsto (r, r + a)$, is an isomorphism of rings.
4. (B) Prove that the lift of a non-unital map is multiplicative, by expanding both sides with `Unitization.snd_mul`.
5. (B) Show that a non-unital subalgebra generated by one element $x$ is the span of the powers $x^n$ with $n \geq 1$.
6. (C) Let $A$ be the even functions in $\mathbb{R}[X]$ with no constant term. Describe `Algebra.adjoin ℝ A` and check `Unitization.lift_range` for the inclusion.
