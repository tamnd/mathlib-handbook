# Characteristic and the integers modulo n

This chapter studies the unique homomorphism $\mathbb{Z} \to R$ and the rings $\mathbb{Z}/n$ that it produces. Section 1 defines the characteristic of a ring as the generator of the kernel of this map, proves that the characteristic of an integral domain is $0$ or a prime, and shows that each field contains $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{Z}/p$ as its prime field. It then proves the identity $(a + b)^p = a^p + b^p$ in characteristic $p$, so that the $p$-th power map is a ring homomorphism, the Frobenius. Section 2 treats $\mathbb{Z}/n$ as the quotient ring $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$: its universal property, its ideals, the equivalence of "$n$ prime", "$\mathbb{Z}/n$ a domain" and "$\mathbb{Z}/n$ a field", the Chinese remainder theorem as a ring isomorphism, and the units, nilpotents and idempotents that the theorem computes. Section 3 describes the library for parity, which is the residue modulo $2$: even and odd elements of a semiring, the sign $(-1)^n$ as a unit of $\mathbb{Z}$, and periodic and antiperiodic functions. Section 4 follows the characteristic through constructions: subrings, quotients, products, algebras and linear maps, and then finite rings, rings of equal and mixed characteristic, and rings of characteristic $2$.

The chapter needs Chapter 2 for quotients and the isomorphism theorems, and Chapter 3 for domains, fields and the field of fractions. Volume 2, Chapter 6 built $\mathbb{Z}/n$ from residues and proved the theorems of Euler, Fermat and Wilson by counting; this chapter does not repeat those proofs, and gives a second proof of Fermat's theorem through the Frobenius.

The Frobenius is the main tool of the theory of finite fields in Volume 11, where every finite field turns out to have $p^n$ elements and a cyclic group of units. Chapter 5 uses $\mathbb{Z}/p$ as the first example of polynomials over a finite field, and the Chinese remainder theorem for ideals of a general ring returns in Volume 9.
