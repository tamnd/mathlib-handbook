# Short complexes and exactness

This chapter studies the smallest piece of a complex: two composable morphisms $X_1 \xrightarrow{f} X_2 \xrightarrow{g} X_3$ with $g \circ f = 0$. Section 1 defines these short complexes and their morphisms, and builds the homology in two dual ways, as the cokernel of $X_1 \to \ker g$ and as the kernel of $\operatorname{coker} f \to X_3$. A homology data joins the two, and the section proves that the comparison map between left and right homology is an isomorphism exactly when homology exists. Section 2 defines exactness as the vanishing of homology, proves that every short complex in an abelian category has homology, and gives the concrete forms of exactness for modules and for maps of sets. It also treats short exact sequences and the splitting lemma. Section 3 proves the snake lemma in an abelian category with refinements, introduces exact sequences of any length, and proves the four and five lemmas for modules. Section 4 makes the morphisms of short complexes an abelian group, defines homotopies of short complexes and quasi-isomorphisms, and characterizes the functors that preserve homology and the exact functors. Section 5 gives limits of short complexes, exactness with elements in a concrete category, the pullback and pushout of a short exact sequence, refinements for complexes and the map from the image to the kernel.

The chapter needs the zero objects, kernels and cokernels of Volume 24, Chapter 8, and the limits of Volume 24, Chapter 4. From Volume 25, Chapter 2, it uses the coimage-image isomorphism, the method of refinements and the abelian four and five lemmas, and it does not prove them again. The examples use modules over $\mathbb{Z}$, over $\mathbb{Z}/n$ and over polynomial rings, as in Volume 7.

Chapter 2 applies the results of this chapter to complexes. The homology of a complex at degree $i$ is the homology of the short complex at $i$, and the long exact homology sequence comes from the snake lemma of Section 3. Chapter 3 uses the splittings of Section 2 for the mapping cone, and Chapter 4 uses the short exact sequences for resolutions and derived functors.
