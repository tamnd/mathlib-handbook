# Schemes

This chapter defines schemes and gives the first constructions with them. Section 1 builds the spectrum of a commutative ring: the Zariski topology on its prime ideals, the structure sheaf of locally fractions, its stalks as localizations at primes and its global sections as the ring itself. It proves the adjunction between global sections and the spectrum, and it shows that affine schemes are the same as commutative rings with the arrows reversed. Section 2 defines schemes as locally ringed spaces that are affine near each point, with open subschemes, open immersions and open covers. It glues schemes from affine pieces, with the affine line with a doubled origin and the projective line as examples, and it describes the morphisms from the spectrum of a field. Section 3 constructs fiber products by gluing tensor products, describes the points of a fiber product, and computes the fibers of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}[i] \to \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}$ over $2$, $3$ and $5$.

The chapter needs Chapter 1 on sheaves and Chapter 2 on locally ringed spaces and gluing. From commutative algebra it needs prime ideals, localization and tensor products, from Volumes 6, 8 and 9, and local rings from Volume 10. From category theory it needs limits, the Yoneda lemma and adjunctions, from Volume 24.

Chapter 4 studies properties of schemes, such as reduced, integral and Noetherian schemes, and constructs the projective line again as a projective spectrum. Chapter 5 defines properties of morphisms by conditions on affine open sets and on fibers, and it uses the fiber product for base change and for the diagonal; the doubled origin is its example of a scheme that is not separated. Chapter 6 puts Grothendieck topologies on the category of schemes with the open covers of Section 2.
