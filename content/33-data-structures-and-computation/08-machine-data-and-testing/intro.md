# Machine data and testing

This chapter studies the data of a real machine and the testing of programs. The first section treats the fixed width unsigned integers `UInt8` to `UInt64` and the bit vectors `BitVec w`. It proves that their arithmetic is the arithmetic of the ring $\mathbb{Z}/2^n\mathbb{Z}$, so that $255 + 1 = 0$ in `UInt8`, and it computes the units of this ring. It then defines the class of lawful exclusive or, proves the cancellation laws, and shows that exclusive or with a fixed value is an involutive permutation. It closes with the orders on characters and strings, the padding of strings, and a small model of floating point numbers that shows why floating point addition is not associative. The second section treats randomness and property based testing. It defines random generators as state monads, proves that the bounded generators respect their bounds, and builds the generated injective functions and finitely supported functions of the testing library Plausible. It ends with the limits of testing: a test can refute a statement, but it does not prove it.

The chapter needs the language of Mathlib, decidable propositions and type classes from Volume 1, rings, the integers modulo $n$ and permutations from Volumes 5 and 6, and the lists of Chapter 1 of this volume. The second section uses permutations of lists from Chapter 2, association lists from Chapter 3, and the state monad and the other monads of Chapter 6.

This is the last chapter of the volume. Earlier chapters model data by inductive and coinductive types and prove facts about the models. This chapter applies the same method to the finite types of a computer, and it compares proof with testing, the method of a programmer to gain confidence in a statement before a proof exists.
