# Solvable, nilpotent and semisimple Lie algebras

This chapter divides Lie algebras by the behaviour of their brackets. Section 1 defines the derived series and the lower central series, the solvable and nilpotent algebras and the radical, proves that solvability passes to subalgebras, quotients and extensions, and proves Engel's theorem in full: a finite-dimensional Lie algebra is nilpotent if and only if each $\operatorname{ad} x$ is nilpotent. It states Lie's theorem on common eigenvectors of solvable algebras, with a counterexample in characteristic $p$, and it treats Engel subalgebras and characters. Section 2 defines the trace forms and the Killing form, proves that they are symmetric and invariant, computes the Killing form of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, and proves that an algebra with a non-degenerate Killing form is the direct sum of simple ideals and has only inner derivations. It states Cartan's criteria for solvability and semisimplicity, and it compares the three notions of semisimplicity in the library.

The chapter needs the ideals, quotients, normalizers and derivations of Chapter 4, eigenvalues and the trace from Volume 7, bilinear forms from Volume 8, and Noetherian modules from Volume 9 for the existence of the radical.

Chapter 6 uses the Engel subalgebras and the Killing form of this chapter to define Cartan subalgebras, to split a semisimple algebra into root spaces, and to build $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triples, whose representation theory rests on the computations of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ here. Chapter 7 studies the root systems that come out of this construction, and Chapter 8 places them among Coxeter groups and further structures.
