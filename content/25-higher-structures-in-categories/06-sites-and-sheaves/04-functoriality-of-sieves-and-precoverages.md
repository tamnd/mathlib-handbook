## Functoriality of sieves and precoverages

### Overview

Section 1 defines a sieve on one object of one category. This section moves sieves between categories, turns them into presheaves, and gives the most general way to name the covers of a site. A functor $F : C \to D$ sends a sieve $S$ on $X$ to the sieve on $F(X)$ that the arrows $F(f)$ with $f \in S$ generate, and it pulls a sieve $R$ on $F(X)$ back to the sieve of the arrows $f$ with $F(f) \in R$. These two operations form a Galois connection. A sieve $S$ on $X$ is also a subfunctor of the representable presheaf $\operatorname{Hom}(-, X)$, and this point of view is the base of the sheaf condition in the form "each morphism from $S$ to $P$ extends to $\operatorname{Hom}(-, X)$".

A precoverage gives a set of covering presieves on each object, with no condition. Pretopologies and coverages of Section 1 are precoverages with extra properties, and each precoverage generates a Grothendieck topology. The main theorem says that a presheaf of types is a sheaf for the generated topology when it satisfies the sheaf condition for the pullbacks of the given families. The section ends with two tools that appear in the later chapters: effective epimorphic families, which are the covers of the canonical topology, and families of objects that cover the terminal object, which give a short test for global sections.

The section uses the limits, colimits and the Yoneda lemma of Volume 24. Chapter 9 uses the functoriality of sieves for continuous and cocontinuous functors, and Chapter 10 uses precoverages for hypercovers.

### The library around it

**Pushforward and pullback of sieves.** The module `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.Sieves.Functoriality` works with a functor $F : C \to D$. At the level of presieves, `Presieve.functorPullback F R` is the preimage of a presieve $R$ on $F(X)$, and `Presieve.map F R` is the presieve on $F(X)$ of the arrows $F(f)$ for $f \in R$. The presieve `Presieve.functorPushforward F R` contains the arrows $g$ that factor as $F(f) \circ h$ with $f \in R$, so it is already a sieve. The structure `Presieve.FunctorPushforwardStructure` and the choice function `Presieve.getFunctorPushforwardStructure` keep one such factorization for each arrow, and later constructions use this fixed choice.

For sieves, `Sieve.functorPullback F R` is a sieve on $X$ and `Sieve.functorPushforward F S` is the sieve on $F(X)$ generated by the image. The lemma `Sieve.functor_galoisConnection` states that the pushforward is left adjoint to the pullback in the sense of order theory:
$$\operatorname{push}_F(S) \le R \iff S \le \operatorname{pull}_F(R).$$
It gives `Sieve.functorPullback_pushforward_le` and the monotonicity lemmas `Sieve.functorPushforward_monotone` and `Sieve.functorPullback_monotone` at once. For a fully faithful functor, `Sieve.fullyFaithfulFunctorGaloisCoinsertion` says that the pullback of the pushforward of $S$ is $S$ again. For a full and essentially surjective functor, `Sieve.essSurjFullFunctorGaloisInsertion` gives the dual statement. The lemmas `Sieve.functorPushforward_comp` and `Sieve.functorPullback_comp` compare the operations for a composition $F \circ G$, the lemma `Sieve.functorPushforward_ofArrows` computes the pushforward of a sieve generated by a family, and `Sieve.functorPushforward_equivalence_eq_pullback` treats an equivalence and its inverse. The lattice lemmas `Sieve.functorPullback_inter`, `Sieve.functorPullback_union` and `Sieve.functorPushforward_union` complete the list.

**Sieves as presheaves.** In `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.Sieves.Presheaf`, a sieve $S$ on $X$ gives the presheaf `Sieve.functor S`, whose value at $Y$ is the type of the arrows $Y \to X$ in $S$. The natural transformation `Sieve.functorInclusion S` into `yoneda.obj X` is the inclusion, and the instance `Sieve.functorInclusion_is_mono` says that it is a monomorphism. For the top sieve this inclusion is an isomorphism, by `Sieve.functorInclusion_top_isIso`. In the other direction, `Sieve.sieveOfSubfunctor` reads a sieve off a natural transformation into `yoneda.obj X`, and `Sieve.sieveOfSubfunctor_functorInclusion` proves that the two operations are inverse on sieves. The morphism `Sieve.natTransOfLe` corresponds to an inclusion of sieves, and `Sieve.toFunctor` sends an arrow $f \in S$ to a morphism from `yoneda.obj Y` to `Sieve.functor S`. The primed family `Sieve.uliftFunctor`, `Sieve.uliftFunctorInclusion` and `Sieve.sieveOfUliftSubfunctor` repeats this with a universe lift. The module `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.Sieves.Shrink` gives `Sieve.shrinkFunctor`, the same subfunctor inside the shrunk Yoneda presheaf of a locally small category, with the comparisons `Sieve.shrinkFunctorUliftFunctorIso` and `Sieve.shrinkFunctorIsoFunctor`. The module `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.Sieves` only imports the sieve files.

**Precoverages.** The structure `Precoverage C` in `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.Precoverage` has one field, `Precoverage.coverings`, a set of presieves for each object. A coercion lets the user write `J X` for this set. The precoverages of $C$ form a complete lattice, by `Precoverage.instCompleteLattice`, and the library states the usual properties as classes.

1. `Precoverage.HasIsos`: each isomorphism, as a one arrow presieve, is a cover.
2. `Precoverage.IsStableUnderBaseChange`: the pullback of a cover along any morphism is a cover. The lemma `Precoverage.mem_coverings_of_isPullback` uses chosen pullback squares, so it needs no `HasPullbacks` instance.
3. `Precoverage.IsStableUnderComposition`: if each member of a cover gets a cover, the composite family is a cover, by `Precoverage.comp_mem_coverings`.
4. `Precoverage.IsStableUnderSup` and `Precoverage.HasPullbacks`.

A functor $F : C \to D$ pulls a precoverage $J$ on $D$ back to `Precoverage.comap F J`. By `Precoverage.mem_comap_iff`, a presieve $R$ is a cover for the pullback when `Presieve.map F R` is a cover for $J$. The classes `Functor.PreservesPairwisePullbacks` and `Precoverage.PullbacksPreservedBy` record that $F$ keeps the pullbacks of the members of each cover. The instances transport the four properties along `Precoverage.comap` under the natural hypotheses.

**The generated topology.** The module `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.PrecoverageToGrothendieck` defines `Precoverage.toGrothendieck J`. Its covering sieves are given by the inductive predicate `Precoverage.Saturate`: the sieve generated by a cover, the top sieve, the pullbacks, and the sieves that are covering by transitivity. The lemma `Precoverage.toGrothendieck_eq_sInf` describes the same topology as the smallest topology in which each `Sieve.generate R` with $R \in J(X)$ is covering. In the other direction, `GrothendieckTopology.toPrecoverage` takes the presieves whose generated sieve is covering. The lemma `Precoverage.toGrothendieck_le_iff_le_toPrecoverage` states the Galois connection between the two maps. When $J$ has the four properties of the list above, `Precoverage.mem_toGrothendieck_iff_of_isStableUnderComposition` gives the simple form: a sieve $S$ is covering when it contains a cover of $J$.

The main theorem is `Precoverage.isSheaf_toGrothendieck_iff`. A presheaf of types $P$ is a sheaf for the generated topology if and only if $P$ satisfies the sheaf condition for each pullback $f^* \langle R \rangle$ of the sieve generated by a cover $R$. The pullbacks are necessary, because the generated topology contains them. The converse direction is `Presieve.IsSheaf.isSheafFor_of_mem_precoverage`. The structure `Precoverage.Generates K J` of `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.Precoverage.Generates` says that the $J$ sheaves are exactly the presheaves that are sheaves for each cover of $K$. Then `Precoverage.Generates.toGrothendieck_eq` gives $K$ generates $J$ only if $J$ is the generated topology, and for a coverage the converse holds: `Coverage.generates_iff` and `Coverage.generates_toGrothendieck`. The lemma `Precoverage.Generates.isSheaf_iff` gives the test for sheaves with values in a general category.

The module `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.Precoverage.Subsheaf` gives `Precoverage.subsheafify K 𝒮`, the smallest subpresheaf of $F$ that contains a family of sets of sections $𝒮$ and that is closed under restriction and under gluing along the covers of $K$. The predicate `Precoverage.SubsheafClosure` is its inductive definition. The lemma `Precoverage.isSheafFor_subsheafify` says that the result is a sheaf for each cover $R$ for which $F$ is a sheaf, and `Precoverage.small_subsheafify_of_small` bounds the size of its sections.

**Jointly surjective families.** In `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.JointlySurjective`, `Types.jointlySurjectivePrecoverage` is the precoverage of the category of types whose covers are the families of maps with jointly surjective images. The lemmas `Types.mem_jointlySurjectivePrecoverage_iff` and `Types.ofArrows_mem_jointlySurjectivePrecoverage_iff` state this, and the instances give the four properties. For a forgetful functor $F : C \to$ Type, the pullback precoverage has the families whose images under $F$ are jointly surjective, by `Presieve.mem_comap_jointlySurjectivePrecoverage_iff`. The lemma `isStableUnderBaseChange_comap_jointlySurjectivePrecoverage` gives stability under base change when the comparison map $F(X \times_S Y) \to F(X) \times_{F(S)} F(Y)$ is surjective.

**Effective epimorphic sieves.** The module `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.EffectiveEpimorphic` defines `Sieve.EffectiveEpimorphic S`: the cocone on $X$ over the diagram of the arrows of $S$ is a colimit cocone. The abbreviation `Presieve.EffectiveEpimorphic` applies this to the generated sieve. The lemma `Sieve.effectiveEpimorphic_singleton` says that a single arrow $f$ is effective epimorphic exactly when $f$ is an effective epimorphism, and `Sieve.effectiveEpimorphic_family` says the same thing for a family and the class `EffectiveEpiFamily`. The helper sieves `Sieve.generateSingleton` and `Sieve.generateFamily` have good definitional properties, and `Sieve.generateFamily_eq` relates them to `Sieve.generate`. The lemma `Presieve.EffectiveEpimorphic.isSheafFor_of_isRepresentable` gives the link with the canonical topology of Section 2: each representable presheaf is a sheaf for an effective epimorphic presieve. Section 9 uses this for the coherent and regular topologies.

**Objects that cover the terminal object.** In `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.CoversTop.Basic`, `GrothendieckTopology.CoversTop J Y` for a family $Y : I \to C$ says that for each $X$ the sieve `Sieve.ofObjects Y X`, of the arrows into $X$ from objects that map to some $Y_i$, is covering. If $C$ has a terminal object $T$, `GrothendieckTopology.coversTop_iff_of_isTerminal` reduces this to one sieve on $T$. A family of elements over the objects, `Presheaf.FamilyOfElementsOnObjects F Y`, is an element of $F(Y_i)$ for each $i$. It is compatible, `Presheaf.FamilyOfElementsOnObjects.IsCompatible`, when its pullbacks to any $Z$ agree. For a sheaf $F$, `Presheaf.FamilyOfElementsOnObjects.IsCompatible.existsUnique_section` gives a unique global section of $F$ with these restrictions, and `Presheaf.FamilyOfElementsOnObjects.IsCompatible.section_` is that section. The lemmas `GrothendieckTopology.CoversTop.sections_ext` and `GrothendieckTopology.CoversTop.ext` give the uniqueness half for sections of a sheaf of types and for morphisms into a limit of a sheaf. The module `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.CoversTop.Over` has one lemma, `GrothendieckTopology.CoversTop.over`: covers of the terminal object compose with covers in the slices `Over (X i)` with the topology of Chapter 9.

#### Common errors

1. `Sieve.functorPushforward F S` is the sieve generated by the image of $S$, not the image. Use `Presieve.map F R` for the plain image of a presieve, as in `Precoverage.mem_comap_iff`.
2. `Precoverage.isSheaf_toGrothendieck_iff` quantifies over the pullbacks of the generated sieves. A presheaf that is a sheaf only for the covers of $J$ need not be a sheaf for the generated topology, unless $J$ is stable under base change or is a coverage.
3. A precoverage has no axioms. Before the use of `Precoverage.mem_toGrothendieck_iff_of_isStableUnderComposition`, give the instances `Precoverage.HasIsos`, `Precoverage.IsStableUnderBaseChange`, `Precoverage.IsStableUnderComposition` and `Precoverage.HasPullbacks`.

### Exercises

1. (A) Use `Sieve.functor_galoisConnection` to prove that the pullback of the pushforward of a sieve $S$ along $F$ contains $S$.
2. (A) Show that the presieve of one identity arrow on $X$ is in `Types.jointlySurjectivePrecoverage.coverings X`.
3. (B) For a fully faithful $F$, use `Sieve.fullyFaithfulFunctorGaloisCoinsertion` to prove that `Sieve.functorPushforward F` is injective on sieves on $X$.
4. (B) State, with `Sieve.effectiveEpimorphic_singleton`, what `Presieve.EffectiveEpimorphic.isSheafFor_of_isRepresentable` gives for a single effective epimorphism, and compare with `Presieve.IsSheafFor.singleton_of_isRepresentable_of_effectiveEpi`.
5. (B) Let $C$ have a terminal object $T$. Show that the family with one member $T$ covers the terminal object for any topology $J$.
6. (C) Let $K$ be a coverage. Use `Coverage.generates_toGrothendieck` and `Precoverage.Generates.isSheaf_iff` to state the sheaf condition for the generated topology without pullbacks, and explain why the coverage axiom makes the pullbacks of `Precoverage.isSheaf_toGrothendieck_iff` unnecessary.
