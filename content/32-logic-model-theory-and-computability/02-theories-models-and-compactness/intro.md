# Theories, models and compactness

This chapter proves the central theorems of first-order model theory. Section 1 defines satisfiable, finitely satisfiable and complete theories and semantic consequence. It constructs ultraproducts of structures, proves the theorem of Łoś, and deduces the compactness theorem: a theory is satisfiable if each finite subset is. Compactness then gives models of arbitrarily large cardinality. The section also builds Skolem functions and studies the equivalence of formulas modulo a theory. Section 2 defines elementary embeddings and elementary substructures and proves the Tarski-Vaught test. It proves the downward and upward Löwenheim-Skolem theorems and the Łoś-Vaught test for completeness. It then studies direct limits of structures, partial equivalences and the back-and-forth method for countably generated structures, and it ends with complete types and the Stone space of types.

The chapter needs all of Chapter 1: terms, formulas, structures, realization, substructures and the bound on the size of a closure. It uses ultrafilters and filters from Volume 13, and for the type space the topology of Volume 13: bases, clopen sets and compact spaces. It uses the arithmetic of infinite cardinals, as Mathlib has it in the set theory of Mathlib, and finite and countable types from Volume 3. The compactness proof is semantic, since the index of this edition has no proof calculus for first-order logic.

Chapter 3 uses the Łoś-Vaught test and back-and-forth to prove that the theory of dense linear orders without endpoints is complete and that Fraïssé limits are unique. Chapter 4 uses the Łoś-Vaught test to prove that each theory of algebraically closed fields of fixed characteristic is complete, and it uses compactness for the Lefschetz principle.
