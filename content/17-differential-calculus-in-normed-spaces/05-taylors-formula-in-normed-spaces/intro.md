# Taylor's formula in normed spaces

A map of class $C^n$ between normed spaces is close to its Taylor polynomial $\sum_{k \le n} \frac{1}{k!} D^k f(x)(h, \dots, h)$. This chapter proves Taylor's formula in this setting and applies it to the extrema of real functions on a normed space.

Section 1 restricts $f$ to the segment from $x$ to $x + h$. For vector values the mean value theorem with an intermediate point fails, and the section replaces it by a comparison inequality. It gives three forms of the remainder: a bound with $\|h\|^{n+1}/(n+1)!$, an integral, and the Peano form $o(\|h\|^n)$. Section 2 proves that the derivative vanishes at a local extremum, and that a critical point is a strict local minimum when the second derivative is bounded below by $m\|h\|^2$. In finite dimension a positive definite Hessian is enough, and an example in $\ell^2$ shows that this fails in infinite dimension. The section also proves that a $C^2$ function with a nonnegative second derivative on a convex set is convex.

The chapter needs the classes $C^n$, the iterated derivatives and the symmetry of the second derivative of Chapter 4, the mean value inequality of Chapter 3, and Taylor's theorem in one variable and the second derivative test of Volume 15, Chapter 4. It uses the Hahn-Banach theorem and the compactness of the unit sphere in finite dimension from Volume 16.

Chapter 7 compares the Taylor polynomials of an analytic map with the partial sums of its power series.
