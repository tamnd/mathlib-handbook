## Ultrametric norms and the lemma of Krasner

### Overview

A norm is ultrametric, or nonarchimedean, when it satisfies the strong triangle inequality $\|x + y\| \le \max(\|x\|, \|y\|)$. The $p$-adic norm on $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is the main example, and each norm on a finite field is another. The strong inequality changes the geometry. Each triangle is isosceles: if $\|x\| \ne \|y\|$, then $\|x + y\| = \max(\|x\|, \|y\|)$. Each point of a ball is a center of the ball, and two balls are either disjoint or one contains the other. A ball at $0$ is an open subgroup, so the topology is totally disconnected, and a series converges as soon as its terms tend to $0$.

For a field there is a simple test. A normed field is ultrametric if and only if $\|n\| \le 1$ for each natural number $n$. So the integers are bounded exactly in the nonarchimedean case. The lemma of Krasner is the main algebraic result of the section. In a complete ultrametric field, if $y$ is closer to a separable algebraic element $x$ than each other conjugate of $x$, then $x$ lies in the field generated by $y$. The lemma shows that the fields generated by close elements are the same, and it is a tool for the study of local fields.

### The library around it

The class `IsUltrametricDist` is defined for metric spaces in the volume on topology: it states $d(x, z) \le \max(d(x, y), d(y, z))$. The module `Mathlib.Analysis.Normed.Group.Ultra` gives the forms for norms. The lemma `IsUltrametricDist.norm_add_le_max` states $\|x + y\| \le \max(\|x\|, \|y\|)$, and `IsUltrametricDist.isUltrametricDist_of_forall_norm_add_le_max_norm` is the converse that builds the class from this inequality. The lemma `IsUltrametricDist.isUltrametricDist_iff_isNonarchimedean_norm` states that the class holds if and only if the norm satisfies `IsNonarchimedean`, the predicate on functions that the volume on rings uses for absolute values. The forms for multiplicative groups, such as `IsUltrametricDist.norm_mul_le_max`, come from the same proofs.

The isosceles property is `IsUltrametricDist.norm_add_eq_max_of_norm_ne_norm`: if $\|x\| \ne \|y\|$, then $\|x + y\| = \max(\|x\|, \|y\|)$. The lemma `IsUltrametricDist.norm_eq_of_add_norm_lt_max` gives the contrapositive, and `IsUltrametricDist.norm_sub_eq_max_of_norm_sub_ne_norm_sub` gives the form with three points. The lemma `IsUltrametricDist.norm_nsmul_le` states that $\|n x\| \le \|x\|$, and `IsUltrametricDist.norm_zsmul_le` is the form with integers. For sums, `IsUltrametricDist.norm_sum_le_of_forall_le_of_nonneg` states that a finite sum of terms of norm at most $C$ has norm at most $C$, and `IsUltrametricDist.exists_norm_finsetSum_le` states that the norm of a sum is at most the norm of one of its terms. For infinite sums, `IsUltrametricDist.norm_tsum_le` states that $\|\sum_i f(i)\| \le \sup_i \|f(i)\|$.

The same lemmas hold for groups written with multiplication, where the norm of a product plays the part of the norm of a sum. The lemma `IsUltrametricDist.norm_pow_le` states that $\|x^n\| \le \|x\|$, and `IsUltrametricDist.norm_zpow_le` is the form with integer powers. The lemmas `IsUltrametricDist.norm_prod_le_of_forall_le_of_nonneg` and `IsUltrametricDist.exists_norm_finsetProd_le` bound finite products, and `IsUltrametricDist.norm_tprod_le` bounds infinite products. In each case the bound is a maximum and not a sum, so no factor of the number of terms appears. This is the reason why estimates in $p$-adic analysis are often simpler than the real estimates.

The balls give subgroups. The definition `IsUltrametricDist.ball_openAddSubgroup` takes a radius $r > 0$ and gives the open ball of radius $r$ at $0$ as an open subgroup, and `IsUltrametricDist.closedBall_openAddSubgroup` does the same for the closed ball, which is also open. The instance `IsUltrametricDist.nonarchimedeanAddGroup` states that the group is a `NonarchimedeanAddGroup`, the class of Volume 14 for topological groups with a basis of open subgroups at $0$. So the results of that volume, such as those on the convergence of sums, apply to each ultrametric normed group.

The modules for rings and fields add the integers. In a ring with an ultrametric norm and `NormOneClass`, the lemma `IsUltrametricDist.norm_natCast_le_one` states that $\|n\| \le 1$ for each natural number $n$, and `IsUltrametricDist.norm_intCast_le_one` gives the same for integers. The lemma `IsUltrametricDist.norm_add_one_le_max_norm_one` states $\|x + 1\| \le \max(\|x\|, 1)$. For a normed division ring, `isUltrametricDist_iff_forall_norm_natCast_le_one` states the converse: the norm is ultrametric if and only if $\|n\| \le 1$ for all $n$. The lemmas `IsUltrametricDist.isUltrametricDist_of_forall_norm_add_one_le_max_norm_one` and `IsUltrametricDist.isUltrametricDist_of_forall_norm_add_one_of_norm_le_one` are two more tests of this kind. The proofs expand $(x + 1)^m$ with the binomial formula and take the $m$-th root as $m \to \infty$.

The module `Mathlib.Analysis.Normed.Algebra.Ultra` passes the property along a normed algebra. For a normed division ring $L$ that is a normed algebra over a normed field $K$, `IsUltrametricDist.of_normedAlgebra` states that $L$ is ultrametric when $K$ is, and `IsUltrametricDist.normedAlgebra_iff` states the equivalence. The direction from $L$ to $K$ holds for each seminormed ring with $\|1\| = 1$. For example, each finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ with a norm that extends the norm of $\mathbb{Q}_p$ is ultrametric.

The two modules on order homomorphisms connect the bundled seminorms of Chapter 8 with the normed classes. A function of a type with `AddGroupSeminormClass` gives a seminormed group, by `AddGroupSeminormClass.toSeminormedAddGroup`, and `AddGroupSeminormClass.toSeminormedAddGroup_norm_eq` states that the norm of $x$ is the value of the function at $x$. The forms `AddGroupNormClass.toNormedAddGroup` and `AddGroupNormClass.toNormedAddCommGroup` give normed groups from norms, and the multiplicative forms such as `GroupSeminormClass.toSeminormedGroup` are also there. These are definitions, not instances, so that they do not compete with the norms that a type already has. The lemma `AddGroupSeminormClass.isUltrametricDist` states that the group is ultrametric when the seminorm satisfies `IsNonarchimedean`.

The module `Mathlib.Analysis.Normed.Field.Krasner` states the lemma of Krasner as a class. The class `IsKrasner K L` states that, for $x, y \in L$ with $x$ separable over $K$, with all conjugates of $x$ in $L$ and with $y$ integral over $K$, if $\|x - y\| < \|x - x'\|$ for each conjugate $x' \ne x$, then $x$ is in $K(y)$. The theorem `IsKrasner.krasner` applies the class. The instance `IsKrasner.of_completeSpace` states that the class holds for each algebraic extension $L$ of a complete ultrametric nontrivially normed field $K$, when the norm of $L$ extends the norm of $K$. The theorem `IsKrasner.of_completeSpace_of_normal` is the first step of the proof, for normal extensions. The proof uses the spectral norm of section 4 to put a norm on the algebraic closure of $K$.

#### Common errors

1. Expecting `IsUltrametricDist` to follow from a valuation. The class is about the metric. For a field with a valuation, use the norm that the valuation gives, from Volume 14, and then prove the class with `IsUltrametricDist.isUltrametricDist_of_forall_norm_add_le_max_norm`.
2. Using `AddGroupSeminormClass.toSeminormedAddGroup` as an instance. It is a definition, so it must be made a local instance or passed explicitly, and it can make two norms on one type.
3. Forgetting the conditions of `IsKrasner.krasner`. The element $x$ must be separable, and all its conjugates must lie in $L$. Without the second condition the distance to the conjugates is not defined in $L$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show with `IsUltrametricDist.norm_add_eq_max_of_norm_ne_norm` that $\|x + y\| = \|x\|$ when $\|y\| < \|x\|$.
2. (A) Use `isUltrametricDist_iff_forall_norm_natCast_le_one` to show that the absolute value on $\mathbb{R}$ is not ultrametric.
3. (B) Show that in an ultrametric normed group each point of an open ball is a center of the ball.
4. (B) Show with `IsUltrametricDist.norm_tsum_le` that a series $\sum_n a_n$ in a complete ultrametric group converges when $a_n \to 0$, by a Cauchy argument on partial sums.
5. (B) Show that in $\mathbb{Q}_p$ the closed ball of radius $1$ at $0$ is open, with `IsUltrametricDist.closedBall_openAddSubgroup`.
6. (C) Prove `IsUltrametricDist.norm_natCast_le_one` by induction on $n$ from `IsUltrametricDist.norm_add_le_max`.
