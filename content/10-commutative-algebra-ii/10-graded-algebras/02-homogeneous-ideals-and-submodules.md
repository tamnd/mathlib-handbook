## Homogeneous ideals and submodules

### Overview

Let $A = \bigoplus_i A_i$ be a graded ring. An ideal $I$ of $A$ is homogeneous when it contains the homogeneous components of each of its elements. Equivalently, $I$ is generated by homogeneous elements. For the polynomial ring $k[X_0, \dots, X_n]$, the homogeneous ideals are the ideals that define subsets of projective space, and the homogeneous prime ideals are the points of the scheme $\operatorname{Proj} A$.

In Mathlib the property is `Ideal.IsHomogeneous 𝒜 I`, and the bundled type is `HomogeneousIdeal 𝒜`. This section describes the definition and its equivalent forms, the complete lattice of homogeneous ideals, the largest homogeneous ideal below an ideal and the smallest one above it, the irrelevant ideal, the test for primality on homogeneous elements, the radical, and the maps along graded ring homomorphisms. The same theory exists for submodules of a graded module and for subsemirings.

### The library around it

#### The definition

Let `𝒜 : ι → σ` be a grading of the semiring $A$, with `[GradedRing 𝒜]`. The definition is `Ideal.IsHomogeneous 𝒜 I`: for every $i$ and every $r \in I$, the component `(decompose 𝒜 r i : A)` is in $I$. It is an abbreviation for `Submodule.IsHomogeneous I 𝒜`, the same property for a submodule of a graded module, so that the two theories share their lemmas.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.mem_iff` | $r \in I \iff r_i \in I$ for all $i$ |
| `Ideal.isHomogeneous_iff_forall_subset` | $I \subseteq \pi_i^{-1}(I)$ for all $i$, with $\pi_i$ = `GradedRing.proj 𝒜 i` |
| `Ideal.isHomogeneous_iff_subset_iInter` | $I \subseteq \bigcap_i \pi_i^{-1}(I)$ |
| `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.iff_exists` | $I$ is the span of a set of homogeneous elements |
| `Ideal.homogeneous_span` | the span of homogeneous elements is homogeneous |
| `Ideal.mul_homogeneous_element_mem_of_mem` | $x$ homogeneous, $x \in I \Rightarrow (rx)_j \in I$ |

The proof that a span of homogeneous elements is homogeneous uses the last line. An element of the span is a sum $\sum_k r_kx_k$ with $x_k$ homogeneous. For $x_k \in A_d$, the component $(r_kx_k)_j$ is $(r_k)_{j-d}\,x_k$ or $0$, so it is in the span. The converse is direct: a homogeneous ideal is the span of the homogeneous elements it contains, since each element is the sum of its components. In `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.iff_exists` the set of generators is a subset of the type `SetLike.homogeneousSubmonoid 𝒜`, the multiplicative monoid of homogeneous elements.

#### The lattice of homogeneous ideals

The structure `HomogeneousIdeal 𝒜` is a submodule with the field `is_homogeneous'`. In fact it is an abbreviation for `HomogeneousSubmodule 𝒜 𝒜`, the homogeneous submodules of $A$ as a module over itself. The forgetful map is `HomogeneousIdeal.toIdeal`, injective by `HomogeneousIdeal.toIdeal_injective`, and `HomogeneousIdeal.isHomogeneous` recovers the property. Use `HomogeneousIdeal.ext` to prove that two homogeneous ideals are equal from the equality of their ideals.

The property is closed under the lattice operations and under products: `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.bot`, `.top`, `.sup`, `.inf`, `.mul`, `.iSup`, `.iInf`, `.sSup` and `.sInf`. The intersection case is direct from the definition. For the sum and the product, write both ideals as spans of homogeneous elements: the sum is the span of the union, and the product is the span of the products, which are homogeneous. So the instance `HomogeneousIdeal.completeLattice` is a complete sublattice of the ideals, and the lemmas `HomogeneousIdeal.toIdeal_sup`, `HomogeneousIdeal.toIdeal_inf`, `HomogeneousIdeal.toIdeal_sSup`, `HomogeneousIdeal.toIdeal_iInf`, `HomogeneousIdeal.toIdeal_mul` and the others say that the forgetful map preserves them.

#### Core and hull

Each ideal $I$ has a largest homogeneous ideal below it and a smallest homogeneous ideal above it.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Ideal.homogeneousCore 𝒜 I` | the largest homogeneous ideal contained in $I$ |
| `Ideal.homogeneousCore'` | the span of the homogeneous elements of $I$, as an ideal |
| `Ideal.toIdeal_homogeneousCore_le` | the core is contained in $I$ |
| `Ideal.mem_homogeneousCore_of_homogeneous_of_mem` | a homogeneous element of $I$ is in the core |
| `Ideal.homogeneousCore.gi` | a Galois coinsertion of the core with `toIdeal` |
| `Ideal.homogeneousHull 𝒜 I` | the smallest homogeneous ideal containing $I$ |
| `Ideal.toIdeal_homogeneousHull_eq_iSup` | the hull is the span of all components $r_i$, $r \in I$ |
| `Ideal.le_toIdeal_homogeneousHull` | $I$ is contained in the hull |
| `Ideal.homogeneousHull.gi` | a Galois insertion of the hull with `toIdeal` |

The core is the span of the homogeneous elements of $I$, which is `Ideal.homogeneousCore'`, and `Ideal.homogeneousCore_eq_sSup` describes it as the supremum of the homogeneous ideals below $I$. The hull is the ideal generated by all components of all elements of $I$. The two Galois connections say that the core is the right adjoint and the hull is the left adjoint of the forgetful map from homogeneous ideals to ideals. If $I$ is homogeneous, then the core and the hull of $I$ are $I$, by `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.toIdeal_homogeneousCore_eq_self` and `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.toIdeal_homogeneousHull_eq_self`.

#### The irrelevant ideal

Let $\iota$ be canonically ordered, for example $\iota = \mathbb{N}$. The irrelevant ideal $A_+ = \bigoplus_{i > 0} A_i$ is `HomogeneousIdeal.irrelevant 𝒜`. In the namespace `HomogeneousIdeal` the scoped notation `𝒜₊` stands for it. Its definition is the kernel of the ring map $a \mapsto a_0$ of Section 1, and `HomogeneousIdeal.toIdeal_irrelevant` records this. The lemma `HomogeneousIdeal.mem_irrelevant_iff` says that $a \in A_+$ if and only if $a_0 = 0$, and `HomogeneousIdeal.irrelevant_eq_span` says that $A_+$ is the span of the homogeneous elements of positive degree. For $A = k[X_0, \dots, X_n]$ the irrelevant ideal is $(X_0, \dots, X_n)$. Its zero set in $k^{n+1}$ is the origin, which is not a point of projective space. So the homogeneous primes that contain $A_+$ are left out of $\operatorname{Proj} A$.

#### Prime ideals

Let $\iota$ be a linearly ordered cancellative additive monoid, such as $\mathbb{N}$ or $\mathbb{Z}$. For a homogeneous ideal it is enough to test primality on homogeneous elements.

**Proposition.** Let $I \ne A$ be a homogeneous ideal. Suppose that for all homogeneous $x, y$ with $xy \in I$, either $x \in I$ or $y \in I$. Then $I$ is prime.

*Proof.* Suppose that $ab \in I$, $a \notin I$ and $b \notin I$. The components of $a$ that lie in $I$ can be removed from $a$, because $I$ is homogeneous: the difference is still not in $I$, and the product with $b$ is still in $I$. So all nonzero components of $a$ are outside $I$, and the same for $b$. Let $a_i$ and $b_j$ be the nonzero components of the largest degree. Since $\iota$ is cancellative and linearly ordered, $i' + j' < i + j$ for every other pair of degrees $i' \le i$, $j' \le j$ with $(i', j') \ne (i, j)$. So $(ab)_{i+j} = a_ib_j$. This element is in $I$, because $I$ is homogeneous. By hypothesis $a_i \in I$ or $b_j \in I$, a contradiction. $\square$

The result is `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.isPrime_of_homogeneous_mem_or_mem`, and `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.isPrime_iff` is the equivalence. Two consequences follow.

1. If $P$ is prime, then its core is prime: `Ideal.IsPrime.homogeneousCore`. Indeed, if $x, y$ are homogeneous and $xy$ is in the core, then $xy \in P$, so $x$ or $y$ is in $P$ and therefore in the core.
2. The radical of a homogeneous ideal is homogeneous: `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.radical`. The radical of $I$ is the intersection of the primes $P \supseteq I$. For each such $P$ the core of $P$ is a prime that contains $I$ and lies in $P$. So the radical is the intersection of the homogeneous primes that contain $I$, which is `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.radical_eq`. An intersection of homogeneous ideals is homogeneous.

The bundled radical is `HomogeneousIdeal.radical`, with `HomogeneousIdeal.coe_radical`. The cancellation hypothesis is needed. The file `Counterexamples/HomogeneousPrimeNotPrime.lean` grades $(\mathbb{Z}/4)^2$ by the two element monoid $\{0, 1\}$ with $1 + 1 = 1$, and gives a homogeneous ideal that passes the test on homogeneous elements but is not prime.

#### Maps along graded homomorphisms

Let `f : 𝒜 →+*ᵍ ℬ` be a graded ring map, as in Section 1. The image and the preimage of homogeneous ideals are homogeneous. For the preimage, $f(x)_i = f(x_i)$, so if $f(x) \in J$ then $f(x_i) \in J$ for all $i$. For the image, $f$ sends the homogeneous generators of $I$ to homogeneous elements that generate $I B$.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `HomogeneousIdeal.map f I` | the homogeneous ideal generated by $f(I)$ |
| `HomogeneousIdeal.comap f J` | the homogeneous ideal $f^{-1}(J)$ |
| `HomogeneousIdeal.toIdeal_map` | its ideal is `I.toIdeal.map f`, by definition |
| `HomogeneousIdeal.toIdeal_comap` | its ideal is `J.toIdeal.comap f` |
| `HomogeneousIdeal.gc_map_comap` | `map f` and `comap f` form a Galois connection |
| `HomogeneousIdeal.map_map` | $g(f(I)) = (g \circ f)(I)$ |
| `HomogeneousIdeal.comap_comap` | the same for preimages |
| `HomogeneousIdeal.isPrime_comap` | the preimage of a prime is prime, as an instance |

For canonically ordered $\iota$, `HomogeneousIdeal.irrelevant_le_map_comp` says that the condition $B_+ \subseteq f(A_+)B$ passes to composites. This condition appears in the construction of morphisms between projective spectra.

#### Submodules and subsemirings

Let $M$ be a module over $A$ with a decomposition `ℳ : ιM → σM`. A submodule $p$ is homogeneous when it contains the components of its elements: `Submodule.IsHomogeneous p ℳ`, with `Submodule.IsHomogeneous.mem_iff`. The definition needs only `[DirectSum.Decomposition ℳ]`, not an action of the grading of $A$. The bundled type `HomogeneousSubmodule 𝒜 ℳ` also asks for `[SetLike.GradedSMul 𝒜 ℳ]`, the condition $A_i M_j \subseteq M_{i+j}$. Its API is `HomogeneousSubmodule.toSubmodule`, `HomogeneousSubmodule.isHomogeneous`, `HomogeneousSubmodule.toSubmodule_injective` and `HomogeneousSubmodule.ext`. For $M = A$ and `ℳ = 𝒜` the homogeneous submodules are the homogeneous ideals. Do not confuse them with a graded piece such as `MvPolynomial.homogeneousSubmodule σ R n`, the polynomials of degree $n$, which is an $R$-submodule and not an ideal.

For subsemirings, `DirectSum.SetLike.IsHomogeneous 𝒜 R` says that $R$ contains the components of its elements. The bundled type is `HomogeneousSubsemiring 𝒜`, an instance of `SubsemiringClass`. The largest homogeneous subsemiring below $R$ is `Subsemiring.homogeneousCore`. The subsemiring generated by a set of homogeneous elements is homogeneous, by `IsHomogeneous.subsemiringClosure_of_isHomogeneousElem`. For example, $A_0$ is a homogeneous subsemiring.

#### Common errors

1. `Ideal.IsHomogeneous 𝒜 I` unfolds to `Submodule.IsHomogeneous I 𝒜`. The arguments come in the opposite order. A lemma found for submodules works for ideals, but the explicit arguments of the two forms differ.
2. The primality test and the radical need a linearly ordered cancellative index, `[LinearOrder ι] [IsOrderedCancelAddMonoid ι]`. A grading by a finite group such as $\mathbb{Z}/2$ does not satisfy it, and the lemmas in `Mathlib.RingTheory.GradedAlgebra.Radical` do not apply.
3. The notation `𝒜₊` is scoped. Without `open HomogeneousIdeal` it does not parse, and the name `HomogeneousIdeal.irrelevant 𝒜` must be used.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $(X^2 + Y)$ is not a homogeneous ideal of $k[X, Y]$, with the grading by total degree. Find its core and its hull.
2. (A) Show that the ideal generated by $A_+$ and a homogeneous element is homogeneous, with `Ideal.homogeneous_span`.
3. (B) Let $I$ be homogeneous. Show that $A/I$ is graded by the images of the $A_i$.
4. (B) Let $P$ be a prime ideal of an $\mathbb{N}$-graded ring. Show that the core of $P$ is the ideal generated by the homogeneous elements of $P$, and that it is prime.
5. (B) Let $f : A \to B$ be a graded ring map and $J$ a homogeneous prime of $B$ that does not contain $B_+$. Show that the preimage of $J$ is a homogeneous prime of $A$. Give an example where it contains $A_+$.
6. (C) Let $A$ be $\mathbb{N}$-graded, $I$ a homogeneous ideal and $x$ a homogeneous element of positive degree. Show that $x$ is in the radical of $I$ if and only if $x$ is in every homogeneous prime $P \supseteq I$ with $A_+ \not\subseteq P$, with `Ideal.IsHomogeneous.radical_eq`.
