# Functors, monads and traversals

This chapter studies the classes that Lean and Mathlib use to compute with values in a context: a value that can be absent, a list of possible values, a value with a log, or a value that may never be defined. The first section treats `Functor`, `Applicative` and `Monad` with their laws. It proves the Kleisli laws and the join laws, and it builds lawful instances for `Sum`, for the composition of two applicative functors, for the continuation monad `ContT` and for the writer monad `WriterT`. It ends with `ULiftable`, `EquivFunctor` and `Bifunctor`. The second section treats `Traversable`, the class of containers that can run an effect at each element, with the four laws of a lawful traversal. It gives the instances for `Option`, `List`, `Sum` and difference lists, and derives `foldl`, `foldr`, `toList` and `length` from `traverse` with the constant functor of a monoid. The third section treats `Fix` and `LawfulFix`. It builds the least fixed point of a functional on partial functions as the limit of its approximations, and uses it to define functions with no proof of termination, such as a Collatz step counter.

The chapter needs the language of Lean and the classes of Volume 1, lists from Chapter 1 and monoids from Volume 5. The third section uses monotone functions and the fixed point theorems of Volume 4, the type `Part`, and the streams of Chapter 4. Volume 24 gives the categorical view of monads, which this chapter does not need.

The next chapter uses functors in a new role. A polynomial functor describes the shape of a data type, and its quotients `QPF` use `Functor.Liftp` and `Functor.supp` from the first section. The last chapter uses the monad interface for random generators and for property-based testing.
