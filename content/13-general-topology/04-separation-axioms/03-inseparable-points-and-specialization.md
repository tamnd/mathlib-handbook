## Inseparable points, specialization and the Kolmogorov quotient

### Overview

Section 1 of this chapter defines the axiom $T_0$: two points with the same neighbourhoods are equal. Before a space satisfies $T_0$, two relations between its points carry information. The point $x$ *specializes* to $y$ when each neighbourhood of $y$ contains $x$, that is, when $\mathcal{N}(x) \le \mathcal{N}(y)$ as filters. The points are *inseparable* when $\mathcal{N}(x) = \mathcal{N}(y)$. In a $T_1$ space both relations are the equality, so a student of analysis never sees them. In algebraic geometry they are everywhere: in the spectrum of a ring, the generic point of an irreducible closed set specializes to each of its closed points.

The module `Mathlib.Topology.Inseparable` holds the theory of the two relations. It gives many equivalent forms of each relation, the behaviour under continuous maps, products and subtypes, the sets that are stable under specialization, the maps that lift specializations, and the Kolmogorov quotient `SeparationQuotient X`, which identifies inseparable points. The definitions themselves are in `Mathlib.Topology.Defs.Filter`. The section needs the neighbourhood filters of Chapter 2 and the closure of Chapter 1.

### The library around it

#### The two relations

The relation `Specializes x y` has the notation `x ⤳ y`. Its definition is `𝓝 x ≤ 𝓝 y`, and `specializes_iff_nhds` states this as a lemma. The relation `Inseparable x y` is `𝓝 x = 𝓝 y`. The main characterization is `specializes_tfae`. It says that the following are equivalent:

1. $x \rightsquigarrow y$;
2. `pure x ≤ 𝓝 y`, that is, each neighbourhood of $y$ contains $x$;
3. each open set that contains $y$ contains $x$;
4. each closed set that contains $x$ contains $y$;
5. $y \in \overline{\{x\}}$;
6. $\overline{\{y\}} \subseteq \overline{\{x\}}$;
7. $y$ is a cluster point of the principal filter of $x$.

The proof is a chain of short steps. Since $\mathcal{N}(x)$ is the smallest filter that contains $x$ and is generated by open sets, $\mathcal{N}(x) \le \mathcal{N}(y)$ holds if and only if each open neighbourhood of $y$ contains $x$. Complements turn open sets into closed sets, which gives the closed form. The closure $\overline{\{x\}}$ is the smallest closed set that contains $x$, so the closed form says $y \in \overline{\{x\}}$, and this is equivalent to $\overline{\{y\}} \subseteq \overline{\{x\}}$.

Each item has its own lemma, named after the form on the right: `specializes_iff_pure`, `specializes_iff_forall_open`, `specializes_iff_forall_closed`, `specializes_iff_mem_closure`, `specializes_iff_closure_subset` and `specializes_iff_clusterPt`. The dot forms use the relation as a hypothesis: `Specializes.mem_open`, `Specializes.mem_closed`, `Specializes.nhds_le_nhds`, `Specializes.closure_subset`. For a basis of neighbourhoods there is `Filter.HasBasis.specializes_iff`.

The relation is a preorder: `specializes_refl`, `specializes_rfl` and `Specializes.trans`. The library packs it as the preorder `specializationPreorder X` on $X$. In this preorder $y \le x$ means $x \rightsquigarrow y$, so that `closure_singleton_eq_Iic` reads $\overline{\{x\}} = (-\infty, x]$. The statement `specializationPreorder_subtype` says that the preorder of a subspace is the restriction of the preorder of the space.

Inseparability is the equivalence relation that this preorder defines. The lemma `inseparable_iff_specializes_and` says $x \sim y$ if and only if $x \rightsquigarrow y$ and $y \rightsquigarrow x$, and `Specializes.antisymm` gives the direction from right to left. With item 6 this gives `inseparable_iff_closure_eq`:

$$x \sim y \iff \overline{\{x\}} = \overline{\{y\}}.$$

The other forms are `inseparable_iff_forall_isOpen`, `inseparable_iff_forall_isClosed` and `inseparable_iff_mem_closure`. The relation has `Inseparable.refl`, `Inseparable.symm`, `Inseparable.trans`, and `Inseparable.specializes` turns it into a specialization. The negative forms produce a separating set: `not_specializes_iff_exists_open`, `not_specializes_iff_exists_closed` and `not_inseparable_iff_exists_open`. The lemma `ker_nhds_eq_specializes` says that the intersection of all neighbourhoods of $x$ is the set of points that specialize to $x$.

#### Maps, products and subtypes

A continuous map keeps both relations: `Specializes.map` and `Inseparable.map`. Each has forms with weaker hypotheses, such as `Specializes.map_of_continuousAt` and `Inseparable.map_of_continuousOn`. In the language of the preorder, `Continuous.specialization_monotone` says that a continuous map is monotone. The proof is item 6: a continuous map sends $\overline{\{x\}}$ into $\overline{\{f(x)\}}$. An inducing map also reflects the relations: `Topology.IsInducing.specializes_iff` and `Topology.IsInducing.inseparable_iff`. For subtypes these are `subtype_specializes_iff` and `subtype_inseparable_iff`.

In a product the relations hold coordinatewise: `specializes_prod`, `inseparable_prod`, `specializes_pi` and `inseparable_pi`. The dot forms `Specializes.prod`, `Specializes.fst` and `Specializes.snd` go in each direction.

Two useful facts concern functions. The lemma `continuous_congr_of_inseparable` says that if $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are inseparable for each $x$, then $f$ is continuous if and only if $g$ is. The lemma `Tendsto.specializes` says that if $g$ tends to $y$ along $l$ and $f(x) \rightsquigarrow g(x)$ for each $x$, then $f$ also tends to $y$. The piecewise lemmas `IsClosed.continuous_piecewise_of_specializes` and `IsOpen.continuous_piecewise_of_specializes` glue two continuous functions along a closed or open set when the values at each point are related by specialization.

#### Stable sets and specializing maps

A set $s$ is *stable under specialization* if $x \in s$ and $x \rightsquigarrow y$ imply $y \in s$. The predicate is `StableUnderSpecialization s`, and the dual is `StableUnderGeneralization s`. A closed set is stable under specialization (`IsClosed.stableUnderSpecialization`), and an open set is stable under generalization (`IsOpen.stableUnderGeneralization`). The lemma `stableUnderSpecialization_iff_Union_eq` says

$$s \text{ is stable under specialization} \iff \bigcup_{x \in s} \overline{\{x\}} = s.$$

The proof is item 5: the union contains $s$, and it is contained in $s$ exactly when each $y \in \overline{\{x\}}$ with $x \in s$ is in $s$. The two families are closed under complement (`StableUnderSpecialization.compl`), arbitrary unions and intersections (`stableUnderSpecialization_iUnion`, `stableUnderSpecialization_sInter` and their duals), and preimages by continuous maps.

A map $f$ is *specializing* if each specialization $f(x) \rightsquigarrow y$ in the target lifts to a specialization $x \rightsquigarrow x'$ with $f(x') = y$. The predicate is `SpecializingMap f`, and the dual is `GeneralizingMap f`. The form `specializingMap_iff_closure_singleton_subset` says that $f$ is specializing if and only if $\overline{\{f(x)\}} \subseteq f(\overline{\{x\}})$ for each $x$. A closed map is specializing (`IsClosedMap.specializingMap`): the set $f(\overline{\{x\}})$ is closed and contains $f(x)$, so it contains $\overline{\{f(x)\}}$. An open embedding is generalizing (`Topology.IsOpenEmbedding.generalizingMap`). The image of a stable set by a specializing map is stable (`SpecializingMap.stableUnderSpecialization_image`). These notions are the topological half of the going up and going down theorems of commutative algebra.

#### The Kolmogorov quotient

The type `SeparationQuotient X` is the quotient of $X$ by inseparability, with the quotient topology. The projection is `SeparationQuotient.mk`, and `SeparationQuotient.mk_eq_mk` says

$$\mathrm{mk}(x) = \mathrm{mk}(y) \iff x \sim y.$$

The instance `SeparationQuotient.instT0Space` in `Mathlib.Topology.Separation.Basic` makes the quotient a $T_0$ space. The projection is better than a general quotient map. It is inducing (`SeparationQuotient.isInducing_mk`), open (`SeparationQuotient.isOpenMap_mk`), closed (`SeparationQuotient.isClosedMap_mk`) and an open quotient map (`SeparationQuotient.isOpenQuotientMap_mk`). The reason is that open sets are saturated: `SeparationQuotient.preimage_image_mk_open` says $\mathrm{mk}^{-1}(\mathrm{mk}(U)) = U$ for an open $U$. The neighbourhood filters match: `SeparationQuotient.comap_mk_nhds_mk` and `SeparationQuotient.map_mk_nhds`. The preimage of a closure, an interior or a frontier is the closure, the interior or the frontier of the preimage (`SeparationQuotient.preimage_mk_closure` and its companions).

A function $f$ on $X$ that takes the same value at inseparable points descends to the quotient as `SeparationQuotient.lift f hf`, with `SeparationQuotient.lift_mk` as the simp lemma. Continuity and limits of the lift are those of $f$: `SeparationQuotient.continuous_lift`, `SeparationQuotient.continuousAt_lift` and `SeparationQuotient.tendsto_lift_nhds_mk`. Functions of two variables use `SeparationQuotient.lift₂` and `SeparationQuotient.continuous_lift₂`. The quotient is a subsingleton exactly when the topology is indiscrete (`SeparationQuotient.subsingleton_iff`), and it carries a zero and a one when $X$ does (`SeparationQuotient.mk_zero`, `SeparationQuotient.mk_one`). Volume 14 uses this to put algebraic structure on the quotient of a topological group.

#### Common errors

1. The arrow points from the general point to the special point. In `x ⤳ y` the point $y$ lies in the closure of $x$, and in `specializationPreorder X` this is $y \le x$, not $x \le y$. Check the direction with `specializes_iff_mem_closure` before a proof.
2. `Inseparable x y` is not the equality, and `simp` does not turn it into the equality. The step to $x = y$ needs a $T_0$ hypothesis and the lemma `Inseparable.eq` of Section 1.
3. A function on `SeparationQuotient X` is built with `SeparationQuotient.lift`, not with `Quotient.lift`. The general quotient lemmas do not see the topology, and the continuity lemmas are stated only for the specialized lift.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that in the Sierpiński space $\{0, 1\}$ with open sets $\emptyset, \{1\}, \{0,1\}$, the point $1$ specializes to $0$, and $0$ does not specialize to $1$.
2. (A) Use `specializes_iff_forall_open` to show that a continuous map keeps specialization, and compare your proof with `Specializes.map`.
3. (B) Prove that a space is $T_1$ if and only if specialization is the equality, and use `specializes_iff_closure_subset` for one direction.
4. (B) Show that in a $T_0$ space the set of closed points of $X$ is the set of minimal elements of `specializationPreorder X`, with the convention of the preorder above.
5. (B) Prove that $\mathrm{mk}^{-1}(\mathrm{mk}(U)) = U$ for each open $U$, from `inseparable_iff_forall_isOpen`.
6. (C) Prove that a continuous closed map $f$ satisfies $\overline{\{f(x)\}} \subseteq f(\overline{\{x\}})$, and deduce that the image of a set stable under specialization is stable under specialization.
