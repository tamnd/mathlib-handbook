# A first look at Mathlib

This chapter is the first contact of the reader with Mathlib. Section 1.1 reads a first theorem, the square of a sum, symbol by symbol, proves it on paper from the axioms of a commutative semiring, and shows the editor view and the companion package of the handbook. Section 1.2 gives the map of the library: the folders and modules, the header of a file, the kinds of declarations, and the definition of a prime number as Mathlib gives it, with a proof that it agrees with the definition of a textbook. Section 1.3 takes four classical results and shows how a statement of a textbook becomes a statement of Mathlib, and why the two forms often differ.

The chapter needs no knowledge of Lean. It needs the mathematics of a first course in algebra and analysis: rings, primes, sums and continuous functions. Chapter 2 builds on it and reads the signature of a declaration in detail. Chapter 3 shows what a proof is in Lean, and Chapter 4 shows how to find a declaration.
