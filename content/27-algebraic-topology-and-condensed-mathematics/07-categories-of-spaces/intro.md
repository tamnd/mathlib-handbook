# Categories of spaces

Section 1 defines `TopCat`, the category of topological spaces and continuous maps. It constructs limits with the initial topology and colimits with the final topology, proves that the discrete and the indiscrete space give the two adjoints of the forgetful functor, and identifies the monomorphisms, the epimorphisms and the effective epimorphisms. It proves the topological König lemma and surveys the open sets as a functor and the sequential, compactly generated and delta-generated spaces. Section 2 studies the compact Hausdorff spaces and three subcategories: profinite sets, light profinite sets and Stonean spaces. It proves that every surjection is an effective epimorphism, that a profinite set is the limit of its finite quotients, and the theorem of Gleason that the projective compact Hausdorff spaces are the extremally disconnected ones. Section 3 proves the theorem of Nöbeling: the locally constant functions with values in $\mathbb{Z}$ on a profinite set form a free abelian group.

The chapter needs the point set topology of Volume 13: open sets and continuity, separation axioms, compactness and the Tychonoff theorem, and connectedness. It uses limits, adjunctions and monads from Volume 24, and projective objects from Volume 25, Chapter 3. Section 3 uses ordinals and transfinite induction from Volume 31, and free modules from Volume 7.

The chapter prepares Chapter 8. A condensed set is a sheaf on compact Hausdorff spaces, and the sheaf condition there uses the effective epimorphisms of Section 2. Stonean spaces give a site with a simple sheaf condition, and light profinite sets give the site of light condensed sets. The theorem of Nöbeling describes the group of continuous maps from a profinite set to $\mathbb{Z}$ as free, and this is the first step in the computation of solid modules.
