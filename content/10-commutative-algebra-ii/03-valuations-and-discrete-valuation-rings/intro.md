# Valuations and discrete valuation rings

A valuation measures the size of the elements of a field in a way that ignores addition up to the ultrametric inequality: the value of a sum is at most the larger of the two values. The $p$-adic valuation of $\mathbb{Q}$ measures divisibility by $p$, and the order of vanishing at a point measures how fast a rational function goes to zero there. The elements of value at most $1$ form a valuation ring, a local ring in which, for two elements, one always divides the other.

Section 1 defines valuations with values in an ordered group with zero, as Mathlib does, and explains the translation to the additive valuations of most textbooks. Section 2 studies valuation rings: they are local, integrally closed, their ideals are totally ordered, and every local subring of a field lies under a valuation ring. Section 3 treats the discrete valuation rings, the valuation rings with value group $\mathbb{Z}$, and proves the main theorem of the chapter: for a Noetherian local domain of dimension $1$, being a discrete valuation ring is the same as being integrally closed, and the same as having a principal maximal ideal.

The chapter uses the local rings and Nakayama's lemma of Chapter 2 and the integral closure of Chapter 1. Noetherian rings and principal ideal domains are in Volume 9, Commutative algebra I. Chapter 4 uses the discrete valuation rings: a Dedekind domain is a Noetherian domain whose localizations at the nonzero primes are discrete valuation rings. The topology of a valued field and the $p$-adic numbers are in Volume 14 and Volume 12.

In Mathlib, a valuation is a term `v : Valuation R Γ₀`, a valuation ring is a domain with the class `[ValuationRing A]`, and a discrete valuation ring is a domain with `[IsDiscreteValuationRing R]`.
