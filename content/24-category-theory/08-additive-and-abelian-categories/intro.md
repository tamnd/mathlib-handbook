# Additive and abelian categories

Abelian groups and modules form categories with more structure than sets: a zero object, zero maps, kernels and cokernels, direct sums that are at once products and coproducts, and hom sets that are abelian groups. This chapter states these features in the language of categories and arrives at abelian categories, the setting of homological algebra.

Section 1 needs no addition of morphisms. It defines zero objects and zero morphisms, proves that zero morphisms are unique when they exist, treats kernels and cokernels as special equalizers and coequalizers, shows that a kernel is a monomorphism and that the kernel of a monomorphism is zero, and defines binary biproducts. Modules give the examples, and groups show that kernels can exist without biproducts. Section 2 adds the group structure on hom sets. It proves that in a preadditive category every binary product is a biproduct, that additive functors preserve biproducts, and that a morphism of an abelian category that is mono and epi is an isomorphism. It defines images and exactness and shows that modules over a ring form an abelian category.

The chapter needs isomorphisms and monomorphisms from Chapter 1 and limits, equalizers and coequalizers from Chapter 4, with groups from Volume 5 and modules from Volume 7 as examples. It is the last chapter of this volume. Volume 25 develops abelian categories in depth, with the five and snake lemmas, and also treats triangulated categories. Volume 26 builds complexes, homology, derived functors and Ext and Tor on top of them.
