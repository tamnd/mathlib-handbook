# Dirichlet characters and L-series

A Dirichlet character modulo $n$ is a homomorphism from the group of units of $\mathbb{Z}/n$ to the nonzero complex numbers, extended by $0$ to the classes that are not units. Dirichlet used them, together with the series $L(s, \chi) = \sum \chi(n) n^{-s}$, to prove that each class of units contains infinitely many primes. Section 1 defines multiplicative characters and Dirichlet characters in the form of Mathlib, proves the orthogonality relations, and treats the conductor and Gauss sums at a first level. Section 2 defines the L-series of an arithmetic function, studies its absolute convergence, proves the Euler product for a completely multiplicative function, and states the nonvanishing of $L(1, \chi)$ as Mathlib has it. Section 3 describes the operations on L-series: linearity, derivatives, convolution, uniqueness, positivity, Abel summation and formal Euler products. Section 4 describes the Hurwitz and Riemann zeta functions and the L-functions of Dirichlet characters on all of $\mathbb{C}$, with their functional equations. Section 5 describes the Bernoulli numbers and polynomials. Section 6 describes the harmonic numbers, the Euler-Mascheroni constant and the expansion of $\zeta$ at $s = 1$.

The chapter needs the arithmetic functions of Chapter 2, the quadratic character of Chapter 3, the finite abelian groups of Volume 5, and the series of Volume 15, Chapter 1. Chapter 7 uses all of it for Dirichlet's theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions.
