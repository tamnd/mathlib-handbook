# Rational maps, cycles and group schemes

This chapter treats three structures that live on schemes beyond morphisms. Section 1 defines partial maps as morphisms on dense opens, rational maps as their classes up to agreement on a dense open, and partial isomorphisms and birational schemes. It proves that equivalent partial maps from a reduced scheme to a separated scheme agree on their common domain, that dominant rational maps compose, and that rational maps from an integral scheme to a scheme locally of finite type are the morphisms from the spectrum of its function field. Section 2 defines algebraic cycles as functions on points with locally finite support, Weil divisors, the pushforward with residue degrees and the degree of zero-cycles. It defines the order of vanishing of a rational function as a length, proves that it is additive on products, computes it for discrete valuation rings, and proves that a function has finitely many zeros and poles. Section 3 defines group schemes as group objects over a base, identifies affine group schemes with commutative Hopf algebras, and proves that proper geometrically integral group schemes are commutative and that geometrically reduced ones are smooth.

The chapter needs localization, Noetherian rings, discrete valuation rings and Krull dimension from Volumes 9 and 10, algebraic closures from Volume 11, adjunctions and monoidal categories from Volume 24, and Hopf algebras from Volume 26. From this volume it uses integral, reduced and separated schemes, dominant, proper and smooth morphisms from Chapters 4 and 5, and fpqc descent from Chapter 6.

Chapter 8 applies the group scheme results to elliptic curves, which are proper geometrically integral group schemes of dimension one, and the order of vanishing gives the divisors of functions on them.
