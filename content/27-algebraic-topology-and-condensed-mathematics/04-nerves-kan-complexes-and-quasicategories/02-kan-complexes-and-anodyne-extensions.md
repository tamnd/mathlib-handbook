## Kan complexes and anodyne extensions

### Overview

A Kan complex is a simplicial set in which every horn has a filler. Kan complexes are the fibrant objects of the Kan-Quillen model structure.

This section defines Kan complexes, $\pi_0$, the fundamental groupoid and homotopies. It proves that in a Kan complex, edges give an equivalence relation and are invertible. It defines anodyne and inner anodyne extensions, proves their closure properties, proves that a regular pairing of Sean Moss gives an anodyne extension, and states the pushout-product theorem.

### Definitions

The horns $\Lambda[n, k]$ and the standard simplices are those of Section 3 of Chapter 3. For a class $S$ of maps, $S^{\mathrm{rlp}}$ is the class of maps with the right lifting property against all maps of $S$, and ${}^{\mathrm{llp}}S$ the class with the left lifting property; see Volume 25, Chapter 3.

**Definition (Kan fibration and Kan complex).** A map $p : X \to Y$ of simplicial sets is a *Kan fibration* if it has the right lifting property against every horn inclusion $\Lambda[n, k] \to \Delta[n]$, $n \geq 1$, $0 \leq k \leq n$. A simplicial set $X$ is a *Kan complex* if each map $\Lambda[n, k] \to X$ extends to $\Delta[n]$.

**Definition (Path components).** For a simplicial set $X$, $\pi_0 X$ is the quotient of $X_0$ by the equivalence relation generated by $x \sim y$ when there is an edge from $x$ to $y$, that is the coequalizer of $d_1, d_0 : X_1 \rightrightarrows X_0$. The simplicial set $X$ is *connected* if $\pi_0 X$ is a point.

**Definition (Fundamental groupoid).** The *fundamental groupoid* $\Pi_1 X$ is the free groupoid on the homotopy category $hX$ of Section 1. Its morphisms are formal composites of edges and their formal inverses.

**Definition (Homotopy of maps).** Let $f, g : X \to Y$. A *homotopy* from $f$ to $g$ is a map $H : X \times \Delta[1] \to Y$ with $H(-, 0) = f$ and $H(-, 1) = g$.

**Definition (Pointed simplices and their product).** Let $X$ be a simplicial set and $x \in X_0$. A *pointed $n$-simplex* at $x$ is a map $\Delta[n] \to X$ that sends $\partial\Delta[n]$ to $x$. For pointed $n$-simplices $f, g, h$ and $0 \leq i < n$, a *product structure* is an $(n+1)$-simplex $\omega$ with $d_i \omega = g$, $d_{i+1} \omega = h$, $d_{i+2} \omega = f$ and $d_j \omega$ constant at $x$ for the other $j$.

**Definition (Anodyne extensions).** The class of *anodyne extensions* is ${}^{\mathrm{llp}}(J^{\mathrm{rlp}})$, where $J$ is the set of horn inclusions. The class of *inner anodyne extensions* is ${}^{\mathrm{llp}}(J_{\mathrm{in}}^{\mathrm{rlp}})$ for the set $J_{\mathrm{in}}$ of inner horn inclusions.

**Definition (Pairing).** Let $A \subseteq X$ be a subcomplex, and let $N$ be the set of nondegenerate simplices of $X$ that are not in $A$. A *pairing* for $A$ is a partition $N = N_{\mathrm{I}} \sqcup N_{\mathrm{II}}$ with a bijection $p : N_{\mathrm{II}} \to N_{\mathrm{I}}$. It is *proper* if for each $y \in N_{\mathrm{II}}$ of dimension $d$, the simplex $p(y)$ has dimension $d + 1$ and there is exactly one index $i$ with $d_i\, p(y) = y$; write $i(y)$ for it. It is *inner* if also $0 < i(y) < d + 1$ for each $y$. For $w, y \in N_{\mathrm{II}}$, $w$ is an *ancestor* of $y$ if $w \neq y$ and $w$ is a face of $p(y)$. A proper pairing is *regular* if the ancestor relation is well founded.

When one simplex is attached along a horn $\Lambda[n, k]$, two nondegenerate simplices are added: the $n$-simplex, of type I, and its face opposite $k$, of type II.

### Theorems and proofs

**Theorem (π₀ of a Kan complex).** Let $X$ be a Kan complex. Then the relation "there is an edge from $x$ to $y$" on $X_0$ is an equivalence relation. So $x$ and $y$ have the same class in $\pi_0 X$ if and only if there is one edge from $x$ to $y$.

*Proof.* Reflexive: $s_0 x$ is an edge from $x$ to $x$. Symmetric: let $e$ be an edge from $x$ to $y$. The pair $(e, s_0 x)$ is a map $\Lambda[2,0] \to X$ with $(0,1) \mapsto e$ and $(0,2) \mapsto s_0 x$. Let $\tau$ be a filler. Its face $d_0 \tau$ is the edge $(1,2)$, from $d_0 e = y$ to $d_0 s_0 x = x$. Transitive: for edges $e$ from $x$ to $y$ and $e'$ from $y$ to $z$, the pair $(e, e')$ is a map $\Lambda[2,1] \to X$. For a filler $\tau$, $d_1 \tau$ is an edge from $x$ to $z$. $\square$

**Theorem (Edges of a Kan complex are invertible).** Let $X$ be a Kan complex and $e$ an edge from $x$ to $y$. There are edges $e', e''$ from $y$ to $x$ and $2$-simplices $\tau, \tau'$ with $(d_2 \tau, d_0 \tau, d_1 \tau) = (e, e', s_0 x)$ and $(d_2 \tau', d_0 \tau', d_1 \tau') = (e'', e, s_0 y)$. So the homotopy category $hX$ is a groupoid, and $hX \to \Pi_1 X$ is an isomorphism. Each morphism of $\Pi_1 X$ is the class of one edge.

*Proof.* The first $2$-simplex is the filler $\tau$ in the proof above, with $e' = d_0 \tau$. For the second, the pair $(s_0 y, e)$ on the edges $(0,2)$ and $(1,2)$ is a map $\Lambda[2,2] \to X$, and a filler $\tau'$ has $d_1 \tau' = s_0 y$, $d_0 \tau' = e$ and $e'' = d_2 \tau'$ from $y$ to $x$. In $hX$, the relations of $\tau$ and $\tau'$ give $[e'][e] = \mathrm{id}_x$ and $[e][e''] = \mathrm{id}_y$. So each generator of $hX$ is invertible, $hX$ is a groupoid, and it is its own free groupoid. Finally, the identity of $x$ is $[s_0 x]$, and for edges $e_1$ from $x$ to $y$ and $e_2$ from $y$ to $z$, a filler $\tau$ of $(e_1, e_2) : \Lambda[2,1] \to X$ gives $[e_2][e_1] = [d_1 \tau]$. By induction on the length, each composite of classes of edges is the class of one edge. $\square$

**Proposition (Closure of lifting classes).** For each class $S$ of maps, the class ${}^{\mathrm{llp}}S$ contains the isomorphisms and is closed under composition, cobase change, coproducts, retracts and transfinite composition. So all anodyne extensions and all inner anodyne extensions have these closure properties, and each horn inclusion is anodyne.

*Proof.* Fix $p : E \to B$ in $S$. For an isomorphism, the lift is the inverse followed by the top map. For a composite $j i$, lift first against $i$ and then against $j$. For a cobase change $i'$ of $i$ along $A \to A'$, the lift for $i$ and the top map on $A'$ glue by the pushout property. For coproducts, lift on each summand. For a retract $i'$ of $i$, compose the square with the retraction data, lift for $i$, and compose back. For a transfinite composition $X_0 \to X_\lambda$, build lifts $X_\alpha \to E$ by induction, lifting at successors and taking colimits at limit ordinals. Finally $J \subseteq {}^{\mathrm{llp}}(J^{\mathrm{rlp}})$. $\square$

**Theorem (Regular pairings give anodyne extensions).** Let $A \subseteq X$ be a subcomplex with a regular pairing. Then $A \to X$ is a transfinite composition of cobase changes of coproducts of horn inclusions. In particular it is anodyne, and if the pairing is inner, it is inner anodyne.

*Proof.* The ancestor relation is well founded, so there is a rank $r : N_{\mathrm{II}} \to \lambda$ in an ordinal with $r(w) < r(y)$ when $w$ is an ancestor of $y$. Let $A_\alpha$ be the subcomplex generated by $A$ and the $p(y)$ with $r(y) < \alpha$. Then $A_0 = A$, $A_\beta = \bigcup_{\alpha < \beta} A_\alpha$ at limits, and $A_\lambda = X$.

Fix $y$ with $r(y) = \alpha$ and $\dim y = d$, and put $\sigma = p(y)$ and $i = i(y)$. First, $\sigma$ sends $\Lambda[d+1, i]$ into $A_\alpha$. Let $z = d_j \sigma \notin A$ with $j \neq i$. By the Eilenberg-Zilber lemma, $z$ is a degeneracy of a nondegenerate $z' \in N$. If $z'$ has type II, then $z' \neq y$, since $z' = y$ forces $z = y = d_j \sigma$, against properness; so $z'$ is an ancestor of $y$ and $z' \subseteq p(z') \subseteq A_\alpha$. If $z' = p(w)$ has type I, then $w$ is a face of $\sigma$ with $\dim w < d$, so $w$ is an ancestor of $y$ and $z' \subseteq A_\alpha$.

Second, $A_{\alpha+1}$ is the pushout of $A_\alpha$ along the coproduct of the $\Lambda[d+1, i(y)] \to \Delta[d+1]$ over the $y$ of rank $\alpha$. In each degree, a pushout along an injection of sets is a disjoint union. A simplex $\theta$ of $\Delta[d+1]$ outside the horn has image containing $[d+1] \setminus \{i\}$, so $\theta = \varepsilon \circ s$ with $s$ surjective and $\varepsilon \in \{\mathrm{id}, \delta_i\}$, and its image is $s^* \sigma$ or $s^* y$. By the uniqueness in the Eilenberg-Zilber lemma, these images are distinct. They are not in $A_\alpha$, since $y$ a face of $p(w)$ with $w \neq y$ makes $y$ an ancestor of $w$, so $r(w) > \alpha$. By the first step, every other simplex of $A_{\alpha+1}$ lies in $A_\alpha$. So $A_{\alpha+1}$ is $A_\alpha$ with exactly these simplices added, which is the pushout.

By the proposition, $A \to X$ is anodyne, and inner anodyne in the inner case. $\square$

**Theorem (Pushout product).** For each $m \geq 0$, $0 \leq l \leq m + 1$ and $n \geq 0$, the inclusion $$\Lambda[m+1, l] \times \Delta[n] \cup \Delta[m+1] \times \partial\Delta[n] \to \Delta[m+1] \times \Delta[n]$$ has a regular pairing, inner when $0 < l < m + 1$. As a consequence, if $i : A \to B$ is anodyne and $j : C \to D$ is a monomorphism, then $A \times D \cup B \times C \to B \times D$ is anodyne, and the same holds for inner anodyne maps. If $Y$ is a Kan complex, then $Y^A$ is a Kan complex for each $A$, and if $Y$ is a quasicategory, so is $Y^A$.

The pairing follows Sean Moss. A nondegenerate simplex of $\Delta[m+1] \times \Delta[n]$ is a strictly increasing walk in the grid $[m+1] \times [n]$, and its type is read off from the walk near the column $l$. The index of this edition proves that the pairing is regular; this section does not reproduce the combinatorics of walks.

*Proof of the consequences.* Write $i \,\square\, j$ for $A \times D \cup B \times C \to B \times D$. For fixed $j$, $- \,\square\, j$ preserves pushouts, coproducts, retracts and transfinite compositions, and so does $i \,\square\, -$ for fixed $i$. For $i$ a horn inclusion, the class of $j$ with $i \,\square\, j$ anodyne contains the boundary inclusions, so it contains all monomorphisms by the cell structure of Section 3 of Chapter 3. For fixed $j$, the class of $i$ then contains $J$, so it contains all anodyne maps, which are retracts of transfinite compositions of cobase changes of coproducts of maps of $J$. For $Y^A$: by adjunction, a horn in $Y^A$ is a map $\Lambda[n, k] \times A \to Y$, and $\Lambda[n,k] \times A \to \Delta[n] \times A$ is $(\Lambda[n,k] \to \Delta[n]) \,\square\, (\emptyset \to A)$, so it is anodyne and the horn has a filler. $\square$

### Examples

**Example (Discrete simplicial sets are Kan).** Let $X$ be the constant simplicial set on a set $S$. A map $\Lambda[n, k] \to X$ with $n \geq 2$ is constant, since the horn is connected; for $n = 1$ the horn is a point. So the constant map on $\Delta[n]$ fills it, and $X$ is a Kan complex. In particular $\Delta[0]$ is a Kan complex, and $\pi_0 X = S$.

**Example (A pairing for Δ[1] × {0} in Δ[1] × Δ[1]).** Write the vertices of $\Delta[1] \times \Delta[1]$ as $ab$ with $a, b \in \{0, 1\}$, and let $A = \Delta[1] \times \{0\}$, the edge from $00$ to $10$. The simplices of $N$ are the vertices $01$, $11$, the edges $(00, 01)$, $(10, 11)$, $(00, 11)$, $(01, 11)$ and the triangles $T_1 = (00, 01, 11)$, $T_2 = (00, 10, 11)$. A pairing is $01 \mapsto (00, 01)$ and $11 \mapsto (10, 11)$, both with index $0$, then $(00, 11) \mapsto T_2$ with index $1$ and $(01, 11) \mapsto T_1$ with index $0$. It is proper. The ancestors of $(01, 11)$ are $01$, $11$ and $(00, 11)$, and $11$ is the ancestor of $(00, 11)$, so the rank $r(01) = r(11) = 0$, $r(00, 11) = 1$, $r(01, 11) = 2$ works. The pairing is regular, and the inclusion $\Delta[1] \times \{0\} \to \Delta[1] \times \Delta[1]$ is anodyne. It is not inner, since three of the indices are outer.

**Counterexample (A boundary inclusion is not anodyne).** The inclusion $\partial\Delta[1] \to \Delta[1]$ is not anodyne. Let $X$ be the discrete simplicial set on $\{0, 1\}$, a Kan complex by the first example. The map $\partial\Delta[1] \to X$ with $0 \mapsto 0$ and $1 \mapsto 1$ does not extend to $\Delta[1]$, since an edge of $X$ has equal endpoints. So $\partial\Delta[1] \to \Delta[1]$ does not lift against the Kan fibration $X \to \Delta[0]$.

**Counterexample (An outer horn is not inner anodyne).** The inclusion $\Lambda[2,0] \to \Delta[2]$ is anodyne but not inner anodyne. Section 1 shows that $\Delta[1]$ is a quasicategory, so $\Delta[1] \to \Delta[0]$ is an inner fibration, and it gives a map $\Lambda[2,0] \to \Delta[1]$ with no filler.

Common errors of a learner:

- `KanComplex` is fibrancy, not a horn condition. Use `KanComplex.hornFilling` to reach the horns.
- `Edge x y` is an edge with fixed endpoints, not a bare $1$-simplex.
- In a general simplicial set, a morphism of the fundamental groupoid is a formal composite; only in a Kan complex is it one edge.
- In `MulStruct f g fg i`, the face `d_{i+2}` is `f` and `d_i` is `g`, in diagrammatic order.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $\pi_0(X \sqcup Y) = \pi_0 X \sqcup \pi_0 Y$ and that $\pi_0 \Delta[n]$ is a point. *Hint:* use the coequalizer description.
2. (A) Show that the horn $\Lambda[2,1]$ is not a Kan complex. *Hint:* a map from $\Lambda[2,0]$ that sends the edge $(0,2)$ to the degenerate edge at $0$.
3. (A) Find a regular pairing for $\Lambda[2,1] \subseteq \Delta[2]$ and check that it is inner. *Hint:* $N$ has two elements.
4. (B) Prove that if $Y$ is a Kan complex, homotopy is an equivalence relation on maps $X \to Y$. *Hint:* a homotopy is an edge of $Y^X$, a Kan complex by the pushout-product theorem.
5. (B) Show that the nerve of a category $C$ is a Kan complex if and only if $C$ is a groupoid. *Hint:* for the direction from Kan to groupoid, fill $\Lambda[2,0]$ with the identity on $(0,2)$.
6. (B) Prove that a homotopy $H$ from $f$ to $g$ induces a natural isomorphism $\Pi_1 f \cong \Pi_1 g$. *Hint:* use the edges $H(x, -)$ and the two triangles of $\Delta[1] \times \Delta[1]$.
7. (C) Let $X$ be a Kan complex and $x \in X_0$. Show that pointed $1$-simplices at $x$ up to homotopy form a group under product structures. *Hint:* fill inner $2$-horns and $3$-horns.

### Notes and history

Kan introduced his extension condition in 1955 and adjoint functors in 1958. Gabriel and Zisman introduced anodyne extensions and proved the pushout-product theorem in 1967, and Quillen made Kan complexes the fibrant objects of a model structure in 1967. Joyal treated inner anodyne maps. The pairing method is due to Sean Moss, *Another approach to the Kan-Quillen model structure*.

The Mathlib Authors of `KanComplex` are Johan Commelin and Joël Riou. `AnodyneExtensions.PushoutProduct` is by Joël Riou and Jack McKoen, the two `Inner` modules by Jack McKoen and Joël Riou, and `SSetPair` by Joël Riou and Andrew Yang. The other modules of this section are by Joël Riou.
