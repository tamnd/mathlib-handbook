## Alexandrov-discrete spaces, constructible sets and the perfect kernel

### Overview

This section describes three modules that refine the lattice of open sets. The first treats the spaces in which an arbitrary intersection of open sets is open. These are the Alexandrov-discrete spaces. Each finite space is one, and each preorder gives one. In such a space each point has a smallest neighbourhood.

The second module treats constructible sets. A set is retrocompact if its intersection with each compact open set is compact, and the constructible sets are the Boolean algebra generated by the retrocompact open sets. The theorem of Chevalley in Volume 10 says that the image of a constructible set under a morphism of finite presentation is constructible.

The third module removes the isolated points of a set again and again, along the ordinals. The process stops at the perfect kernel, the largest perfect subset. This is the Cantor-Bendixson process of the theory of closed sets of reals.

### The library around it

#### Alexandrov-discrete spaces

The class `AlexandrovDiscrete α` says that the intersection of each family of open sets is open. The rule `alexandrovDiscrete_iff` states the definition, and the main rule `isOpen_sInter` uses it: for each family $S$ of open sets, $\bigcap S$ is open. Its forms for indexed families are `isOpen_iInter` and `isOpen_iInter₂`. By complements, each union of closed sets is closed (`isClosed_iUnion`). The interior commutes with all intersections and the closure with all unions (`interior_iInter`, `closure_iUnion`).

The neighbourhood kernel `nhdsKer s` is the intersection of all open sets that contain $s$. Section 5 of Chapter 2 defines it for each space. In an Alexandrov-discrete space it is open (`isOpen_nhdsKer`), so it is the smallest open set that contains $s$. In particular, `nhdsKer {a}` is the smallest open neighbourhood of $a$, and the neighbourhood filter of $a$ is principal:

$$\mathcal{N}(a) = \mathcal{P}(\mathrm{nhdsKer}\{a\}).$$

This is `principal_nhdsKer_singleton`, with the basis form `nhds_basis_nhdsKer_singleton`. A set $s$ is open if and only if `nhdsKer s ⊆ s` (`nhdsKer_subset_iff_isOpen`), and the kernel and the interior form a Galois connection (`gc_nhdsKer_interior`).

The open sets are the sets that are closed under generalization. Write $x \rightsquigarrow y$ if $y$ is in the closure of $\{x\}$, that is, $x$ specializes to $y$.

**Proposition.** In an Alexandrov-discrete space, a set $s$ is open if and only if $x \rightsquigarrow y$ and $y \in s$ give $x \in s$.

*Proof sketch.* If $s$ is open and $y \in s$, then $s$ is a neighbourhood of $y$. If $x \rightsquigarrow y$, then each neighbourhood of $y$ contains $x$, so $x \in s$. Conversely, let $s$ have the property. For $y \in s$, the set `nhdsKer {y}` is the set of the points $x$ with $x \rightsquigarrow y$, so it is a subset of $s$. Thus $s$ is a union of open sets. $\square$

This is `isOpen_iff_forall_specializes`. So an Alexandrov-discrete topology is the same as a preorder, the specialization order.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `isOpen_sInter` | each intersection of open sets is open |
| `isOpen_nhdsKer` | `nhdsKer s` is open |
| `principal_nhdsKer_singleton` | $\mathcal{N}(a) = \mathcal{P}(\mathrm{nhdsKer}\{a\})$ |
| `isOpen_iff_forall_specializes` | open sets are the sets closed under generalization |
| `AlexandrovDiscrete.toFirstCountable` | the space is first countable |
| `AlexandrovDiscrete.toLocallyCompactSpace` | the space is locally compact |

The instances give many examples. A discrete space, an indiscrete space and a finite space are Alexandrov-discrete (`DiscreteTopology.toAlexandrovDiscrete`, `IndiscreteTopology.toAlexandrovDiscrete`, `Finite.toAlexandrovDiscrete`). The class passes to subspaces, quotients, sums, sigma types, finite products and coinduced topologies (`alexandrovDiscrete_coinduced`, `Prod.instAlexandrovDiscrete`, `Pi.instAlexandrovDiscreteOfFinite`). The product of infinitely many such spaces is not Alexandrov-discrete in general: in $\{0, 1\}^{\mathbb{N}}$ with the discrete factors, a point is an intersection of open sets but it is not open.

#### Retrocompact and constructible sets

The predicate `IsRetrocompact s` says that $s \cap U$ is compact for each compact open set $U$. The empty set, the whole space, finite unions and intersections of retrocompact open sets are retrocompact. In a compact quasi-separated space, an open set is retrocompact if and only if it is compact (`QuasiSeparatedSpace.isRetrocompact_iff_isCompact`). Spectral spaces, such as the prime spectrum of a ring, are the main examples of such spaces.

The predicate `Topology.IsConstructible s` says that $s$ is in the Boolean algebra generated by the retrocompact open sets. So the empty set and the whole space are constructible, and the class is closed under complements, finite unions, finite intersections and differences (`Topology.IsConstructible.compl`, `Topology.IsConstructible.union`, `Topology.IsConstructible.sdiff`). A retrocompact open set is constructible (`IsRetrocompact.isConstructible`), and in a quasi-separated space a compact open set is constructible (`IsCompact.isConstructible`). Preimages under continuous maps for which preimages of retrocompact sets are retrocompact keep the class (`Topology.IsConstructible.preimage`). The induction principle `Topology.IsConstructible.induction_of_isTopologicalBasis` reduces a statement about all constructible sets of a compact quasi-separated space to finite unions of sets of the form $U \setminus V$, with $U$ and $V$ basic.

The predicate `Topology.IsLocallyConstructible s` says that each point has an open neighbourhood $U$ such that $s \cap U$ is constructible in $U$. In a compact, quasi-separated and prespectral space, a locally constructible set is constructible (`Topology.IsLocallyConstructible.isConstructible`), and the converse holds in each space.

#### The perfect kernel

For a set $s$, the relative derived set `relDerivedSet s` is the set of the points of $s$ that are accumulation points of $s$. It removes the isolated points of $s$. The iterated derived sets `iteratedDerivedSet s a` are defined by transfinite recursion on the ordinal $a$. The step $a + 1$ applies the derived set once (`iteratedDerivedSet_succ`), and a limit ordinal takes the intersection of the earlier sets (`iteratedDerivedSet_limit`). The sequence is decreasing (`iteratedDerivedSet_antitone`), and it is constant if and only if $s$ is preperfect, that is, $s$ has no isolated points (`iteratedDerivedSet_constant_iff_preperfect`).

The perfect kernel `perfectKernel s` is the intersection of all the iterated derived sets. For a closed set $s$ it is closed and perfect (`isClosed_perfectKernel`, `perfect_perfectKernel`), and each perfect subset of $s$ is a subset of it (`Perfect.subset_perfectKernel`). So it is the largest perfect subset of $s$. The sequence reaches it: once an iterated derived set is a fixed point of the derived set, it equals the perfect kernel (`perfectKernel_eq_iteratedDerivedSet_of_mem_fixedPoints`). The Cantor-Bendixson theorem adds that in a second countable space the sequence stops at a countable ordinal and $s \setminus \mathrm{perfectKernel}(s)$ is countable. The module `Mathlib.Topology.Perfect` gives the related decomposition of a closed set into a perfect set and a countable set, `exists_countable_union_perfect_of_isClosed`.

#### Common errors

1. In an Alexandrov-discrete space each point has a smallest neighbourhood, but the space need not be discrete. The Sierpiński space on $\{0, 1\}$ with the open sets $\emptyset$, $\{1\}$ and $\{0, 1\}$ is an example.
2. A compact open set need not be constructible in a space that is not quasi-separated. The rule `IsCompact.isConstructible` has the hypothesis `QuasiSeparatedSpace X`.
3. The iterated derived sets are indexed by ordinals in the universe of the space, not by natural numbers. A countable closed subset of $\mathbb{R}$ can need each countable ordinal of steps before it reaches its perfect kernel, so a recursion along $\mathbb{N}$ does not suffice in general.

### Exercises

1. (A) Prove `isClosed_iUnion` from `isOpen_iInter` in an Alexandrov-discrete space.
2. (A) Show that each finite topological space is Alexandrov-discrete.
3. (B) Let $(P, \le)$ be a preorder, and call a set open if it is a lower set. Show that this is an Alexandrov-discrete topology and that its specialization order is $\le$.
4. (B) Show that $\mathbb{R}$ with the usual topology is not Alexandrov-discrete.
5. (B) Compute the iterated derived sets and the perfect kernel of $\{0\} \cup \{1/n : n \ge 1\} \cup [2, 3]$ in $\mathbb{R}$.
6. (C) Show that in a compact quasi-separated space the constructible sets are the finite unions of sets $U \setminus V$ with $U$ and $V$ compact open.
