# Homological complexes

This chapter defines complexes and their homology, and proves the long exact homology sequence. Section 1 introduces complex shapes, which say for which pairs of indices a differential may be nonzero. The same definition covers chain complexes, cochain complexes, complexes indexed by $\mathbb{Z}$ and more exotic index sets. The section defines complexes of a given shape, their morphisms, the single complexes $A[j]$, the action of additive functors, the augmentation and truncation of chain complexes, and the opposite complex. Section 2 defines the homology of a complex at an index as the homology of the short complex at that index, so that all of Chapter 1 applies without new proofs. It computes the homology of single complexes and of small complexes over $\mathbb{Z}$ and over polynomial rings, defines quasi-isomorphisms, and shows that the complexes in an abelian category form an abelian category in which exactness is degreewise. Section 3 builds the connecting morphism of a short exact sequence of complexes from the snake lemma, proves that the long homology sequence is exact and natural, and shows that limits of complexes are computed degreewise.

The chapter needs Chapter 1 of this volume for short complexes, homology data, exactness and the snake lemma. From Volume 24 it uses categories with zero morphisms, kernels and cokernels, and limits, and from Volume 25 the abelian categories and the five lemma. The examples use modules over $\mathbb{Z}$, over $\mathbb{Z}/n$ and over polynomial rings, as in Volume 7.

Chapter 3 studies homotopies between morphisms of complexes and the mapping cone. Chapter 4 uses the long exact sequence for derived functors, and Chapter 5 inverts the quasi-isomorphisms of Section 2.
