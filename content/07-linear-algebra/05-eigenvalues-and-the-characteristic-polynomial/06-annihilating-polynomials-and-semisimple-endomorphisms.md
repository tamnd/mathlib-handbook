## Annihilating polynomials, semisimple endomorphisms and more on eigenspaces

### Overview

Section 2 defines the minimal polynomial of an endomorphism $f$ as the monic polynomial of least degree with $p(f) = 0$. The polynomials that vanish at $f$ form an ideal of $K[X]$, and over a field this ideal is principal, generated by the minimal polynomial. This section starts with this ideal. Then it describes the semisimple endomorphisms, those for which each invariant subspace has an invariant complement. Over an algebraically closed field these are the diagonalizable maps, and the theorem of Jordan and Chevalley of Section 2 writes each map as the sum of a semisimple map and a nilpotent map. The section ends with results on eigenspaces: the eigenvalue $0$, the dimension of the generalized eigenspaces, the eigenvalues of diagonal matrices, the common eigenspaces of a family of commuting maps, and the eigenspaces of continuous maps.

### The library around it

#### The annihilating ideal

**Definition (`Polynomial.annIdeal`).** For a commutative semiring $R$, an $R$-algebra $A$ and $a \in A$, `Polynomial.annIdeal R a` is the ideal of the polynomials $p \in R[X]$ with $p(a) = 0$.

It is the kernel of the evaluation map `Polynomial.aeval a`, and `Polynomial.mem_annIdeal_iff_aeval_eq_zero` states the membership. Over a field $K$ the ring $K[X]$ is a principal ideal domain, so the ideal has a generator.

**Definition (`Polynomial.annIdealGenerator`).** For a field $K$ and $a$ in a $K$-algebra, `Polynomial.annIdealGenerator K a` is the monic generator of `Polynomial.annIdeal K a`, or $0$ when the ideal is zero.

The generator is in the ideal (`Polynomial.annIdealGenerator_mem`), it generates it (`Polynomial.span_singleton_annIdealGenerator`), and a polynomial is in the ideal if and only if the generator divides it (`Polynomial.mem_iff_annIdealGenerator_dvd`). It has the least degree among the nonzero elements of the ideal (`Polynomial.degree_annIdealGenerator_le_of_mem`), and it is monic when it is not zero (`Polynomial.monic_annIdealGenerator`).

**Theorem (`Polynomial.annIdealGenerator_eq_minpoly`).** The generator of the annihilating ideal of $a$ is `minpoly K a`.

*Proof sketch.* If $a$ is not integral, both are $0$. Otherwise the minimal polynomial is monic, vanishes at $a$, and divides each polynomial that vanishes at $a$, so it generates the ideal; a monic generator of a principal ideal is unique (`Polynomial.monic_generator_eq_minpoly`). $\square$

For an endomorphism $f$ of a vector space, the module $V$ with $X$ acting as $f$ is a $K[X]$-module, `Module.AEval' f`, and its annihilator is the ideal generated by the minimal polynomial (`Polynomial.span_minpoly_eq_annihilator`). This module is the link between the linear algebra of one endomorphism and the modules over the principal ideal domain $K[X]$ of Chapter 7.

#### Semisimple endomorphisms

**Definition (`Module.End.IsSemisimple`).** An endomorphism $f$ of a module $M$ over a commutative ring $R$ is semisimple when the $R[X]$-module `Module.AEval' f` is a semisimple module.

**Theorem (`Module.End.isSemisimple_iff`).** The endomorphism $f$ is semisimple if and only if each $f$-invariant submodule $p$ has an $f$-invariant complement $q$.

*Proof.* The $R[X]$-submodules of `Module.AEval' f` are the $f$-invariant submodules of $M$. A module is semisimple if and only if its lattice of submodules is complemented. $\square$

The identity and $0$ are semisimple (`Module.End.isSemisimple_id`, `Module.End.isSemisimple_zero`), and semisimplicity does not change under $f \mapsto f - \mu$ (`Module.End.isSemisimple_sub_algebraMap_iff`), under $f \mapsto -f$ and under nonzero scalar multiples. It passes to restrictions to invariant submodules (`Module.End.IsSemisimple.restrict`) and is invariant under conjugation by a linear equivalence (`LinearEquiv.isSemisimple_iff`). A map that is nilpotent and semisimple is $0$: the invariant subspace $\ker f$ has an invariant complement on which $f$ is injective and nilpotent, so the complement is $0$.

Over a field, semisimplicity is a property of the minimal polynomial.

**Theorem (`Module.End.isSemisimple_of_squarefree_aeval_eq_zero`).** Over a field $K$, if $p(f) = 0$ for a squarefree polynomial $p$, then $f$ is semisimple.

*Proof sketch.* The module `Module.AEval' f` is annihilated by $p$, so it is a module over $K[X]/(p)$. Since $p$ is squarefree, this ring is a finite product of fields by the Chinese remainder theorem, and each module over such a ring is semisimple. $\square$

For a space of finite dimension the converse holds: `Module.End.IsSemisimple.minpoly_squarefree` says that the minimal polynomial of a semisimple $f$ is squarefree. So, over $\mathbb{C}$, a matrix is semisimple if and only if its minimal polynomial has distinct roots, that is, if and only if it is diagonalizable. Each polynomial in a semisimple map is semisimple (`Module.End.IsSemisimple.aeval`, `Module.End.IsSemisimple.pow`).

**Theorem (`Module.End.IsSemisimple.add_of_commute`).** Over a perfect field, the sum of two commuting semisimple endomorphisms of a space of finite dimension is semisimple.

The proof shows that each element of the algebra generated by $f$ and $g$ is semisimple (`Module.End.IsSemisimple.of_mem_adjoin_pair`): this algebra is a quotient of a ring of the form $K[X]/(p) \otimes K[X]/(q)$ with $p$ and $q$ squarefree, which over a perfect field is again a product of fields. Without the hypothesis on the field the result fails: over a field of characteristic $p$ that is not perfect, a sum of two commuting semisimple maps can be nilpotent and nonzero. The cases of the difference and the product are `Module.End.IsSemisimple.sub_of_commute` and `Module.End.IsSemisimple.mul_of_commute`. These results give the uniqueness in the theorem of Jordan and Chevalley.

For modules of infinite dimension, `Module.End.IsFinitelySemisimple` asks the condition only on the restrictions to finitely generated invariant submodules. The forms with complements are `Module.End.isFinitelySemisimple_iff`, and for a finitely generated module the two notions agree (`Module.End.isFinitelySemisimple_iff_isSemisimple`).

#### Eigenspaces of semisimple maps

**Theorem (`Module.End.IsFinitelySemisimple.maxGenEigenspace_eq_eigenspace`).** If $f$ is finitely semisimple, then the maximal generalized eigenspace of $f$ for $\mu$ is the eigenspace for $\mu$.

*Proof sketch.* Let $v$ satisfy $(f - \mu)^k v = 0$. The span of $v, f v, f^2 v, \dots$ is a finitely generated invariant submodule, and on it $f - \mu$ is nilpotent and semisimple. So it is $0$ there, and $f v = \mu v$. $\square$

The version for a finite order $k > 0$ is `Module.End.IsFinitelySemisimple.genEigenspace_eq_eigenspace`. So a semisimple map is triangularizable if and only if it is diagonalizable (`Module.End.IsSemisimple.iSup_maxGenEigenspace_eq_top_iff`). Over an algebraically closed field and in finite dimension, the eigenspaces of a semisimple map span the whole space (`Module.End.IsSemisimple.iSup_eigenspace_eq_top`), which is the statement that it is diagonalizable.

#### The eigenvalue zero and the dimension of eigenspaces

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Eigenspace.Zero` collects the conditions that are equivalent to $0$ being an eigenvalue.

**Theorem (`LinearMap.hasEigenvalue_zero_tfae`).** For an endomorphism $\varphi$ of a vector space of finite dimension, the following are equivalent: $0$ is an eigenvalue of $\varphi$; $0$ is a root of the minimal polynomial; the constant coefficient of the characteristic polynomial is $0$; $\det \varphi = 0$; the kernel is not zero; and $\varphi m = 0$ for some $m \ne 0$.

The negated form is `LinearMap.not_hasEigenvalue_zero_tfae`. For nilpotent maps over a domain, `LinearMap.charpoly_nilpotent_tfae` says that $\varphi$ is nilpotent if and only if $\chi_\varphi = X^n$, if and only if each vector is killed by a power of $\varphi$; the main case is `LinearMap.isNilpotent_iff_charpoly`.

**Theorem (`LinearMap.finrank_maxGenEigenspace_eq`).** In finite dimension, the dimension of the maximal generalized eigenspace of $\varphi$ for $\mu$ is the multiplicity of $\mu$ as a root of $\chi_\varphi$.

*Proof sketch.* Apply the case $\mu = 0$, `LinearMap.finrank_maxGenEigenspace_zero_eq`, to $\varphi - \mu$. For $\mu = 0$, the space splits into the maximal generalized eigenspace $V_0$, on which $\varphi$ is nilpotent, and an invariant complement on which $\varphi$ is invertible. The characteristic polynomial is the product of $X^{\dim V_0}$ and a polynomial with a nonzero constant term. $\square$

So the geometric multiplicity is at most the algebraic multiplicity (`LinearMap.finrank_eigenspace_le`).

#### Diagonal matrices, families of maps and continuous maps

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Eigenspace.Matrix` treats the linear map of a diagonal matrix. Its eigenvalues are the diagonal entries (`hasEigenvalue_toLin_diagonal_iff`), the basis vectors are eigenvectors (`hasEigenvector_toLin_diagonal`), its eigenspaces span the space (`Matrix.iSup_eigenspace_toLin_diagonal_eq_top`), and its spectrum is the set of the diagonal entries (`spectrum_diagonal`). A matrix and its linear map have the same spectrum (`Matrix.spectrum_toLin`, `LinearMap.spectrum_toMatrix`).

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Eigenspace.Pi` treats a family $(f_i)$ of endomorphisms together. For a function $\chi$ from the indices to the scalars, the common generalized eigenspace is $\bigcap_i V_{\chi(i)}(f_i)$. For distinct $\chi$ these spaces are independent (`Module.End.independent_iInf_maxGenEigenspace_of_forall_mapsTo`). If the maps commute and each is triangularizable, the common generalized eigenspaces span the space (`Module.End.iSup_iInf_maxGenEigenspace_eq_top_of_iSup_maxGenEigenspace_eq_top_of_commute`). This is the simultaneous triangularization of commuting maps, used for the weight spaces of Lie algebras.

For a continuous linear map on a $T_0$ topological module, with a continuous addition and scalar multiplication, the eigenspaces and the generalized eigenspaces of finite order are closed (`ContinuousLinearMap.isClosed_eigenspace`, `ContinuousLinearMap.isClosed_genEigenspace`), as kernels of continuous maps.

#### Common errors

1. `Polynomial.annIdealGenerator K a` is $0$ when $a$ is not integral; it is not monic in that case. Use `Polynomial.monic_annIdealGenerator` with the hypothesis that it is not zero.
2. `Module.End.IsSemisimple` is defined with the module `Module.AEval' f`, not with eigenvectors. Over $\mathbb{R}$ a rotation of the plane is semisimple and has no eigenvector.
3. `Module.End.IsSemisimple.add_of_commute` needs a perfect field and finite dimension. Over a general field, prove semisimplicity from a squarefree polynomial.

### Exercises

1. (A) Find `Polynomial.annIdealGenerator ℚ A` for $A$ = `!![1, 0; 0, 2]` and for $A$ = `!![1, 1; 0, 1]`.
2. (A) Show that a projection $P$ with $P^2 = P$ is semisimple, with `Module.End.isSemisimple_of_squarefree_aeval_eq_zero`.
3. (B) Prove that the map of `!![1, 1; 0, 1]` on $\mathbb{Q}^2$ is not semisimple, by finding an invariant line with no invariant complement.
4. (B) Show that the rotation by a right angle on $\mathbb{R}^2$ is semisimple and has no eigenvalue.
5. (B) For a nilpotent $3 \times 3$ matrix $N \ne 0$, compute the dimension of the generalized eigenspace for $0$ and of the eigenspace for $0$ in an example, and compare them with `LinearMap.finrank_maxGenEigenspace_eq`.
6. (C) Prove that over a field of characteristic $0$ each endomorphism $f$ of finite order, $f^n = 1$, is semisimple.
