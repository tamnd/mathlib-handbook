## Gluing spaces and spaces generated by a family

### Overview

Section 3 builds new spaces from old ones by universal properties: subspaces, products, sums and quotients. This section treats two further constructions that the library states in the language of categories. The first is gluing. A manifold or a scheme is often given by charts: a family of spaces $U_i$, open subsets $V_{ij} \subseteq U_i$, and homeomorphisms $t_{ij} : V_{ij} \to V_{ji}$ that tell how the charts overlap. The glued space is the union of the $U_i$ with the overlaps identified. The second construction changes the topology of a space. Given a family of test spaces $X_i$, a set is open in the $X$-generated topology when each continuous map from a test space pulls it back to an open set. Compactly generated spaces arise in this way, with the compact Hausdorff spaces as test spaces.

The section uses the category `TopCat` of topological spaces and the notions of functor, adjunction and colimit. Volume 24 treats these notions. A reader who does not know them can read the statements about open sets and continuous maps, which carry the mathematics, and skip the categorical packaging.

### The library around it

#### Gluing data

The module `Mathlib.Topology.Gluing` defines the structure `TopCat.GlueData`. It extends the general `CategoryTheory.GlueData TopCat` by one condition: each map $f_{ij} : V_{ij} \to U_i$ is an open embedding (`TopCat.GlueData.f_open`). The glued space is `D.glued`, and the maps into it are `D.ι i : D.U i ⟶ D.glued`. In the general theory the glued space is a multicoequalizer, a colimit, so the general colimit API applies. The library asks the user to treat it as a black box and to use only the lemmas below.

The relation `TopCat.GlueData.Rel` on the disjoint union $\coprod_i U_i$ identifies $x \in U_i$ with $y \in U_j$ when some point of $V_{ij}$ maps to $x$ by $f_{ij}$ and to $y$ by $f_{ji} \circ t_{ij}$. It is an equivalence relation (`TopCat.GlueData.rel_equiv`), and it describes the points of the glued space:

$$\iota_i(x) = \iota_j(y) \iff (i, x) \sim (j, y),$$

which is `TopCat.GlueData.ι_eq_iff_rel`. Each point of the glued space comes from some chart (`TopCat.GlueData.ι_jointly_surjective`), and the projection `D.π` from the disjoint union is surjective (`TopCat.GlueData.π_surjective`).

The topology of the glued space is the quotient topology. A set $W$ is open if and only if each preimage $\iota_i^{-1}(W)$ is open (`TopCat.GlueData.isOpen_iff`). From this and the openness of the $f_{ij}$ comes the main geometric fact: each $\iota_i$ is an open embedding (`TopCat.GlueData.ι_isOpenEmbedding`). Proof sketch: $\iota_i$ is injective (`TopCat.GlueData.ι_injective`) because $t_{ii}$ is the identity. For $U \subseteq U_i$ open, the preimage of $\iota_i(U)$ in $U_j$ is $f_{ji}(t_{ij}(f_{ij}^{-1}(U)))$ (`TopCat.GlueData.preimage_image_eq_image`). This set is open because $f_{ij}$ is continuous, $t_{ij}$ is a homeomorphism and $f_{ji}$ is open. So $\iota_i(U)$ is open by `TopCat.GlueData.isOpen_iff` (`TopCat.GlueData.open_image_open`). The charts overlap as they should: the images of $U_i$ and $U_j$ meet in the image of $V_{ij}$ (`TopCat.GlueData.image_inter`, `TopCat.GlueData.preimage_range`).

A gluing datum is tedious to build with pullbacks. The structure `TopCat.GlueData.MkCore` asks for elementwise data: the spaces `U`, the open sets `V i j`, the maps `t i j`, and the conditions `TopCat.GlueData.MkCore.V_id` ($V_{ii} = U_i$), `TopCat.GlueData.MkCore.t_id` ($t_{ii} = \mathrm{id}$), `TopCat.GlueData.MkCore.t_inter` and `TopCat.GlueData.MkCore.cocycle`, the cocycle condition $t_{jk} \circ t_{ij} = t_{ik}$ on triple overlaps. The function `TopCat.GlueData.mk'` turns these data into a `TopCat.GlueData`. The cocycle condition implies that $t_{ji}$ is the inverse of $t_{ij}$ (`TopCat.GlueData.MkCore.t_inv`).

The simplest example glues open subsets of one space. For a family $U : J \to$ `TopologicalSpace.Opens α`, the datum `TopCat.GlueData.ofOpenSubsets U` glues the $U_i$ along their intersections. The canonical map `TopCat.GlueData.fromOpenSubsetsGlue U` into $\alpha$ is injective, open and an open embedding (`TopCat.GlueData.fromOpenSubsetsGlue_isOpenEmbedding`), and its range is $\bigcup_i U_i$ (`TopCat.GlueData.range_fromOpenSubsetsGlue`). When the $U_i$ cover $\alpha$, the map is a homeomorphism, `TopCat.GlueData.openCoverGlueHomeo`. So a space is the gluing of the members of an open cover.

#### The topology generated by a family of spaces

Fix a family of topological spaces $X : \iota \to$ `Type u`. For a topological space $Y$, the $X$-generated topology on $Y$ is the finest topology for which each continuous map $X_i \to Y$ stays continuous. It is the supremum of the coinduced topologies:

$$\tau_X = \textstyle\bigvee_{i} \bigvee_{f \in C(X_i, Y)} f_*(\tau_{X_i}).$$

This is `TopologicalSpace.generatedBy` in `Mathlib.Topology.Convenient.GeneratedBy`. Because the order on topologies in Mathlib is reversed (Section 1), "finer" means "smaller", and `TopologicalSpace.generatedBy_le` says that $\tau_X$ is finer than the given topology of $Y$. A set is open in $\tau_X$ if and only if $f^{-1}(U)$ is open for each $i$ and each $f \in C(X_i, Y)$ (`Topology.WithGeneratedByTopology.isOpen_iff`). The equality `TopologicalSpace.generatedBy_eq_coinduced` writes $\tau_X$ as one coinduced topology, from the sigma type of all pairs of a map and a point.

To keep two topologies on one type apart, the library uses the type synonym `Topology.WithGeneratedByTopology X Y` with the topology $\tau_X$, and the identity equivalence `Topology.WithGeneratedByTopology.equiv` to $Y$. This equivalence is continuous (`Topology.WithGeneratedByTopology.continuous_equiv`). The class `Topology.IsGeneratedBy X Y` says that its inverse is continuous too, so that the original topology of $Y$ is already $X$-generated (`Topology.isGeneratedBy_iff`). Then `Topology.IsGeneratedBy.homeomorph` is a homeomorphism, and `Topology.IsGeneratedBy.generatedBy_eq` states the equality of topologies.

The point of the class is a test for continuity. If $Y$ is $X$-generated, then a map $g : Y \to Z$ is continuous if and only if $g \circ f$ is continuous for each $i$ and each continuous $f : X_i \to Y$:

`Topology.IsGeneratedBy.continuous_iff`.

Proof: $g$ is continuous if and only if $g^{-1}(V)$ is open for each open $V$. By the criterion for open sets this means that $f^{-1}(g^{-1}(V)) = (g \circ f)^{-1}(V)$ is open for each $f$, which is the continuity of each $g \circ f$.

Many spaces are $X$-generated. Each $X_i$ is (`Topology.IsGeneratedBy.inst`), and so are the one point space, sums (`Sum.isGeneratedBy`), sigma types (`Sigma.isGeneratedBy`) and quotients (`Quotient.isGeneratedBy`). More generally, the target of a quotient map from an $X$-generated space is $X$-generated (`Topology.IsQuotientMap.isGeneratedBy`). Proof: a set $V$ with open preimages under all test maps into $Z$ has an open preimage under $p$, because the composites of test maps into $Y$ with $p$ are test maps into $Z$. So $V$ is open, because $p$ is a quotient map. Products are not in this list. A product of two $X$-generated spaces need not be $X$-generated, and this failure is the reason for the next part.

The module `Mathlib.Topology.Convenient.OpenClosed` adds subspaces. If each open subset of each $X_i$ is $X$-generated, then each open subset of an $X$-generated space is $X$-generated (`IsOpen.isGeneratedBy`). The closed form `IsClosed.isGeneratedBy` and the forms for open and closed embeddings (`Topology.IsOpenEmbedding.isGeneratedBy`, `Topology.IsClosedEmbedding.isGeneratedBy`) follow the same pattern.

#### X-continuous maps

The module `Mathlib.Topology.Convenient.ContinuousMapGeneratedBy` defines the weaker notion of continuity that matches $\tau_X$. A map $g : Y \to Z$ is $X$-continuous, `Topology.ContinuousGeneratedBy X g`, if $g \circ f$ is continuous for each continuous $f : X_i \to Y$ (`Topology.continuousGeneratedBy_def`). Each continuous map is $X$-continuous (`Continuous.continuousGeneratedBy`), and $X$-continuous maps compose (`Topology.ContinuousGeneratedBy.comp`). The map $g$ is $X$-continuous if and only if it is continuous from the $X$-generated topology on $Y$ to the $X$-generated topology on $Z$ (`Topology.continuousGeneratedBy_iff`). The bundled type is `Topology.ContinuousMapGeneratedBy X Y Z`, with `Topology.ContinuousMapGeneratedBy.id` and `Topology.ContinuousMapGeneratedBy.comp`.

The module `Mathlib.Topology.Convenient.HomSpace` gives the type `Topology.ContinuousMapGeneratedBy X Z T` a topology: the coarsest one for which each precomposition `Topology.ContinuousMapGeneratedBy.precomp f` with a continuous $f : X_i \to Z$ is continuous into `C(X i, T)` with the compact open topology. Under two hypotheses, that each $X_i$ is locally compact and that each product $X_i \times X_j$ is $X$-generated, currying is a bijection

$$\{X\text{-continuous } Y \times Z \to T\} \;\cong\; \{X\text{-continuous } Z \to C_X(Y, T)\},$$

which is `Topology.ContinuousMapGeneratedBy.curryEquiv`. The evaluation map `Topology.ContinuousMapGeneratedBy.ev` is the map that corresponds to the identity. This is the exponential law that ordinary topological spaces lack, and it is the reason for the name "convenient category".

#### The categories

The module `Mathlib.Topology.Convenient.Category` packages the results as categories. The category `GeneratedByTopCat X` is the full subcategory of `TopCat` of the $X$-generated spaces. The functor `TopCat.toGeneratedByTopCat` gives a space the $X$-generated topology, and it is right adjoint to the inclusion `GeneratedByTopCat.toTopCat` (`GeneratedByTopCat.adj`). So $X$-generated spaces form a coreflective subcategory of `TopCat`. This category has all limits and all colimits (`GeneratedByTopCat.instHasLimits`, `GeneratedByTopCat.instHasColimits`). A limit in it is the limit in `TopCat` with the $X$-generated topology, so the product is not the product topology in general.

The second category `ContinuousGeneratedByCat X` has all topological spaces as objects and the $X$-continuous maps as morphisms. The functor `ContinuousGeneratedByCat.fromGeneratedByTopCat` is an equivalence (`ContinuousGeneratedByCat.equivalence`). The module `Mathlib.Topology.Convenient.Localization` explains this second view. The class `TopCat.morphismPropertyWithGeneratedByTopologyEquiv X` contains the identity maps from the $X$-generated topology to the original topology. Both `TopCat.toGeneratedByTopCat` and `TopCat.toContinuousGeneratedByCat X` are localization functors for this class: to pass to $X$-generated spaces is to invert these maps.

#### Common errors

1. Using the gluing relation `TopCat.GlueData.Rel` as if it were the glued space. The glued space is `D.glued`, and the relation describes only when two points of the charts have the same image (`TopCat.GlueData.ι_eq_iff_rel`).
2. Reading `TopologicalSpace.generatedBy_le` in the wrong direction. The $X$-generated topology has more open sets than the original topology, so it is smaller in the order of Mathlib.
3. Expecting the product of two $X$-generated spaces with the product topology to be $X$-generated. This fails in general, even for compactly generated spaces. The hypothesis `[∀ i j, IsGeneratedBy X (X i × X j)]` of the curry equivalence asks for it only on the test spaces.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $U_1 = U_2 = \mathbb{R}$, $V_{12} = V_{21} = \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ and $t_{12} = t_{21} = \mathrm{id}$. Describe the glued space and show that it is not Hausdorff.
2. (A) Show with `Topology.IsGeneratedBy.iff_le_generatedBy` that each discrete space is $X$-generated, for each family $X$.
3. (B) Prove `Topology.IsQuotientMap.isGeneratedBy` from `Topology.IsGeneratedBy.isOpen_iff`.
4. (B) Let $X$ be the family with one member, the one point space. Show that the $X$-generated topology on each space is the discrete topology.
5. (B) Show that a sequential space is $X$-generated for the family with one member $\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$, the one point compactification of $\mathbb{N}$.
6. (C) Use `TopCat.GlueData.MkCore` to glue two copies of $\mathbb{R}$ along $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}$ by $x \mapsto 1/x$, and show that the result is homeomorphic to a circle.
