# Homotopy and the homotopy category

This chapter studies maps of complexes up to homotopy. Section 1 defines a chain homotopy for complexes of every shape and proves that homotopic maps induce the same map on homology. It defines homotopy equivalences and contractible complexes, and it shows by a complex of $\mathbb{Z}/4$-modules that an acyclic complex need not be contractible. It constructs the homotopy cofiber of a map and the cylinder of a complex, and it forms the homotopy category as the quotient by the homotopy relation, with its universal property. Section 2 treats cochain complexes indexed by $\mathbb{Z}$. It defines the shift with its sign and the mapping cone, proves with explicit formulas that the triangles of mapping cones make the homotopy category triangulated, and proves that each cohomology functor is homological. Section 3 defines the Hom complex of two cochain complexes, whose cochains give the formulas of Section 2 and whose cohomology computes the morphisms to shifts in the homotopy category. Section 4 adds the mapping cocone, the homotopy fiber and the path object, the triangles of degreewise split short exact sequences, the compatibility of homology with shifts, and the homotopy category of bounded below complexes.

The chapter needs the additive and abelian categories of Volume 24, Chapter 8, and Volume 25, Chapters 1 and 2. Section 2 uses the shifts, triangles and triangulated categories of Volume 25, Chapter 5. From this volume it needs the short complexes of Chapter 1 and the complexes, homology and long exact homology sequence of Chapter 2.

Chapter 4 uses homotopies to prove that projective and injective resolutions are unique up to homotopy equivalence, so that derived functors are well defined. Chapter 5 obtains the derived category as the Verdier quotient of the homotopy category by the acyclic complexes, and the triangulated structure of Section 2 passes to it. The long exact sequences of Ext groups in Chapter 5 come from these triangles.
