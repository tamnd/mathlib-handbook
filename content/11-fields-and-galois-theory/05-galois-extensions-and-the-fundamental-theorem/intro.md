# Galois extensions and the fundamental theorem

This chapter is the center of the volume. It attaches to a finite extension $L/K$ the group $\operatorname{Aut}(L/K)$ of the automorphisms of $L$ that fix $K$, and it compares the fields between $K$ and $L$ with the subgroups of this group. The comparison is perfect exactly when $L/K$ is normal and separable. Such an extension is a Galois extension, and its automorphism group is its Galois group. The fundamental theorem says that the map $H \mapsto L^H$, which sends a subgroup to its fixed field, is a bijection from the subgroups of the Galois group onto the intermediate fields, that it reverses the inclusions, and that it turns indices into degrees.

Section 1 studies normal extensions. A finite extension is normal if and only if it is the splitting field of a polynomial, and each embedding of a normal extension into a larger field maps it onto itself. Section 2 proves the inequality $|\operatorname{Aut}(L/K)| \le [L:K]$ with the lemma of Dedekind on characters, proves the theorem of Artin that a finite group $G$ of automorphisms of $L$ has $[L : L^G] = |G|$, and gives the equivalent definitions of a Galois extension. Section 3 proves the fundamental theorem, identifies the normal subextensions with the normal subgroups, and works out the full correspondence for $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 2, \sqrt 3)$ and for the splitting field of $X^3 - 2$. It ends with a statement of the infinite form of the theorem, with the Krull topology.

The chapter needs splitting fields and their uniqueness (Chapter 3), separability and the count of embeddings (Chapter 4), and from Volume 5 subgroups, cosets and Lagrange's theorem, normal subgroups and quotients, and group actions. Chapters 6 to 8 apply the fundamental theorem: to the Galois groups of polynomials, to finite fields, and to the question of solution by radicals.
