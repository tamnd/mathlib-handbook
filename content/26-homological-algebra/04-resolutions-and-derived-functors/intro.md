# Resolutions and derived functors

This chapter builds the classical derived functors from resolutions. Section 1 defines projective and injective resolutions, proves that they exist in a category with enough projectives or injectives, and proves the comparison theorem: a map of objects lifts to a map of resolutions, and any two lifts are homotopic. So a resolution is unique up to homotopy equivalence, and the choice of a resolution for each object is a functor to the homotopy category. Section 2 applies an additive functor to these resolutions and takes homology. This gives the left and right derived functors, which do not depend on the choices, vanish on projective or injective objects, and recover a right or left exact functor in degree zero. Section 3 treats the two main examples, Ext and Tor, with the projective and injective dimension of an object.

The chapter needs the additive and abelian categories of Volume 24, Chapter 8, the projective and injective objects and exact functors of Volume 25, Chapter 2, and the monoidal categories of Volume 24, Chapter 7, for Tor. From this volume it needs the snake lemma of Chapter 1, the complexes and long exact homology sequence of Chapter 2, and the homotopies and homotopy category of Chapter 3.

The computations are over $\mathbb{Z}$, over $\mathbb{Z}/4$ and over polynomial rings: $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbb{Z}/n, \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/n$, $\operatorname{Tor}_1(\mathbb{Z}/m, \mathbb{Z}/n) = \mathbb{Z}/\gcd(m, n)$, and the infinite projective dimension of $\mathbb{Z}/2$ over $\mathbb{Z}/4$. Mathlib now defines Ext in the derived category. Chapter 5 constructs it, and this chapter shows how resolutions compute it. The long exact sequences of derived functors are proved on paper here, and Chapter 5 gives the library form for Ext.
