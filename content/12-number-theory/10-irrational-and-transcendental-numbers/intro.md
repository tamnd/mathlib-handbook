# Irrational and transcendental numbers

A real number is irrational when it is not a quotient of two integers, and transcendental when it is not a root of a nonzero integer polynomial. This chapter describes the part of the library that proves numbers irrational or transcendental and that measures how well real numbers are approximated by rationals. It is an api chapter: it explains the definitions and the main results, with short proofs where they exist.

Section 1 gives the closure rules of the predicate `Irrational`, the criterion for roots of integers, the golden ratio with Binet's formula, Niven's theorem on rational values of the cosine, and Rayleigh's theorem on Beatty sequences. Section 2 proves that Liouville numbers are transcendental, shows that they form a dense set of measure zero, describes well approximable points and Gallagher's theorem, and ends with Siegel's lemma and the house of an algebraic number.

The chapter needs the rational numbers of Volume 2, Chapter 4, the trigonometric functions of Volume 15, Chapter 6, the Baire category theorem of Volume 13 and the Lebesgue measure of Volume 18. Dirichlet's approximation theorem is in Section 4.3, and the number fields of Chapter 8 return in the house of an algebraic number.
