## Setoids and partitions

### Overview

An equivalence relation on a type $\alpha$ and a partition of $\alpha$ into nonempty disjoint blocks are two descriptions of the same thing. The classes of an equivalence relation form a partition, and the relation "in the same block" is an equivalence relation. In Mathlib an equivalence relation is a `Setoid α`, and the quotient type `Quotient s` is built from it. This section treats the order structure on these objects. The equivalence relations on $\alpha$ form a complete lattice, and the partitions form an isomorphic complete lattice.

The section describes three generations of partitions in the library. The first is `Setoid.IsPartition`, a predicate on a set of sets that covers the whole type. The second is `Partition s`, a structure for an element $s$ of a complete lattice: a set of nonzero, independent elements with supremum $s$. The third is `Finpartition a`, the finite form of the same idea, with the cardinality results that combinatorics uses. The independence of the parts is the notion `sSupIndep` or `Finset.SupIndep` of the previous section. The section uses the complete lattices and the Galois insertions only through their names. They come in Chapters 4 and 6.

### The library around it

#### The lattice of setoids

The order on `Setoid α` is inclusion of relations: by `Setoid.le_def`, $r \le s$ when $r\,x\,y$ gives $s\,x\,y$. So a smaller relation has smaller classes and is finer. The bottom element is equality, `Setoid.bot_def`, and the top element relates all pairs, `Setoid.top_def`. The instance `Setoid.completeLattice` gives the complete lattice.

The infimum is the intersection of the relations: `Setoid.inf_iff_and` says that $(r \sqcap s)\,x\,y$ holds if and only if $r\,x\,y$ and $s\,x\,y$, and `Setoid.sInf_iff` gives the same for a set of setoids. The supremum is not the union, because the union of two equivalence relations need not be transitive. By `Setoid.sup_eq_eqvGen`, $r \sqcup s$ is the equivalence relation generated by the union. The function `Relation.EqvGen.setoid` gives the generated setoid, and `Setoid.eqvGen_le` says that it is the least setoid that contains the relation. Together, these give the Galois insertion `Setoid.gi` between relations and setoids.

#### Kernels, images and the quotient

For $f : \alpha \to \beta$, the kernel `Setoid.ker f` relates $x$ and $y$ when $f(x) = f(y)$, by `Setoid.ker_def`. The kernel is the bottom setoid exactly when $f$ is injective, `Setoid.ker_eq_bot_iff`. The map `Setoid.kerLift f` is the induced map from `Quotient (Setoid.ker f)` to $\beta$, and `Setoid.kerLift_injective` says that it is injective. The equivalences `Setoid.quotientKerEquivRange` and `Setoid.quotientKerEquivOfSurjective` are the set form of the first isomorphism theorem: the quotient by the kernel is in bijection with the range.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Setoid.comap f r` | the setoid on $\alpha$ with $x \sim y$ iff $r\,(f x)\,(f y)$ |
| `Setoid.comap_rel` | the defining property of `Setoid.comap` |
| `Setoid.map r f` | the setoid on $\beta$ generated by the images of the pairs of $r$ |
| `Setoid.gc_map_comap` | `Setoid.map` and `Setoid.comap` form a Galois connection |
| `Setoid.ker_mk_eq` | the kernel of the projection onto `Quotient r` is $r$ |
| `Setoid.correspondence` | $\{ s \mid r \le s \} \simeq_o$ `Setoid (Quotient r)` |
| `Setoid.prodQuotientEquiv` | the quotient of a product by a product setoid |

The order isomorphism `Setoid.correspondence r` is the correspondence theorem for setoids. A setoid $s$ above $r$ is constant on each class of $r$, so it descends to a setoid on `Quotient r`. In the other direction, a setoid on the quotient pulls back along the projection by `Setoid.comap`. The pullback contains $r$, because $r$ is the kernel of the projection by `Setoid.ker_mk_eq`.

#### Partitions of a type

For a setoid $r$, `Setoid.classes r` is the set of its equivalence classes. A set $c$ of subsets of $\alpha$ is a partition, `Setoid.IsPartition c`, when $\emptyset \notin c$ and each $a \in \alpha$ lies in exactly one element of $c$. The two directions of the correspondence are:

1. `Setoid.isPartition_classes`: the classes of a setoid form a partition;
2. `Setoid.mkClasses`: a set of sets with the unique membership property defines a setoid, and `Setoid.classes_mkClasses` says that its classes are the sets of the partition.

The type `Setoid.Partitions α` bundles the partitions of $\alpha$, and `Setoid.Partition.orderIso` is an order isomorphism from `Setoid α` to it. So the partitions form a complete lattice, `Setoid.Partition.completeLattice`, with the order transported from the setoids: a finer partition is smaller. The lemmas `Setoid.IsPartition.pairwiseDisjoint` and `Setoid.IsPartition.sUnion_eq_univ` give the two usual properties, and `Setoid.rel_iff_exists_classes` says that two elements are related exactly when they lie in a common class.

For counting, `Setoid.IsPartition.ncard_eq_finsum` says that the cardinality of a finite set $s$ is the sum of the cardinalities of the traces $s \cap t$ on the parts $t$. The finite sum is a `finsum`, because the partition can have infinitely many parts that miss $s$. The lemma `Setoid.card_classes_ker_le` bounds the number of classes of a kernel by the size of the codomain.

The structure `IndexedPartition s` describes a partition by a family $s : \iota \to \operatorname{Set} \alpha$, with a function `IndexedPartition.index` that sends each element to the index of its block and a chosen element `IndexedPartition.some` in each block. The function `IndexedPartition.piecewise` defines a function on $\alpha$ by a different formula on each block.

#### Partitions in a complete lattice

The structure `Partition s` is defined for an element $s$ of a complete lattice $\alpha$. A partition of $s$ is a set of parts that is independent in the sense of `sSupIndep`, does not contain $\bot$, and has supremum $s$. The lemmas `Partition.sSupIndep`, `Partition.bot_notMem` and `Partition.sSup_eq` give the three conditions, and `Partition.disjoint` gives the disjointness of two distinct parts. A partition is a `SetLike` object, so `x ∈ P` means that $x$ is a part. For $\alpha = \operatorname{Set} \beta$ and $s = u$ a set, this is a partition of $u$ in the usual sense, and the lemma `Partition.sUnion_eq` says that the union of the parts is $u$.

The order is refinement: by `Partition.le_def`, $P \le Q$ when each part of $P$ is below a part of $Q$. The top partition has the one part $s$, `Partition.parts_top`, when $s \ne \bot$. In a frame, `Partition.instSemilatticeInf` gives the infimum, and `Partition.mem_inf_iff` describes its parts as the nonzero infima $p \sqcap q$ of a part of each partition. The function `Partition.removeBot` builds a partition from an independent set with supremum $s$ by removing $\bot$.

For a partition $P$ of a set $u$, `Partition.Rel P` relates $a$ and $b$ when they lie in a common part. It is symmetric and transitive, but it relates no element outside $u$, so it is a partial equivalence relation. The lemma `Partition.rel_rfl_iff` says that $a$ is related to itself exactly when $a \in u$. The part of an element is `Partition.partOf`, and `Partition.rel_le_iff_le` says that the order on the relations is the order on the partitions. A representative function, `Partition.IsRepFun P f`, sends each element of $u$ to a chosen element of its part and fixes the elements outside $u$. The lemma `Partition.IsRepFun.nonempty` says that one exists.

#### Finite partitions

The structure `Finpartition a` is defined for an element $a$ of a lattice with a bottom element. Its field `Finpartition.parts` is a `Finset` of parts with `Finpartition.supIndep`, `Finpartition.sup_parts` and `Finpartition.bot_notMem`. The main case is $a = s$ a `Finset α`, where the parts are finsets.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Finpartition.part` | the part that contains a given element, or $\emptyset$ |
| `Finpartition.mem_part_iff_exists` | $a \in P.\mathrm{part}\,b$ iff some part contains $a$ and $b$ |
| `Finpartition.sum_card_parts` | $\sum_{t \in P} \lvert t \rvert = \lvert s \rvert$ |
| `Finpartition.card_bot` | the partition into singletons has $\lvert s \rvert$ parts |
| `Finpartition.card_mono` | a finer partition has more parts |
| `Finpartition.ofSetoid` | the partition of `Finset.univ` into the classes of a setoid |
| `Finpartition.atomise` | the partition of $s$ by the membership pattern in the sets of $F$ |
| `Finpartition.card_atomise_le` | this partition has at most $2^{\lvert F \rvert}$ parts |
| `Finpartition.bind` | refine each part by a partition of it, in a modular lattice |

The proof of `Finpartition.sum_card_parts` uses the pairwise disjointness of the parts. In the distributive lattice `Finset α`, independence is the same as pairwise disjointness, by `Finset.supIndep_iff_pairwiseDisjoint`. The cardinality of a disjoint union is the sum of the cardinalities, and the union of the parts is $s$ by `Finpartition.sup_parts`.

#### Equipartitions

A finite partition $P$ of $s$ is an equipartition, `Finpartition.IsEquipartition P`, when the sizes of two parts differ by at most $1$. By `Finpartition.isEquipartition_iff_card_parts_eq_average`, each part then has $\lfloor \lvert s \rvert / k \rfloor$ or $\lfloor \lvert s \rvert / k \rfloor + 1$ elements, where $k$ is the number of parts. The lemma `Finpartition.IsEquipartition.card_large_parts_eq_mod` says that exactly $\lvert s \rvert \bmod k$ parts are large. The partition into singletons and the partition with one part are equipartitions, by `Finpartition.bot_isEquipartition` and `Finpartition.indiscrete_isEquipartition`. Equipartitions are the input of the regularity lemma of Szemerédi in graph theory.

#### Common errors

1. The order on `Setoid α` puts the finest relation, equality, at the bottom. A proof that expects the discrete partition at the top gets every inequality in the wrong direction.
2. `Partition.Rel` is not a `Setoid`. It is not reflexive outside the support $u$, so `Partition.rel_rfl_iff` needs the hypothesis $a \in u$.
3. `Finpartition.part P a` is $\emptyset$ when $a \notin s$. The lemma `Finpartition.part_mem` gives $P.\mathrm{part}\,a \in P.\mathrm{parts}$ only under $a \in s$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show with `Setoid.ker_eq_bot_iff` that the kernel of $n \mapsto n \bmod 3$ on $\mathbb{N}$ is not $\bot$, and describe its classes.
2. (A) List the five partitions of a set with three elements, and draw the lattice that they form under refinement.
3. (B) Prove that the union of two equivalence relations need not be transitive, and compute $r \sqcup s$ for the kernels of $n \mapsto n \bmod 2$ and $n \mapsto n \bmod 3$ on $\mathbb{Z}$.
4. (B) Prove `Finpartition.card_mono` for finite partitions of a finset: if $P \le Q$, then $Q$ has at most as many parts as $P$.
5. (B) Prove that an equipartition of a set with $10$ elements into $4$ parts has exactly $2$ parts with $3$ elements.
6. (C) Prove `Setoid.isPartition_classes` and `Setoid.classes_mkClasses` from the definitions, and conclude that `Setoid.Partition.orderIso` is a bijection.
