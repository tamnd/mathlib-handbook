## The universal property of localization

### Overview

In algebra, the localization $S^{-1}R$ of a commutative ring makes the elements of $S$ invertible in the most economical way. The same construction exists for categories. For a class $W$ of morphisms of $C$, the *localization* $C[W^{-1}]$ is a category with a functor $L : C \to C[W^{-1}]$ that sends each element of $W$ to an isomorphism, and that is universal with this property. The derived category of Volume 26 is the localization of the category of complexes at the quasi-isomorphisms, and the homotopy category of a model category is the localization at the weak equivalences.

This section constructs $C[W^{-1}]$ by paths, proves the universal property, and then works with the predicate "$L$ is a localization functor" in place of the construction. That predicate is invariant under equivalences of categories, and it is the form Mathlib uses for all later work. The class of functors that invert $W$ is the one of Chapter 3, Section 1. In the text $g \circ f$ is the textbook composite, and Mathlib writes `f ≫ g`.

### Definitions

**Definition (Strict localization).** Let $W$ be a class of morphisms of $C$. A functor $L : C \to D$ has the *strict universal property* of the localization if $L$ inverts $W$, and for each category $E$ and each functor $F : C \to E$ that inverts $W$ there is a unique functor $\bar F : D \to E$ with $\bar F \circ L = F$.

**Definition (The constructed localization).** Let $Q$ be the quiver whose vertices are the objects of $C$, whose arrows $X \to Y$ are the morphisms $X \to Y$ of $C$, and which has in addition one formal arrow $\bar w : Y \to X$ for each $w : X \to Y$ in $W$. The category $C[W^{-1}]$ is the quotient of the path category of $Q$ by the congruence generated by four relations:

1. the path of $1_X$ is the empty path at $X$;
2. the path of $g \circ f$ is the path $f$ followed by $g$;
3. the path $w$ followed by $\bar w$ is the empty path at $X$, for $w : X \to Y$ in $W$;
4. the path $\bar w$ followed by $w$ is the empty path at $Y$.

The functor $L_W : C \to C[W^{-1}]$ sends $X$ to $X$ and $f$ to the class of the path $f$.

A morphism of $C[W^{-1}]$ is the class of a zigzag $X \to X_1 \leftarrow X_2 \to \cdots$ in which the backward arrows are elements of $W$. The relations make each backward arrow an inverse.

**Definition (Localization functor).** A functor $L : C \to D$ is a *localization functor* for $W$ if it inverts $W$ and the functor $\bar L : C[W^{-1}] \to D$ induced by the universal property is an equivalence of categories.

### Theorems and proofs

**Theorem (The strict universal property).** The functor $L_W : C \to C[W^{-1}]$ inverts $W$. If $F : C \to E$ inverts $W$, there is a unique functor $\bar F : C[W^{-1}] \to E$ with $\bar F \circ L_W = F$.

*Proof.* For $w \in W$, relations 3 and 4 say that $L_W(w)$ and the class of $\bar w$ are inverse. So $L_W$ inverts $W$.

Let $F$ invert $W$. A functor from a path category is the same as a map of quivers, so define a map $\tilde F$ from $Q$ to $E$ by $\tilde F(X) = FX$, $\tilde F(f) = Ff$ and $\tilde F(\bar w) = (Fw)^{-1}$. It gives a functor on paths. This functor respects the four relations: relations 1 and 2 hold because $F$ is a functor, and relations 3 and 4 hold because $(Fw)^{-1}$ is the inverse of $Fw$. So it passes to the quotient, and gives $\bar F$ with $\bar F \circ L_W = F$.

For uniqueness, let $G_1, G_2 : C[W^{-1}] \to E$ with $G_1 \circ L_W = G_2 \circ L_W$. They agree on objects, since each object is $L_W X$. They agree on the classes of the paths $f$. On the class of $\bar w$, both are the inverse of $G_i(L_W w)$, which is the same isomorphism. Since each morphism is a composite of such classes, $G_1 = G_2$. ∎

The last step of the proof is a general fact. A property of morphisms of $C[W^{-1}]$ that is stable under composition and holds for each $L_W f$ and each inverse of $L_W w$, with $w \in W$, holds for each morphism. This is `Localization.Construction.morphismProperty_eq_top`.

**Theorem (The universal property up to equivalence).** Let $L : C \to D$ be a localization functor for $W$, and let $E$ be a category.

1. $L$ is essentially surjective.
2. The functor $L^\ast : D^E \to (C^E)_W$, $G \mapsto G \circ L$, from functors $D \to E$ to functors $C \to E$ that invert $W$, is an equivalence of categories.
3. In particular, for $F : C \to E$ that inverts $W$, there is a functor $\bar F : D \to E$ with an isomorphism $\bar F \circ L \cong F$, and $\bar F$ is unique up to a unique isomorphism compatible with this isomorphism. Two natural transformations $\tau, \tau' : G_1 \to G_2$ of functors $D \to E$ are equal if $\tau_{LX} = \tau'_{LX}$ for each $X$.

*Proof.* Write $L = \bar L \circ L_W$ with $\bar L$ an equivalence. Since $L_W$ is the identity on objects and $\bar L$ is essentially surjective, $L$ is essentially surjective. For part 2 it is sufficient to treat $L_W$, since composition with the equivalence $\bar L$ gives an equivalence $D^E \to C[W^{-1}]^E$. For $L_W$, the strict universal property shows that $L_W^\ast$ is bijective on objects. It remains to show that it is full and faithful. Let $G_1, G_2 : C[W^{-1}] \to E$ and $\sigma : G_1 \circ L_W \to G_2 \circ L_W$. The components $\sigma_X : G_1 X \to G_2 X$ are defined at each object of $C[W^{-1}]$. Naturality holds for each $L_W f$ by hypothesis. If it holds for an isomorphism $e$, it holds for $e^{-1}$, by composition with $G_1(e^{-1})$ and $G_2(e^{-1})$. So the class of morphisms at which $\sigma$ is natural is stable under composition, contains the $L_W f$ and the inverses of the $L_W w$, and by the remark above it is all morphisms. So $\sigma$ is a natural transformation $G_1 \to G_2$, and it is the only one with these components. Part 3 follows from part 2: $\bar F$ is a preimage of $F$ under an equivalence, and the last claim is faithfulness, with part 1 to reach each object of $D$ up to isomorphism. ∎

**Theorem (Uniqueness and invariance).** Let $L_1 : C \to D_1$ and $L_2 : C \to D_2$ be localization functors for $W$. Then there is an equivalence $D_1 \simeq D_2$ compatible with $L_1$ and $L_2$ up to isomorphism. If $L : C \to D$ is a localization functor and $\Phi : D \to D'$ is an equivalence, then each functor $L' \cong \Phi \circ L$ is a localization functor for $W$. If $W$ is contained in the isomorphisms, each equivalence $C \to D$ is a localization functor for $W$, in particular the identity of $C$.

*Proof.* Both $\bar L_1 : C[W^{-1}] \to D_1$ and $\bar L_2$ are equivalences, so $\bar L_2 \circ \bar L_1^{-1}$ is an equivalence $D_1 \to D_2$, and it is compatible with the functors $L_i$ up to isomorphism. For the second claim, $L'$ inverts $W$, since $\Phi \circ L$ does and isomorphic functors invert the same morphisms. The induced functor $\bar{L'}$ is isomorphic to $\Phi \circ \bar L$ by the uniqueness in the universal property, so it is an equivalence. For the last claim, if $W$ is contained in the isomorphisms, each functor inverts $W$, and the identity $1_C$ has the strict universal property with $\bar F = F$. So the induced functor $C[W^{-1}] \to C$ is an equivalence, and $1_C$ is a localization functor. The case of an equivalence follows from the second claim. ∎

**Theorem (Opposite categories).** If $L : C \to D$ is a localization functor for $W$, then $L^{\mathrm{op}} : C^{\mathrm{op}} \to D^{\mathrm{op}}$ is a localization functor for $W^{\mathrm{op}}$.

*Proof.* A functor $F : C^{\mathrm{op}} \to E$ inverts $W^{\mathrm{op}}$ if and only if $F^{\mathrm{op}} : C \to E^{\mathrm{op}}$ inverts $W$. So the strict universal property of $L_W$ with target $E^{\mathrm{op}}$ gives the strict universal property of $L_W^{\mathrm{op}}$ with target $E$. Thus $L_W^{\mathrm{op}}$ is a localization functor, and the claim for $L$ follows from the invariance under equivalences. ∎

### Examples

**Example (A monoid and its group).** Let $M$ be a monoid and $BM$ the category with one object $\ast$ and $\mathrm{End}(\ast) = M$. Let $W$ be all morphisms. A functor from $BM$ to a groupoid of one object is a homomorphism from $M$ to a group, and the localization $BM[W^{-1}]$ is $BG(M)$, with $G(M)$ the *group completion* of $M$: the group generated by the elements of $M$ with the relations of $M$. For $M = \mathbb{N}$ under addition, $G(M) = \mathbb{Z}$. For the multiplicative monoid $\{1, 0\}$, the relation $0 \cdot 0 = 0$ gives $0 = 1$ in the group, so $G(M)$ is trivial.

**Example (A reflective subcategory).** Let $\iota : D \to C$ be the inclusion of a full reflective subcategory, with left adjoint $r$. Then $r$ is a localization functor for the class of morphisms $f$ with $r(f)$ an isomorphism. For example, abelianization $\mathrm{Grp} \to \mathrm{Ab}$ is a localization of the category of groups at the homomorphisms that induce isomorphisms of abelianizations. In Mathlib this is `Adjunction.isLocalization`, in the file `Localization.Adjunction`, and the proof builds an inverse equivalence from the unit and the counit.

**Example (Two parallel arrows).** Let $C$ have objects $X, Y$ and two morphisms $u, v : X \to Y$ other than identities, and let $W = \{u\}$. In $C[W^{-1}]$, $X \cong Y$ by $u$. The endomorphism $a = u^{-1} \circ v$ of $X$ has no relation with the identity, and the morphisms $X \to X$ are the powers $a^n$ for $n \ge 0$. So the localization of a finite category can have infinite Hom sets.

The objects of `W.Localization` are the objects of `C`, but its morphisms are in the universe `max u v` when `C : Type u` and `[Category.{v} C]`. The class `MorphismProperty.HasLocalization.{w} W` records a localization with morphisms in the universe `w`, and `W.Q' : C ⥤ W.Localization'` is the chosen functor. The derived category of Volume 26 uses this class. The file `Localization.Equivalence` gives the transport of the predicate along equivalences of the source, `IsLocalization.of_equivalence_source`, and the file `Localization.Opposite` gives `IsLocalization.op`.

Common errors of a learner:

- `Localization.Construction.lift` is the strict lift for `W.Q`, with an equality `W.Q ⋙ lift G hG = G`. The lift `Localization.lift` for a general localization functor has only an isomorphism, given by the class `Lifting`.
- To prove that two functors from `D` are isomorphic, it is sufficient to give an isomorphism after composition with `L`. To prove that two natural transformations are equal, use `Localization.natTrans_ext`.
- A localization functor need not be full or faithful. Two morphisms of `C` can become equal in `D` without any relation in `C`, as in the example of the monoid $\{1, 0\}$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that a functor inverts $W$ if and only if it inverts the multiplicative closure $W^{\ast}$, and conclude that $C[W^{-1}] \simeq C[(W^{\ast})^{-1}]$.
2. (A) Let $C$ be the category $0 \to 1$ and $W$ all morphisms. Show that $C[W^{-1}]$ is equivalent to the category with one object and one morphism.
3. (B) Prove directly from the universal property that $C[W^{-1}]$ is a groupoid when $W$ is the class of all morphisms of $C$, and that $C[W^{-1}]$ is then the free groupoid on $C$.
4. (B) Let $L : C \to D$ be a localization functor for $W$, and let $W'$ be the class of morphisms $f$ of $C$ with $Lf$ an isomorphism. Show that $W \le W'$, that $L$ is a localization functor for $W'$, and give an example with $W \neq W'$.
5. (B) Let $X$ be an object of $C$ such that $\mathrm{Hom}(-, X)$ sends each $w \in W$ to a bijection. Show that $\mathrm{Hom}_C(Y, X) \to \mathrm{Hom}_{C[W^{-1}]}(L Y, L X)$ is a bijection for each $Y$. *Hint:* the functor $\mathrm{Hom}_C(-, X)$ factors through $C[W^{-1}]^{\mathrm{op}}$; use the Yoneda lemma.
6. (C) In the example of two parallel arrows, prove that $\mathrm{Hom}(X, X)$ in $C[W^{-1}]$ is the free monoid on $a = u^{-1} \circ v$. *Hint:* construct a functor from $C[W^{-1}]$ to the category with one object and endomorphisms $\mathbb{N}$.

### Notes and history

The localization of categories and the calculus of fractions are from the book of Gabriel and Zisman, *Calculus of fractions and homotopy theory*, of 1967, which also gives the construction by zigzags. Verdier used localization to define derived categories in his thesis of 1967. The use of a predicate in place of one fixed construction follows the practice of Kashiwara and Schapira, and it lets Mathlib give the homotopy category or the derived category any model with the same universal property.
