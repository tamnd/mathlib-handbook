# Logic, model theory and computability

This volume teaches first-order logic, model theory and the theory of computation, and their form in Mathlib. Model theory studies mathematical structures through the sentences that are true in them. A group, a field or a linear order is a structure for a first-order language, and the theorems of model theory say which properties a set of axioms can express and which it cannot. The theory of computation asks which functions an algorithm can compute. It gives exact definitions of an algorithm, as a recursive function, as a Turing machine and as a finite automaton, and it proves that some problems have no algorithm.

Chapter 1 defines first-order languages, terms, formulas and structures, and the realization of a formula in a structure. Chapter 2 proves the compactness theorem with ultraproducts and the theorems of Löwenheim and Skolem, and it treats elementary embeddings and the space of types. Chapter 3 studies definable sets, the theory of dense linear orders and the Fraïssé limits, with the rational numbers as the main example. Chapter 4 applies model theory to algebra. It proves that the theory of algebraically closed fields of a given characteristic is complete, it derives the Lefschetz principle, and it treats Presburger arithmetic and the semilinear sets.

Chapter 5 treats formal languages, regular expressions, finite automata, the Myhill-Nerode theorem and context-free grammars. Chapter 6 defines the primitive recursive and the partial recursive functions, constructs codes for programs, and proves the undecidability of the halting problem and the theorem of Rice. It ends with reductions and Turing degrees. Chapter 7 defines Turing machines and shows that they compute the partial recursive functions. Chapter 8 proves the Akra-Bazzi theorem on the recurrences of divide-and-conquer algorithms.

The volume needs the language of Mathlib from Volume 1, sets, lists and finite types from Volume 3, and orders from Volume 4. Chapter 4 uses rings and fields from Volumes 6 and 11, and the space of types in Chapter 2 uses the topology of Volume 13. Chapters 1 to 4 form the first part, on model theory, and Chapters 5 to 8 form the second part, on computation. Each part can be read without the other. The level is that of a graduate course. The references are Marker, *Model Theory: An Introduction*, Hodges, *A Shorter Model Theory*, Sipser, *Introduction to the Theory of Computation*, and Soare, *Recursively Enumerable Sets and Degrees*.

In Mathlib, a first-order language is a term of `FirstOrder.Language`, a structure for it is an instance of `L.Structure M`, and the bounded formulas `L.BoundedFormula α n` use de Bruijn indices for the bound variables. The recursive functions are `Nat.Primrec` and `Nat.Partrec` on the natural numbers, and `Primrec`, `Partrec` and `Computable` on types with an encoding. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
