## Linear disjointness and free products of algebras

### Overview

The files of this section use bases and ranks inside algebras. Let $S$ be an algebra over a commutative ring $R$, and let $M$, $N$ be $R$-submodules of $S$. The product $M N$ is the submodule spanned by the products $m n$. Each element of it is a sum $\sum_k m_k n_k$, and the question is whether these sums behave like elements of the tensor product $M \otimes_R N$. The submodules are *linearly disjoint* when the multiplication map $M \otimes_R N \to S$ is injective. For subfields $K$ and $L$ of a field, this is the classical condition that a basis of $K$ stays independent over $L$. The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.LinearDisjoint` defines the notion and proves its tests with bases, its behaviour under smaller submodules, and a bound on the rank of $M \cap N$.

Three smaller modules sit next to it. `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.LinearIndependent.Algebra` relates linear independence in $S$ and in an algebra over $S$, and bounds the rank of the subalgebra generated by a set. `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.FreeAlgebra` gives the basis of the free algebra $R\langle X \rangle$, the noncommutative polynomials. `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.Basic` constructs the free product of a family of $R$-algebras, the coproduct in the category of $R$-algebras. Tensor products, which this section uses as a tool, are the topic of volume 8.

### The library around it

#### Linearly disjoint submodules

The multiplication map is `Submodule.mulMap M N : M ⊗[R] N →ₗ[R] S`, which sends $m \otimes n$ to $m n$. Its range is the product $M N$.

**Definition (`Submodule.LinearDisjoint`).** `M.LinearDisjoint N` holds when `Submodule.mulMap M N` is injective.

The definition is a structure with one field; `Submodule.linearDisjoint_iff` states the equivalence with injectivity, and `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.mk` builds a proof from it. An injective map onto its range is an equivalence.

**Definition (`Submodule.LinearDisjoint.mulMap`).** If `H : M.LinearDisjoint N`, then `H.mulMap : M ⊗[R] N ≃ₗ[R] (M * N)` is the multiplication map, as a linear equivalence onto $M N$.

The lemma `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.val_mulMap_tmul` computes it on pure tensors. Some cases are immediate. The submodule $1$, the image of $R$ in $S$, is linearly disjoint from each submodule (`Submodule.LinearDisjoint.one_left` and `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.one_right`), and so is $\bot$ (`Submodule.LinearDisjoint.bot_left`). The order of $M$ and $N$ matters when $S$ is not commutative: `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.symm_of_commute` swaps them when the elements commute, and `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.symm` does so in a commutative algebra. An injective algebra map keeps the relation (`Submodule.LinearDisjoint.map`).

#### Tests with bases

When $M$ and $N$ are free, a basis of the tensor product is the set of products of basis vectors. Linear disjointness then becomes a statement about linear independence in $S$.

**Proposition (`Submodule.LinearDisjoint.of_basis_mul`).** Let $(m_i)$ be a basis of $M$ and $(n_j)$ a basis of $N$. If the products $m_i n_j$ are linearly independent in $S$, then $M$ and $N$ are linearly disjoint.

*Proof.* The vectors $m_i \otimes n_j$ form a basis of $M \otimes_R N$. The multiplication map sends this basis to the family $m_i n_j$. A linear map that sends a basis to an independent family is injective. $\square$

A second test uses a basis of only one side. The lemmas `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.of_basis_left` and `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.of_basis_right` use a basis of only one side: $M$ and $N$ are linearly disjoint if the map $(N)^{(\iota)} \to S$, $(x_i) \mapsto \sum_i m_i x_i$, is injective. This map is `Submodule.mulLeftMap` of Section 9 of Chapter 1. In the other direction, linear disjointness carries independent families of the two sides to an independent family of products, under a flatness hypothesis.

**Proposition (`Submodule.LinearDisjoint.linearIndependent_mul_of_flat`).** Let $M$ and $N$ be linearly disjoint, and let $M$ or $N$ be flat over $R$. If the families $(m_i)$ in $M$ and $(n_j)$ in $N$ are linearly independent, then the products $m_i n_j$ are linearly independent.

Over a field each module is flat, so the hypothesis holds. The variants `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.linearIndependent_mul_of_flat_left` and `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.linearIndependent_mul_of_flat_right` name the flat side. Flatness also lets the relation pass to smaller submodules: `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.of_le_left_of_flat` and `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.of_le_of_flat_left`. Conversely, linear disjointness can be checked on finitely generated submodules (`Submodule.LinearDisjoint.of_linearDisjoint_fg`).

#### The intersection of linearly disjoint submodules

The intersection of two linearly disjoint submodules is small.

**Theorem (`Submodule.LinearDisjoint.rank_inf_le_one_of_flat`).** Let $R$ and $S$ be commutative, let $M$ and $N$ be linearly disjoint, and let one of them be flat. Then $M \cap N$ has rank at most $1$.

*Proof sketch.* Let $a, b \in M \cap N$ be independent. The two tensors $a \otimes b$ and $b \otimes a$ in $M \otimes_R N$ have the same image $a b = b a$ under multiplication, so by injectivity they are equal. With flatness, the inclusion of the span of $a, b$ into $M$ and into $N$ gives an injective map from the tensor square of a free module of rank $2$, where $a \otimes b \neq b \otimes a$. This contradiction is `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.not_linearIndependent_pair_of_flat`. $\square$

For noncommutative $S$ the variant `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.rank_inf_le_one_of_commute_of_flat` asks that the elements of $M \cap N$ commute. A submodule that is linearly disjoint from itself has rank at most $1$ (`Submodule.LinearDisjoint.rank_le_one_of_flat_of_self`). For subalgebras and intermediate fields, volume 10 builds `Subalgebra.LinearDisjoint` on these results.

#### Linear independence inside algebras

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.LinearIndependent.Algebra` has the following test.

**Proposition (`LinearIndependent.algebraMap_comp_iff`).** Let $R \to S \to A$ be a tower of algebras with $S \to A$ injective. A family $v$ in $S$ is linearly independent over $R$ if and only if its image in $A$ is.

The forms for sets are `LinearIndepOn.algebraMap_comp_iff` and `LinearIndepOn.id_image_algebraMap_iff`. The file also proves `Algebra.rank_adjoin_le`: the subalgebra generated by a set $s$ has rank at most $\max(|s|, \aleph_0)$, because it is spanned by the finite products of elements of $s$. The lemma `DirectSum.induction_lon` is an induction principle for direct sums of modules, used in the next construction.

#### The free algebra and free products

The free algebra `FreeAlgebra R X` of volume 6 is the algebra of noncommutative polynomials in the variables $X$. Its monomials are the words in $X$, the elements of the free monoid.

**Definition (`FreeAlgebra.basisFreeMonoid`).** `FreeAlgebra.basisFreeMonoid R X` is the basis of `FreeAlgebra R X` indexed by `FreeMonoid X`, whose vector for a word is the product of its letters.

The instance `FreeAlgebra.instFree` follows, and `FreeAlgebra.rank_eq` says that the rank is the cardinality of the type of lists in $X$: countable when $X$ is finite and nonempty.

The free product is the coproduct of algebras. Let $(A_i)_{i \in I}$ be a family of $R$-algebras.

**Definition (`LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct`).** `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct R A` is the quotient of the tensor algebra of $\bigoplus_i A_i$ by the relations that identify the unit of each $A_i$ with $1$ and the product of two elements of the same $A_i$ with their product in $A_i$.

The intermediate algebra `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.FreeTensorAlgebra R A` is that tensor algebra, `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.rel` is the relation, and `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.mkAlgHom` is the quotient map. The lemmas `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.identify_one` and `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.mul_injections` state the two relations in the quotient. The algebra maps `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.ι R A i : A i →ₐ[R] LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct R A` are the injections of the factors.

**Theorem (`LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.lift`).** For an $R$-algebra $B$, the families of algebra maps $A_i \to B$ correspond to the algebra maps from the free product to $B$.

*Proof sketch.* A family $f_i : A_i \to B$ gives a linear map $\bigoplus_i A_i \to B$, and by the universal property of the tensor algebra an algebra map from `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.FreeTensorAlgebra R A` to $B$. This map respects the two relations, because each $f_i$ preserves $1$ and products, so it passes to the quotient. Uniqueness holds because the images of the $A_i$ generate the free product. $\square$

The lemmas `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.lift_comp_ι` and `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.lift_unique` are the two halves of the theorem. The type `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.asPowers R A` is a second model built from the graded powers of $\bigoplus_i A_i$, and `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.asPowersEquiv` identifies the two models.

#### Common errors

1. `M.LinearDisjoint N` is about the tensor product over $R$ of the two submodules, not about $M \cap N = 0$. Two linearly disjoint submodules both contain the submodule $1$ when $S$ has a unit, so their intersection is not zero.
2. In a noncommutative algebra, `M.LinearDisjoint N` and `N.LinearDisjoint M` are different statements. Use `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.symm_of_commute` only after a proof that the elements commute.
3. Many lemmas about linear disjointness have three versions, whose names end in of_flat, of_flat_left and of_flat_right. The first takes a disjunction of flatness hypotheses as an explicit argument; the other two take one flatness instance.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that the submodule $1$ of $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ is linearly disjoint from each submodule $N$.
2. (A) Compute the rank of `FreeAlgebra ℚ (Fin 1)` and compare it with the polynomial ring $\mathbb{Q}[x]$.
3. (B) In $S = \mathbb{Q}[x, y]$, show that the submodules spanned by $1, x$ and by $1, y$ are linearly disjoint over $\mathbb{Q}$, with `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.of_basis_mul`.
4. (B) In $S = \mathbb{Q}[x]$, show that the submodule $M$ spanned by $1, x$ is not linearly disjoint from itself.
5. (B) Describe the algebra map from the free product of two copies of $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ to $\mathbb{Q}[x, y]$ that `LinearAlgebra.FreeProduct.lift` gives for the two inclusions $x \mapsto x$ and $x \mapsto y$.
6. (C) Use `Submodule.LinearDisjoint.rank_inf_le_one_of_flat` to show that the subfields $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ of $\mathbb{R}$ are not linearly disjoint over $\mathbb{Q}$.
