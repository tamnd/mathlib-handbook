# Quadratic forms

A quadratic form is a homogeneous polynomial of degree 2 in the coordinates of a vector, such as $x^2 + y^2 - z^2$ or $x y$. Quadratic forms are older than bilinear forms: they appear in the arithmetic of Fermat, Lagrange and Gauss, in the classification of conics and quadrics, and in the second derivative test of analysis. This chapter treats them as functions $Q : M \to N$ on a module, with $Q(a x) = a^2 Q(x)$ and a bilinear polar $Q(x + y) - Q(x) - Q(y)$.

Section 1 defines quadratic maps and their polar, and compares them with the bilinear forms of Chapter 5. When $2$ is invertible, quadratic forms and symmetric bilinear forms are the same thing: the associated form $\frac{1}{2} \operatorname{polar}_Q$ recovers $Q$ on the diagonal. When $2$ is not invertible this fails, and the form $x y$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$ shows how. The section also defines isometries, equivalence, orthogonal sums, anisotropic and positive definite forms. Section 2 diagonalizes quadratic forms over a field of characteristic not 2, gives the normal forms over algebraically closed fields and over $\mathbb{R}$, and proves Sylvester's law of inertia: the numbers of positive and of negative squares in a real diagonal form do not depend on the basis. The signature of $x^2 + y^2 - z^2$ is $(2, 1)$.

The chapter needs Chapter 5, mainly the orthogonal bases of its Section 2, and the matrices of Volume 7, Chapter 3. Chapter 7 uses this chapter directly: the Clifford algebra of a quadratic form $Q$ is generated by $M$ with the relation $v^2 = Q(v)$, and the polar of $Q$ appears in the relation $u v + v u = \operatorname{polar}_Q(u, v)$.
