# Algebras, star rings and continued fractions

This chapter collects three topics that later volumes use. Section 1 defines an algebra over a commutative ring $R$: a ring $A$ with a ring homomorphism $R \to A$ whose image is central. It proves that this agrees with the definition through a module structure, shows that every ring is a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra and every division ring of characteristic zero a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra, proves the universal property of $R[X]$ as an algebra, and describes the subalgebra generated by one element. It explains why Mathlib's class `Algebra` holds the scalar multiplication as data. Section 2 defines star rings, with complex conjugation, the conjugate transpose and quaternion conjugation as examples, and it studies self-adjoint and normal elements and star-ordered rings. Section 3 develops simple continued fractions: the algorithm, the recurrences for the convergents, the determinant identity, the finite expansions of rational numbers, and the error bound that makes the convergents tend to the number.

The chapter needs rings, homomorphisms, subrings and division rings from Chapters 1 to 3, the polynomial ring of Chapter 5, and the ordered fields and the floor function of Chapter 7. Section 1 uses modules only through the definition of a scalar multiplication. Sections 2 and 3 can be read in either order after Section 1.

Volume 7 on linear algebra uses algebras for matrices and endomorphisms, and the conjugate transpose of Section 2 for inner product spaces. Volume 9 on commutative algebra takes up algebras, adjoining elements and finitely generated algebras in full. Volume 16 builds C*-algebras on the star rings of Section 2. Continued fractions return in number theory, in the study of Pell's equation and of quadratic irrationals.
