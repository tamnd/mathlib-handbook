# Coxeter groups and further structures

This chapter closes the volume with structures that grow out of Lie theory but do not fit into the earlier chapters. Section 1 treats Coxeter groups, the groups with a presentation by involutions $s_i$ and the relations $(s_i s_j)^{m_{ij}} = 1$. It defines Coxeter matrices and Coxeter systems, proves the universal property and the braid relations, and studies the length function, its parity, reduced words, descents and reflections. It closes with the inversion sequence of a word and the proof that a reduced word has distinct inversions. The examples are the dihedral Weyl groups of $A_2$, $B_2$ and $G_2$ and the infinite dihedral group.

Section 2 treats four newer parts of the library. Lie-Rinehart algebras are Lie algebras that are also modules over a commutative algebra and act on it by derivations, as vector fields act on functions; the section proves that kernels are strict ideals and that quotients by strict ideals exist. Graded Lie algebras give derivations from additive maps on the grading group. The loop algebra of a Lie algebra with an invariant form carries a 2-cocycle, which defines the central extension behind the affine Kac-Moody algebras, and the section proves $d^2 = 0$ in low degree for this purpose. Vertex operators are formal series of operators, and the section proves that their composition is well defined.

Section 1 uses presented groups and permutations from Volume 5 and the Weyl groups of Chapter 7. Section 2 uses linear algebra and bilinear forms from Volumes 7 and 8 and the Lie algebras of Chapter 4. In the index of this edition the two sections are not linked: no declaration identifies the Weyl group of a root system with a Coxeter group.
