# Real analysis in one variable

This volume teaches the analysis of functions of one real variable and its form in Mathlib. It starts from limits of sequences and ends with Stirling's formula for $n!$. Each main theorem has a complete proof on paper: the intermediate value theorem, the extreme value theorem, the theorem of Heine-Cantor, the theorems of Rolle, Lagrange and Cauchy, the rule of l'Hôpital, Taylor's theorem with remainder, the irrationality of $e$ and Stirling's formula. Each chapter also computes: the limit $(1 + 1/n)^n \to e$, the bound $\pi > 3.14$, the minimum of $x^x$ at $x = 1/e$, and the first values of $n!$ against $\sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^n$.

Chapter 1 studies limits of sequences and series of real numbers: the algebra of limits, monotone sequences, the theorem of Bolzano-Weierstrass, Cauchy sequences and the standard tests for series. Chapter 2 studies limits of functions and continuity, and proves the intermediate value theorem, the extreme value theorem and uniform continuity on a closed bounded interval. Chapter 3 defines the derivative and proves the rules of differentiation, the mean value theorems and the rule of l'Hôpital. Chapter 4 proves Taylor's theorem and applies it to extrema, convexity and numerical bounds. Chapter 5 builds the exponential function, the logarithm and the real powers as Mathlib defines them. Chapter 6 does the same for the sine, the cosine and the number $\pi$, and proves that $e$ and $\pi$ are irrational. Chapter 7 introduces the comparison of functions with $O$, $o$ and $\sim$, and proves Stirling's formula.

The volume needs Volume 2 for the real numbers: their construction, the least upper bound property, the Archimedean property and the absolute value. It needs Volume 13 for the topology that Mathlib uses to state each limit: filters, the limit `Tendsto`, the neighbourhood filter `𝓝`, metric spaces, compactness and connectedness. A reader who knows these topics only for $\mathbb{R}$ can read the mathematical text of this volume without Volume 13. The Mathlib boxes need it.

Mathlib states almost every theorem of this volume in more generality than a course in one variable. A limit is a statement about filters, so one theorem covers the limit of a sequence, the limit at a point, the one-sided limits and the limits at infinity. The derivative is defined for functions from a normed field to a normed space, so the same lemma serves for real and for complex functions. The text names the general form, explains how to read it for $\mathbb{R}$, and gives the junk values that the library uses for total functions: the derivative of a function that is not differentiable is $0$, $\log 0 = 0$, $\log(-x) = \log x$ and $0^0 = 1$. The chapters are in the order of a first course, and each chapter uses the earlier ones. Volume 16 on normed spaces, Volume 17 on differential calculus in normed spaces and Volume 18 on measure and integration continue from here. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
