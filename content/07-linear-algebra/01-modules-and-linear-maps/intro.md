# Modules and linear maps

This chapter gives the language of the volume. Section 1 defines modules over a ring, with vector spaces as the case of a field, and studies submodules, spans and the lattice of submodules. Section 2 defines linear maps, their kernels and ranges, linear equivalences and the ring of endomorphisms. Section 3 builds the quotient of a module by a submodule and proves the three isomorphism theorems. Sections 4 to 7 describe the library around these notions. Section 4 lists the module structures on the standard types, such as functions, products, opposites and ulifts. Section 5 treats the images, preimages and restrictions of scalars of submodules. Section 6 gives the tools that make new linear equivalences and linear maps from old ones. Section 7 covers the pointwise products of submodules, the congruences of modules, bimodules and graded modules. Section 8 treats the product of two modules and the product of a family of modules, with graphs of linear maps and the lemma of Goursat. Section 9 relates spans to linear combinations and describes the module of functions of finite support. Section 10 treats complements of submodules, projections and idempotents, the fixed vectors of an endomorphism, and partially defined linear maps.

The chapter needs the groups, subgroups and quotient groups of Volume 5 and the lattices of Volume 4. A module is an abelian group with an action of a ring, so most proofs follow the proofs for groups with one more line for the scalars. Chapter 2 uses spans to define bases, Chapter 3 encodes linear maps as matrices, and Chapter 7 applies quotients to modules over the integers.
