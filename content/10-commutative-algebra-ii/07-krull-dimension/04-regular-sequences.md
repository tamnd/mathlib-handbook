## Regular elements and regular sequences

### Overview

An element $r$ of $R$ is regular on a module $M$ when multiplication by $r$ is injective on $M$. A sequence $r_1, \dots, r_n$ is regular on $M$ when each $r_i$ is regular on $M/(r_1, \dots, r_{i-1})M$ and the last quotient is not zero. Regular sequences are the algebraic form of a complete intersection. The coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_n$ of $k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ are the main example. The length of a maximal regular sequence in the maximal ideal is the depth, and the regular local rings of Section 2 are the local rings with a regular sequence that generates the maximal ideal.

This section describes the quotient $M/rM$, the criteria for a regular element, the two classes for sequences, the permutation theorem for local rings, the behavior under flat maps, and the projective dimension of the quotient by a regular sequence.

### The library around it

#### The quotient by one element

The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.QuotSMulTop` defines `QuotSMulTop r M` as the quotient $M/rM$, written `M ⧸ r • ⊤`. It is $R/(r) \otimes_R M$, by `QuotSMulTop.equivQuotTensor`, and $M \otimes_R R/(r)$, by `QuotSMulTop.equivTensorQuot`. A linear map $f$ induces `QuotSMulTop.map r f`, and this construction is a right exact functor: `QuotSMulTop.map_id`, `QuotSMulTop.map_comp`, `QuotSMulTop.map_surjective` and `QuotSMulTop.map_exact`. The element $r$ kills the quotient, `QuotSMulTop.mem_annihilator`. Base change commutes with the quotient, `QuotSMulTop.algebraMapTensorEquivTensorQuotSMulTop`. Over a local ring, `nontrivial_quotSMulTop_of_mem_maximalIdeal` says that $M/rM \ne 0$ for a nonzero finitely generated $M$ and $r \in \mathfrak m$, by Nakayama's lemma.

#### Regular elements

The predicate `IsSMulRegular M r` says that $m \mapsto rm$ is injective. The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Regular.IsSMulRegular` relates it to submodules and quotients.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `isSMulRegular_iff_ker_lsmul_eq_bot` | $r$ is regular if and only if the kernel of $r \cdot$ is $0$ |
| `IsSMulRegular.submodule` | a regular element is regular on each submodule |
| `isSMulRegular_quotient_iff_mem_of_smul_mem` | $r$ is regular on $M/N$ if and only if $rx \in N$ gives $x \in N$ |
| `isSMulRegular_of_isSMulRegular_on_submodule_on_quotient` | regular on $N$ and on $M/N$ gives regular on $M$ |
| `isSMulRegular_of_range_eq_ker` | the same for a short exact sequence |
| `IsSMulRegular.lTensor` | regularity passes to $M \otimes M'$ from $M'$ |
| `LinearEquiv.isSMulRegular_congr` | regularity is invariant under isomorphism |

**Theorem.** Let $R$ be Noetherian. The elements that are not regular on $M$ are the elements of the union of the associated primes of $M$. This is `biUnion_associatedPrimes_eq_compl_regular`.

*Proof sketch.* If $r \in \mathfrak p = \operatorname{Ann}(m)$ for an associated prime, then $rm = 0$ with $m \ne 0$. Conversely, let $rm = 0$ with $m \ne 0$. The set of annihilators of nonzero elements that contain $\operatorname{Ann}(m)$ has a maximal element since $R$ is Noetherian. A maximal annihilator is prime, so it is an associated prime that contains $r$. $\square$

The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Regular.LinearMap` gives a test with maps: `IsSMulRegular.subsingleton_linearMap_iff` says that $\operatorname{Hom}(N, M) = 0$ if and only if some element of the annihilator of $N$ is regular on $M$, for $N$ and $M$ finitely generated over a Noetherian ring. This is the first step of the description of depth by Ext. The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Regular.Category` gives the short complex $M \xrightarrow{r} M \to M/rM$ in `ModuleCat R`, `ModuleCat.smulShortComplex`, and `IsSMulRegular.smulShortComplex_shortExact` says that it is short exact for a regular $r$.

#### Regular sequences

The ideal generated by a list is `Ideal.ofList rs`, with `Ideal.ofList_cons` and `Ideal.ofList_append`. The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Regular.RegularSequence` defines two structures.

1. `RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular M rs` says that each $r_i$ is regular on $M/(r_1, \dots, r_{i-1})M$, by `RingTheory.Sequence.isWeaklyRegular_iff`.
2. `RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular M rs` adds the condition $M \ne (r_1, \dots, r_n)M$, by `RingTheory.Sequence.isRegular_iff`.

The inductive form is the usual tool. `RingTheory.Sequence.isRegular_cons_iff` says that $r :: rs$ is regular on $M$ if and only if $r$ is regular on $M$ and $rs$ is regular on $M/rM$. The same holds for weak regularity, `RingTheory.Sequence.isWeaklyRegular_cons_iff`, and for a concatenation, `RingTheory.Sequence.isWeaklyRegular_append_iff`. The recursors `RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular.recIterModByRegular` and `RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular.recIterModByRegular` prove statements by induction on the sequence while passing to $M/rM$.

**Theorem.** Let $R$ be a Noetherian local ring and $M$ a finitely generated module. A permutation of a regular sequence on $M$ is regular. This is `IsLocalRing.isRegular_of_perm`.

*Proof sketch.* It is enough to swap two adjacent elements, and after passing to a quotient, the first two. So let $r, s$ be regular on $M$ in this order. Then $s$ is regular on $M$: if $sm = 0$, then $sm \in rM$, so $m = rm'$ with $srm' = 0$, so $sm' = 0$. Repeat with $m'$. This gives $m \in r^n M$ for all $n$, and $m = 0$ by the Krull intersection theorem. A similar argument shows that $r$ is regular on $M/sM$. $\square$

The general form is `RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular.of_perm_of_subset_jacobson_annihilator`, for sequences in the Jacobson radical of the annihilator. Over a local ring, for a sequence in the maximal ideal and a nonzero finitely generated module, weakly regular and regular are the same, `IsLocalRing.isRegular_iff_isWeaklyRegular_of_subset_maximalIdeal`.

#### Change of rings

The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Regular.Flat` proves that regular sequences are stable under flat maps.

1. `RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular.of_flat`: a weakly regular sequence on $R$ maps to a weakly regular sequence on a flat $R$-algebra $S$.
2. `RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular.of_faithfullyFlat`: the same for regular sequences and a faithfully flat $S$.
3. `RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular.of_isLocalization`: localization preserves weak regularity.
4. `RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular.isRegular_of_isLocalization_of_mem`: a weakly regular sequence in a prime $\mathfrak p$ is regular in $R_{\mathfrak p}$.

The forms `RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular.of_flat_of_isBaseChange` and `RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular.of_isLocalizedModule` treat modules.

#### Freeness and projective dimension

The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Regular.Free` has `Module.free_quotSMulTop_iff_free`: for a finitely presented $M$ and an element $x$ of the Jacobson radical that is regular on $M$, the module $M/xM$ is free over $R/(x)$ if and only if $M$ is free over $R$.

The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Regular.ProjectiveDimension` computes projective dimensions over a Noetherian local ring. For $x \in \mathfrak m$ regular on a finitely generated $M$, `ModuleCat.projectiveDimension_quotSMulTop_eq_succ_of_isSMulRegular` says that $\operatorname{pd}(M/xM) = \operatorname{pd}(M) + 1$. By induction, `ModuleCat.projectiveDimension_quotient_eq_length` says that $\operatorname{pd}(R/(r_1, \dots, r_n)) = n$ for a regular sequence on $R$. The criterion `ModuleCat.hasProjectiveDimensionLT_of_forall_finite` reduces a bound on projective dimension to the vanishing of Ext into finitely generated modules.

#### Common errors

1. `RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular` takes a list, not a set. Order matters in general, and permutation needs the local hypotheses of `IsLocalRing.isRegular_of_perm`.
2. `RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular M rs` holds for a sequence that contains a unit. Use `IsRegular` to exclude $M = (r_1, \dots, r_n)M$.
3. `QuotSMulTop r M` is a quotient by the submodule `r • ⊤`, not by the ideal $(r)$ of $R$. It is an $R$-module, and an $R/(r)$-module only through `QuotSMulTop.equivQuotTensor`.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $x, y$ is a regular sequence on $k[x, y]$.
2. (A) Show that $x, 1 - x$ is weakly regular on $k[x]$ but not regular.
3. (B) Let $R = k[x, y, z]$. Show that $x, y(1 - x), z(1 - x)$ is regular but $y(1 - x), z(1 - x), x$ is not regular. Explain why this does not contradict `IsLocalRing.isRegular_of_perm`.
4. (B) Show that the zero divisors of $k[x, y]/(xy)$ are the elements of $(x) \cup (y)$.
5. (B) Prove `isSMulRegular_of_isSMulRegular_on_submodule_on_quotient` with the snake lemma or by hand.
6. (C) Let $(R, \mathfrak m)$ be a Noetherian local ring and $x_1, \dots, x_n$ a regular sequence in $\mathfrak m$. Show that $\dim R/(x_1, \dots, x_n) = \dim R - n$, with the result of Section 2.
