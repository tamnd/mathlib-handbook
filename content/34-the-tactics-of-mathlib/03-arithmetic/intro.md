# Arithmetic

This chapter is about the tactics that decide a class of arithmetic goals: `linarith` and `nlinarith` for the inequalities of an ordered field, `omega` for the linear arithmetic of `ℤ` and `ℕ`, and `decide` for a closed proposition with a decidable instance. Inside its class, each tactic always succeeds. Outside it, the tactic fails, and the message often shows a point that satisfies the facts that the tactic sees. The sections give the class of each tactic and show how to read this point.

The first section proves the theorem of Fourier and Motzkin: a system of linear inequalities has no solution if and only if a combination of the facts with coefficients that are not negative gives $0 < 0$. This combination is the certificate that `linarith` finds and that `ring` checks. The section also explains the products that `nlinarith` adds, and the hints that it needs. The second section proves the division theorem and the tightening theorem that `omega` uses over the integers, and the theorem of the bounded quantifier that makes `decide` work on statements such as $\forall n < 10,\ P(n)$.

The chapter needs Chapter 2, because `linarith` uses the normal form of `ring`, and a hint for `nlinarith` is often a square from a proof by `ring`. It prepares Chapter 4, where `grind` has its own procedures for linear arithmetic, and Chapter 5, where a proof by induction ends with a step of `linarith` or `omega`.
