# Group actions

A group action lets a group move the points of a set. Section 1 defines actions, orbits and stabilizers, proves the orbit-stabilizer theorem and the theorem of Cayley, and studies the action on cosets. Section 2 applies orbit-stabilizer to the action by conjugation: the class equation, the fixed point congruence for $p$-groups, the nontrivial center of a $p$-group, the commutativity of groups of order $p^2$, and the lemma of Burnside. Section 3 describes torsors, the free and transitive actions, with the quotient of two points, point reflections and torsors of products. Section 4 introduces shelves, racks and quandles, the structures that keep only the conjugation of a group, with the enveloping group of a rack. Section 5 describes equivariant maps, with their versions that respect an additive, a multiplicative or a ring structure, and sub-actions with their closures and products. Section 6 describes blocks and block systems, the correspondence between blocks that contain a point and subgroups that contain its stabilizer, primitive and quasiprimitive actions, and the Iwasawa criterion for simplicity. Section 7 describes multiply transitive and multiply primitive actions, the actions of stabilizers and fixing subgroups on the complement, the theorems of Jordan on primitive groups that contain a transposition or a 3-cycle, and the action on subsets of a fixed size. Section 8 describes the fixed points of one element, fixing subgroups and their Galois connection with fixed points, the period of a point, and the actions by conjugation, by the iterates of a map and through the domain of a function. Section 9 describes the actions that an action induces on functions, products, sums, `Option`, the opposite type, the units and the type tags, and the transfer of an action along an equivalence. Section 10 describes an action as a homomorphism into the endomorphism monoid or the permutation group, the change of the acting monoid, faithful and pretransitive actions, and equidecompositions.

The chapter needs Chapters 4 and 5. The fixed point congruence is the main tool of the proofs of Cauchy and Sylow in Chapter 8, and Chapter 7 uses the class equation for $S_n$.
