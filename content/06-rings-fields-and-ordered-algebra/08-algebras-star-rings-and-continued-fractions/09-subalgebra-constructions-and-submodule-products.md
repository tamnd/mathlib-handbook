## Constructions of subalgebras and products of submodules

### Overview

Section 1 defines subalgebras and the subalgebra generated by a set. This section collects the constructions on subalgebras that later volumes use: products and powers of submodules of an algebra, centralizers, unions of chains, subalgebras of products, of matrices and of the opposite algebra, and restriction of scalars. The last two modules give a degree formula for the join of two subalgebras and a criterion for a subalgebra lattice with only two elements.

The central object is the product $MN$ of two $R$-submodules $M, N$ of an $R$-algebra $A$. It is the submodule spanned by the products $mn$. With this product the submodules of $A$ form a semiring, and the ideals of a commutative ring are a part of it. Volume 9 uses this semiring for fractional ideals and for the ideal theory of Dedekind domains. The section needs Sections 1 and 2 and the ideals of Chapter 2.

### The library around it

#### The semiring of submodules

Let $A$ be an $R$-algebra. The instance `Submodule.mul` defines $MN = \operatorname{span}_R \{ mn : m \in M, n \in N \}$. The membership lemma `Submodule.mul_mem_mul` says $m \in M$, $n \in N$ give $mn \in MN$. The theorem `Submodule.mul_le` characterizes the product by its universal property: $MN \leq P$ exactly when $mn \in P$ for all $m \in M$ and $n \in N$. Proof: $P$ is a submodule, so it contains the span of a set exactly when it contains the set. The induction principle `Submodule.mul_induction_on` reduces a property of an element of $MN$ to products and sums.

The unit of this product is the submodule `1`, the image of $R$ in $A$. The theorem `Submodule.mem_one` says $x \in 1$ exactly when $x = \operatorname{algebraMap}(y)$ for some $y \in R$, and `Submodule.one_eq_span` says $1 = R \cdot 1$. Products of spans are spans of products: `Submodule.span_mul_span` says $\operatorname{span}(S) \operatorname{span}(T) = \operatorname{span}(ST)$. Proof: each side is contained in the other by `Submodule.mul_le`, since $s t \in ST$ for $s \in S$, $t \in T$, and the span is bilinear. In the same way `Submodule.pow_eq_span_pow_set` says $M^n = \operatorname{span}(M^n)$, with the set power on the right.

The instance `Submodule.idemSemiring` makes the submodules an idempotent semiring: the sum is the join $M \sqcup N$, which satisfies $M \sqcup M = M$. The product distributes over joins, by `Submodule.mul_sup` and `Submodule.sup_mul`, and over arbitrary suprema, by `Submodule.mul_iSup` and `Submodule.iSup_mul`. For a commutative algebra, `Submodule.instIdemCommSemiring` and `Submodule.mul_comm` make it commutative. With no zero divisors, `Submodule.mul_eq_bot` says $MN = 0$ exactly when $M = 0$ or $N = 0$. The ring homomorphism `Submodule.span.ringHom` sends a set to its span; `Submodule.mapHom f` sends a submodule to its image under an algebra map, which respects products by `Submodule.map_mul`.

In a commutative algebra there is also a quotient. The submodule $I / J$ is the set of $x$ with $xJ \subseteq I$. The theorem `Submodule.mem_div_iff_forall_mul_mem` states this, and `Submodule.le_div_iff_mul_le` is the adjunction $K \leq I/J \iff KJ \leq I$. The proof of the adjunction is the definition read twice: $K \leq I / J$ means $kj \in I$ for all $k \in K$, $j \in J$, which is $KJ \leq I$ by `Submodule.mul_le`. This quotient gives the inverse of a fractional ideal in Volume 9.

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Algebra.Operations` has more families. The pointwise action of a scalar, $x \cdot M$, has the lemmas `Submodule.smul_one_eq_span` ($x \cdot 1 = R x$) and `Submodule.mul_mem_smul_iff` (cancellation by a non-zero-divisor). The equivalence `Submodule.equivOpposite` identifies the submodules of $A^{\mathrm{op}}$ with the opposite semiring of submodules of $A$. The theorem `Submodule.mem_span_mul_finite_of_mem_mul` says that an element of $PQ$ lies in the product of two finite subsets. The theorem `Submodule.top_mul_eq_top_of_mul_eq_one` is a small fact for invertible submodules.

#### Subalgebras as submodules

A subalgebra $S$ is a submodule that contains $1$ and is closed under products. The theorem `Subalgebra.mul_toSubmodule` says that for subalgebras $S$ and $T$ of a commutative algebra, the product of the underlying submodules is the submodule of the join $S \sqcup T$. Proof: $ST \subseteq S \sqcup T$ because the join is closed under products. Conversely $ST$ contains $S = S \cdot 1$ and $T = 1 \cdot T$, and it is closed under products in the commutative case, since $(s t)(s' t') = (s s')(t t')$. So it is a subalgebra that contains $S$ and $T$. The case $S = T$ gives `Subalgebra.isIdempotentElem_toSubmodule`: $SS = S$.

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Algebra.Subalgebra.Pointwise` also gives the action of a group on subalgebras, `Subalgebra.pointwiseMulAction`. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Algebra.Subalgebra.Operations` gives the fixed points of a group action as a subalgebra, `FixedPoints.subalgebra`, and the fact `AlgHom.ker_rangeRestrict` that the corestriction of $f$ to its range has the kernel of $f$. The theorem `Subalgebra.mem_of_span_eq_top_of_smul_pow_mem` is a local criterion for membership: if $s$ generates the unit ideal and $r^n x \in S'$ for each $r \in s$, then $x \in S'$.

#### Centralizers and directed unions

The centralizer of a set $s$ is the subalgebra of elements that commute with each element of $s$. The theorem `Subalgebra.le_centralizer_iff` says $S \leq C(T) \iff T \leq C(S)$; both sides say that each element of $S$ commutes with each element of $T$. The theorem `Subalgebra.centralizer_coe_sup` says $C(S \sqcup T) = C(S) \sqcap C(T)$. Proof: an element commutes with $S \sqcup T$ exactly when it commutes with $S$ and with $T$, because the elements that commute with a fixed $x$ form a subalgebra.

For tensor products the module computes centralizers of the two factors. `Subalgebra.centralizer_tensorProduct_eq_center_tensorProduct_right` says that, for $A$ free over $R$, the centralizer of $R \otimes B$ in $A \otimes_R B$ is $A \otimes Z(B)$. The theorem `Subalgebra.centralizer_coe_map_includeLeft_eq_center_tensorProduct` is a variant in terms of `Algebra.TensorProduct.includeLeft`. These are the first steps of the double centralizer theorem for central simple algebras.

The union of a directed family of subalgebras is a subalgebra. `Subalgebra.coe_iSup_of_directed` says that the supremum of a directed family is the union of its members as a set. `Subalgebra.iSupLift` builds an algebra map on the supremum from compatible maps on the members, and the lemmas `Subalgebra.iSupLift_inclusion` and `Subalgebra.iSupLift_of_mem` give its values. The instance `Subalgebra.instIsMulCommutative_iSup` says that a directed union of commutative subalgebras is commutative.

#### Products, matrices and opposites

`Subalgebra.prod S T` is the subalgebra $S \times T$ of $A \times B$, and `Subalgebra.pi s S` is the product $\prod_{i \in s} S_i$ in $\prod_i A_i$, with no condition at $i \notin s$. The centers behave as expected: `Subalgebra.center_prod` says $Z(A \times B) = Z(A) \times Z(B)$, and `Subalgebra.center_pi` says $Z(\prod_i A_i) = \prod_i Z(A_i)$. Proof of the second: a family commutes with all families exactly when it commutes in each coordinate, since the family with one nonzero coordinate $a$ tests the coordinate $i$ against $a$. `AlgHom.range_prodMap` says that the range of $f \times g$ is the product of the ranges. `Subalgebra.matrix S` is the subalgebra $M_n(S)$ of $M_n(A)$, the matrices with entries in $S$.

The opposite algebra has its own subalgebras. `Subalgebra.op S` is the subalgebra of $A^{\mathrm{op}}$ with the same elements, and `Subalgebra.mem_op` says $x \in S^{\mathrm{op}} \iff \operatorname{unop}(x) \in S$. The order isomorphism `Subalgebra.opEquiv` says that $S \mapsto S^{\mathrm{op}}$ is a bijection of lattices, and so it commutes with joins, meets and the bounds (`Subalgebra.op_sup`, `Subalgebra.op_inf`, `Subalgebra.op_bot`). The theorem `Subalgebra.op_adjoin` says that the opposite of the subalgebra generated by $s$ is the subalgebra generated by the same elements in $A^{\mathrm{op}}$. Proof: $\operatorname{op}$ is an order isomorphism, so it sends the least subalgebra that contains $s$ to the least subalgebra that contains the image of $s$. `Subalgebra.algEquivOpMop` identifies $S$ with the opposite of $S^{\mathrm{op}}$.

#### Towers and orders

In a tower $R \to S \to A$, an $S$-subalgebra is an $R$-subalgebra. `Subalgebra.restrictScalars R U` is this subalgebra, with the same elements by `Subalgebra.coe_restrictScalars`; `Subalgebra.restrictScalars_injective` says that it determines $U$. `Subalgebra.ofRestrictScalars` turns a map on the restricted subalgebra into a map of $S$-algebras. The instance `IsScalarTower.subalgebra` makes $S_0 \to S \to A$ a tower for a subalgebra $S_0$ of $S$.

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Algebra.Subalgebra.Order` has two instances. A subalgebra of an ordered ring is an ordered ring, by `Subalgebra.toIsOrderedRing`, and in the same way for a strict ordered ring by `Subalgebra.toIsStrictOrderedRing`. The order is the restriction of the order of $A$.

#### Degrees and simple lattices

Let $A$ and $B$ be subalgebras of an $R$-algebra. The theorem `Subalgebra.finrank_sup_eq_finrank_left_mul_finrank_of_free` is the tower law for their join: if $A$ is free over $R$ and the subalgebra $A[B]$ generated by $B$ over $A$ is free over $A$, then $[A \sqcup B : R] = [A : R] \cdot [A[B] : A]$. The proof uses that $A \sqcup B$ and $A[B]$ have the same elements, and the tower law for free modules. The corollary `Subalgebra.finrank_left_dvd_finrank_sup_of_free` says $[A : R]$ divides $[A \sqcup B : R]$. The `rank` forms hold for infinite ranks.

The theorem `Subalgebra.isSimpleOrder_of_finrank_prime` says that a domain $A$ of prime dimension $p$ over a field $F$ has only two subalgebras, $F$ and $A$. Proof: a subalgebra $S$ is a domain of finite dimension over $F$, so it is a field. The tower law gives $[A : F] = [A : S][S : F]$, so $[S : F]$ is $1$ or $p$. In the first case $S = F$ and in the second case $S = A$. Field theory uses this for extensions of prime degree.

#### Common errors

1. The sum of two submodules in the semiring `Submodule R A` is the join $M \sqcup N$, not a set of sums written with `+` on elements. A lemma about $M + N$ is a lemma about $M \sqcup N$.
2. `Subalgebra.mul_toSubmodule` and the quotient $I / J$ need a commutative algebra. In a noncommutative algebra the product of two subalgebras need not be a subalgebra.
3. `Subalgebra.pi s S` constrains only the coordinates in $s$. To get the full product, use `Set.univ` as the first argument, as in `Subalgebra.center_pi`.

### Exercises

1. (A) Prove that $M \cdot 1 = M$ for an $R$-submodule $M$ of $A$, with `Submodule.mul_le` and `Submodule.mem_one`.
2. (A) Show that $(\mathbb{Z} \cdot 2)(\mathbb{Z} \cdot 3) = \mathbb{Z} \cdot 6$ as submodules of $\mathbb{Q}$, with `Submodule.span_mul_span`.
3. (B) In $\mathbb{Q}$ as a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra, compute $(\mathbb{Z} \cdot 1) / (\mathbb{Z} \cdot 2)$ and $(\mathbb{Z} \cdot 2) / (\mathbb{Z} \cdot 4)$.
4. (B) Prove that the centralizer of the center of $A$ is $A$, and that the center of the centralizer of a set $s$ contains $s$ when $s$ is commutative.
5. (B) Prove that $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2})$ has no subfields other than $\mathbb{Q}$ and itself, by `Subalgebra.isSimpleOrder_of_finrank_prime`.
6. (C) Prove that the product $MN$ of two finitely generated submodules is finitely generated, with a bound on the number of generators in terms of those of $M$ and $N$.
