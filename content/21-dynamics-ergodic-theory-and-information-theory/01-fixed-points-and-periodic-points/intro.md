# Fixed points and periodic points

A discrete dynamical system is a set $X$ with a map $f : X \to X$, studied through its iterates $f^{[n]}$. The simplest orbits are the ones that do not move: the fixed points, with $f(x) = x$, and the periodic points, with $f^{[n]}(x) = x$ for some $n \ge 1$. This chapter studies them in Mathlib, where they live in the namespace `Function` and need no structure on $X$ at all.

Section 1 defines fixed points, shows how semiconjugacies and commuting maps carry fixed points to fixed points, proves that a limit of iterates of a continuous map is a fixed point, and proves the Banach contraction principle. It ends with the fixed support of a map and the Prüfer lemma. Section 2 defines periodic points and the minimal period, proves that the periods of a point are exactly the multiples of its minimal period, computes the period of an iterate and of a product, and studies maps of a finite set. The doubling map of the circle and the order of a group element are the main examples. Section 3 turns to Newton's map, a fixed point method for roots of polynomials. Mathlib has it in the algebraic form: from an approximate root with a nilpotent defect, the iteration reaches the unique nearby root in finitely many steps, which gives Hensel's lemma and the Jordan-Chevalley decomposition.

The chapter needs Volume 13 for topology and metric spaces, Volume 5 for orders of group elements and group actions, and Volume 6 for polynomials. Later chapters use its language throughout: flows in Chapter 2, rotation numbers in Chapter 4 and entropy in Chapter 7.
