# Sums of squares and Diophantine equations

A Diophantine equation is a polynomial equation whose solutions are sought in the integers. This chapter solves three classical families of them. Section 1 finds all integer solutions of $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$, the Pythagorean triples, and uses the classification to prove by descent that $x^4 + y^4 = z^2$ has no solution in nonzero integers, which gives Fermat's last theorem for the exponent $4$. Section 2 decides which numbers are sums of two squares and proves Lagrange's theorem that every natural number is a sum of four squares. Section 3 solves Pell's equation $x^2 - d y^2 = 1$: it proves that a nontrivial solution exists for every positive nonsquare $d$ and that all solutions are powers of a fundamental one. Section 4 describes the Diophantine sets of the library and the proof that the power function is Diophantine, a key step towards Hilbert's tenth problem. Section 5 describes elliptic divisibility sequences over a commutative ring and the normalised sequence `normEDS`.

The chapter needs the divisibility and the primes of Volume 2, Chapter 2, and the Gaussian integers of Volume 6, Chapter 6, where the theorem of Fermat on primes that are sums of two squares is proved. It does not prove that theorem again. Chapter 8 returns to Pell's equation: its solutions are the units of a real quadratic field, and Dirichlet's unit theorem generalizes Section 3.
