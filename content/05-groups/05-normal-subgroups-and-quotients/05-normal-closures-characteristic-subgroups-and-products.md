## Normal closures, characteristic subgroups and products of subgroups

### Overview

Section 1 of this chapter defines a normal subgroup and the quotient by it. This section collects the constructions around normality that the library keeps in `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Basic`. A set $s$ in a group $G$ lies in a smallest normal subgroup, its normal closure. A subgroup $H$ contains a largest normal subgroup of $G$, its normal core. The normalizer of $H$ is the largest subgroup in which $H$ is normal. A characteristic subgroup is fixed by every automorphism, and so it is normal. The same module builds the product $H \times K$ of two subgroups and the product of a family of subgroups inside a product of groups.

The other five modules are small. `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Finite` compares the cardinalities of subgroups and puts finite products into a subgroup. `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Order` gives the modular law for the subgroups of a commutative group and the maximal subgroups. `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.ZPowers.Lemmas` computes the subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}$ that two integers generate. `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Finsupp` describes the closure of a set in a commutative group by finite products of powers, and `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Even` defines the subgroup of squares. A reader needs these results to build normal subgroups from generators, as in a presentation of a group, and to move between subgroups of a product and products of subgroups.

### The library around it

#### Normal closures and conjugates

For a set $s \subseteq G$, the set `Group.conjugatesOfSet s` holds all conjugates $g a g^{-1}$ with $a \in s$; the membership lemma `Group.mem_conjugatesOfSet_iff` states that $x$ is in it exactly when `IsConj a x` for some $a \in s$. The normal closure `Subgroup.normalClosure s` is the subgroup generated by this set. The instance `Subgroup.normalClosure_normal` makes it normal, and `Subgroup.subset_normalClosure` states $s \subseteq \langle\langle s \rangle\rangle$.

**Theorem (Normal closure).** Let $N$ be a normal subgroup of $G$ with $s \subseteq N$. Then $\langle\langle s \rangle\rangle \le N$.

*Proof.* The subgroup $\langle\langle s \rangle\rangle$ is generated by the conjugates $g a g^{-1}$ with $a \in s$. Each such $a$ lies in $N$, and $N$ is normal, so $g a g^{-1} \in N$. A subgroup that contains a generating set contains the subgroup it generates. $\square$

This is `Subgroup.normalClosure_le_normal`, and `Subgroup.normalClosure_subset_iff` is the form with an equivalence. So the normal closure is the meet of all normal subgroups that contain $s$, which is `Subgroup.normalClosure_eq_iInf`. The order lemmas follow the closure pattern: `Subgroup.normalClosure_mono`, `Subgroup.normalClosure_idempotent`, `Subgroup.normalClosure_union`, `Subgroup.normalClosure_empty`, and `Subgroup.normalClosure_eq_self` for a normal subgroup. In a commutative group every subgroup is normal, and `Subgroup.normalClosure_eq_closure` turns the normal closure into the closure. Under a surjective homomorphism $f$ the image of a normal closure is the normal closure of the image, by `Subgroup.map_normalClosure`. The normal closure of the closure is the normal closure, by `Subgroup.normalClosure_closure_eq_normalClosure`.

#### The normal core

The normal core `Subgroup.normalCore H` is the dual object: the largest normal subgroup of $G$ inside $H$. On paper it is $\bigcap_{g \in G} g H g^{-1}$, and the library proves this description as `Subgroup.normalCore_eq_iInf_comap_conj`. The basic facts are `Subgroup.normalCore_le`, the instance `Subgroup.normalCore_normal`, and `Subgroup.normalCore_eq_self` for a normal $H$.

**Theorem (Normal core).** Let $N$ be a normal subgroup of $G$. Then $N \le \operatorname{core}(H)$ if and only if $N \le H$.

*Proof.* If $N \le \operatorname{core}(H)$, then $N \le H$ because the core lies in $H$. Conversely, let $N \le H$ and $g \in G$. Since $N$ is normal, $N = g N g^{-1} \le g H g^{-1}$. So $N$ lies in each conjugate of $H$, and so in their intersection. $\square$

This is `Subgroup.normal_le_normalCore`. The core of $H$ is the kernel of the action of $G$ on the left cosets of $H$. So the core of a subgroup of finite index has finite index.

#### Normalizers

The normalizer `Subgroup.normalizer` is defined in `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Defs` for a set. This module gives its properties for a subgroup $H$. The lemma `Subgroup.le_normalizer_iff` states that $K \le N_G(H)$ if and only if $k h k^{-1} \in H$ for all $k \in K$ and $h \in H$. The instance `Subgroup.normal_in_normalizer` says that $H$ is normal in its normalizer, in the form `(H.subgroupOf (normalizer H)).Normal`, and `Subgroup.normal_subgroupOf_iff_le_normalizer` states that for $H \le K$, the subgroup $H$ is normal in $K$ exactly when $K \le N_G(H)$. So `Subgroup.maximal_normal_subgroupOf_normalizer` says that the normalizer is the largest subgroup in which $H$ is normal.

**Theorem.** A subgroup $H$ is normal if and only if $N_G(H) = G$.

*Proof.* The normalizer is the set of $g$ with $g H g^{-1} = H$. The subgroup $H$ is normal exactly when every $g$ has this property, that is, when the normalizer is all of $G$. $\square$

This is `Subgroup.normalizer_eq_top_iff`, and `Subgroup.normalizer_eq_top` is the direction that instance search uses. The normalizer moves well under maps: `Subgroup.le_normalizer_comap`, `Subgroup.comap_normalizer_eq_of_surjective`, `Subgroup.map_equiv_normalizer_eq` for an isomorphism, and `Subgroup.normalizer_le_normalizer_closure` for the closure of a set. The predicate `NormalizerCondition G` says that every proper subgroup is strictly smaller than its normalizer. The lemma `normalizerCondition_iff_only_full_group_self_normalizing` restates it: the only subgroup equal to its normalizer is $G$. Under this condition a maximal subgroup is normal, by `Subgroup.NormalizerCondition.normal_of_coatom` in the module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Order`. Nilpotent groups satisfy the normalizer condition.

The module also proves the stability of normality: `Subgroup.normal_comap` and `Subgroup.Normal.map` for a surjective map, `MulEquiv.normal_map_iff` for an isomorphism, `Subgroup.normal_inf_normal`, the instance `Subgroup.normal_subgroupOf` (a normal subgroup of $G$ meets each subgroup $H$ in a normal subgroup of $H$), and `Subgroup.normal_iff_map_conj_eq` with the inner automorphisms `MulAut.conj`.

**Theorem (Disjoint normal subgroups commute).** Let $H_1$ and $H_2$ be normal subgroups of $G$ with $H_1 \cap H_2 = 1$. Then $x y = y x$ for all $x \in H_1$ and $y \in H_2$.

*Proof.* Put $c = x y x^{-1} y^{-1}$. Write $c = x \cdot (y x^{-1} y^{-1})$: the second factor is a conjugate of $x^{-1}$, so it lies in $H_1$, and $c \in H_1$. Write $c = (x y x^{-1}) \cdot y^{-1}$: the first factor is a conjugate of $y$, so $c \in H_2$. Hence $c = 1$, and $x y = y x$. $\square$

The Lean name is `Subgroup.commute_of_normal_of_disjoint`, and the hypothesis is `Disjoint H₁ H₂` in the lattice of subgroups. This is the first step of the theorem that a group with two such subgroups that generate it is their direct product.

#### Characteristic subgroups

The class `Subgroup.Characteristic H` states that $\varphi(H) = H$ for every automorphism $\varphi$ of $G$. The library gives six equivalent forms with the names `Subgroup.characteristic_iff_comap_eq`, `Subgroup.characteristic_iff_comap_le`, `Subgroup.characteristic_iff_le_comap`, `Subgroup.characteristic_iff_map_eq`, `Subgroup.characteristic_iff_map_le` and `Subgroup.characteristic_iff_le_map`. The inner automorphisms are automorphisms, so the instance `Subgroup.normal_of_characteristic` makes every characteristic subgroup normal. The instances `Subgroup.botCharacteristic`, `Subgroup.topCharacteristic`, `Subgroup.characteristic_inf`, `Subgroup.characteristic_sup` and their forms for families keep the lattice operations inside the class. For a characteristic $H$, the map `MulAut.characteristic` restricts each automorphism of $G$ to an automorphism of $H$.

**Theorem (Transitivity).** Let $H$ be characteristic in $G$ and let $K$ be characteristic in $H$. Then $K$ is characteristic in $G$.

*Proof.* Let $\varphi$ be an automorphism of $G$. Since $\varphi(H) = H$, the restriction of $\varphi$ is an automorphism $\psi$ of $H$. Since $K$ is characteristic in $H$, $\psi(K) = K$, so $\varphi(K) = K$. $\square$

In Lean, $K$ is a subgroup of the subtype `↥H`, and the instance `Subgroup.characteristic_of_characteristic_of_characteristic` gives the class for its image `Subgroup.map H.subtype K`. The same argument shows that a characteristic subgroup of a normal subgroup is normal. The analogous statement for normal in normal is false: normality is not transitive.

#### Products of subgroups

For $H \le G$ and $K \le N$, the subgroup `Subgroup.prod H K` of $G \times N$ is $H \times K$. Its membership lemma `Subgroup.mem_prod` states $p \in H \times K$ if and only if $p_1 \in H$ and $p_2 \in K$, and `Subgroup.prodEquiv` is the isomorphism `↥(H.prod K) ≃* ↥H × ↥K`. The lemmas `Subgroup.prod_mono`, `Subgroup.top_prod_top`, `Subgroup.bot_prod_bot`, `Subgroup.prod_eq_bot_iff` and `Subgroup.prod_le_iff` give the order structure; the last one states that $H \times K \le J$ exactly when $J$ contains the images of $H$ and $K$ under the inclusions `MonoidHom.inl` and `MonoidHom.inr`. The kernel and range of a product map split: `MonoidHom.ker_prodMap` and `MonoidHom.range_prodMap`. The kernel of the first projection is $1 \times N$, by `MonoidHom.ker_fst`. When $1 \in s$ and $1 \in t$, `Subgroup.closure_prod` gives $\langle s \times t \rangle = \langle s \rangle \times \langle t \rangle$. The instance `Subgroup.prod_normal` makes a product of normal subgroups normal.

For a family of groups $(f_i)_{i \in \eta}$, a set $I \subseteq \eta$ and subgroups $H_i \le f_i$, the subgroup `Subgroup.pi I H` holds the functions $p$ with $p_i \in H_i$ for each $i \in I$, by `Subgroup.mem_pi`. Its lemmas are `Subgroup.pi_top`, `Subgroup.pi_bot`, `Subgroup.pi_empty`, `Subgroup.le_pi_iff` and `Subgroup.mulSingle_mem_pi`. For a finite index type, `Subgroup.pi_mem_of_mulSingle_mem` states that a subgroup that contains all `Pi.mulSingle i (x i)` contains $x$, and `Subgroup.closure_pi` is the form of `Subgroup.closure_prod` for families. These two lemmas are in `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Finite`.

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Basic` also has the lifts of homomorphisms through a surjection. Given a surjection $f : G_1 \to G_2$ with a right inverse, `MonoidHom.liftOfRightInverse` is an equivalence between the homomorphisms $g : G_1 \to G_3$ with $\ker f \le \ker g$ and the homomorphisms $G_2 \to G_3$. The variant `MonoidHom.liftOfSurjective` takes only the surjectivity. This is the universal property of a quotient without the quotient type. The set `ConjClasses.noncenter G` of conjugacy classes with more than one element, which the class equation of Chapter 6 uses, is also defined here.

#### Cardinalities and finite products

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Finite` measures subgroups with `Nat.card`. The lemmas `Subgroup.card_bot`, `Subgroup.card_top`, `Subgroup.card_le_of_le` and `Subgroup.card_le_card_group` are the first facts. A subgroup of a finite group is the whole group when it has the same cardinality, by `Subgroup.card_eq_iff_eq_top`, and it is trivial when its cardinality is at most $1$, by `Subgroup.card_le_one_iff_eq_bot`.

**Theorem.** Let $H < K$ be subgroups with $K$ finite. Then $|H| < |K|$.

*Proof.* The inclusion $H \le K$ gives $|H| \le |K|$. If $|H| = |K|$, then $H$, seen as a subgroup of the finite group $K$, has the cardinality of $K$, so it is all of $K$. This contradicts $H \ne K$. $\square$

This is `Subgroup.card_lt_of_lt`. An injective map keeps the cardinality of a subgroup, by `Subgroup.card_map_of_injective` and `Subgroup.card_subtype`. The same module has the closure of a subgroup under finite products: `Subgroup.list_prod_mem`, `Subgroup.multiset_prod_mem`, `Subgroup.prod_mem` for a commutative group, and `Subgroup.noncommProd_mem` for a product of pairwise commuting elements. The coercion lemmas `Subgroup.val_list_prod` and `Subgroup.val_finsetProd` compute a product in $H$ as a product in $G$. For a finite set $S$ that is stable under conjugation by $x$, the lemma `Subgroup.mem_normalizer_fintype` gives $x \in N_G(S)$: stability $x S x^{-1} \subseteq S$ is enough because $S$ is finite.

#### The lattice, the integers and the squares

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Order` has the instance `instIsModularLatticeSubgroup`: the subgroups of a commutative group form a modular lattice, so $H \le K$ gives $H \sqcup (L \sqcap K) = (H \sqcup L) \sqcap K$. The lemmas `Subgroup.isCoatom_comap` and `Subgroup.isCoatom_map` transport maximal subgroups along isomorphisms. In a linearly ordered group, `mabs_mem_iff` states $|x|_m \in H$ if and only if $x \in H$.

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.ZPowers.Lemmas` computes in the additive group $\mathbb{Z}$. The lemma `Int.closure_eq_zmultiples` states that the subgroup generated by $a$ and $b$ is $\gcd(a, b)\mathbb{Z}$, and `Int.closure_eq_zmultiples_finsetGcd` extends this to a finite set.

**Theorem.** For integers $a$ and $b$, $a\mathbb{Z} + b\mathbb{Z} = \gcd(a, b)\mathbb{Z}$ and $a\mathbb{Z} \cap b\mathbb{Z} = \operatorname{lcm}(a, b)\mathbb{Z}$.

*Proof.* Put $d = \gcd(a, b)$. Since $d$ divides $a$ and $b$, both $a$ and $b$ lie in $d\mathbb{Z}$, so $a\mathbb{Z} + b\mathbb{Z} \le d\mathbb{Z}$. By the identity of Bezout, $d = u a + v b$ for some integers $u$ and $v$, so $d \in a\mathbb{Z} + b\mathbb{Z}$ and $d\mathbb{Z} \le a\mathbb{Z} + b\mathbb{Z}$. For the intersection, an integer lies in $a\mathbb{Z} \cap b\mathbb{Z}$ exactly when it is a common multiple of $a$ and $b$, that is, a multiple of $\operatorname{lcm}(a, b)$. $\square$

The Lean names are `Int.zmultiples_sup` and `Int.zmultiples_inf`, and the forms for a finite family are `Int.finsetSup_zmultiples` and `Int.finsetInf_zmultiples`. In any group, the range of `zpowersHom G g` is `Subgroup.zpowers g`, by `Subgroup.range_zpowersHom`, and the join of the cyclic subgroups of the elements of a finite set is the closure of the set, by `Subgroup.finsetSup_zpowers`.

In a commutative group $G$, the closure of a set has a direct description. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Finsupp` states that $x \in \langle f(\iota) \rangle$ if and only if $x = \prod_i f(i)^{a_i}$ for a finitely supported $a : \iota \to \mathbb{Z}$; this is `Subgroup.mem_closure_range_iff`, with the finite form `Subgroup.mem_closure_range_iff_of_fintype` and the form for a finite set `Subgroup.mem_closure_iff_of_fintype`. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Even` defines the subgroup `Subgroup.square G` of squares of a commutative group, with `Subgroup.mem_square` stating $a \in G^2$ if and only if `IsSquare a`. The same file defines `Submonoid.square` and `Subsemigroup.square`, and `Subgroup.square_toSubmonoid` links them. The squares form a subgroup only in the commutative case, because $a^2 b^2 = (a b)^2$ needs $a b = b a$.

#### Common errors

1. A learner looks for `Subgroup.normalizer` of a subgroup and finds that it takes a set. Write `Subgroup.normalizer (H : Set G)`, or `normalizer ↑H`; the lemmas of this section are stated in this form.
2. A learner tries to prove that normality is transitive. It is false; the true statements are for characteristic subgroups, through `Subgroup.characteristic_of_characteristic_of_characteristic`. A subgroup of a subgroup lives on the subtype `↥H`, so the statement needs `Subgroup.map H.subtype K`.
3. A learner applies `Subgroup.normalClosure_le_normal` without an instance for the normality of $N$. The hypothesis is a class argument `[N.Normal]`, so it must be available by instance search or given with `haveI`.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that the normal closure of a single transposition in $S_3$ is $S_3$, and that the normal core of the subgroup it generates is trivial.
2. (A) Prove that the center $Z(G)$ is a characteristic subgroup of $G$.
3. (B) Let $H$ have index $n$ in $G$. Prove that the normal core of $H$ has finite index, and that this index divides $n!$.
4. (B) Give a group $G$ with subgroups $K \le H \le G$ such that $K$ is normal in $H$, $H$ is normal in $G$, and $K$ is not normal in $G$.
5. (B) Use `Int.zmultiples_sup` to prove that $6\mathbb{Z} + 10\mathbb{Z} = 2\mathbb{Z}$, and compute $6\mathbb{Z} \cap 10\mathbb{Z}$.
6. (C) Let $G$ be a finite group that satisfies `NormalizerCondition G`. Prove that every maximal subgroup of $G$ is normal and has prime index.
