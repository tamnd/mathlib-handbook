## The category of topological spaces

### Overview

This section puts all spaces into one category, `TopCat`, and asks the questions of Volume 24 about it. The answers are the base of the next two sections and of Chapter 8.

The forgetful functor from `TopCat` to sets has a left adjoint and a right adjoint. The left adjoint gives a set the discrete topology, and the right adjoint gives it the indiscrete topology. The forgetful functor preserves all limits and all colimits, so a limit of spaces has the set limit as its points. Monomorphisms are the injective maps, and epimorphisms are the surjective maps.

The topology on a limit is the coarsest one that makes the projections continuous, and the topology on a colimit is the finest one that makes the inclusions continuous. With this description, the section identifies the effective epimorphisms with the quotient maps, and it proves the topological König lemma: a cofiltered limit of nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces is nonempty.

The section then surveys the rest of the folder `Topology/Category`.

### Definitions

**Definition (The category TopCat).** An object of `TopCat` is a type $X$ with a topology. A morphism $f : X \to Y$ is a continuous map. Composition is composition of maps, and the identity is the identity map. The *forgetful functor* $U : \mathrm{TopCat} \to \mathrm{Type}$ sends a space to its type of points.

**Definition (Discrete and indiscrete space).** For a type $S$, the *discrete space* $\mathrm{D}(S)$ is $S$ with the topology in which every set is open. The *indiscrete space* $\mathrm{I}(S)$ is $S$ with the topology in which only $\emptyset$ and $S$ are open. Each map of sets is continuous between discrete spaces and between indiscrete spaces, so $\mathrm{D}$ and $\mathrm{I}$ are functors.

**Definition (Initial and final topology).** Let $X$ be a set and $(f_j : X \to Y_j)_j$ a family of maps to spaces. The *initial topology* on $X$ is the coarsest topology that makes each $f_j$ continuous. Its open sets are generated by the sets $f_j^{-1}(V)$ with $V$ open in $Y_j$. Dually, for maps $(g_j : Y_j \to X)_j$ from spaces, the *final topology* on $X$ declares $W$ open when each $g_j^{-1}(W)$ is open. It is the finest topology that makes each $g_j$ continuous.

**Definition (Quotient map).** A continuous map $f : X \to Y$ is a *quotient map* if it is surjective and $Y$ has the final topology for the single map $f$. That is, $V \subseteq Y$ is open if and only if $f^{-1}(V)$ is open.

**Definition (Effective epimorphism).** A morphism $f : X \to B$ in a category is an *effective epimorphism* if for each morphism $e : X \to W$ with the property

$$\text{for all } g_1, g_2 : Z \to X, \quad f \circ g_1 = f \circ g_2 \implies e \circ g_1 = e \circ g_2,$$

there is a unique $d : B \to W$ with $d \circ f = e$. 

**Definition (Functor of open sets).** For a space $X$, the open sets form a complete lattice $\mathcal{O}(X)$, which is a category with one morphism $U \to V$ when $U \subseteq V$. A continuous $f : X \to Y$ gives the monotone map $f^{-1} : \mathcal{O}(Y) \to \mathcal{O}(X)$. In Mathlib this functor is `Opens.map f`.

**Definition (Sequential, compactly generated and delta-generated spaces).** A space $X$ is *sequential* if each set that is closed under limits of convergent sequences is closed. It is *compactly generated* if a set $A$ is closed whenever $g^{-1}(A)$ is closed for every continuous map $g : K \to X$ from a compact Hausdorff space $K$. It is *delta-generated* if a set is open whenever its preimage under each continuous map $\mathbb{R}^n \to X$ is open.

### Theorems and proofs

**Theorem (Discrete and indiscrete adjoints).** There are adjunctions $\mathrm{D} \dashv U$ and $U \dashv \mathrm{I}$.

*Proof.* For $\mathrm{D} \dashv U$ it suffices to give a bijection $\mathrm{TopCat}(\mathrm{D}(S), X) \cong \mathrm{Type}(S, U X)$, natural in $S$ and $X$. Every map out of a discrete space is continuous, since every preimage is open. So a continuous map $\mathrm{D}(S) \to X$ is the same as a map of sets $S \to X$. The bijection is the identity on underlying maps, so it is natural. For $U \dashv \mathrm{I}$ the bijection is $\mathrm{Type}(U X, S) \cong \mathrm{TopCat}(X, \mathrm{I}(S))$. Every map into an indiscrete space is continuous, since the preimages of $\emptyset$ and $S$ are $\emptyset$ and $X$. Again the bijection is the identity on underlying maps. $\square$

**Corollary (The forgetful functor is continuous and cocontinuous).** The functor $U$ preserves all limits and all colimits that exist in `TopCat`.

*Proof.* A right adjoint preserves limits and a left adjoint preserves colimits (Volume 24, Chapter 5). The functor $U$ is both. $\square$

**Theorem (Limits in TopCat).** Let $F : J \to \mathrm{TopCat}$ be a small diagram. Let $L$ be the set limit of $U \circ F$, that is, the set of families $(x_j)_j$ with $x_j \in F(j)$ and $F(u)(x_i) = x_j$ for each $u : i \to j$. Give $L$ the initial topology for the projections $p_j : L \to F(j)$. Then $(L, (p_j)_j)$ is a limit cone. Dually, the set colimit with the final topology for the inclusions is a colimit cocone.

*Proof.* Each $p_j$ is continuous by the choice of the topology, and $F(u) \circ p_i = p_j$ holds on points. So the data is a cone. Let $(T, (q_j)_j)$ be another cone. On points there is a unique map $h : T \to L$ with $p_j \circ h = q_j$, namely $h(t) = (q_j(t))_j$. It remains to show that $h$ is continuous. The open sets of $L$ are generated by the sets $p_j^{-1}(V)$, and $h^{-1}(p_j^{-1}(V)) = q_j^{-1}(V)$ is open. A map is continuous when the preimages of a family of generators are open, so $h$ is continuous. Uniqueness holds already on points. The colimit case is dual: a map $h$ out of the colimit is continuous because $h^{-1}(W)$ is open in the final topology exactly when its preimage under each inclusion is open, and that preimage is $q_j^{-1}(W)$. $\square$

So a product carries the product topology, a pullback of $f : X \to Z$ and $g : Y \to Z$ is the subspace $\{(x, y) \mid f(x) = g(y)\}$ of $X \times Y$, a coproduct is the disjoint union, and a coequalizer is a quotient space.

**Theorem (Monomorphisms and epimorphisms).** A morphism $f$ in `TopCat` is a monomorphism if and only if it is injective, and an epimorphism if and only if it is surjective.

*Proof.* If $f$ is injective, then $f \circ g_1 = f \circ g_2$ implies $g_1 = g_2$ on points, so $f$ is a monomorphism. If $f$ is surjective, then $g_1 \circ f = g_2 \circ f$ implies $g_1 = g_2$, so $f$ is an epimorphism. Conversely, let $f : X \to Y$ be a monomorphism and let $f(x) = f(x')$. The two maps from the one point space to $X$ with values $x$ and $x'$ are continuous and have equal composites with $f$. So they are equal, and $x = x'$. Let $f$ be an epimorphism. Let $Z$ be the indiscrete space on $\{0, 1\}$. Let $g_1 : Y \to Z$ be the constant map $1$, and let $g_2$ send $f(X)$ to $1$ and the rest of $Y$ to $0$. Both maps are continuous because $Z$ is indiscrete, and $g_1 \circ f = g_2 \circ f$. So $g_1 = g_2$, and $f(X) = Y$. $\square$

Mathlib proves the theorem through the adjunctions: $U$ preserves monomorphisms and epimorphisms and is faithful.

**Theorem (Effective epimorphisms are quotient maps).** A morphism $f : X \to B$ in `TopCat` is an effective epimorphism if and only if it is a quotient map.

*Proof.* Let $f$ be a quotient map, and let $e : X \to W$ have the property in the definition. Take $Z$ the one point space. Then $f(x) = f(x')$ implies $e(x) = e(x')$. Since $f$ is surjective, there is a unique map of sets $d : B \to W$ with $d \circ f = e$. For $V$ open in $W$, $f^{-1}(d^{-1}(V)) = e^{-1}(V)$ is open, so $d^{-1}(V)$ is open because $f$ is a quotient map. So $d$ is continuous, and it is unique because $f$ is an epimorphism.

Conversely, let $f$ be an effective epimorphism. An effective epimorphism is an epimorphism, so $f$ is surjective by the previous theorem. Let $B'$ be the set of $B$ with the final topology for $f$. This topology is finer than the topology of $B$, since $f$ is continuous. The map $e = f : X \to B'$ is continuous, and it has the property in the definition, because it equals $f$ on points. So there is a continuous $d : B \to B'$ with $d \circ f = f$. As $f$ is surjective, $d$ is the identity of the set $B$. So the identity $B \to B'$ is continuous, and the topology of $B$ is finer than the quotient topology. The two topologies are equal, and $f$ is a quotient map. $\square$

**Theorem (Topological König lemma).** Let $J$ be a cofiltered category and $F : J \to \mathrm{TopCat}$ a diagram such that each $F(j)$ is nonempty, compact and Hausdorff. Then the limit of $F$ is nonempty.

*Proof.* Let $P = \prod_j F(j)$. It is compact by the Tychonoff theorem (Volume 13, Chapter 5). For a finite set $G$ of morphisms of $J$, let $P_G \subseteq P$ be the set of families $x$ with $F(u)(x_i) = x_k$ for each $u : i \to k$ in $G$. The limit is the intersection of all $P_G$.

Each $P_G$ is closed. For one morphism $u : i \to k$, the set $\{x \mid F(u)(x_i) = x_k\}$ is the preimage of the diagonal of $F(k) \times F(k)$ under the continuous map $x \mapsto (F(u)(x_i), x_k)$. The diagonal is closed because $F(k)$ is Hausdorff. A finite intersection of closed sets is closed.

Each $P_G$ is nonempty. Let $O$ be the finite set of sources and targets of the morphisms in $G$. Since $J$ is cofiltered, there is an object $m$ with morphisms $v_i : m \to i$ for $i \in O$ such that $u \circ v_i = v_k$ for each $u : i \to k$ in $G$. Choose $y \in F(m)$. Put $x_i = F(v_i)(y)$ for $i \in O$, and choose $x_j \in F(j)$ freely for $j \notin O$, which is possible since each $F(j)$ is nonempty. For $u : i \to k$ in $G$, $F(u)(x_i) = F(u \circ v_i)(y) = F(v_k)(y) = x_k$. So $x \in P_G$.

The family is directed downwards, since $P_{G \cup H} \subseteq P_G \cap P_H$. A downward directed family of nonempty closed sets in a compact space has a nonempty intersection, because a finite subfamily has a nonempty intersection. So the limit is nonempty. $\square$

**Proposition (Open maps and the open sets functor).** Let $f : X \to Y$ be an open map. Then $U \mapsto f(U)$ is a functor $\mathcal{O}(X) \to \mathcal{O}(Y)$, and it is left adjoint to $f^{-1} : \mathcal{O}(Y) \to \mathcal{O}(X)$.

*Proof.* The image of an open set is open because $f$ is open, and $U \subseteq U'$ implies $f(U) \subseteq f(U')$. Between posets, an adjunction is the condition $f(U) \subseteq V \iff U \subseteq f^{-1}(V)$. If $f(U) \subseteq V$ and $x \in U$, then $f(x) \in V$, so $x \in f^{-1}(V)$. If $U \subseteq f^{-1}(V)$, then $f(U) \subseteq f(f^{-1}(V)) \subseteq V$. $\square$

### Examples

**Example (The circle as a coequalizer).** Let $\ast$ be the one point space and let $a, b : \ast \to [0, 1]$ have the values $0$ and $1$. The coequalizer of $a$ and $b$ is the set $[0,1]/(0 \sim 1)$ with the quotient topology. The map $t \mapsto (\cos 2\pi t, \sin 2\pi t)$ induces a continuous bijection from it to the circle $S^1$. The source is compact and $S^1$ is Hausdorff, so the bijection is a homeomorphism. So the coequalizer is the circle.

**Counterexample (TopCat is not balanced).** Let $f : \mathrm{D}(\mathbb{R}) \to \mathbb{R}$ be the identity map from the discrete real line to the usual real line. It is continuous, injective and surjective, so it is a monomorphism and an epimorphism. It is not an isomorphism, because its inverse is not continuous: the preimage of $\{0\}$ under the inverse is $\{0\}$, which is not open in $\mathbb{R}$.

**Counterexample (König needs compactness).** Let $J = \mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{op}}$ and $F(k) = \{n \in \mathbb{N} \mid n \ge k\}$ with the discrete topology, with the inclusions as transition maps. Each $F(k)$ is nonempty and Hausdorff, but not compact. The limit is $\bigcap_k F(k) = \emptyset$.

The other modules of the folder give the following. `TopCat.I` in `TopCat.Monoidal` is the unit interval as an object, with the cartesian monoidal structure on `TopCat`. `TopCat.uliftFunctor` moves a space to a higher universe. `ContinuousMap.yonedaPresheaf` is the presheaf $S \mapsto C(S, X)$ on a category over `TopCat`. `UniformSpaceCat` is the category of uniform spaces, and `UniformSpaceCat.adj` makes completion left adjoint to the inclusion of `CpltSepUniformSpace`, the complete separated uniform spaces. `Born` is the category of bornologies with locally bounded maps. `Locale` is the opposite of the category of frames, and `topToLocale` sends a space to its frame of open sets. `FinTopCat` is the category of finite spaces, and `TopCommRingCat` the category of topological commutative rings.

Common errors of a learner:

- `TopCat.trivial` is the indiscrete topology, not the discrete one. The discrete functor is `TopCat.discrete`.
- A morphism of `TopCat` is not a function. Apply it with `f x` through the coercion, or take `f.hom` for the `ContinuousMap`. The lemma `TopCat.hom_ext` reduces equality of morphisms to equality on points.
- A bijective morphism need not be an isomorphism. The counterexample above shows this. For compact Hausdorff spaces it is true, and Section 2 uses that.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that the product of $\mathrm{D}(S)$ and $\mathrm{D}(T)$ in `TopCat` is $\mathrm{D}(S \times T)$, and that the product of two indiscrete spaces is indiscrete. *Hint:* compute the product topology on basic open sets.
2. (A) Show that the equalizer of $f, g : X \to Y$ is the subspace $\{x \mid f(x) = g(x)\}$, and that it is closed when $Y$ is Hausdorff. *Hint:* the limit theorem with the initial topology for one inclusion.
3. (A) Find the coequalizer of the identity and the map $x \mapsto -x$ on $\mathbb{R}$, and show that it is homeomorphic to $[0, \infty)$. *Hint:* the map $x \mapsto |x|$.
4. (B) Show that a continuous surjection that is open, or closed, is a quotient map, and so an effective epimorphism. *Hint:* $f(f^{-1}(V)) = V$ for a surjection.
5. (B) Show that the discrete functor $\mathrm{D}$ has no left adjoint. *Hint:* a left adjoint would make $\mathrm{D}$ preserve limits; compare the product of countably many copies of $\mathrm{D}(\{0, 1\})$ with $\mathrm{D}(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}})$.
6. (B) Let $f : X \to Y$ be continuous. Show that $f^{-1} : \mathcal{O}(Y) \to \mathcal{O}(X)$ preserves finite meets and arbitrary joins. Show that it has the right adjoint $U \mapsto Y \setminus \overline{f(X \setminus U)}$. Show that the inclusion $\{0\} \to \mathbb{R}$ gives a map $f^{-1}$ with no left adjoint. *Hint:* a monotone map of complete lattices has a left adjoint exactly when it preserves all meets, and the meet of open sets is the interior of their intersection.
7. (C) Let $F : \mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathrm{TopCat}$ be a sequence of nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces with surjective transition maps. Show that each projection from the limit is surjective. *Hint:* apply the König lemma to the fibres over a point of $F(n)$.

### Notes and history

The name of the König lemma comes from the lemma of Dénes König on infinite trees. Compactly generated spaces are used in homotopy theory because their category is cartesian closed.
