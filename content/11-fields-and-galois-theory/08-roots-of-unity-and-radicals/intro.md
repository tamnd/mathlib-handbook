# Roots of unity and radicals

The last chapter of the volume answers the question that started Galois theory: which polynomial equations can be solved by radicals, that is, by the field operations and the extraction of $n$th roots. Two kinds of extensions are the steps of such a solution. Adjoining a root of unity gives an abelian extension, and over a field that contains the $n$th roots of unity, adjoining an $n$th root gives a cyclic extension. So a polynomial that is solvable by radicals has a Galois group built from abelian pieces, a solvable group.

Section 1 studies the roots of unity in a field, the cyclotomic extensions and their Galois groups, the irreducibility of the cyclotomic polynomials over $\mathbb{Q}$, and the Kummer extensions generated by the roots of $X^n - a$. Section 2 defines solvability by radicals, proves the theorem of Abel and Ruffini in the form of Galois, that a polynomial solvable by radicals has a solvable Galois group, and gives a quintic over $\mathbb{Q}$ that is not solvable by radicals.

The chapter needs the fundamental theorem (Chapter 5), the Galois group of a polynomial and the example $X^5 - 4X + 2$ (Chapter 6), finite fields (Chapter 7), and from Volume 5 the symmetric and alternating groups.
