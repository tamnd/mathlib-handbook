# Measure and integration

This volume teaches measure theory and the Lebesgue integral, and their form in Mathlib. It starts from σ-algebras and ends with the fundamental theorem of calculus for the Lebesgue integral and the change of variables formula in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Each main theorem has a proof on paper: the π-λ theorem of Dynkin, the extension theorem of Carathéodory, the construction of Lebesgue measure, the uniqueness of translation invariant measures on $\mathbb{R}^n$, the monotone convergence theorem, the lemma of Fatou, the dominated convergence theorem, the theorems of Tonelli and Fubini, the inequalities of Hölder and Minkowski, the completeness of $L^p$ and the fundamental theorem of calculus. The examples compute: the measure of the Cantor set, the area of a parallelogram, the Gaussian integral by Fubini, and the integrals that fail, such as $1/x$ on $(0, 1]$ and the iterated integrals of a function that is not integrable on a square.

Chapter 1 defines measurable spaces, the Borel σ-algebra and measurable functions, and proves the π-λ theorem. Chapter 2 defines measures and outer measures, proves the theorem of Carathéodory and introduces null sets and the filter of almost everywhere. Chapter 3 constructs Lebesgue measure on the line as a Lebesgue-Stieltjes measure, and on $\mathbb{R}^n$ as a Haar measure. Chapter 4 defines the integral of a nonnegative function, `lintegral`, and proves the monotone convergence theorem, the lemma of Fatou and the theorem of Radon-Nikodym. Chapter 5 defines integrable functions and the Bochner integral of functions with values in a Banach space, and proves the dominated convergence theorem. Chapter 6 constructs product measures and proves the theorems of Tonelli and Fubini. Chapter 7 studies the spaces $L^p$. Chapter 8 studies the integral over an interval, the fundamental theorem of calculus, the change of variables formula and the differentiation theorem of Lebesgue. Chapters 1 to 6 are the core of a first course, and the reading path U takes them.

The volume needs Volume 13 for the topology of $\mathbb{R}^n$, compactness and the filters that Mathlib uses to state limits, and Volume 15 for limits, series and derivatives in one variable. Volume 15 has no integral, and this volume supplies it. Chapter 3 uses the determinant of Volume 7. Normed spaces, Volume 16, have no text yet, so the sections explain the little that they need: a Banach space is a complete normed vector space, and the reader can take it to be $\mathbb{R}$ or $\mathbb{C}$ throughout.

Mathlib has two integrals. The lower Lebesgue integral `∫⁻ x, f x ∂μ` takes a function with values in `ℝ≥0∞`, the nonnegative reals with $\infty$, and is defined for every function. The Bochner integral `∫ x, f x ∂μ` takes a function with values in a Banach space, and it has the junk value $0$ when the function is not integrable. Most theorems come in both forms, and the text says which form a theorem has. Measures take values in `ℝ≥0∞`, the measure on a type is often the instance `volume`, and statements hold almost everywhere through the filter `ae μ`. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
