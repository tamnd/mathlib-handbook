## Nakayama's lemma

### Overview

Over a field, every spanning set of a vector space contains a basis, and the number of elements of a basis is the dimension. Over a local ring $(R, m)$ there is a replacement: a finitely generated module $M$ with $M = mM$ is zero, and elements of $M$ whose images span the vector space $M/mM$ generate $M$. This is Nakayama's lemma. It reduces questions about generators to linear algebra over the residue field.

The section proves the lemma by the determinant trick, an application of the adjugate matrix of Volume 7, Chapter 4 to a module. The same trick proves a theorem of Vasconcelos: a surjective endomorphism of a finitely generated module is injective.

### Definitions

In this section $R$ is a commutative ring and $M$ is an $R$-module. For an ideal $I$, the submodule $IM$ is the set of finite sums $\sum a_i x_i$ with $a_i \in I$ and $x_i \in M$. The Jacobson radical $J(R)$ is the intersection of all maximal ideals. Chapter 1, Section 2 proves that $b \in J(R)$ exactly when $1 + rb$ is a unit for every $r \in R$. A local ring is a ring with exactly one maximal ideal $m$, and then $J(R) = m$ (Chapter 2).

**Definition (Minimal number of generators).** For a finitely generated module $M$, $\mu(M)$ is the least $n$ such that $M$ is generated by $n$ elements.

### Theorems and proofs

**Theorem (Determinant trick).** Let $M$ be generated by $x_1, \dots, x_n$, let $I$ be an ideal, and let $\varphi : M \to M$ be $R$-linear with $\varphi(M) \subseteq IM$. Then there are $a_k \in I^k$ with
$$\varphi^n + a_1 \varphi^{n-1} + \cdots + a_{n-1}\varphi + a_n = 0$$
as an endomorphism of $M$.

*Proof.* Each $\varphi(x_i)$ lies in $IM$, so $\varphi(x_i) = \sum_j a_{ij} x_j$ with $a_{ij} \in I$. Let $A = R[t]$ act on $M$ with $t$ acting as $\varphi$. Since $\varphi$ is $R$-linear, this makes $M$ a module over the commutative ring $A$. Let $B$ be the $n \times n$ matrix over $A$ with entries $B_{ij} = \delta_{ij} t - a_{ij}$, and let $x$ be the column $(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ in $M^n$. Then $Bx = 0$, since its $i$-th entry is $\varphi(x_i) - \sum_j a_{ij} x_j$. Volume 7, Chapter 4 proves $\operatorname{adj}(B) B = \det(B) \cdot 1$ over every commutative ring. So
$$\det(B)\, x = \operatorname{adj}(B)(Bx) = 0,$$
that is, $\det(B)$ kills every $x_j$, and so it kills $M$. Expand $\det(B)$ as a sum over permutations. Each term is a product of $n$ entries, each entry is $t - a_{ii}$ or $-a_{ij}$, and a term of degree $n - k$ in $t$ comes from products with exactly $k$ factors in $I$. So $\det(B) = t^n + a_1 t^{n-1} + \cdots + a_n$ with $a_k \in I^k$. Replace $t$ by $\varphi$. $\square$

**Corollary (Annihilating element).** If $M$ is finitely generated and $IM = M$, then there is $a \in R$ with $a \equiv 1 \pmod{I}$ and $aM = 0$.

*Proof.* Apply the theorem to $\varphi = \mathrm{id}$. Then $(1 + a_1 + \cdots + a_n)$ acts as $0$ on $M$, and $a = 1 + a_1 + \cdots + a_n \equiv 1 \pmod{I}$. $\square$

**Theorem (Nakayama's lemma).** Let $M$ be finitely generated and $I \subseteq J(R)$. If $IM = M$, then $M = 0$.

*Proof.* The corollary gives $a = 1 + b$ with $b \in I \subseteq J(R)$ and $aM = 0$. By Chapter 1, $a$ is a unit. For $x \in M$, $x = a^{-1}(ax) = 0$. $\square$

**Corollary (Submodule form).** Let $M$ be finitely generated, $N \subseteq M$ a submodule and $I \subseteq J(R)$. If $M = N + IM$, then $M = N$.

*Proof.* The quotient $M/N$ is finitely generated, by the images of generators of $M$. Every element of $M/N$ is the image of some $y + \sum a_i x_i$ with $y \in N$, $a_i \in I$, $x_i \in M$, so it equals $\sum a_i \bar{x}_i \in I(M/N)$. Thus $I(M/N) = M/N$, and Nakayama's lemma gives $M/N = 0$. $\square$

**Corollary (Generators over a local ring).** Let $(R, m)$ be local with residue field $k = R/m$ and let $M$ be finitely generated. Then:

1. elements $x_1, \dots, x_n \in M$ generate $M$ if and only if their images span the $k$-vector space $M/mM$;
2. $\mu(M) = \dim_k M/mM$;
3. every generating set of $M$ that is minimal for inclusion has exactly $\mu(M)$ elements.

*Proof.* The module $M/mM$ is killed by $m$, so it is a vector space over $k$. (1) If the $x_i$ generate $M$, their images span $M/mM$. Conversely, let $N = Rx_1 + \cdots + Rx_n$. If the images span, then every $x \in M$ satisfies $x - \sum r_i x_i \in mM$ for some $r_i$, so $M = N + mM$. Since $J(R) = m$, the submodule form gives $M = N$. (2) By (1), a generating set has at least $d = \dim_k M/mM$ elements, and lifts of a basis of $M/mM$ generate $M$. (3) Let $x_1, \dots, x_n$ be a generating set that is minimal for inclusion. The images span $M/mM$. If they are linearly dependent, one image lies in the span of the others, the others still span, and by (1) the other $x_j$ generate $M$. That contradicts minimality, so the images form a basis and $n = d$. $\square$

**Theorem (Vasconcelos).** Let $M$ be finitely generated. Every surjective $R$-linear map $\varphi : M \to M$ is injective.

*Proof.* Let $A = R[t]$ act on $M$ with $t$ acting as $\varphi$. Then $M$ is a finitely generated $A$-module, generated by the same elements. Let $I = tA$. Then $IM = \varphi(M) = M$. The corollary on an annihilating element, applied over $A$, gives $a \in A$ with $a \equiv 1 \pmod{t}$ and $aM = 0$. Write $a = 1 + t g(t)$ with $g \in A$. For $x \in \ker \varphi$,
$$0 = a x = x + g(\varphi)(\varphi(x)) = x.$$
So $\ker \varphi = 0$. $\square$

### Examples

**Example (Generators of a maximal ideal).** Let $k$ be a field and $R = k[x, y]/(x, y)^3$. Every element outside $m = (\bar x, \bar y)$ has the form $c + n$ with $c \in k^\times$ and $n^3 = 0$, so it is a unit, and $R$ is local with maximal ideal $m$. Here $m^2 = (\bar x^2, \bar x \bar y, \bar y^2)$, and $m/m^2$ has basis $\bar x, \bar y$. So $\mu(m) = 2$. The elements $u = \bar x + \bar y^2$ and $v = \bar y$ have images $\bar x, \bar y$ in $m/m^2$, so by the corollary they generate $m$. Directly, $\bar x = u - v^2$.

**Counterexample (Finite generation is needed).** Let $R = \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$, a local ring with maximal ideal $p\mathbb{Z}_{(p)} = J(R)$, and $M = \mathbb{Q}$. Every $q \in \mathbb{Q}$ is $p \cdot (q/p)$, so $pM = M$, and $M \ne 0$. Nakayama's lemma fails because $\mathbb{Q}$ is not finitely generated over $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$.

**Counterexample (The Jacobson radical is needed).** Let $R = \mathbb{Z}$, $I = 2\mathbb{Z}$ and $M = \mathbb{Z}/3$. Multiplication by $2$ is bijective on $M$, so $IM = M$, and $M \ne 0$. Here $J(\mathbb{Z}) = 0$, so $I \not\subseteq J(R)$. The corollary on an annihilating element still applies: $a = 3$ satisfies $a \equiv 1 \pmod 2$ and $aM = 0$. But $3$ is not a unit of $\mathbb{Z}$, so the proof of Nakayama's lemma stops there.

**Counterexample (Injective endomorphisms).** Multiplication by $2$ on $\mathbb{Z}$ is injective and not surjective. On the $k$-vector space $k^{(\mathbb{N})}$ with basis $e_0, e_1, \dots$, the map with $e_0 \mapsto 0$ and $e_{i+1} \mapsto e_i$ is surjective and not injective.

### The library around it

The Nakayama lemmas take submodules of one ambient module and the ideal `Ring.jacobson R`. For a module, use `N' = ⊤` and `Module.Finite.fg_top`.

Nakayama's lemma describes the idempotent ideals. If $I$ is finitely generated and $I^2 = I$, then $I = (e)$ for an idempotent $e$: this is `Ideal.isIdempotentElem_iff_of_fg` in `Mathlib.RingTheory.Ideal.IdempotentFG`, and `Ideal.isIdempotentElem_iff_eq_bot_or_top` gives the case of a domain, where only $0$ and $R$ remain. The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Ideal.Pure` treats pure ideals, the ideals $I$ with $R / I$ flat over $R$, written `Ideal.Pure`. For a pure ideal, `Ideal.inf_eq_mul_of_pure` gives $I \cap J = I J$ for each ideal $J$, so `Ideal.isIdempotentElem_of_pure` gives $I^2 = I$. Conversely `Ideal.Pure.of_isIdempotentElem` says that a finitely generated idempotent ideal is pure, and `Ideal.zeroLocus_inj_of_pure` says that a pure ideal is determined by its zero locus in the prime spectrum.

Common errors of a learner:

- The hypothesis is `N' ≤ N ⊔ I • N'`, with `N'` on both sides. A statement with `I • ⊤` on the right is a different lemma.
- Old names with `jacobson ⊥` are deprecated. The current names use `Ring.jacobson R`.
- `Submodule.top_ne_ideal_smul_of_le_jacobson_annihilator` is the contrapositive form `⊤ ≠ I • ⊤` for a nontrivial finite module.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $(R, m)$ be local and $M = R^2$. Show that $(1, a), (b, 1)$ generate $M$ if and only if $1 - ab \notin m$.
2. (A) Let $(R, m)$ be local and $I \subseteq R$ an ideal with $I = mI$, $I$ finitely generated. Show $I = 0$. Use it to show that a finitely generated idempotent ideal $I = I^2$ of a local ring is $0$ or $R$.
3. (A) Find $\mu(M)$ for the $\mathbb{Z}_{(2)}$-module $M = \mathbb{Z}_{(2)} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/4 \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2$. Hint: compute $M/2M$.
4. (B) Let $I = I^2$ be a finitely generated ideal of any commutative ring $R$. Show that $I = eR$ for an idempotent $e$. Hint: the corollary on an annihilating element gives $a \equiv 1 \pmod I$ with $aI = 0$; take $e = 1 - a$.
5. (B) Let $(R, m)$ be local, $M$ finitely generated, and $\varphi : M \to N$ linear with $N$ finitely generated. Show that $\varphi$ is surjective if $\varphi \otimes k : M/mM \to N/mN$ is surjective.
6. (B) Show that every surjective $R$-linear map $R^n \to R^n$ is an isomorphism, and deduce that $R^m \cong R^n$ implies $m = n$ for a nonzero commutative ring.
7. (C) Let $(R, m)$ be local and $P$ a finitely generated projective $R$-module. Show that $P$ is free. Hint: lift a basis of $P/mP$ to a surjection $R^n \to P$, split it, and apply Nakayama's lemma to the kernel.

### Notes and history

The lemma is named after Tadasi Nakayama. The theorem on surjective endomorphisms is due to Wolmer Vasconcelos. The proof by the determinant trick follows Atiyah and Macdonald, *Introduction to Commutative Algebra*, Proposition 2.4.
