# Successor and predecessor

In `ℕ` each number $n$ has a next number $n + 1$, and no number lies strictly between them. Many other orders have the same feature: `ℤ`, `Fin n`, `ℕ+`, `ℕ∞`, the ordinals and the cardinals. Mathlib describes it with the class `SuccOrder`, which gives a map `Order.succ` with $a \le \operatorname{succ} a$ and $\operatorname{succ} a \le b$ for each $b > a$. The dual class `PredOrder` gives `Order.pred`. The first section defines the two classes, explains why the main equivalences need the hypothesis that $a$ is not maximal, and lists the instances on the number types. It ends with the sums and unions over the intervals $[f(n), f(\operatorname{succ} n))$ that come from a monotone map.

The second section studies the orders in which each element above $a$ is reached from $a$ by finitely many steps, the class `IsSuccArchimedean`. These orders support induction from any starting point. A linear order of this kind with a predecessor is countable, and it is isomorphic to `ℤ`, to `ℕ` or to a finite range of `ℕ`. The section also treats the locally finite linear orders, which get such a structure, and the rooted trees of Mathlib, which are built from a predecessor order in which the parent of a vertex is its predecessor.

The third section treats the elements that are not successors: the limit elements. In a well-founded order each element is minimal, a successor or a limit, and this gives the transfinite recursion `SuccOrder.limitRecOn`. The section then defines the normal functions, the strictly monotone maps that are continuous at limits, and the transfinite iteration of a map on a complete lattice.

The fourth section treats the types where the successor of $x$ is $x + 1$, such as `ℕ`, `ℤ` and the ordinals. The classes `SuccAddOrder` and `PredSubOrder` let the lemmas about successors, monotone maps, limits and intervals be stated with $x + 1$ and $x - 1$. The section ends with the linearly ordered groups whose intervals are finite, which are trivial or isomorphic to `ℤ`. It uses the ordered groups of the chapter on ordered groups.

The chapter needs the chapter on orders, for the covering relation $a \lessdot b$ and the maximal elements, and the chapter on monotone maps. The intervals come from the chapter on intervals, and the third section uses the suprema of the chapters on complete and conditionally complete lattices. The ordinals and the cardinals, the main examples of limit elements, are in the set theory volume.
