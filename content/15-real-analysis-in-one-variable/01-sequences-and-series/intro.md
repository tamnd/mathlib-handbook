# Sequences and series

A sequence of real numbers is the first object of analysis. Each later notion of the volume, the limit of a function, the derivative, the number $e$ and the constant $\pi$, is built from limits of sequences or reduces to them. This chapter gives the theory of limits of real sequences and of infinite series, with the form that Mathlib gives to both.

Section 1 defines the limit of a sequence with $\varepsilon$ and $N$, translates the definition into the language of filters that Mathlib uses, proves the algebra of limits and the squeeze theorem, and computes the standard limits $1/n \to 0$, $q^n \to 0$, $n^{1/n} \to 1$ and $a^n/n! \to 0$. Section 2 proves the monotone convergence theorem from the least upper bound property, the theorem of Bolzano-Weierstrass, and the completeness of $\mathbb{R}$ in the form of Cauchy sequences, and it introduces the upper and lower limits. Section 3 studies series: geometric series, the comparison and ratio tests, the Cauchy condensation test and the $p$-series, absolute convergence and the alternating series test. It explains a point where Mathlib differs from the textbooks: the sum `tsum` of Mathlib is an unconditional sum, so for real series it exists exactly for the absolutely convergent ones.

The chapter needs the real numbers of Volume 2: the least upper bound property, the Archimedean property and the absolute value. It uses the language of filters, neighbourhoods and metric spaces of Volume 13 only through the translations that it states. Chapter 2 uses the sequences of this chapter to study limits of functions and continuity, and Chapters 5 and 6 use the series of Section 3 to define the exponential and the trigonometric functions.
