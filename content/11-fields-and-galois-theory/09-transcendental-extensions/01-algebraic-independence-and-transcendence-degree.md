## Algebraic independence and transcendence degree

### Overview

Chapter 2, Section 1 defines an algebraically independent family and a transcendence basis, and states that two transcendence bases have the same cardinality. This section is the library that makes these notions usable. It answers the questions that come up in a proof: when does a family stay independent after one more element is added, how does independence behave in a tower, does it survive when the base field grows by an algebraic extension, and how large is a field of given transcendence degree.

The modules work over commutative rings, not only over fields. A family $x = (x_i)_{i \in I}$ in an $R$-algebra $A$ is `AlgebraicIndependent R x` when the evaluation map $R[X_i : i \in I] \to A$, $X_i \mapsto x_i$, is injective. For a set $s$ of indices, `AlgebraicIndepOn R x s` says that the subfamily indexed by $s$ is independent. The transcendence degree `Algebra.trdeg R A` is a cardinal: the supremum of the cardinalities of the algebraically independent subsets of $A$. Over a field, or more generally for a domain $A$, it is the common cardinality of all transcendence bases.

The section also treats two small topics: the class `FinTrdeg` of field extensions of finite transcendence degree, and the strongly transcendental elements of a ring.

### The library around it

#### Basic properties

The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.AlgebraicIndependent.Basic` collects the first consequences of the definition. The lemma `algebraicIndependent_iff_ker_eq_bot` restates independence as the triviality of the kernel of `MvPolynomial.aeval x`. An independent family over a nontrivial ring is injective (`AlgebraicIndependent.injective`), has no zero member (`AlgebraicIndependent.ne_zero`), and is linearly independent (`AlgebraicIndependent.linearIndependent`), because linear polynomials are polynomials. The variables of a polynomial ring are the first example: `MvPolynomial.algebraicIndependent_X`.

An injective algebra map keeps independence: `AlgebraicIndependent.map'` sends an independent family $x$ to $f \circ x$, and `AlgHom.algebraicIndependent_iff` is the equivalence. An isomorphism `e : A ≃ₐ[R] A'` keeps transcendence bases (`AlgEquiv.isTranscendenceBasis`) and the transcendence degree (`AlgEquiv.trdeg_eq`). For a map that is only injective or only surjective, the degree moves in one direction: `trdeg_le_of_injective` and `trdeg_le_of_surjective`.

Independence has finite character: a family is independent if and only if each finite subfamily is. This is `algebraicIndependent_of_finite` for sets and `algebraicIndependent_of_finite_type` for families. The reason is that a polynomial involves only finitely many variables. So the union of a chain of independent sets is independent (`algebraicIndependent_iUnion_of_directed`), and the lemma of Zorn gives `exists_maximal_algebraicIndependent`: inside a set $t$, each independent subset $s \subseteq t$ extends to a maximal one.

Two junk values need care. If the map $R \to A$ is not injective, then no family is independent, not even the empty one (`algebraicIndependent_empty_iff`), and `trdeg_eq_zero_of_not_injective` gives the degree $0$. If $R$ is the zero ring, then each family is independent, and `trdeg_subsingleton` gives the degree $1$.

#### Adjoining one element

The key step in all exchange arguments is the following theorem. Write $R[x]$ for the subalgebra `Algebra.adjoin R (Set.range x)` generated by a family $x$.

**Theorem.** Let $x$ be a family in $A$ and $a \in A$. The family $(x, a)$ is algebraically independent over $R$ if and only if $x$ is algebraically independent over $R$ and $a$ is transcendental over $R[x]$.

*Proof.* Both conditions imply that $x$ is independent, since a subfamily of an independent family is independent. So assume that $x$ is independent. Then evaluation is an isomorphism $R[X_i] \cong R[x]$, and it extends to an isomorphism of polynomial rings in one more variable:
$$R[X_i, Y] = R[X_i][Y] \cong R[x][Y].$$
Under this isomorphism, evaluation of a polynomial at $(x, a)$ becomes evaluation of a polynomial with coefficients in $R[x]$ at $Y = a$. The first map is injective exactly when the second one is, that is, when $a$ is transcendental over $R[x]$. $\square$

In Lean the family $(x, a)$ is indexed by `Option ι`, with the extra index for the new element, and the statement is `AlgebraicIndependent.option_iff`. The isomorphism of the proof is `AlgebraicIndependent.mvPolynomialOptionEquivPolynomialAdjoin`, an `R`-algebra isomorphism from `MvPolynomial (Option ι) R` to `Polynomial` over the adjoined subalgebra. For a family that is already known to be independent, `AlgebraicIndependent.option_iff_transcendental` keeps only the second condition. The versions for sets are `AlgebraicIndepOn.insert` and `AlgebraicIndepOn.insert_iff`, and the version for one index of a family is `AlgebraicIndependent.iff_transcendental_adjoin_image`. The case of a single element is `algebraicIndependent_iff_transcendental`: the family with the single member $x$ is independent if and only if $x$ is transcendental.

The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.AlgebraicIndependent.Transcendental` also proves the consequences of the theorem for a whole independent family. Each member is transcendental over the subalgebra generated by the others (`AlgebraicIndependent.transcendental_adjoin`), and two disjoint subfamilies are independent over each other (`AlgebraicIndependent.adjoin_of_disjoint`). For two families $x$ and $y$, `AlgebraicIndependent.sumElim_iff` says that the family $y \sqcup x$, indexed by a sum type, is independent over $R$ if and only if $x$ is independent over $R$ and $y$ is independent over $R[x]$. This is the theorem above with many elements at once.

#### The transcendence degree

The transcendence degree is $0$ exactly for algebraic extensions.

**Theorem.** `Algebra.trdeg R A = 0` if and only if `Algebra.IsAlgebraic R A`.

*Proof.* The degree is a supremum of cardinals, so it is $0$ if and only if no nonempty subset of $A$ is independent. A nonempty independent set contains an independent singleton $\{a\}$, and $\{a\}$ is independent if and only if $a$ is transcendental. So the degree is $0$ if and only if no element of $A$ is transcendental. $\square$

This is `trdeg_eq_zero_iff`, with the forms `trdeg_ne_zero_iff` and `trdeg_pos` for an extension of class `Algebra.Transcendental`. An independent family has at most `Algebra.trdeg R A` members (`AlgebraicIndependent.cardinalMk_le_trdeg`), and an algebraic extension has no nonempty independent family (`AlgebraicIndependent.isEmpty_of_isAlgebraic`). In a tower $R \subseteq S \subseteq A$, the theorem `trdeg_add_le` gives
$$\mathrm{trdeg}_R S + \mathrm{trdeg}_S A \le \mathrm{trdeg}_R A,$$
by the union of a transcendence basis of $S/R$ and one of $A/S$, which is independent by `AlgebraicIndependent.sumElim_iff`. Under the hypothesis that $A$ has no zero divisors, equality holds, and this is `trdeg_add_eq`. The variants `lift_trdeg_add_le` and the other names with `lift` allow the three types to live in different universes, as in the rest of the theory of cardinals.

#### Changing the base by an algebraic extension

**Theorem.** Let $R \subseteq S \subseteq A$ be a tower with $S$ algebraic over $R$ and without zero divisors. If a family $x$ in $A$ is algebraically independent over $R$, then it is algebraically independent over $S$.

*Proof sketch, for fields.* Let $K \subseteq S \subseteq A$ be fields and suppose that $p(x) = 0$ for a nonzero $p$ with coefficients in $S$. The coefficients generate a finite extension $S_0/K$, since they are algebraic. Let $N(p)$ be the product of the images of $p$ under the $K$-embeddings of $S_0$ into an algebraic closure, made into a polynomial over $K$ with the norm of Chapter 2, Section 3. Then $N(p)$ is a nonzero polynomial over $K$, and $p$ divides it, so $N(p)(x) = 0$. This contradicts the independence of $x$ over $K$. $\square$

This is `AlgebraicIndependent.extendScalars` in the module `Mathlib.RingTheory.AlgebraicIndependent.AlgebraicClosure`. The special cases are `AlgebraicIndependent.algebraicClosure`, over the relative algebraic closure `algebraicClosure F E`, and `AlgebraicIndependent.integralClosure`. With the transitivity of algebraic extensions it gives `Algebra.IsAlgebraic.isTranscendenceBasis_iff`: a family is a transcendence basis over $R$ if and only if it is one over $S$. For an intermediate field generated by a set $s$, the lemmas `IntermediateField.algebraicIndependent_adjoin_iff` and `IntermediateField.transcendental_adjoin_iff` replace the field $F(s)$ by the ring $F[s]$ as the base. The field $F(s)$ is algebraic over the fraction field of $F[s]$, so the two bases give the same notions.

#### The field generated by an independent family

The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.AlgebraicIndependent.Adjoin` identifies the field generated by an independent family. For $x$ independent over a field $F$, the ring $F[x]$ is a polynomial ring, so its field of fractions is a field of rational functions:
$$F(X_i : i \in I) \cong F(x_i : i \in I).$$
This is `AlgebraicIndependent.aevalEquivField`, an isomorphism from `FractionRing (MvPolynomial ι F)` to the intermediate field `IntermediateField.adjoin F (Set.range x)`. It sends a fraction $p/q$ of polynomials to $p(x)/q(x)$ (`AlgebraicIndependent.aevalEquivField_apply_coe`). Its inverse is `AlgebraicIndependent.reprField`. With a transcendence basis $B$ of $E/F$, this gives the tower $F \subseteq F(B) \subseteq E$ of the overview: a field of rational functions, then an algebraic extension.

#### Cardinality

**Theorem.** Let $E$ be a domain, $F \to E$ an algebra, and $B$ a nonempty transcendence basis of $E$ over $F$. Then
$$|E| = \max(|F|, |B|, \aleph_0).$$

*Proof sketch.* The polynomial ring $F[X_b : b \in B]$ embeds into $E$, and it has cardinality $\max(|F|, |B|, \aleph_0)$, since $B$ is nonempty. So $|E|$ is at least this cardinal. Conversely, $E$ is algebraic over the image $F[B]$ of the polynomial ring, and an algebraic extension has at most $\max(|F[B]|, \aleph_0)$ elements, by the bound of Chapter 2, Section 1. $\square$

This is `IsTranscendenceBasis.lift_cardinalMk_eq_max_lift` in the module `Mathlib.RingTheory.AlgebraicIndependent.RankAndCardinality`, with the universe lifts of the general case. The same module proves `IsTranscendenceBasis.lift_rank_eq_max_lift` for the dimension of $E$ as a vector space over a field $F$, and `Algebra.Transcendental.rank_eq_cardinalMk`: if $E/F$ is transcendental, then $\dim_F E = |E|$. The module `Mathlib.FieldTheory.MvRatFunc.Rank` gives the dimension of the field of rational functions itself: `MvRatFunc.rank_eq_max_lift` says that $\dim_F F(X_s : s \in \sigma) = \max(|F|, |\sigma|, \aleph_0)$ for a nonempty $\sigma$. For example, $\mathbb{C}$ has transcendence degree $2^{\aleph_0}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$: a countable basis would make $\mathbb{C}$ countable. The module also has the bound `IntermediateField.rank_sup_le` on the dimension of a compositum, $\dim_F(A B) \le \dim_F A \cdot \dim_F B$, valid also for infinite dimensions.

#### Finite transcendence degree

The module `Mathlib.FieldTheory.FinTrdeg` defines the class `FinTrdeg K L` for a field extension: there is a finitely generated intermediate field $E$ with $L/E$ algebraic. The lemma `finTrdeg_iff_trdeg` says that this is equivalent to `Algebra.trdeg K L < ℵ₀`. Under the class, each independent family and each transcendence basis is finite (`finite_of_algebraicIndependent`, `finite_of_isTranscendenceBasis`), and `exists_finset_isTranscendenceBasis` gives a basis as a `Finset`. The class has instances for algebraic extensions and for extensions that are essentially of finite type, and it is transitive in towers (`FinTrdeg.trans`).

#### Polynomials in several variables

The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Algebraic.MvPolynomial` proves transcendence statements for the variables. Each variable $X_i$ of `MvPolynomial σ R` is transcendental over $R$ (`MvPolynomial.transcendental_X`). Over the subring of polynomials in the variables of a set $s$, the variable $X_i$ is transcendental if and only if $i \notin s$: this is `MvPolynomial.transcendental_supported_X_iff`, with the subring `MvPolynomial.supported R s`. *Proof.* If $i \in s$, then $X_i$ lies in the subring and is a root of $Y - X_i$. If $i \notin s$, the ring `MvPolynomial σ R` is a polynomial ring in $X_i$ over a ring that contains the subring, so $X_i$ satisfies no nonzero polynomial equation over it. $\square$ The lemma `MvPolynomial.algebraicIndependent_polynomial_aeval_X` goes further: if each $f_i$ is a transcendental element of $R[X]$, the family $f_i(X_i)$ is independent.

#### Strongly transcendental elements

The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Algebraic.StronglyTranscendental` defines `IsStronglyTranscendental R x` for an element $x$ of an $R$-algebra $S$. It asks that for all $u \in S$ and $p \in R[X]$ with $p(x) u = 0$, each coefficient of $p$ maps to an element of $S$ that kills $u$. For $u = 1$ this says that $p(x) = 0$ forces $p$ to vanish in $S$, so a strongly transcendental element is transcendental when $R \to S$ is injective (`IsStronglyTranscendental.transcendental`). The extra condition matters when $S$ has zero divisors. In a field the two notions agree (`isStronglyTranscendental_iff_of_field`), and in a domain with fraction field $K$, $x$ is strongly transcendental if and only if its image in $K$ is transcendental (`IsStronglyTranscendental.iff_of_isFractionRing`). The notion is stable under localization (`IsStronglyTranscendental.of_isLocalization`) and passes to the quotient by a minimal prime of a reduced ring (`isStronglyTranscendental_mk_of_mem_minimalPrimes`). It is a tool for the proof of the main theorem of Zariski in commutative algebra.

#### Common errors

1. `AlgebraicIndependent R x` is about a family `x : ι → A`, not a set. For a set `s : Set A`, the statement is `AlgebraicIndependent R ((↑) : s → A)` or `AlgebraicIndepOn R id s`. The lemmas `AlgebraicIndependent.to_subtype_range` and `AlgebraicIndependent.image` move between the two forms.
2. The base in `AlgebraicIndependent.option_iff` and `AlgebraicIndependent.sumElim_iff` is the subalgebra `Algebra.adjoin R (Set.range x)`, a ring, not the intermediate field generated by $x$. Over a field, use `IntermediateField.transcendental_adjoin_iff` to pass to the field.
3. `Algebra.trdeg R A` is a `Cardinal` in the universe of $A$. To compare degrees of types in different universes, use the versions with lift in the name, for example `lift_trdeg_add_le` in place of `trdeg_add_le`.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that the family $(t, t^2)$ in $K(t)$ is not algebraically independent over $K$, and find the polynomial of the relation.
2. (A) Use `AlgebraicIndependent.option_iff` to show that $x_1, x_2$ are algebraically independent over $K$ if and only if $x_1$ is transcendental over $K$ and $x_2$ is transcendental over $K[x_1]$.
3. (B) Let $E/K$ have transcendence degree $n < \aleph_0$, and let $x_1, \dots, x_n \in E$ be algebraically independent. Show that $E$ is algebraic over $K(x_1, \dots, x_n)$.
4. (B) Show that $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ have transcendence degree $2^{\aleph_0}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Hint: the theorem on cardinality.
5. (B) Let $L/K$ be an extension of finite transcendence degree and $E$ an intermediate field. Show that $E/K$ and $L/E$ have finite transcendence degree.
6. (C) Prove the theorem on algebraic extension of the base for fields in full detail, with the norm of a polynomial over a finite extension.
