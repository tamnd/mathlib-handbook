# Ordered rings and fields

This chapter studies rings and fields with an order that respects the ring operations. Section 1 defines ordered semirings and strict ordered semirings in the unbundled form that Mathlib uses, with the mixins `IsOrderedRing` and `IsStrictOrderedRing` over a separate order. It proves the rules of computation with inequalities, shows that a nontrivial ordered ring has characteristic zero and that squares are nonnegative, and concludes that $\mathbb{C}$ admits no order. It introduces positive cones, proves that $\mathbb{Z}$ has exactly one ordering, and orders $\mathbb{R}[X]$ by the sign of the leading coefficient. Section 2 turns to ordered fields: the rules for inverses and quotients, the mediant, the uniqueness of the order on $\mathbb{Q}$, the embedding of $\mathbb{Q}$ in every ordered field, the field $\mathbb{R}(x)$ with an infinite element, and formally real fields. Section 3 defines the archimedean property, proves it for $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{R}$, constructs the floor function, proves that $\mathbb{Q}$ is dense in each archimedean ordered field, and states that every archimedean ordered field embeds in $\mathbb{R}$. Sections 4 to 11 describe the library around these three sections. Section 4 lists the lemmas on ordered semirings by topic. Section 5 gives the nonnegative elements of an ordered ring as a type, and the ordered semirings with a top element added. Section 6 gives the floor, the ceiling, the fractional part and rounding. Section 7 reduces an element to an interval modulo a period. Section 8 defines the archimedean classes of an ordered field and the standard part of a finite element. Section 9 treats absolute values and the orderings of a ring as subsemirings. Section 10 gives the small classes for monotone multiplication by nonnegative elements, and the lemmas on products, powers and inverses that need only these classes. Section 11 describes the linearly ordered groups with zero that are the targets of valuations, and the divisibility order on associated elements.

The chapter needs rings, integral domains, fields and the field of fractions from Chapters 1 to 3, and the characteristic from Chapter 4 in one proof. Section 9 also needs the Euclidean domains of Chapter 6, and Section 11 the associated elements of Chapter 6. It uses partial orders, linear orders, suprema and Galois connections from Volume 4.

Chapter 8 uses ordered rings for the star-ordered rings of Section 2 and the archimedean floor function for the continued fractions of Section 3. Volume 15 on real analysis builds the real numbers as a complete archimedean ordered field and uses the results of this chapter in each estimate.
