# Definability, orders and Fraïssé limits

This chapter studies the sets that formulas define and the countable structures that are determined by their finite parts. Section 1 defines the $A$-definable subsets of $M^{\alpha}$, proves that they form a Boolean algebra closed under projection, reduces each set of parameters to a finite one, and proves that an automorphism that fixes the parameters fixes each definable set. It then sets up the language of orders, the theories of preorders, partial orders and linear orders, and the theory $\mathrm{DLO}$ of dense linear orders without endpoints, proves Cantor's theorem by back-and-forth, and deduces that $\mathrm{DLO}$ is complete. The language of graphs closes the section. Section 2 defines the age of a structure, the hereditary, joint embedding and amalgamation properties, Fraïssé classes, ultrahomogeneous structures and Fraïssé limits. It characterizes the ages of countable structures, proves that two Fraïssé limits of one class are isomorphic, and shows that the rationals are the Fraïssé limit of the finite linear orders.

The chapter needs the syntax and semantics of Chapter 1: formulas with free variables, realization, embeddings, substructures and finitely generated structures. From Chapter 2 it uses complete theories, the Łoś-Vaught test, direct limits and partial equivalences. It also uses finite and countable sets from Volume 3 and linear orders from Volume 4.

Chapter 4 applies the same ideas to algebra and arithmetic. There the definable sets of an algebraically closed field contain the zero loci of polynomials, and the definable sets of the natural numbers with addition are the semilinear sets. The test for completeness that this chapter uses for $\mathrm{DLO}$ proves there that each theory $\mathrm{ACF}_p$ is complete.
