# Elliptic curves

This chapter treats elliptic curves through Weierstrass equations over an arbitrary commutative ring, as Mathlib does. Section 1 defines a Weierstrass curve by its five coefficients, computes the quantities $b_2, b_4, b_6, b_8, c_4, c_6$, the discriminant $\Delta$ and the $j$-invariant, and proves how they change under an admissible change of variables. It gives the short normal form, the models with a given $j$-invariant, the theorem that two elliptic curves over a separably closed field with the same $j$-invariant are isomorphic, and the reduction types at a prime. Section 2 defines the nonsingular affine points, the chord and tangent construction, and the group law, which Mathlib proves through ideal classes of the coordinate ring. It also gives Jacobian and projective coordinates. Section 3 defines the division polynomials $\psi_n$ and $\phi_n$ from an elliptic divisibility sequence, gives their univariate forms, and proves the formulas for their degrees and leading coefficients.

The chapter needs polynomial rings and quotients from Volume 6, ideals and localization from Volume 9, and Dedekind domains, class groups and discrete valuation rings from Volume 10. It uses separable closures and finite fields from Volume 11, and number fields and $p$-adic valuations from Volume 12. Sections 1 and 2 use only a little of the scheme theory of this volume, and a reader can take them after Chapter 3.

This is the last chapter of the volume. An elliptic curve is the first example of an abelian variety, and Chapter 7 treats group schemes and abelian varieties in general. The projective model of a Weierstrass curve is a closed subscheme of the projective plane built by the Proj construction of Chapter 4. The reduction types and the $L$-function of Section 1 return to the arithmetic of Volume 12, where valuations and number fields live.
