# Root systems

This chapter studies root systems on their own, as Mathlib's root pairings: a family of roots in a module $M$ and coroots in a dual module $N$ with $\langle \alpha_i, \alpha_i^\vee \rangle = 2$. Section 1 defines reflections and root pairings, proves that the roots determine the coroots, relates Coxeter weight $4$ to dependent roots, and introduces the Weyl group, reduced, irreducible and crystallographic pairings, and pairings from a bilinear form. Section 2 assumes finitely many roots. It builds the canonical invariant form, proves it positive, bounds the pairings, shows that root chains have no gaps and length at most $3$, proves that bases exist, and shows that the Cartan matrix of a base is of finite type. Section 3 treats $G_2$, proving that two roots with pairing $-3$ span a twelve-root plane, and then builds a simple Lie algebra from a root system with a base, by Serre's relations and by Geck's matrices.

The chapter needs modules, duality and the dual basis from Volume 7, Chapters 2 and 6, and bilinear and quadratic forms from Volume 8, Chapters 5 and 6. Section 3 also uses Lie algebras and their ideals from Chapter 4, the radical and semisimplicity from Chapter 5, and Cartan subalgebras, $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triples and the root system of a Lie algebra from Chapter 6. The running examples are $A_1$, $A_2$, $B_2$ and $G_2$.

Chapter 6 turned a semisimple Lie algebra into a root system, and Section 3 of this chapter goes back the other way, so over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ simple Lie algebras and irreducible reduced root systems determine each other. Chapter 8 takes up the Weyl group again as a Coxeter group, with the Weyl groups of $A_2$ and $B_2$ as dihedral examples.
