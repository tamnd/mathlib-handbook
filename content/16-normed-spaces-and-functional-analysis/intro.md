# Normed spaces and functional analysis

This volume teaches the theory of normed spaces and the first theorems of functional analysis, together with their form in Mathlib. It starts from the axioms of a norm on an abelian group, reaches the spectral theorem for a symmetric operator on a finite dimensional inner product space in Chapter 7, and ends with the convexity library and the theory of C*-algebras. Each main theorem has a complete proof on paper: the equivalence of norms in finite dimension, the lemma of Riesz, the completeness of the space of bounded operators, the theorem of Banach-Steinhaus, the open mapping theorem, the closed graph theorem, the theorem of Hahn-Banach in its analytic and its geometric forms, the inequality of Cauchy-Schwarz, the inequality of Bessel, the theorem of the nearest point, the representation theorem of Riesz and the spectral theorem in finite dimension. Each chapter also computes: the operator norm of a matrix, the distance $\sqrt 2$ between the unit vectors of $\ell^2$, a norming functional, an orthogonal projection, and the eigenbasis of a symmetric $2 \times 2$ matrix.

A normed space is a vector space with a notion of length. The length gives a metric, so all of the topology of Volume 13 applies, and the linear structure gives the algebra of Volume 7. Functional analysis studies the interaction of the two in infinite dimension. Three facts of finite dimension fail there: two norms need not be equivalent, a linear map need not be continuous, and the closed unit ball is not compact. The theory replaces them by completeness. A complete normed space is a Banach space, and the Baire category theorem of Volume 13 gives the three main theorems on Banach spaces. An inner product gives more: angles, orthogonality and projections, and in a complete inner product space, a Hilbert space, the geometry is almost as good as in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

The chapters are in the order of a first course.

- Chapter 1 defines normed groups, normed fields and normed spaces, gives the norms $\|\cdot\|_1$, $\|\cdot\|_2$ and $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$, studies series in normed groups and the spaces $\ell^p$ of sequences.
- Chapter 2 proves that a linear map is continuous if and only if it is bounded, defines the operator norm, proves that the space of bounded operators into a Banach space is complete, and studies isometries, equivalences and the dual space.
- Chapter 3 proves that all norms on a finite dimensional space are equivalent, that each linear map from such a space is continuous, and, with the lemma of Riesz, that the closed unit ball is compact only in finite dimension.
- Chapter 4 studies Banach spaces, their closed subspaces, quotients and completions, and proves the theorem of Banach-Steinhaus, the open mapping theorem and the closed graph theorem from the Baire category theorem.
- Chapter 5 proves the theorem of Hahn-Banach for real and complex spaces, and derives the norming functionals, the isometric embedding into the double dual and the separation of convex sets.
- Chapter 6 studies inner product spaces: the inequality of Cauchy-Schwarz, the parallelogram law, orthonormal families, the process of Gram-Schmidt, Hilbert spaces, the space $\ell^2$ and Hilbert bases.
- Chapter 7 proves the theorem of the nearest point in a closed convex set, constructs the orthogonal projection onto a closed subspace, proves the representation theorem of Riesz, defines the adjoint of an operator and proves the spectral theorem for a symmetric operator in finite dimension.
- Chapter 8 defines seminorms on groups and vector spaces, studies locally convex spaces with the topology of a family of seminorms, bounded sets and the topologies on spaces of operators, and weak topologies with their polars.
- Chapter 9 studies normed rings, normed fields and Banach algebras: the spectrum, the formula of Gelfand for the spectral radius, the theorem of Gelfand and Mazur, the exponential of a matrix and of a quaternion, and the unitization of an algebra.
- Chapter 10 studies ultrametric norms and the lemma of Krasner, the completion of a field at an absolute value, bundled seminorms on rings, and the spectral norm that extends an ultrametric norm to an algebraic extension.
- Chapter 11 collects the convexity library: convex sets and hulls, convex functions and the inequality of Jensen, the topology of convex sets, proper cones and their duals, the theorem of Birkhoff on doubly stochastic matrices, and strictly and uniformly convex spaces.
- Chapter 12 studies C*-algebras: the C*-identity and the spectral radius, the continuous functional calculus, the order on a C*-algebra, Hilbert C*-modules, the commutative theorem of Gelfand and Naimark, the GNS construction, approximate units and the unitary group.

The volume needs Volume 7 for vector spaces, linear maps, bases, dimension, matrices, eigenvalues and the algebraic dual. It needs Volume 13 for metric spaces, completeness, compactness, the completion and the Baire category theorem, and Volume 15 for the real numbers, the absolute value, series and their convergence. Volume 14 on topological algebra defines the topological vector spaces and the continuous linear maps in the generality that Mathlib uses. This volume uses those definitions only for normed spaces and explains them where they first appear.

Mathlib states most theorems of this volume in more generality than a course. The scalar field is often any nontrivially normed field, so one theorem covers real and complex spaces and also the $p$-adic numbers. A norm is a structure on top of a metric space, so a normed space carries a metric, a uniform structure and a topology that agree by definition. A continuous linear map is a bundled object `E →L[𝕜] F`, and its norm is the operator norm. Many maps are semilinear, `E →SL[σ] F`, so that conjugate linear maps such as the map of the Riesz theorem fit in the same frame. The inner product of Mathlib is conjugate linear in its first argument, the convention of physics, and the text says so wherever it matters. The text names the general form, explains how to read it for $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$, and says where the encoding differs from the textbook. Volume 17 on differential calculus in normed spaces and Volume 18 on measure and integration continue from here. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
