## Derivations

### Overview

A derivation is a linear map that satisfies the product rule. Let $A$ be an $R$-algebra and $M$ an $A$-module. An $R$-derivation $D : A \to M$ is an $R$-linear map with $D(ab) = a\,D(b) + b\,D(a)$ for all $a, b \in A$. The derivative $d/dX$ on $R[X]$ is the first example. Section 1 of this chapter uses derivations to define the module of Kähler differentials $\Omega_{A/R}$, which is the universal target of a derivation. This section describes the type of derivations itself.

The modules of this section hold the definition and the calculus rules, the module structure on the set of derivations, the Lie bracket of two derivations of $A$, the action of a derivation on the coefficients of a polynomial, differential rings with the notation $x'$, and the link between derivations and lifts to a square zero extension. The ring of dual numbers $R[\varepsilon]$, with $\varepsilon^2 = 0$, is the simplest square zero extension, so its module is here too.

### The library around it

#### The type of derivations

The structure `Derivation R A M` has three parts: a linear map `Derivation.toLinearMap` of type `A →ₗ[R] M`, the field `Derivation.map_one_eq_zero'` with $D(1) = 0$, and the field `Derivation.leibniz'` with the product rule. The ring $R$ and the algebra $A$ are commutative semirings, and $M$ is an additive commutative monoid. The condition $D(1) = 0$ is part of the structure because it does not follow from the product rule when $M$ has no cancellation. When $M$ is an additive group, `Derivation.mk'` builds a derivation from a linear map and the product rule alone.

A derivation coerces to a function and to a linear map. The simp normal form is the coercion to a function. The lemma `Derivation.coeFn_coe` says that the two coercions agree, and `Derivation.ext` proves equality from equality at each point. The type has the instances of a module: `Derivation.instAddCommMonoid`, `Derivation.instAddCommGroup` when $M$ is a group, and `Derivation.instModule` with the action $(a \cdot D)(x) = a\,D(x)$. So the derivations form an $A$-module, and `Derivation.add_apply`, `Derivation.smul_apply` and `Derivation.neg_apply` compute pointwise.

#### The calculus rules

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Derivation.leibniz` | $D(ab) = a\,D(b) + b\,D(a)$ |
| `Derivation.map_one_eq_zero` | $D(1) = 0$ |
| `Derivation.map_algebraMap` | $D(r \cdot 1) = 0$ for $r \in R$ |
| `Derivation.map_natCast` | $D(n) = 0$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$ |
| `Derivation.leibniz_pow` | $D(a^n) = n\,a^{n-1}\,D(a)$ |
| `Derivation.leibniz_of_mul_eq_one` | $ab = 1$ implies $D(a) = -a^2\,D(b)$ |
| `Derivation.leibniz_inv` | $D(a^{-1}) = -a^{-2}\,D(a)$ in a field |
| `Derivation.leibniz_div` | $D(a/b) = b^{-2}\,(b\,D(a) - a\,D(b))$ in a field |
| `Derivation.leibniz_zpow` | $D(a^n) = n\,a^{n-1}\,D(a)$ for $n \in \mathbb{Z}$ in a field |
| `Derivation.map_aeval` | $D(P(x)) = P'(x)\,D(x)$ for $P \in R[X]$ |

The power rule has a short proof by induction on $n$. For $n = 0$, $D(1) = 0$. For the step, $D(a^{n+1}) = D(a \cdot a^n) = a\,D(a^n) + a^n D(a) = a \cdot n\,a^{n-1} D(a) + a^n D(a) = (n+1)\,a^n D(a)$. The rule for an inverse follows from the product rule: if $ab = 1$, then $0 = D(1) = a\,D(b) + b\,D(a)$, and multiplication by $a$ gives $D(a) = -a^2 D(b)$. In a field, take $b = a^{-1}$.

A derivation is determined by its values on generators. The lemma `Derivation.eqOn_adjoin` says that two derivations that agree on a set $s$ agree on the subalgebra generated by $s$. The proof is an induction on the subalgebra: the set where $D_1 = D_2$ contains $R$, by `Derivation.map_algebraMap`, and it is closed under sums and products, by the product rule. The form `Derivation.ext_of_adjoin_eq_top` gives $D_1 = D_2$ when $s$ generates $A$.

#### Change of module and of ring

A derivation can be moved along a linear map or a ring map.

1. `LinearMap.compDer` composes a derivation $A \to M$ with an $A$-linear map $M \to N$. As a bilinear map this is `Derivation.llcomp`, and `LinearEquiv.compDer` is the equivalence for a linear equivalence.
2. `Derivation.compAlgebraMap` restricts a derivation $B \to M$ to $A$ along a tower $R \to A \to B$. The linear form is `Derivation.compAlgebraMapL`.
3. `Derivation.restrictScalars` sees an $S$-derivation as an $R$-derivation, for a tower $R \to S$.
4. `Derivation.liftOfSurjective` moves a derivation of $A$ to a derivation of a quotient, when the map $A \to M$ kills the kernel.

#### The Lie algebra of derivations

The derivations $A \to A$ form a Lie algebra over $R$. The bracket is the commutator $[D_1, D_2] = D_1 \circ D_2 - D_2 \circ D_1$, by `Derivation.commutator_apply`. The composition $D_1 \circ D_2$ is not a derivation in general, but the commutator is. Proof: expand both terms,
$$D_1 D_2(ab) = D_1(a\,D_2 b + b\,D_2 a) = D_1a\,D_2b + a\,D_1D_2b + D_1b\,D_2a + b\,D_1D_2a.$$
The mixed terms $D_1a\,D_2b + D_1b\,D_2a$ are symmetric in $D_1$ and $D_2$. So they cancel in $D_1D_2(ab) - D_2D_1(ab)$, and what remains is $a\,[D_1, D_2](b) + b\,[D_1, D_2](a)$. $\square$

The instances are `Derivation.instLieRing` and `Derivation.instLieAlgebra`. The algebra $A$ is a Lie module over the derivations, `Derivation.instLieModule`, with $[D, a] = D(a)$, `Derivation.bracket_eq_fun`. For an algebra map $\sigma : A \to A'$, `Derivation.couple` is the Lie subalgebra of the pairs $(d', d)$ with $d' \circ \sigma = \sigma \circ d$.

#### Derivations and polynomials

A derivation $d : A \to M$ acts on the coefficients of a polynomial. The map `Derivation.mapCoeffs` sends $\sum a_i X^i$ to $\sum d(a_i) X^i$, a polynomial with coefficients in $M$, and it is again a derivation, with `Derivation.mapCoeffs_X` giving $X \mapsto 0$. The chain rule for a polynomial with coefficients in $A$ is `Derivation.apply_eval_eq`:
$$d\big(p(x)\big) = (d\,p)(x) + p'(x)\,d(x),$$
where $d\,p$ is the polynomial with the derived coefficients. Proof: both sides are additive in $p$, so it suffices to take $p = aX^n$. Then $d(ax^n) = d(a)\,x^n + a\,n\,x^{n-1}d(x)$ by the product rule and the power rule, and these are the two terms. The lemmas `Derivation.apply_aeval_eq` and `Derivation.apply_aeval_eq'` are the forms for `Polynomial.aeval`.

#### Differential rings

A differential ring is a commutative ring with one chosen derivation. The class `Differential R` has the field `Differential.deriv`, a term of `Derivation ℤ R R`, with the postfix notation $x'$ for its value at $x$ (the character is the prime sign U+2032). The class `DifferentialAlgebra A B` says that the algebra map $A \to B$ commutes with the derivations, by `DifferentialAlgebra.deriv_algebraMap`. The class `Differential.ContainConstants A B` says that each element of $B$ with derivative $0$ comes from $A$; this is the setting of the theory of integration in finite terms.

On $A[X]$ for a differential ring $A$, `Differential.mapCoeffs` derives the coefficients, and `Differential.implicitDeriv v` is the unique derivation that extends the derivation of $A$ and sends $X$ to $v$, by `Differential.implicitDeriv_C` and `Differential.implicitDeriv_X`. The lemma `Differential.deriv_aeval_eq` is the chain rule for a differential algebra, and `Differential.algHom_deriv` says that an injective $A$-algebra map $f$ into a domain satisfies $f(x') = f(x)'$ when $x$ is separable over $A$. The proof applies the derivation to $p(x) = 0$, for the minimal polynomial $p$, and divides by $p'(x) \ne 0$.

#### Lifts to a square zero extension

Let $B$ be an $R$-algebra and $I$ an ideal of $B$ with $I^2 = 0$. Let $A$ be an $R$-algebra with a map $A \to B/I$. The lifts of this map to $A \to B$ are related to derivations. Two lifts $f_1, f_2$ differ by a map $f_1 - f_2 : A \to I$, `diffToIdealOfQuotientCompEq`. This map is a derivation. The computation is
$$f_1(ab) - f_2(ab) = f_1(a)\big(f_1(b) - f_2(b)\big) + f_2(b)\big(f_1(a) - f_2(a)\big),$$
and since $I^2 = 0$, the products of $I$ with $f_1(a)$ and with $f_2(a)$ agree. So $I$ is an $A$-module through either lift, and the right side is $a\,(f_1 - f_2)(b) + b\,(f_1 - f_2)(a)$.

When $A \to B$ is the algebra map of a tower $R \to A \to B$, `derivationToSquareZeroEquivLift` is the full correspondence: the $R$-derivations $A \to I$ are in bijection with the lifts $A \to B$ of the canonical map $A \to B/I$. The two directions are `derivationToSquareZeroOfLift` and `liftOfDerivationToSquareZero`. This bijection is the bridge from derivations to the infinitesimal lifting criteria of Section 2: a formally unramified algebra has at most one lift, so each derivation into a square zero ideal is $0$.

#### Dual numbers

The dual numbers over $R$ are $R[\varepsilon] = R \oplus R\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon^2 = 0$. In Mathlib this is `DualNumber R`, a special case of the trivial square zero extension `TrivSqZeroExt R M`, with the element `DualNumber.eps`. The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.DualNumber` proves the ring theory facts.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `DualNumber.isNilpotent_eps` | $\varepsilon$ is nilpotent |
| `DualNumber.isNilpotent_iff_eps_dvd` | $x$ is nilpotent if and only if $\varepsilon \mid x$, for a division ring $R$ |
| `DualNumber.instIsLocalRing` | $K[\varepsilon]$ is a local ring for a division ring $K$ |
| `DualNumber.maximalIdeal_eq_span_singleton_eps` | its maximal ideal is $(\varepsilon)$ |
| `DualNumber.ideal_trichotomy` | its only ideals are $0$, $(\varepsilon)$ and $K[\varepsilon]$ |
| `DualNumber.instIsPrincipalIdealRing` | $K[\varepsilon]$ is a principal ideal ring |
| `TrivSqZeroExt.isUnit_or_isNilpotent` | each element of a trivial square zero extension of a division ring is a unit or nilpotent |

The trichotomy has a short proof for a field $K$. An element $a + b\varepsilon$ with $a \ne 0$ is a unit, with inverse $a^{-1} - a^{-2}b\,\varepsilon$. So a proper ideal $I$ is contained in $K\varepsilon = (\varepsilon)$. This is a vector space of dimension $1$ over $K$, so $I$ is $0$ or $(\varepsilon)$. $\square$

For an $R$-algebra $A$, a derivation $D : A \to A$ gives the algebra map $A \to A[\varepsilon]$, $a \mapsto a + D(a)\,\varepsilon$. The product rule is exactly the condition that this map is multiplicative, since $(a + D(a)\varepsilon)(b + D(b)\varepsilon) = ab + (a\,D(b) + b\,D(a))\varepsilon$. This is the case $B = A[\varepsilon]$, $I = (\varepsilon)$ of the correspondence above.

#### Common errors

1. `Derivation R A M` needs `[Module A M]` and `[Module R M]`, and most lemmas also need `[IsScalarTower R A M]`. A goal with a missing tower instance fails at elaboration, not at the use of the lemma.
2. The derivation of a differential ring is a term of `Derivation ℤ R R`, not of `Derivation R R R`. A lemma about $R$-derivations applies only after a change of scalars.
3. `derivationToSquareZeroEquivLift` asks for the hypothesis `I ^ 2 = ⊥` as an argument. The derivations land in the subtype of $I$, so the values must be coerced to $B$ before they are compared with $f(x)$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that $D(a^2 b) = 2ab\,D(a) + a^2 D(b)$ with `Derivation.leibniz` and `Derivation.leibniz_pow`.
2. (A) Show that a derivation of $\mathbb{Q}$ into a $\mathbb{Q}$-module is $0$, with `Derivation.eqOn_adjoin`.
3. (B) Show that the derivations $R[X] \to M$ are in bijection with $M$, by the value at $X$.
4. (B) Show that $d/dX$ on $\mathbb{F}_p[X]$ kills $X^p$, and find all the derivations of $\mathbb{F}_p[X]$ that kill $X$.
5. (B) Prove that $K[\varepsilon]$ has exactly three ideals, and find all the units of $\mathbb{Z}[\varepsilon]$.
6. (C) Let $A = k[X, Y]$ with $k$ a field of characteristic not $2$. Compute the bracket of $X\,\partial/\partial Y$ and $Y\,\partial/\partial X$, and show that these two derivations and their bracket span a Lie algebra isomorphic to $\mathfrak{sl}_2(k)$.
