# Linear algebra

This volume teaches linear algebra and its form in Mathlib, from the axioms of a module to the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain. Each main theorem has a complete proof on paper: the exchange lemma, the invariance of dimension, rank-nullity, the multiplicativity of the determinant, Cramer's rule and the theorem of Cayley and Hamilton. Each section computes with explicit vectors and matrices, mostly over $\mathbb{Q}$, $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/p$.

Mathlib works with modules over a ring from the start. A vector space is a module over a field, and there is no separate class for it. So the volume also states each definition for a module over a ring, and it tells which theorems need a field, which need a commutative ring, and which hold in general. This point of view pays off in Chapter 7, where the same language describes finite abelian groups.

Chapter 1 gives the language: modules, submodules and spans, linear maps with their kernels and ranges, quotient modules and the isomorphism theorems. Chapter 2 studies linear independence and bases, proves the exchange lemma and the invariance of dimension, and proves the rank-nullity theorem. Chapter 3 introduces matrices, the matrix of a linear map in a pair of bases, the change of basis, and the rank of a matrix. Chapter 4 defines the determinant by the Leibniz formula, proves that it is multiplicative, and proves Cramer's rule with the adjugate. Chapter 5 studies eigenvalues and eigenspaces, the characteristic polynomial, the minimal polynomial and the theorem of Cayley and Hamilton. Chapter 6 studies the dual space, annihilators and the transpose of a linear map. Chapter 7 proves that a submodule of a free module over a PID is free, and states the structure theorem with its Smith normal form. Chapter 8 builds the localization, the direct limit and the presentations of a module, and studies the projective, injective and stably free modules and the number of generators. Chapter 9 studies ordered modules, the archimedean classes with the Hahn embedding, and the lattices in a real vector space. Chapter 10 studies the linear groups: the general linear group, the special linear group, the symplectic group and the unitary and orthogonal groups.

Read Volume 1 for the classes and the structures of Mathlib, Volume 3 for finite sums and products, Volume 4 for lattices, and Volume 5 for groups, quotients and the sign of a permutation. The volume uses rings, fields and polynomials in an elementary way. Inner product spaces are in Volume 16 in Mathlib's order, and Chapter 6 gives only a pointer to them. The minimal polynomial of an element of an algebra has its home in Volume 11, and this volume uses it for endomorphisms only. The volume has no Lean companion yet.
