# Algebraic geometry

Algebraic geometry studies the solutions of polynomial equations. The circle $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ over the real numbers, the same equation over a finite field, and the equation $y^2 = x^3 - x$ over the rational numbers are three examples. Grothendieck put all such objects into one theory. A commutative ring $R$ gives a space $\operatorname{Spec} R$, its points are the prime ideals of $R$, and its functions are the elements of $R$ and their fractions. A scheme is a space that is glued from such pieces. This volume develops the theory of schemes in its form in Mathlib, from sheaves on a space to the group law on an elliptic curve.

Chapter 1 gives presheaves and sheaves on a topological space, stalks, sheafification and skyscraper sheaves. Chapter 2 treats ringed and locally ringed spaces, open immersions and gluing. Chapter 3 constructs the spectrum of a ring with its structure sheaf, defines schemes, open subschemes and the gluing of schemes, and constructs fiber products. Chapter 4 treats the properties of schemes: reduced, integral and Noetherian schemes, the projective spectrum of a graded ring, ideal sheaves and closed subschemes. Chapter 5 is the longest. It studies the properties of morphisms, with immersions, finite, flat, separated, proper, smooth and étale morphisms, and the valuative criteria.

Chapter 6 gives the Grothendieck topologies on the category of schemes: the Zariski, fpqc, étale and proétale sites. Chapter 7 treats rational and birational maps, algebraic cycles, the order of vanishing of a function and group schemes. Chapter 8 gives elliptic curves by Weierstrass equations, the group law on their points with the proof of Mathlib through the class group, and the division polynomials.

The volume needs Volume 13 for topology, Volumes 6, 9 and 10 for rings and commutative algebra, Volume 11 for fields, Volume 24 for categories and Volume 25 for sites and sheaves. Chapters 1 to 3 come first, in order. Chapter 4 and Chapter 5 need Chapter 3, and Chapter 6 needs Chapter 5. Chapter 7 needs Chapters 4 and 5. Chapter 8 needs mostly the algebra of Volumes 6 and 11, and a reader can study it after Chapter 3. The level is that of a graduate course. The references are Hartshorne, *Algebraic Geometry*, the Stacks project, Görtz and Wedhorn, *Algebraic Geometry I*, and Silverman, *The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves*.

The conventions are those of Mathlib. A presheaf on a space $X$ is a functor on the opposite of the category of open sets of $X$, and a scheme is a locally ringed space with an open cover by affine schemes. Many results are statements about classes of morphisms, so the boxes give the names of the classes and of the instances. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
