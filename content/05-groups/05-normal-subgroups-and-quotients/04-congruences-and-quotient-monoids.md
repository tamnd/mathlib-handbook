## Congruences and quotient monoids

### Overview

A group has a quotient for each normal subgroup, and the first section of this chapter builds it. A monoid has no such description of its quotients. The kernel of a monoid homomorphism, the set of elements that go to $1$, does not determine the homomorphism: the map from the additive monoid $(\mathbb{N}, +)$ to the multiplicative monoid $\{0, 1\}$ with $0 \mapsto 1$ and $n \mapsto 0$ for $n \ge 1$ is a homomorphism. It sends only $0$ to the identity, and it is not injective. The right notion is a congruence: an equivalence relation that is compatible with the multiplication. Each congruence gives a quotient monoid, and each homomorphism has a congruence as its kernel.

This section describes the library of congruences. The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Congruence.Defs` defines `Con M`, the congruence generated by a relation, the complete lattice of congruences and the quotient structures. The modules `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Congruence.Hom` and `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Congruence.Basic` give the kernels, the universal property and the isomorphism theorems. Three short modules treat products, opposites and star operations. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.PresentedMonoid.Basic` uses congruences to build a monoid from generators and relations. The theory is parallel to the theory of normal subgroups, and for a group the two theories agree.

### The library around it

#### Congruences and their lattice

Let $M$ be a type with a multiplication. A congruence on $M$ is an equivalence relation $\sim$ such that $w \sim x$ and $y \sim z$ imply $w y \sim x z$. In Lean, `Con M` is a structure that extends an equivalence relation, with the field `Con.toSetoid` and the compatibility field. A congruence coerces to a function `M → M → Prop`, so `c x y` means that $x$ and $y$ are related. The basic lemmas are `Con.refl`, `Con.symm`, `Con.trans` and `Con.mul`. The extensionality lemma `Con.ext` says that two congruences with the same relation are equal. The additive version is `AddCon M`, and `to_additive` translates each lemma.

The congruences on $M$ form a complete lattice (`Con.instCompleteLattice`), ordered by inclusion of the relations (`Con.le_def`). The infimum is the intersection of the relations (`Con.inf_iff_and`, `Con.coe_sInf`). The supremum is not the union, because a union of equivalence relations need not be transitive. The library describes the supremum with generated congruences.

The congruence generated by a relation $r$ is `conGen r`. It is defined by the inductive relation `ConGen.Rel r`, with the constructors `ConGen.Rel.of`, `ConGen.Rel.refl`, `ConGen.Rel.symm`, `ConGen.Rel.trans` and `ConGen.Rel.mul`. So two elements are related if a finite chain of these steps connects them.

**Proposition (`Con.conGen_le`).** For a relation $r$ and a congruence $c$, $\mathrm{conGen}(r) \le c$ if and only if $r \le c$.

*Proof.* If $\mathrm{conGen}(r) \le c$, then $r \le c$, since $r \le \mathrm{conGen}(r)$ by `Con.le_conGen`. Conversely, let $r \le c$. Induct on a proof of `ConGen.Rel r x y`. The case of a step of $r$ is the hypothesis, and the other cases are the reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity and compatibility of $c$. $\square$

So `conGen` is the left adjoint of the coercion from congruences to relations. The library packages this as the Galois insertion `Con.gi`. The equality `Con.conGen_of_con` says that a congruence generates itself. The supremum of two congruences is `Con.sup_eq_conGen`: $c \sqcup d = \mathrm{conGen}(c \lor d)$. The lemmas `Con.conGen_sup`, `Con.conGen_iSup` and `Con.conGen_mono` follow from the adjunction.

#### The quotient

The quotient of $M$ by $c$ is the type `c.Quotient`. There is a coercion `M → c.Quotient` (`Con.toQuotient`), and `Con.eq` says that the classes of $a$ and $b$ are equal if and only if `c a b`. The quotient inherits each algebraic structure of $M$ that the compatibility allows: `Con.semigroup`, `Con.monoid`, `Con.commMonoid`, `Con.group` and `Con.commGroup`. For a group, the compatibility with multiplication already implies the compatibility with inverses (`Con.inv`), because $x \sim y$ gives $x^{-1} = x^{-1} y y^{-1} \sim x^{-1} x y^{-1} = y^{-1}$. The coercion is multiplicative: `Con.coe_mul` and `Con.coe_one` are simp lemmas, and `Con.pow` and `Con.zpow` give compatibility with powers.

Proofs about the quotient use `Con.induction_on`, which reduces a statement to the classes of elements. Definitions on the quotient use `Con.liftOn` and `Con.liftOn₂`. The units of the quotient have their own induction principle `Con.induction_on_units`, and `Con.liftOnUnits` defines a function on them from pairs $(x, y)$ with $x y \sim 1$ and $y x \sim 1$.

The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Congruence.BigOperators` extends the compatibility to finite products. The lemma `Con.finsetProd` says that $f(i) \sim g(i)$ for all $i \in s$ implies $\prod_{i \in s} f(i) \sim \prod_{i \in s} g(i)$, and `Con.coe_finsetProd` says that the coercion sends a product to the product of the classes. The same holds for lists (`Con.list_prod`), multisets (`Con.multiset_prod`) and finitely supported functions (`Con.finsuppProd`).

#### Kernels and the universal property

The quotient map is `c.mk'`, a monoid homomorphism `M →* c.Quotient`. It is surjective (`Con.mk'_surjective`), and the simp lemma `Con.coe_mk'` says that it is the coercion. The kernel of a multiplicative map $f$ is the congruence `Con.ker f`, with $x \sim y$ if and only if $f(x) = f(y)$ (`Con.ker_rel`). The kernel of the quotient map is $c$ itself (`Con.mk'_ker`).

**Theorem (`Con.lift`).** Let $f \colon M \to P$ be a monoid homomorphism and let $c \le \ker f$. There is a unique homomorphism $\bar f \colon M/c \to P$ with $\bar f(\bar x) = f(x)$.

*Proof.* If $c(x, y)$, then $f(x) = f(y)$ by the hypothesis. So $\bar f(\bar x) = f(x)$ is well defined. It is a homomorphism because $f$ is one and the operations of $M/c$ are computed on representatives. It is unique because each element of $M/c$ is a class $\bar x$. $\square$

The computation rule is `Con.lift_mk'`, the uniqueness is `Con.lift_unique`, and `Con.hom_ext` is the extensionality principle for maps out of the quotient. For $c \le d$, the induced map is `Con.map c d h`. The kernel lift `Con.kerLift f` is the map $M/\ker f \to P$, and it is injective (`Con.kerLift_injective`).

**Theorem (first isomorphism theorem, `Con.quotientKerEquivRange`).** For a monoid homomorphism $f \colon M \to P$, $M/\ker f$ is isomorphic to the image of $f$.

*Proof.* The map `Con.kerLift f` lands in the image of $f$ and is surjective onto it. It is injective, because $\bar f(\bar x) = \bar f(\bar y)$ means $f(x) = f(y)$, which is $\ker f(x, y)$, which is $\bar x = \bar y$. $\square$

The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Congruence.Basic` gives the other forms. The isomorphism `Con.quotientKerEquivOfSurjective` treats a surjective $f$. The isomorphism `Con.comapQuotientEquiv` describes the quotient by a pulled back congruence. The third isomorphism theorem is `Con.quotientQuotientEquivQuotient`: for $c \le d$, the quotient of $M/c$ by the kernel of the map to $M/d$ is $M/d$. The correspondence theorem `Con.correspondence` is an order isomorphism between the congruences above $c$ and the congruences on $M/c$.

The same module gives a second view of a congruence. A congruence on a monoid is a submonoid of $M \times M$ that is an equivalence relation, by `Con.submonoid` and `Con.ofSubmonoid`. It also lifts scalar actions: if $\alpha$ acts on $M$ and the action is compatible with the multiplication (the class `IsScalarTower`), then $\alpha$ acts on the quotient (`Con.instSMul`, `Con.mulAction`).

Congruences move along maps. For a multiplicative map $f \colon M \to N$ and a congruence $d$ on $N$, the pullback is `Con.comap f H d`, where `H` is the proof that $f$ is multiplicative. The image of a congruence along an arbitrary map is the generated congruence `Con.mapGen`. For a surjective homomorphism whose kernel lies in $c$, `Con.mapOfSurjective` gives the image directly. Products and families of congruences are `Con.prod` and `Con.pi`.

#### Opposites and star operations

The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Congruence.Opposite` moves congruences to the opposite monoid $M^{\mathrm{op}}$, with the same underlying set and the reversed product. A congruence on $M$ is also a congruence on $M^{\mathrm{op}}$, because compatibility is symmetric in the two factors. The maps are `Con.op` and `Con.unop`, and `Con.orderIsoOp` is the order isomorphism between the two lattices. The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Congruence.Star` proves `conGen_star`: if a relation is stable under the star operation of a `StarMul` monoid, so is the congruence that it generates.

#### Groups and normal subgroups

For a group $G$, congruences correspond to normal subgroups. A normal subgroup $N$ gives the congruence `QuotientGroup.con N`, where $x \sim y$ if and only if $x^{-1} y \in N$. A congruence $c$ gives the normal subgroup of the elements related to $1$. The two quotients agree. The library uses normal subgroups for groups, because the quotient `G ⧸ N` has a better API, and uses congruences for monoids and for other operations. The ring analog is `RingCon`, in Volume 6.

#### Presented monoids

Let $\alpha$ be a type of generators and let `rels` be a relation on the free monoid `FreeMonoid α`. The presented monoid `PresentedMonoid rels` is the quotient of the free monoid by `conGen rels`. The quotient map is `PresentedMonoid.mk rels`, and the image of a generator is `PresentedMonoid.of rels x`. The lemma `PresentedMonoid.mk_eq_mk_iff` says that two words have the same image if and only if `conGen rels` relates them. The generators generate the monoid (`PresentedMonoid.closure_range_of`).

**Theorem (`PresentedMonoid.lift`).** Let $f \colon \alpha \to M$ be a map to a monoid such that the extension `FreeMonoid.lift f` takes equal values on each pair of related words. Then $f$ extends to a unique homomorphism from `PresentedMonoid rels` to $M$.

*Proof.* The kernel of `FreeMonoid.lift f` is a congruence that contains `rels`. By `Con.conGen_le` it contains `conGen rels`, so `Con.lift` gives the map. The uniqueness holds because the generators generate the monoid. $\square$

The computation rule is `PresentedMonoid.lift_of`, and `PresentedMonoid.ext` says that two homomorphisms that agree on the generators are equal. For groups, the analog is `PresentedGroup`, a quotient of the free group by the normal closure of a set of relators.

#### Common errors

1. The kernel of a monoid homomorphism as a congruence is `Con.ker f`, not `MonoidHom.mker f`. The second is the submonoid of the elements that go to $1$, and it does not determine the quotient of a monoid.
2. `Con.comap` takes the proof that the map is multiplicative as an explicit argument. With a bundled homomorphism `f`, the argument is `map_mul f`, and `Con.comap_eq` rewrites the pullback as a kernel.
3. The supremum of two congruences is not their union. Rewrite it with `Con.sup_eq_conGen` before working with its elements.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that the relation $x \sim y \iff x \equiv y \pmod n$ is a congruence on the monoid $(\mathbb{N}, \cdot)$, and that its quotient is the multiplicative monoid of $\mathbb{Z}/n$.
2. (A) Prove `Con.inv` by hand: in a group, a congruence is compatible with inverses.
3. (B) Show that in a group $G$ the elements related to $1$ by a congruence form a normal subgroup $N$, and that the congruence is `QuotientGroup.con N`.
4. (B) Find two congruences $c, d$ on $(\mathbb{N}, +)$ whose union is not transitive, and describe $c \sqcup d$.
5. (B) Describe `PresentedMonoid rels` for one generator $a$ and the single relation $a^2 = a$.
6. (C) Prove the correspondence theorem `Con.correspondence` on paper: the congruences on $M/c$ correspond to the congruences on $M$ that contain $c$, with the order preserved.
