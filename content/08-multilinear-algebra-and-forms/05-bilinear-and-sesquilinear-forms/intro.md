# Bilinear and sesquilinear forms

A bilinear form is a bilinear map $M \times M \to R$ with values in the ring of scalars. Forms measure lengths and angles in Euclidean geometry, but they also exist without any notion of positivity: the Minkowski form of relativity, the determinant form on $K^2$ and the trace form on matrices are examples. This chapter develops the general theory over a commutative ring, and over a field when dimensions are needed.

Section 1 defines bilinear forms and their matrices, and proves the rule $A \mapsto P^{\mathsf{T}} A P$ for a change of basis. It defines symmetric, alternating, reflexive and nondegenerate forms, and proves that a nondegenerate form on a finite-dimensional space identifies the space with its dual. It also proves that a reflexive form over a domain is symmetric or alternating, and it introduces the sesquilinear and Hermitian forms. Section 2 studies orthogonality: the dimension formula for the orthogonal of a subspace, the criterion for $V = W \oplus W^{\perp}$, the existence of an orthogonal basis for a symmetric form when $2$ is invertible, and the normal form of an alternating form. Section 3 constructs adjoints, describes isometries and the orthogonal and symplectic groups by matrix equations, and ends with perfect pairings of two modules.

The chapter uses the bilinear maps of Chapter 1, and the matrices, determinants and dual spaces of Volume 7, Chapters 3, 4 and 6. Chapter 6 builds on it: a quadratic form is a form evaluated on the diagonal, and its classification starts from the orthogonal bases of Section 2. Positive definite forms and inner product spaces are the subject of Volume 16.
