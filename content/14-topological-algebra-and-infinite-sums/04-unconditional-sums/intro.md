# Unconditional sums

A first course defines the sum of a series $\sum_{n \ge 0} a_n$ as the limit of the partial sums $a_0 + \cdots + a_{N-1}$. This definition uses the order of $\mathbb{N}$, and the value can change when the terms are put in another order. Mathlib takes a different definition. A family $(f_i)_{i \in I}$, indexed by any type $I$, has the sum $a$ when the finite partial sums $\sum_{i \in F} f_i$ tend to $a$ as the finite set $F \subseteq I$ grows. This sum does not depend on an order of $I$. It is the unconditional sum of Bourbaki, and it is the sum `tsum` of Mathlib, written `∑' i, f i`.

Section 1 defines the predicates `HasSum` and `Summable` and the function `tsum`, with the filter `atTop` on the finite subsets of $I$. It proves uniqueness and reindexing, explains the junk value $0$ of `tsum`, and shows that $\sum (-1)^{n+1}/n$ has a conditional sum and no unconditional sum. Section 2 proves the rules of computation: sums of sums, scalar multiples, the image under a continuous additive map, sums over a subset and its complement, sums over a product or a sigma type, and the Cauchy criterion in a complete group. Section 3 specializes to the index types $\mathbb{N}$ and $\mathbb{Z}$: partial sums over $\{0, \dots, n-1\}$, the tails of a sum, and sums over $\mathbb{Z}$ as two sums over $\mathbb{N}$.

The chapter needs Volume 13 for filters, limits and uniform spaces, Volume 15, Chapter 1, for real series, and Chapters 1 and 3 of this volume for topological groups and their uniform structure. Chapter 5 uses it to give criteria for summability, Chapter 6 for sums in $[0, \infty]$, and Chapter 7 for products.
