# Dynamics, ergodic theory and information theory

A dynamical system is a space $X$ and a rule that moves its points: a map $f : X \to X$ that one iterates, a flow $(\phi_t)_{t \in \mathbb{R}}$, or more generally the action of a group or a monoid. The subject asks what happens in the long run. Some points return to themselves, some orbits fill the space, some orbits converge to a closed set. When $X$ carries a measure that the map preserves, the questions become statistical: how often an orbit visits a set, and whether the time average of a function along an orbit equals its space average. Entropy measures how fast a system creates new information, and the same idea, for one random variable, is the subject of information theory. This volume teaches these three subjects as far as Mathlib has them, and their form in the library.

Chapter 1 studies the iterates $f^{[n]}$ of a map: fixed points, the contraction principle, periodic points and the minimal period, and Newton's map of a polynomial. Chapter 2 defines flows and invariant sets in a topological space, and the $\omega$-limit set of an orbit. Chapter 3 studies minimal and topologically transitive actions, the shift spaces of symbolic dynamics, and the Delone sets of aperiodic order. Chapter 4 studies the lifts of circle maps of degree one and Poincaré's rotation number, the first invariant of the theory: it is rational if and only if the map has a periodic orbit.

Chapter 5 starts the measured theory. It defines measure preserving maps and conservative maps, and proves the recurrence theorem of Poincaré: almost every point of a set of positive measure returns to the set infinitely often. Chapter 6 defines ergodicity, proves that irrational rotations of the circle and the maps $x \mapsto nx$ are ergodic, studies ergodic group actions, and proves the maximal ergodic theorem for Birkhoff sums. It states the mean ergodic theorem of von Neumann and the pointwise theorem of Birkhoff, and it constructs invariant measures on compact spaces by the method of Krylov and Bogolyubov. Chapter 7 defines the topological entropy of a map on a uniform space by dynamical covers and nets, after Bowen and Dinaburg, and proves that it does not increase under a factor map. Chapter 8 is information theory: prefix-free and uniquely decodable codes with the inequality of Kraft and McMillan, Shannon entropy, the Hamming distance, and the Kullback-Leibler divergence with its chain rule and the data processing inequality.

The volume needs Volume 13, General topology, for compactness, filters and uniform spaces; Volume 18, Measure and integration, for measures, null sets, the integral and the Radon-Nikodym derivative; and Volume 20, Probability, for probability measures, conditional expectation and kernels. Chapter 1 uses group actions from Volume 5, and Chapter 6 uses Hilbert spaces from Volume 16 for the mean ergodic theorem. Chapters 1 to 4 need no measure theory, and a reader who wants topological dynamics only can stop after Chapter 4 and then read Chapter 7. Chapters 5 and 6 need Chapter 1, and Chapter 8 needs only Volume 18 and Volume 20.

Mathlib states most notions for an action of a monoid or a group and gets the case of one map through the action of $\mathbb{N}$ or $\mathbb{Z}$ by iteration. It writes the iterate of a map as `f^[n]`. Its fixed and periodic points live in the namespace `Function`, its measured dynamics in `MeasureTheory` (for example `MeasureTheory.MeasurePreserving`), the topological entropy in `Dynamics`, and the information theory in `InformationTheory`. A theorem that needs only a quasi measure preserving map is stated for one, and the stronger hypothesis is a corollary. The library has several large theorems of the subject and lacks others: the index of this edition has the maximal ergodic theorem and the mean ergodic theorem, but no declaration for the pointwise ergodic theorem of Birkhoff, for the measure theoretic entropy of Kolmogorov and Sinai, or for Shannon's coding theorems. The text says in each section what the index has. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
