# The Lebesgue integral

This chapter defines the integral of a measurable function with values in $[0, \infty]$ and proves the limit theorems that make it useful. Section 1 defines simple functions and their integral, proves that every measurable function $f \ge 0$ is the increasing limit of simple functions, and defines $\int f \, d\mu$ as the supremum of the integrals of the simple functions below $f$. It also explains why Mathlib states this definition for every function, measurable or not, and why the values live in the extended nonnegative reals `ℝ≥0∞`. Section 2 proves the monotone convergence theorem, the additivity of the integral, the lemma of Fatou, the inequality of Markov and the dominated convergence theorem for functions with values in $[0, \infty]$. Section 3 turns the integral back into measures: a density $f$ defines the measure $\nu(A) = \int_A f \, d\mu$. The section proves the theorem of Radon and Nikodym, which says that every measure $\nu \ll \mu$ has this form when the measures are $\sigma$-finite, and the Lebesgue decomposition of a measure into an absolutely continuous part and a singular part.

The chapter needs the measurable functions of Chapter 1 and the measures, the almost everywhere filter and the restriction of a measure of Chapter 2. Lebesgue measure from Chapter 3 gives the examples on $\mathbb{R}$. It uses the order and the arithmetic of $[0, \infty]$ from Volume 4 and the filters and limits of Volume 13. Chapter 5 builds the Bochner integral of functions with values in a Banach space on top of this chapter, and its dominated convergence theorem rests on the one of Section 2. Chapter 6 uses the monotone convergence theorem in the proof of the theorem of Tonelli, and Chapter 7 uses the lemma of Fatou to prove that the spaces $L^p$ are complete.
