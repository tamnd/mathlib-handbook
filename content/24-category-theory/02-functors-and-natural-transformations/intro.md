# Functors and natural transformations

This chapter compares categories. A functor is a map between categories that sends objects to objects and morphisms to morphisms, and that keeps identities and composites. A natural transformation is a map between two functors, made of one morphism for each object, that commutes with the images of all morphisms. Equivalences of categories are the right notion of sameness for categories, weaker than isomorphism.

Section 1 defines functors, their composition and the properties of being faithful, full and fully faithful. It shows that a functor keeps isomorphisms, that a fully faithful functor reflects them, and that the functors between preorders and between monoids are the monotone maps and the homomorphisms. Section 2 defines natural transformations, the functor category, whiskering and horizontal composition. Its examples are the double dual of a vector space, the determinant and the abelianization. It also shows why the isomorphism of a finite-dimensional space with its dual is not natural. Section 3 defines equivalences of categories, with unit and counit, and proves that a functor is an equivalence when it is fully faithful and essentially surjective. Skeletons and the equivalence of matrices with finite-dimensional vector spaces end the chapter.

The chapter needs Chapter 1 and the examples of Volume 4, Chapter 2 (monotone maps), Volume 5 (homomorphisms and quotients), Volume 6, Chapter 1 and Volume 7 (duality and bases). Chapter 3 applies these notions to the functors of morphisms and proves the Yoneda lemma. Chapters 4 and 5 use functor categories for limits and adjunctions.
