# Connectedness

A space is connected if it does not split into two disjoint nonempty open pieces. This is the property behind the intermediate value theorem: the image of an interval under a continuous real function is again an interval. A stronger property asks that each two points are joined by a continuous path. The two properties differ, and the topologist's sine curve shows the difference.

Section 1 defines connected and preconnected sets, proves that intervals of $\mathbb{R}$ are connected, and derives the intermediate value theorem. It also gives the closure and union rules and the connected components. Section 2 studies paths and path connected sets. It proves that path connected sets are connected, that open connected subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ are path connected, and that the topologist's sine curve is connected and not path connected. Section 3 describes the library of irreducible sets and irreducible components, locally closed sets, Jacobson spaces, and the Krull and small inductive dimensions of a space. Section 4 describes the library of locally constant functions, discrete quotients and extremally disconnected spaces. Section 5 describes the library of clopen sets, locally connected spaces, totally disconnected and totally separated spaces, and the space of connected components.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 and 2. Section 2 uses the unit interval of Chapter 5. Sections 3 to 5 also use the separation axioms of Chapter 4 and compactness of Chapter 5.
