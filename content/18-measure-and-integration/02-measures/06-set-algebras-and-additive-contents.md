## Set algebras and additive contents

### Overview

The standard way to build a measure has three steps. First, choose a small family of sets on which the size is clear: the intervals of $\mathbb{R}$, the boxes of $\mathbb{R}^n$, or the cylinder sets of an infinite product. Second, define the size on this family and check that it is finitely additive. Third, prove countable additivity and extend to the generated σ-algebra by the theorem of Carathéodory. Section 2 gives the third step for outer measures. This section gives the language for the first two steps and the bridge to the third.

The families of sets are semirings, rings and algebras of sets. Semirings and rings come from Chapter 1, Section 2; this section adds algebras of sets and the algebra generated by a family. A finitely additive function on such a family is an additive content, the structure `MeasureTheory.AddContent`. The section gives the elementary properties of contents, two criteria for countable additivity, and the construction of the measure `MeasureTheory.AddContent.measure` from a σ-subadditive content. The Lebesgue measure of Chapter 3 and the product measures on infinite products of Chapter 6 are the main users.

### The library around it

#### Algebras of sets

A family $\mathcal{A}$ of subsets of $\alpha$ is an algebra of sets when it contains $\emptyset$ and is closed under complement and finite union. The predicate is `MeasureTheory.IsSetAlgebra`, and the constructor `MeasureTheory.IsSetAlgebra.mk` takes exactly these three facts. The other closure properties follow: `MeasureTheory.IsSetAlgebra.univ_mem`, `MeasureTheory.IsSetAlgebra.inter_mem`, `MeasureTheory.IsSetAlgebra.sdiff_mem`, and the finite versions `MeasureTheory.IsSetAlgebra.biUnion_mem` and `MeasureTheory.IsSetAlgebra.biInter_mem`. An algebra of sets is a ring of sets, `MeasureTheory.IsSetAlgebra.isSetRing`, so every result for rings applies.

The algebra generated by $\mathcal{A}$ is `MeasureTheory.generateSetAlgebra 𝒜`, an inductive definition with the generators, the empty set, complements and binary unions. Its induction principle is `MeasureTheory.generateSetAlgebra_induction`. It is the smallest algebra of sets that contains $\mathcal{A}$: `MeasureTheory.isSetAlgebra_generateSetAlgebra`, `MeasureTheory.self_subset_generateSetAlgebra` and `MeasureTheory.IsSetAlgebra.generateSetAlgebra_subset`. Two facts relate it to σ-algebras.

The algebra and the family generate the same σ-algebra, `MeasureTheory.generateFrom_generateSetAlgebra_eq`. The σ-algebra generated by $\mathcal{A}$ contains $\mathcal{A}$ and is an algebra of sets, so it contains the generated algebra; the other inclusion is clear. This lets a proof replace a generating family by an algebra, which has better closure properties.

A countable family generates a countable algebra, `MeasureTheory.countable_generateSetAlgebra`. Each member of the generated algebra is a finite union of finite intersections of generators and their complements, by `MeasureTheory.mem_generateSetAlgebra_elim`, and there are only countably many finite expressions of this kind. The σ-algebra generated by a countable family is not countable in general, so this result is the reason that countably generated σ-algebras have countable generating algebras.

#### Additive contents

Let $C$ be a family of sets and $G$ an additive commutative monoid. A term `m : MeasureTheory.AddContent G C` is a function $m$ on sets with $m(\emptyset) = 0$ and
$$m\Bigl(\bigcup_{u \in I} u\Bigr) = \sum_{u \in I} m(u)$$
for every finite set $I$ of pairwise disjoint members of $C$ whose union is in $C$. This is the constructor `MeasureTheory.AddContent.mk`. The function is defined on all sets, but only its values on $C$ matter. Two contents are equal when they agree on $C$, `MeasureTheory.AddContent.ext`. On a ring of sets the definition is easier to check: `MeasureTheory.IsSetRing.addContent_of_union` builds a content from additivity on pairs of disjoint sets.

The basic rules have names that start with addContent. On a ring of sets, `MeasureTheory.addContent_union` gives $m(s \cup t) = m(s) + m(t)$ for disjoint $s, t \in C$; on a semiring, `MeasureTheory.addContent_union'` needs the extra hypothesis $s \cup t \in C$. For an ordered monoid $G$ in which every element is nonnegative, such as `ℝ≥0∞`, a content is monotone, `MeasureTheory.addContent_mono`, and finitely subadditive, `MeasureTheory.addContent_sUnion_le_sum`: $m(\bigcup J) \le \sum_{u \in J} m(u)$ for a finite $J \subseteq C$ with union in $C$. The proof of subadditivity on a semiring is the main work of the file. One writes the union as a disjoint union of pieces $u \setminus (\text{earlier members})$; each piece is a finite disjoint union of members of $C$ by the semiring axiom, so additivity applies, and each piece lies inside one member of $J$. The rule `MeasureTheory.addContent_accumulate` gives the additivity for the partial unions of a disjoint sequence.

The main example is `MeasureTheory.AddContent.onIoc f` for a function $f : \alpha \to G$ on a linear order with values in a commutative group. It is defined on the semiring of half open intervals $(u, v]$ with $u \le v$, and `MeasureTheory.AddContent.onIoc_apply` gives
$$m\bigl((u, v]\bigr) = f(v) - f(u).$$
Additivity is a telescoping sum: if $(u, v]$ is a disjoint union of intervals $(u_i, u_{i+1}]$, the sum of the differences is $f(v) - f(u)$. With $f(x) = x$ this is the length of intervals, the starting point of the Lebesgue measure. A content on a semiring also extends to the finite unions of its members, `MeasureTheory.AddContent.supClosure`, and `MeasureTheory.AddContent.extend` gives the value $\infty$ to the sets outside $C$.

#### From finite to countable additivity

A content $m$ with values in `ℝ≥0∞` is σ-subadditive, `MeasureTheory.AddContent.IsSigmaSubadditive`, when $m(\bigcup_i f_i) \le \sum_i m(f_i)$ for every sequence $(f_i)$ in $C$ with union in $C$. On a semiring, a σ-subadditive content is σ-additive, `MeasureTheory.addContent_iUnion_eq_tsum_of_disjoint_of_IsSigmaSubadditive`: the inequality $\ge$ comes from monotonicity and finite additivity. On a ring the converse holds too, `MeasureTheory.isSigmaSubadditive_of_addContent_iUnion_eq_tsum`.

The usual criterion is continuity at the empty set. Let $C$ be a ring and let $m$ take finite values on $C$. Assume that $m(s_n) \to 0$ for every decreasing sequence $(s_n)$ in $C$ with empty intersection. Then `MeasureTheory.addContent_iUnion_eq_sum_of_tendsto_zero` says that $m$ is σ-additive. Proof sketch: for a disjoint sequence $(f_i)$ in $C$ with union $F \in C$, the sets $s_n = F \setminus \bigcup_{i \le n} f_i$ are in $C$, decrease and have empty intersection. Finite additivity gives $m(F) = \sum_{i \le n} m(f_i) + m(s_n)$, and the last term tends to $0$.

The file `Mathlib.MeasureTheory.Measure.AddContent.CompactSystem` gives a way to check continuity at the empty set with compactness. A compact system is a family $K$ of sets with this property: if a countable subfamily has empty intersection, then a finite subfamily already has empty intersection. The predicate is `IsCompactSystem`; the compact sets of a Hausdorff space form one. Assume that $m$ is inner regular for a compact system $K \subseteq C$: each $A \in C$ has a member of $K$ inside it with $m(A \setminus K) \le \varepsilon$. Then `MeasureTheory.tendsto_zero_of_innerRegular_addContent` proves continuity at the empty set, and `MeasureTheory.addContent_iUnion_eq_sum_of_innerRegular` gives σ-additivity. The idea: approximate each $s_n$ from inside by a compact $K_n$ with an error $\varepsilon 2^{-n}$. The intersection of the $K_n$ is empty, so a finite intersection $K_1 \cap \dots \cap K_N$ is empty, and then $m(s_N) \le \sum_{n \le N} m(s_n \setminus K_n) \le 2\varepsilon$. This is the argument behind the Kolmogorov extension theorem.

#### The measure of a σ-subadditive content

The file `Mathlib.MeasureTheory.OuterMeasure.OfAddContent` is the theorem of Carathéodory for contents. Let $C$ be a semiring and $m$ a σ-subadditive content on $C$. The outer measure induced by $m$, from Section 2, agrees with $m$ on $C$, `MeasureTheory.AddContent.inducedOuterMeasure_eq`. The inequality $\le$ uses the cover of $s$ by itself; the inequality $\ge$ is σ-subadditivity applied to the pieces $s \cap u_i$ of a cover $(u_i)$, after each piece is split into members of $C$. Every member of $C$ is Carathéodory measurable, `MeasureTheory.AddContent.isCaratheodory_inducedOuterMeasure_of_mem`, so every set of the generated σ-algebra is, `MeasureTheory.AddContent.isCaratheodory_inducedOuterMeasure`.

Two measures come out. The measure `MeasureTheory.AddContent.measureCaratheodory` lives on the σ-algebra of Carathéodory measurable sets. The measure `MeasureTheory.AddContent.measure` lives on a given σ-algebra $m_\alpha$ with $m_\alpha \le \sigma(C)$, and is the restriction of the first. When $C$ generates $m_\alpha$, `MeasureTheory.AddContent.measure_eq` says that the measure agrees with the content on $C$:
$$\mu(s) = m(s) \quad \text{for } s \in C.$$
Uniqueness comes from Section 3: two finite measures that agree on a generating π-system are equal, and a semiring is a π-system.

#### Common errors

1. Using `MeasureTheory.addContent_union` on a semiring. It needs a ring; on a semiring use `MeasureTheory.addContent_union'` and prove that the union is in $C$.
2. Reading the values of a content outside $C$. The function `⇑m` is defined on all sets, but no lemma controls its values outside $C$. Use `MeasureTheory.AddContent.extend` when a value $\infty$ outside $C$ is needed.
3. Forgetting the finiteness hypothesis in `MeasureTheory.addContent_iUnion_eq_sum_of_tendsto_zero`. Continuity at the empty set does not give σ-additivity for a content with infinite values, because $m(F) = \sum_{i \le n} m(f_i) + m(s_n)$ gives no information when $m(s_n) = \infty$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that the finite subsets of $\mathbb{N}$ and their complements form an algebra of sets, and that it is the algebra generated by the singletons.
2. (A) Use `MeasureTheory.AddContent.onIoc_apply` to compute the content of $(0, 3]$ for $f(x) = x^2$, and check additivity on $(0, 1] \cup (1, 3]$.
3. (B) Prove `MeasureTheory.generateFrom_generateSetAlgebra_eq` from `MeasureTheory.IsSetAlgebra.generateSetAlgebra_subset`.
4. (B) On the algebra of Exercise 1, let $m(s) = 0$ for finite $s$ and $m(s) = 1$ for cofinite $s$. Show that $m$ is an additive content that is not σ-additive.
5. (B) Let $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be monotone and right continuous. Prove that `MeasureTheory.AddContent.onIoc f` is continuous at the empty set on decreasing sequences of intervals.
6. (C) Prove the compact system argument of `MeasureTheory.tendsto_zero_of_innerRegular_addContent` in full.
