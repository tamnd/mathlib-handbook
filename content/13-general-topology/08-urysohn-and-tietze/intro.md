# Urysohn and Tietze

Normality is a statement about open sets: two disjoint closed sets have disjoint open neighbourhoods. The two theorems of this chapter turn it into a statement about functions. The lemma of Urysohn gives a continuous function that is $0$ on one closed set and $1$ on the other. The theorem of Tietze extends each continuous real function on a closed set to the whole space.

Section 1 proves the lemma of Urysohn with dyadic rationals, and derives that compact Hausdorff spaces and metric spaces have many continuous functions. It defines completely regular spaces. Section 2 proves the theorem of Tietze by uniform approximation, first for bounded functions and then for all real functions.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 to 5. The proofs use the normality results of Chapter 4 and the uniform limits of Chapter 3.
