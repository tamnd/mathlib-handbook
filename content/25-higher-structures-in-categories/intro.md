# Higher structures in categories

This volume continues Volume 24. Volume 24 taught the core of category theory: categories, functors, the Yoneda lemma, limits, adjunctions, monads and monoidal categories. It ended with the definitions of preadditive, additive and abelian categories. This volume studies the structures that modern algebra and geometry put on a category, and their form in Mathlib. The categories of modules, of sheaves and of chain complexes up to homotopy are the main examples. Each of them has more structure than a bare category: addition of morphisms, exact sequences, a shift functor, a topology of covers, or a small set of objects that builds all others. The volume gives each structure with its axioms, proves the main theorems on paper, and shows the Mathlib names.

Chapter 1 treats preadditive, additive and linear categories. It proves that morphisms between biproducts are matrices, it builds the category of matrices, and it gives the idempotent completion. Chapter 2 develops abelian categories from the definition to the diagram lemmas. It proves the factorization of each morphism, the four, five and snake lemmas with pseudoelements, and it treats exact functors, projective objects and Grothendieck abelian categories. Chapter 3 gives the language of classes of objects and morphisms that the later chapters use. It treats separators, simple and Noetherian objects, lifting properties and the small object argument.

Chapter 4 constructs the localization of a category at a class of morphisms, by its universal property and by the calculus of fractions. Chapter 5 defines shifts, pretriangulated categories and triangulated categories. It proves that representable functors turn distinguished triangles into long exact sequences, and it gives the octahedral axiom and triangulated functors. These two chapters are the base of the derived category, which is in Volume 26.

Chapter 6 gives sites and sheaves. It defines sieves and Grothendieck topologies, states the sheaf condition, constructs sheafification by the plus construction, and treats the coherent, regular and extensive topologies. Chapter 7 treats size and exactness. It defines presentable and accessible categories, and it studies extensive, adhesive, regular and locally cartesian closed categories and subobject classifiers. Chapter 8 gives the Galois categories of Grothendieck. It proves that a Galois category with a fiber functor is equivalent to the category of the finite sets with a continuous action of a profinite group, the fundamental group. Chapter 9 studies continuous and cocontinuous functors between sites, dense subsites and points of a site. Chapter 10 treats hypercovers, descent data and stacks, and the cohomology of abelian sheaves.

The volume needs Volume 24 in full. The examples use groups from Volume 5, rings from Volume 6, modules from Volume 7, and topological spaces and topological groups from Volumes 13 and 14. Chapters 1 and 2 come first, and Chapter 3 is used by Chapters 4 and 5. Chapter 5 uses Chapter 4. Chapters 7 and 8 need only Chapters 1 to 3, except that Chapter 7 uses the sheaves of Chapter 6 as examples. Chapter 6 also uses the localization of Chapter 4, and Chapters 9 and 10 build on Chapter 6. The level is that of a research seminar. The references are Kashiwara and Schapira, *Categories and Sheaves*, the book of Adámek and Rosický on presentable categories, and the notes of Lenstra on Galois theory for schemes.

The conventions are those of Volume 24. Mathlib writes `f ≫ g` for the composite $g \circ f$ of the textbook, and a construction such as `kernel f` takes a class like `HasKernel f` as a hypothesis. Many statements in these folders are instances, so the boxes give the instance names where the theorem has no other name. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
