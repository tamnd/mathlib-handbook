# Tensor powers, graded algebras and the tensor and symmetric algebras

Chapter 2 makes one module $M \otimes_R N$ out of two. This chapter makes one ring out of all the tensor powers of one module. The ring is the tensor algebra $T(M)$, the most general associative algebra that contains $M$. Its commutative quotient is the symmetric algebra $S(M)$, which is a coordinate free form of the polynomial ring.

Section 1 builds the tensor product $\bigotimes_i M_i$ of a family of modules and proves its universal property: it turns each multilinear map of Chapter 1 into a linear map. It proves that the pure tensors of basis vectors form a basis, so that $M^{\otimes n}$ has rank $d^n$ when $M$ has rank $d$. The section then defines direct sums and graded algebras, in the external and the internal form, and shows that the tensor powers form a graded algebra.

Section 2 defines $T(M)$ and $S(M)$ and proves their universal properties. A linear map from $M$ to any algebra extends to $T(M)$, and a linear map to a commutative algebra extends to $S(M)$. For a free module with a basis indexed by $\kappa$, the tensor algebra is the free algebra on $\kappa$ and the symmetric algebra is the polynomial ring in the variables $\kappa$.

Section 3 is a guide to the library on gradings by submodules: the components of a product, graded actions and graded maps, idempotents and traces from a decomposition, and the graded tensor product with the Koszul sign. Section 4 is a guide to the library on $\bigotimes_i M_i$: generators, tensor products of direct sums, multilinear maps out of direct sums, the dual of a tensor product, symmetric tensor powers, and holors with their rank.

The chapter needs the multilinear maps of Chapter 1, the tensor product of Chapter 2, the bases of Volume 7, Chapter 2, and the polynomial rings of Volume 6, Chapter 5. All the results hold over a commutative ring.

Chapter 4 applies the method of Section 2 to the relation $x \otimes x = 0$. The quotient is the exterior algebra, the home of the determinant. Chapter 7 uses the relation $x \otimes x = Q(x)$ for a quadratic form $Q$, and the quotient is the Clifford algebra. Both chapters use the gradings of Section 1: by $\mathbb{N}$ for the exterior algebra and by $\mathbb{Z}/2$ for the Clifford algebra.
