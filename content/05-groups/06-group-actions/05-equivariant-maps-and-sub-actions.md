## Equivariant maps and sub-actions

### Overview

An action of a monoid $M$ on a set $X$ is a structure on $X$, in the same way as a group law is a structure on a set. So it has its own maps and its own substructures. A map $f : X \to Y$ between two $M$-sets is equivariant when $f(m \cdot x) = m \cdot f(x)$ for all $m$ and $x$. A subset $S \subseteq X$ is a sub-action when $m \cdot s \in S$ for all $m \in M$ and $s \in S$. The orbits of a group action are the smallest nonempty sub-actions, and the orbit map $g \mapsto g \cdot x$ is an equivariant map from $G$ to $X$.

This section describes the library of these two notions. The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.GroupAction.Hom` defines equivariant maps, also the maps that are equivariant with respect to a change of the acting monoid, and the versions that also respect an additive, a multiplicative or a ring structure. The module `Mathlib.GroupTheory.GroupAction.SubMulAction` defines sub-actions, and its two companion modules give the sub-action generated by a set and the product of two sub-actions. The last module, `Mathlib.GroupTheory.GroupAction.Embedding`, lets a group act on the injective maps into an $M$-set.

The section uses the actions, orbits and stabilizers of the first section of this chapter, and the homomorphisms and subgroups of Chapters 2 and 3. Modules over a ring are the main source of examples in later volumes: a linear map is an equivariant map that also respects addition.

### The library around it

#### Equivariant maps

The structure `MulActionHom φ X Y` takes a map $\varphi : M \to N$ of the acting types, an $M$-set $X$ and an $N$-set $Y$. Its terms are the maps $f : X \to Y$ with $f(m \cdot x) = \varphi(m) \cdot f(x)$. The notation is `X →ₑ[φ] Y`. When $M = N$ and $\varphi$ is the identity, the notation is `X →[M] Y`, and the condition is the usual equivariance $f(m \cdot x) = m \cdot f(x)$. The map $\varphi$ is only a function here, not a monoid homomorphism. It is a homomorphism in all the examples, but the definition does not need this.

The class `MulActionSemiHomClass F φ X Y` says that the terms of a type `F` of functions are $\varphi$-equivariant, and `MulActionHomClass F M X Y` is the case $\varphi = \mathrm{id}$. The general lemma `map_smul` states $f(m \cdot x) = m \cdot f(x)$ for each term of such a class. So a linear map, a ring homomorphism that is equivariant, and a bundled equivariant map all use the same lemma. The function `MulActionSemiHomClass.toMulActionHom` turns a term of such a class into a bundled `MulActionHom`, and a coercion does the same automatically. The lemma `MulActionHom.map_smul` is the version for the bundled structure.

The category structure has the usual names. `MulActionHom.id` is the identity, `MulActionHom.comp` is the composition, and `MulActionHom.ext` says that two maps with the same values are equal. The composition of a $\psi$-equivariant map with a $\varphi$-equivariant map is $\chi$-equivariant when $\chi = \psi \circ \varphi$. Lean finds $\chi$ through the class `CompTriple φ ψ χ`, so the composition of two maps over the identity is again over the identity with no cast. When the two functions $\varphi$ and $\varphi'$ are equal but not syntactically equal, `MulActionHom.ofEq` moves a map from one to the other.

**Proposition.** Let $f : X \to Y$ be an equivariant bijection. Then its inverse map is equivariant.

*Proof.* Let $g$ be the inverse of $f$. For $m \in M$ and $y \in Y$ the element $y$ is $f(g(y))$. So $g(m \cdot y) = g(m \cdot f(g(y))) = g(f(m \cdot g(y))) = m \cdot g(y)$. $\square$

The library form is `MulActionHom.inverse`. It takes $f$, a function $g$ and the two proofs `Function.LeftInverse g f` and `Function.RightInverse g f`, and it gives `Y →[M] X`. The function `MulActionHom.inverse'` is the version over a map $\varphi$ that has an inverse $\varphi'$ on the acting side, and `MulActionHom.comp_inverse'` computes the composite.

Injective equivariant maps carry some properties back. The lemma `FaithfulSMul.of_injective` says: if $f : X \to Y$ is injective and equivariant and the action on $X$ is faithful, then the action on $Y$ is faithful. Proof: if $m_1 \cdot y = m_2 \cdot y$ for all $y$, then $f(m_1 \cdot x) = m_1 \cdot f(x) = m_2 \cdot f(x) = f(m_2 \cdot x)$ for all $x$, so $m_1 \cdot x = m_2 \cdot x$ by injectivity, and $m_1 = m_2$ by faithfulness on $X$. The lemma `IsSMulRegular.of_injective` is the same argument for one element.

#### Constructions of equivariant maps

The module gives the standard constructions. The projections `MulActionHom.fst` and `MulActionHom.snd` and the pairing `MulActionHom.prod` make the product $X \times Y$ the product in the category of $M$-sets, with `MulActionHom.fst_comp_prod` as the first defining equation. The map `MulActionHom.prodMap` is $f \times g$. On a product of a family, `Pi.evalMulActionHom` evaluates at one index. When the actions of $M$ and $N$ on $X$ commute, the action of a fixed element of $M$ is an $N$-equivariant map: this is `SMulCommClass.toMulActionHom`.

The maps from a fixed $X$ into $Y$ inherit the algebra of $Y$. If $Y$ is an additive commutative group with an action that is compatible with the addition, then `X →ₑ[φ] Y` is an additive commutative group, with the pointwise operations. If $Y$ is a monoid with a multiplicative action, then `MulActionHom.instMonoid` makes the maps a monoid, and `MulActionHom.instSemiring` makes them a semiring when $Y$ is a semiring with a ring action. The lemma `MulActionHom.coe_smul` states that the scalar action on maps is pointwise.

**Proposition.** The equivariant maps from a monoid $M$ to itself, for the action by left multiplication, form a monoid isomorphic to the opposite monoid $M^{\mathrm{op}}$.

*Proof.* Let $f$ be equivariant. Then $f(m) = f(m \cdot 1) = m \cdot f(1) = m f(1)$. So $f$ is right multiplication by $a = f(1)$, and each $a$ gives such a map. If $f$ and $g$ are right multiplication by $a$ and $b$, then $f \circ g$ sends $m$ to $m b a$. So the map $f \mapsto f(1)$ reverses the order of the product, and it is an isomorphism onto $M^{\mathrm{op}}$. $\square$

The library form is `MulActionHom.End.equivMulOpposite : (M →[M] M) ≃* Mᵐᵒᵖ`. The companion `MulActionHom.End.mulOppositeEquiv` is the isomorphism with $M$ for the action of $M^{\mathrm{op}}$ by right multiplication.

#### Equivariant maps with more structure

Three structures add an algebraic condition to equivariance. Each has a notation of the same shape and a coercion to `MulActionHom`.

| Lean name | Notation | Extra condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `DistribMulActionHom` | `A →ₑ+[φ] B`, `A →+[M] B` | $f$ is an additive monoid homomorphism |
| `MulDistribMulActionHom` | `A →ₑ*[φ] B`, `A →*[M] B` | $f$ is a monoid homomorphism |
| `MulSemiringActionHom` | `R →ₑ+*[φ] S`, `R →+*[M] S` | $f$ is a ring homomorphism |

In these three structures $\varphi$ is a monoid homomorphism `M →* N`, and the composition uses the class `MonoidHom.CompTriple`. The forgetful maps have the expected names: `DistribMulActionHom.toMulActionHom`, `DistribMulActionHom.toAddMonoidHom`, `MulDistribMulActionHom.toMonoidHom`, `MulSemiringActionHom.toRingHom` and `MulSemiringActionHom.toDistribMulActionHom`. The class `DistribMulActionSemiHomClass` plays the role of `MulActionSemiHomClass`. The lemma `DistribMulActionHom.ext_ring` says that an equivariant additive map out of a semiring $R$, for the action of $R$ on itself, is fixed by its value at $1$, by the same computation as in the proposition above.

#### Sub-actions

The structure `SubMulAction R M` has a carrier set and the field that the set is closed under the action of $R$. It is a `SetLike` structure, so membership, the coercion to a set and the extensionality lemma `SubMulAction.ext` work as for subgroups. The class `SMulMemClass S R M` is the general version for a type `S` of subsets that are closed under the action, and `SMulMemClass.smul_mem` is its closure lemma. Submodules and ideals are instances, so lemmas that are stated for the class apply to all of them. The lemma `SMulMemClass.ofIsScalarTower` moves the class along a scalar tower.

A sub-action is again an $R$-set: `SubMulAction.mulAction` gives the action on the subtype, and `SubMulAction.val_smul` says that the coercion to $M$ commutes with the action. The inclusion is the equivariant map `SubMulAction.subtype`, with `SubMulAction.subtype_injective`. The sub-actions of $M$ form a complete lattice by `SubMulAction.instCompleteLattice`. The infimum is the intersection, and the supremum is the union: `SubMulAction.mem_iSup` states $x \in \sup_i p_i \iff \exists i, x \in p_i$. This differs from subgroups, where the union of two subgroups is not a subgroup in general. A union of sub-actions is a sub-action, because the closure condition involves one element at a time.

**Proposition.** Let $G$ be a group acting on $X$, and let $S \subseteq X$ be a sub-action. Then the complement $X \setminus S$ is a sub-action.

*Proof.* Let $x \notin S$ and $g \in G$. If $g \cdot x \in S$, then $x = g^{-1} \cdot (g \cdot x) \in S$, a contradiction. So $g \cdot x \notin S$. $\square$

The library form is the instance `SubMulAction.instCompl`, with `SubMulAction.compl_def` for the carrier. So for a group action the sub-actions form a Boolean algebra of sets, and each one is a union of orbits. The same argument gives `SubMulAction.smul_mem_iff'`: $g \cdot x \in p \iff x \in p$ for a group element $g$. The lemma `SubMulAction.smul_mem_iff` is the version for a nonzero element of a group with zero.

Orbits and stabilizers in a sub-action are the same as in the whole set. The lemma `SubMulAction.stabilizer_of_subMul` says that the stabilizer of an element $m$ of $p$ for the action on $p$ is the stabilizer of $m$ in $M$. The lemmas `SubMulAction.mem_orbit_subMul_iff` and `SubMulAction.val_image_orbit` say the same for orbits, and `SubMulAction.orbitRel_of_subMul` gives the orbit relation. For a normal subgroup $H$ of $G$, the fixed points of $H$ form a $G$-sub-action, `fixedPointsSubMulOfNormal`: if $h \cdot x = x$ for all $h \in H$, then $h \cdot (g \cdot x) = g \cdot ((g^{-1} h g) \cdot x) = g \cdot x$. For a module, a nonempty sub-action contains $0$ by `SubMulAction.zero_mem`, and `SubMulAction.neg_mem` says that it is closed under negation.

#### Generated sub-actions

The sub-action generated by a set $s$ is `SubMulAction.closure R s`. It is the smallest sub-action that contains $s$. The Galois connection with the coercion to sets is `SubMulAction.closure_le`: $\operatorname{closure}(s) \le p \iff s \subseteq p$. From it come `SubMulAction.subset_closure`, `SubMulAction.mem_closure_of_mem` and `SubMulAction.closure_mono`. The lemma `SubMulAction.mem_closure` states membership as an intersection of all sub-actions that contain $s$. For a monoid action, the closure of $s$ is the set of the $m \cdot x$ with $m \in R$ and $x \in s$, but the library states only the abstract form. A sub-action is finitely generated, `SubMulAction.FG`, when it is the closure of a finite set, and `SubMulAction.fg_iff` states this with a `Finset`.

#### Products of sub-actions

Let $M$ be a monoid on which $R$ acts, with `IsScalarTower R M M`, so that $r \cdot (y z) = (r \cdot y) z$. Then the product set $p q = \{y z : y \in p, z \in q\}$ of two sub-actions is again a sub-action, because $r \cdot (y z) = (r \cdot y) z$. The library gives `SubMulAction.coe_mul` and `SubMulAction.mem_mul` for this product. The unit is the orbit of $1$: `SubMulAction.coe_one` states $1 = \{r \cdot 1 : r \in R\}$, and `SubMulAction.mem_one` gives the membership form. With `SMulCommClass R M M` also, `SubMulAction.instMonoid` makes the sub-actions a monoid, and `SubMulAction.coe_pow` computes $p^n$ for $n \ne 0$. The power $p^0$ is the unit, not the set $\{1\}$, so the hypothesis $n \ne 0$ is needed.

#### Actions on embeddings

A group $G$ that acts on $\beta$ acts on the set of injective maps $\alpha \hookrightarrow \beta$ by composition: $(g \cdot f)(a) = g \cdot f(a)$. The composite is again injective, because the action of $g$ is a bijection of $\beta$. The instance is `Function.Embedding.instMulAction`. The instance `Function.Embedding.instIsSMulApply` says that the action is pointwise, so the general lemmas `smul_apply` and `FunLike.coe_smul` give the values. The lemma `Function.Embedding.smul_def` writes $g \cdot f$ as the composite of $f$ with the embedding of the permutation `MulAction.toPerm g`. For $\alpha = \operatorname{Fin} n$ this action is the action of $G$ on ordered $n$-tuples of distinct points, which is the action in the definition of an $n$-transitive group.

#### Common errors

1. The notation `X →[M] Y` means `MulActionHom (@id M) X Y`. A map over a monoid homomorphism `φ : M →* N` uses `X →ₑ[⇑φ] Y`, with the coercion of $\varphi$ to a function. The notation for the additive, multiplicative and ring versions takes `MonoidHom.id M` instead, so the plain function `id` does not work there.
2. The supremum of sub-actions is the union, but the supremum of submodules is the sum. A lemma about `SubMulAction` does not transfer to `Submodule` by a change of name, because the two lattices are different.
3. The product `p * q` of sub-actions needs `IsScalarTower R M M`, and the monoid structure also needs `SMulCommClass R M M`. For a noncommutative ring acting on itself on the left, the second instance can fail, and then only the semigroup `SubMulAction.instSemigroup` is there.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $G$ act on $X$ and let $x \in X$. Show that $g \mapsto g \cdot x$ is an equivariant map from $G$, with the action by left multiplication, to $X$, and that its image is the orbit of $x$.
2. (A) Show that the image of a sub-action under an equivariant map is a sub-action, and that the preimage of a sub-action is a sub-action.
3. (A) Let $G$ act on $X$. Show that the sub-actions of $X$ are exactly the unions of orbits. Deduce that the lattice of sub-actions is isomorphic to the lattice of subsets of the set of orbits.
4. (B) Let $H \le G$. Show that the $G$-equivariant maps from $G/H$ to a $G$-set $X$ correspond to the points of $X$ that are fixed by $H$.
5. (B) Let $G$ act on $\beta$, and let $n \le |\beta|$. Show that $G$ acts transitively on the embeddings $\operatorname{Fin} n \hookrightarrow \beta$ if and only if $G$ acts transitively on the ordered $n$-tuples of distinct points of $\beta$.
6. (C) Let $M$ be a commutative monoid acting on itself by multiplication. Describe the sub-actions of $M$ and the product of two of them. Show that for $M = \mathbb{N}$ under multiplication the finitely generated sub-actions are the sets of multiples of the elements of a finite set.
