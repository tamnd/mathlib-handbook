# Bounded linear maps and the operator norm

This chapter studies the linear maps between normed spaces. Section 1 proves that a linear map is continuous if and only if it is bounded, that is, if it multiplies norms by at most a constant. It explains the bundled type `E →L[𝕜] F` of continuous linear maps and the constructor `LinearMap.mkContinuous` that turns a bound into a continuous map. Its examples show the two sides: integration on $C[0,1]$ is bounded, differentiation on the polynomials is not, and each infinite dimensional normed space has a discontinuous linear functional. Section 2 defines the operator norm, the best constant of the bound, and proves that it is a norm, that it is submultiplicative and that the space of bounded operators into a complete space is complete. It then proves with the Neumann series that the invertible operators on a Banach space form an open set, computes the operator norm of a matrix, and introduces linear isometries and the dual space. Section 3 studies the bounded homomorphisms between seminormed groups without scalars, the type `NormedAddGroupHom`. Section 4 gives the continuous multilinear and alternating maps with their operator norm. Section 5 treats continuous bilinear maps as maps into a space of operators, with multiplication in a normed algebra as the main example.

The chapter needs Chapter 1 and the linear algebra of Volume 7. Chapter 3 shows that in finite dimension each linear map is continuous. Chapter 4 uses the completeness of the space of operators and the Baire category theorem to prove the theorem of Banach-Steinhaus and the open mapping theorem, and Chapter 5 studies the dual space with the theorem of Hahn-Banach.
