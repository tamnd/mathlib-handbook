# Charted spaces and structure groupoids

A manifold is a space that looks like a model space near each of its points. This chapter builds the first two layers of that notion. It does not yet use any derivative. All its objects are topological, and the model space $H$ is an arbitrary topological space.

Section 1 defines partial homeomorphisms and charted spaces. A charted space is a topological space $M$ with an atlas of partial homeomorphisms $M \to H$ whose sources cover $M$, and with a preferred chart at each point. The atlas is data. It carries no condition on the changes of charts. The section proves that local properties of $H$ pass to $M$, such as local compactness and the separation axiom $T_1$, and shows that the Hausdorff property does not pass. It builds the charted spaces of products and the charted space of a set with charts but no topology.

Section 2 adds the condition on the changes of charts. A structure groupoid on $H$ is a family of partial homeomorphisms of $H$ that is closed under composition, inverse, restriction and gluing. A charted space has a groupoid structure if all its changes of charts are in the groupoid. The section defines the maximal atlas, groupoids from pregroupoids, and local invariant properties. Its main theorem says that a local invariant property of maps in the model gives a property of maps between charted spaces that one can test in any pair of compatible charts. Chapter 3 uses this theorem to define smooth maps.

The chapter needs topological spaces, open sets, neighbourhoods and continuity from Volume 13, Chapter 1, and the separation axioms from Volume 13, Chapter 4. Chapter 2 adds the models with corners, which embed $H$ into a normed space so that the changes of charts can be differentiated.
