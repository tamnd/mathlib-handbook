# Integral domains and fields

This chapter studies the multiplication of a ring: which elements have inverses, and which products are zero. Section 1 defines units, zero divisors, nilpotent elements and integral domains, proves that a ring is a domain exactly when the cancellation law holds, and computes the units of $\mathbb{Z}$, of $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, of products and of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2]$. Section 2 defines division rings, fields and subfields, proves that a finite integral domain is a field and that homomorphisms from a field are injective, and constructs the field $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt 2)$, a field with four elements and the quaternions. It also explains the convention $0^{-1} = 0$ of Mathlib. Section 3 builds the field of fractions of an integral domain, proves its universal property, and shows that it is unique up to a unique isomorphism.

The chapter needs Chapter 1 for rings, homomorphisms and subrings, and Chapter 2 for ideals and quotients, which give the construction of the field with four elements and the proof that homomorphisms from fields are injective. The group of units uses the units of a monoid from Volume 5, Chapter 1.

Chapter 4 applies the results to $\mathbb{Z}/n$: it is a field exactly when $n$ is prime, and the characteristic of an integral domain is $0$ or a prime. Chapter 5 shows that a polynomial ring over a domain is a domain, and Chapter 6 develops the arithmetic of integral domains: divisibility, primes and unique factorization. The ordered fields of Chapter 7 are fields with an order, and the field of fractions returns in Volume 9 as the localization at the non-zero-divisors.
