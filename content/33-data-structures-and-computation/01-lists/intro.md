# Lists

This chapter studies the list, the first data structure of functional programming and of Mathlib. The first section defines the basic operations: length, append, map, filter, take and drop, access by index, the lists built from ranges and functions, zips, the cuts of a list into blocks, products, sections and the antidiagonal of a natural number. It proves the induction principles that start from the right end or take two steps at a time, and it introduces the predicate TFAE for a list of equivalent propositions. The second section compares lists. It defines sublists, prefixes, suffixes and infixes, counts the $2^n$ sublists of a list of length $n$, and studies the lexicographic and shortlex orders, the lifting of a relation to lists, palindromes, periods with the lemma of Fine and Wilf, lists of primes, sign variations, off-diagonal pairs, symmetric powers and duplicates.

The chapter needs the language of Lean and Mathlib, induction on natural numbers and on inductive types, and the elementary facts on sets, finite types and orders. The basic API of lists is in the core library of Lean, so the text often names a core lemma in prose and a Mathlib lemma in a box. A reader who knows the binomial coefficients and the greatest common divisor can check all the counts and the periodicity proof by hand.

Chapter 2 continues with the lists that have no duplicates, the permutation relation, sorting by insertion and by merging, and the cycles obtained from lists by rotation; it uses the sublist relation and the induction principles of this chapter. Chapter 3 builds finite maps from lists of key and value pairs. The vectors of Chapter 4 are lists of a fixed length, and the lazy sequences of Chapter 5 are the possibly infinite analogues of lists.
