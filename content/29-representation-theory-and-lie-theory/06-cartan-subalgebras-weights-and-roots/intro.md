# Cartan subalgebras, weights and roots

This chapter takes a Lie algebra apart along a nilpotent subalgebra. Section 1 defines Cartan subalgebras as nilpotent self-normalizing subalgebras, proves their existence over infinite fields through Engel subalgebras and regular elements, and develops the generalized weight spaces of a module over a nilpotent Lie algebra: they are submodules, they are independent, they fill a triangularizable module, and in characteristic $0$ every weight is linear. Applied to a Cartan subalgebra $H$ acting on $L$, this gives the root space decomposition, with $L_0 = H$. Section 2 adds the hypothesis that the Killing form is nondegenerate. It builds an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triple for each nonzero root, proves that the eigenvalues of $h$ on primitive vectors are natural numbers, and deduces that root spaces are lines, that root chains have no gaps and that the roots form a reduced crystallographic root system, irreducible exactly when $L$ is simple.

The chapter needs Lie algebras, Lie modules, subalgebras, ideals and normalizers from Chapter 4, and nilpotent and semisimple Lie algebras, Engel's theorem and the Killing form from Chapter 5. It uses eigenvalues, generalized eigenspaces and the characteristic polynomial from Volume 7, Chapter 5, duality from Volume 7, Chapter 6, and nondegenerate bilinear forms from Volume 8, Chapter 5. The examples are $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with its $2 \times 2$ matrices and $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ with its six roots.

Chapter 7 studies root systems on their own, as root pairings in Mathlib: reflections, the Weyl group, the canonical bilinear form, bases and Cartan matrices. The root system of Section 2 is one instance of that theory. The last section of Chapter 7 goes back to Lie algebras and builds a simple Lie algebra from a root system, so it inverts the construction of this chapter.
