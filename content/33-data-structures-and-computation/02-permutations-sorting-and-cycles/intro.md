# Permutations, sorting and cycles

This chapter studies three ways to forget part of the structure of a list. The first section treats lists without duplicates and their bijection with `Fin n`, the operation `dedup`, the permutation relation with its characterization by counts, the relation `Subperm`, the invariance of left commutative folds, the list of all permutations with its length $n!$, and union, intersection and difference. The second section treats the predicates `Pairwise`, `Triplewise` and `IsChain`, the operation `destutter`, the sorted predicates `SortedLE` and `SortedLT`, insertion sort and merge sort, the proofs that both give sorted permutations, the uniqueness of the sorted permutation, and the functions `argmax` and `maximum`. The third section treats rotations, the list of cyclic permutations, the type `Cycle` as the quotient of lists by rotation, chains on cycles, and the functions `next` and `prev`, which link a cycle without duplicates to a cyclic permutation of group theory.

The chapter needs the list operations, the induction principles and the sublist relation of Chapter 1, the counts and multisets of the basic volume on finite structures, orders and monotone maps, and, for the last section, the permutations of a finite type and their cycles. The proofs use induction on lists and on inductive relations, and arithmetic modulo the length of a list.

The permutation relation of this chapter is the relation of the quotient that defines multisets, and lists without duplicates represent finite sets. Chapter 3 uses lists without duplicate keys to build finite maps, and sorted lists return in the search trees of that chapter. The vectors of Chapter 4 are lists of fixed length, and the traversals of Chapter 6 act on lists in the same order as the folds of this chapter.
