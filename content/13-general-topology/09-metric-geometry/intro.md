# Metric geometry

Chapter 3 uses a metric to define a topology. This chapter studies the metric itself: the maps that keep or control distances, the distance between sets, the distance between spaces, and the size of a set at a small scale. These notions belong to the geometry of metric spaces, and the library treats them with the same classes `PseudoMetricSpace`, `MetricSpace` and `EMetricSpace` as Chapter 3.

Section 1 treats isometries, dilations and their bijective forms, and the congruence and similarity of families of points. Section 2 treats actions by isometries, such as translations in a group, and the classes for Lipschitz addition, Lipschitz multiplication and bounded scalar multiplication. Section 3 defines the distance from a point to a set, the Hausdorff distance, thickenings and thickened indicators, the metric spaces of closed and compact sets, and the separation of a set. Section 4 glues metric spaces, embeds separable spaces isometrically in $\ell^\infty$, and builds the Gromov-Hausdorff space of compact metric spaces up to isometry, with the Gromov product. Section 5 puts the ultrametric on spaces of sequences, defines Polish spaces and clopenable sets, and uses schemes to map the Cantor space into perfect sets and onto compact metric spaces. Section 6 defines covers, separated sets, covering and packing numbers and the covering exponent, proves the pair reduction lemma, and states the main properties of Hausdorff dimension. Section 7 treats the variation of a function on a set, functions of bounded variation and the Jordan decomposition into monotone functions. Section 8 treats contracting maps and the Banach fixed point theorem, ultrametric distances, the snowflake of a metric space, the extended metric of uniform convergence and equicontinuity in metric terms.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 to 7. The sections are independent of each other, unless a section says otherwise.
