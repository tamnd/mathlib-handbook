# Fourier series

A periodic function is a function on a circle. This chapter writes such a function as a sum of characters, the exponentials $e^{2\pi i n x/T}$, and studies when and in what sense the sum converges. The circle is a compact abelian group, and the whole chapter uses only its group structure, its Haar measure and the orthogonality of its characters. The normalisation is the one of Mathlib: the circle carries the Haar measure of total mass $1$, so a Fourier coefficient is a mean value.

Section 1 sets up the circle $\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}$, its characters and the Fourier coefficients, proves that the characters are orthonormal, and proves that a derivative multiplies the coefficients by $2\pi i n/T$. It computes the coefficients of the sawtooth and the square wave. Section 2 proves with the Stone-Weierstrass theorem that the trigonometric polynomials are dense. It deduces that the characters form a Hilbert basis, proves Parseval's identity, and proves that an absolutely summable Fourier series of a continuous function converges uniformly to it. It computes $\sum 1/n^2 = \pi^2/6$ and treats the torus $(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})^d$. Section 3 does the same on a finite abelian group, where the transform is a finite sum, and proves Pontryagin duality in this case. For $\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$ it is the discrete Fourier transform.

The chapter needs the exponential and the circle of Chapter 1, the Hilbert spaces and Hilbert bases of Volume 16, Chapter 6, and the integrals and $L^p$ spaces of Volume 18. Chapter 7 replaces the circle by $\mathbb{R}^n$ and the series by an integral, and uses Section 2 in the proof of the Poisson summation formula.
