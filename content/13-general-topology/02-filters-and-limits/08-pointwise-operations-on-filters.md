## Pointwise operations on filters

### Overview

Let $\alpha$ carry a multiplication. For two sets $s, t \subseteq \alpha$, the pointwise product is $s t = \{a b : a \in s,\ b \in t\}$. The module `Mathlib.Order.Filter.Pointwise` lifts this operation to filters: the product $f g$ of two filters on $\alpha$ is the filter generated by the products $s t$ with $s \in f$ and $t \in g$. It does the same for the unit, the inverse, the division, the powers and the scalar actions, with the additive forms for $0$, $-f$, $f + g$, $f - g$ and $f +ᵥ g$.

The operations matter for topological groups. Continuity of the multiplication at $(x, y)$ says that $\mathcal{N}(x) \mathcal{N}(y) \le \mathcal{N}(x y)$, and in a topological group $\mathcal{N}(x y) = \mathcal{N}(x) \mathcal{N}(y)$. Volume 14 uses this form. This section describes the operations as an API: the membership rules, the point filters, the algebraic laws, and the interaction with `map`.

### The library around it

#### The operations and their sets

Each binary operation is the image of the product filter under the operation, so it is a case of `Filter.map₂` from Section 7. For example, `Filter.map₂_mul` says that `Filter.map₂ (· * ·) f g = f * g`. The membership rule follows from the rule for `Filter.map₂`:

$$u \in f g \iff \exists s \in f,\ \exists t \in g,\ s t \subseteq u.$$

This is `Filter.mem_mul`, with the additive form `Filter.mem_add`, and `Filter.mul_mem_mul` is the easy direction: $s \in f$ and $t \in g$ give $s t \in f g$. The unary operations are images under a map. The inverse $f^{-1}$ is the image of $f$ under $x \mapsto x^{-1}$, so $s \in f^{-1}$ if and only if $\{x : x^{-1} \in s\} \in f$ (`Filter.mem_inv`). The unit $1$ is the point filter `pure 1`, so $s \in 1$ if and only if $1 \in s$ (`Filter.mem_one`). The scalar action of a filter on a filter is `Filter.mem_smul`, and the action of an element $a$ on a filter is `Filter.mem_smul_filter`.

The table gives the names of the operations and of their instances.

| Operation | Instance | Membership rule |
|---|---|---|
| $1$, $0$ | `Filter.instOne`, `Filter.instZero` | `Filter.mem_one`, `Filter.mem_zero` |
| $f^{-1}$, $-f$ | `Filter.instInv`, `Filter.instNeg` | `Filter.mem_inv`, `Filter.mem_neg` |
| $f g$, $f + g$ | `Filter.instMul`, `Filter.instAdd` | `Filter.mem_mul`, `Filter.mem_add` |
| $f / g$, $f - g$ | `Filter.instDiv`, `Filter.instSub` | `Filter.mem_div`, `Filter.mem_sub` |
| $f \cdot g$, $f +ᵥ g$ | `Filter.instSMul`, `Filter.instVAdd` | `Filter.mem_smul`, `Filter.mem_vadd` |
| $a \cdot f$ | `Filter.instSMulFilter` | `Filter.mem_smul_filter` |

These instances are scoped. They are active only after `open Pointwise`, as for the pointwise operations on sets in Volume 5. Without this command, the expression `f * g` for filters does not elaborate.

#### Point filters and the bottom

The point filters behave as the elements: `Filter.pure_mul_pure` says that $\mathrm{pure}(a)\,\mathrm{pure}(b) = \mathrm{pure}(a b)$. The proof is a computation with the membership rule. A set $u$ is in the product if it contains $s t$ for some $s \ni a$ and $t \ni b$. Take $s = \{a\}$ and $t = \{b\}$: then $u \in \mathrm{pure}(a)\mathrm{pure}(b)$ if and only if $a b \in u$, which is the membership rule of $\mathrm{pure}(a b)$. The library packs this fact as a homomorphism: `Filter.pureMonoidHom` is the monoid homomorphism $a \mapsto \mathrm{pure}(a)$, and `Filter.pureMulHom` and `Filter.pureOneHom` are its weaker forms.

The bottom filter absorbs the product: $f g = \bot$ if and only if $f = \bot$ or $g = \bot$ (`Filter.mul_eq_bot_iff`). The proof uses the product filter, because $f g$ is the image of $f \times g$ and an image is $\bot$ only when the filter is $\bot$. The product of two proper filters is proper (`Filter.NeBot.mul`, with the instance `Filter.mul.instNeBot`). The top filter is idempotent in a monoid: `Filter.top_mul_top` says $\top \top = \top$, because $\alpha \alpha = \alpha$.

#### Algebraic structure

The operations satisfy the laws that the operations on $\alpha$ satisfy, up to one exception. The library gives definitions such as `Filter.semigroup`, `Filter.monoid`, `Filter.commMonoid`, `Filter.divisionMonoid` and `Filter.addCommMonoid`. They are scoped instances in `Pointwise`. The exception is the inverse law: $f f^{-1}$ is not $1$ in general, so the filters on a group form a division monoid and not a group. For a proper filter $f$, the set $f f^{-1}$ contains $1$, but it is $1$ only when $f$ is a point filter. The units confirm this: a filter is a unit if and only if it is a point filter $\mathrm{pure}(a)$ with $a$ a unit (`Filter.isUnit_iff`).

The operations are monotone in each argument. The instances `Filter.mulLeftMono` and `Filter.mulRightMono` say that $f_1 \le f_2$ gives $g f_1 \le g f_2$ and $f_1 g \le f_2 g$, and `Filter.div_le_div` is the form for the division. These are the rules behind a calculation such as $\mathcal{N}(x)\mathcal{N}(y) \le \mathcal{N}(xy)$.

A division expresses disjointness in a group. The filter $f / g$ is the filter of the quotients $a b^{-1}$ with $a$ near $f$ and $b$ near $g$. If $f$ and $g$ meet, then quotients close to $1$ appear, and conversely.

**Proposition.** In a group, $1 \not\le f / g$ if and only if $f$ and $g$ are disjoint.

*Proof sketch.* The inequality $1 \le f / g$ says that each set $s / t$ with $s \in f$ and $t \in g$ contains $1$, that is, that $s \cap t \ne \emptyset$ for each $s \in f$ and $t \in g$. This is the statement that $f \sqcap g \ne \bot$. $\square$

This is `Filter.not_one_le_div_iff`. The additive form `Filter.nonneg_sub_iff` says that $0 \le f - g$ if and only if $f$ and $g$ are not disjoint.

#### Images under homomorphisms

A multiplicative map $m$ commutes with the product: $m_{*}(f_1 f_2) = m_{*}(f_1)\, m_{*}(f_2)$, by `Filter.map_mul`. The proof is the identity $m(s t) = m(s)\,m(t)$ for sets and the description of the sets of an image. The inverse and the division have the same rules, `Filter.map_inv'` and `Filter.map_div`. The preimage satisfies only an inequality, `Filter.comap_mul_comap_le`. The map `Filter.mapMonoidHom` packs `map m` as a monoid homomorphism of the filters. The rule `Filter.Tendsto.mul_mul` says that if $m$ sends $f_1$ to $f_2$ and $g_1$ to $g_2$, then it sends $f_1 g_1$ to $f_2 g_2$.

In an ordered group, the inverse exchanges the two ends: `Filter.inv_atTop` says that $\mathrm{atTop}^{-1} = \mathrm{atBot}$, and `Filter.neg_atTop` says that $-\mathrm{atTop} = \mathrm{atBot}$. Both follow from the fact that the inverse is an order isomorphism of $\alpha$ onto its order dual.

#### Scalar actions

The scalar operations give $f \cdot g$ for a filter $f$ on the scalars and a filter $g$ on the space, and $a \cdot g$ for a scalar $a$. The library has the instances `Filter.mulAction` and `Filter.mulActionFilter` for monoid actions, `Filter.distribMulActionFilter` for distributive actions, and the compatibility classes `Filter.isScalarTower` and `Filter.smulCommClass`. A nonzero scalar of a group with zero can be cancelled in a limit: `Filter.smul_tendsto_smul_iff₀` says that $c m$ tends to $c g$ if and only if $m$ tends to $g$, for $c \ne 0$.

#### Approximate units

The module `Mathlib.Topology.ApproximateUnit` uses filters on a topological type with a multiplication. The structure `Filter.IsApproximateUnit l` says that $l$ is a proper filter and that $m x$ and $x m$ tend to $m$ along $l$ for each $m$, with the fields `Filter.IsApproximateUnit.tendsto_mul_left` and `Filter.IsApproximateUnit.tendsto_mul_right`. The examples are `Filter.IsApproximateUnit.pure_one` and, when multiplication is separately continuous, `Filter.IsApproximateUnit.nhds_one`. In that case `Filter.IsApproximateUnit.iff_neBot_and_le_nhds_one` says that an approximate unit is a proper filter finer than the neighborhood filter of $1$. A finer proper filter is again an approximate unit, by `Filter.IsApproximateUnit.mono`. The notion is useful in algebras without a unit, such as the C*-algebras of Volume 16, where $1$ is not an element and the filter replaces it.

#### Common errors

1. The operations on filters are scoped instances. A file that uses `f * g` for filters must contain `open Pointwise`, or the expression does not elaborate.
2. The filters on a group do not form a group. The rule $f f^{-1} = 1$ is false for a filter that is not a point filter, so `group` and `inv_mul_cancel` do not apply.
3. The filter $1$ is the point filter `pure 1`, not the top filter `⊤`. So $f \le 1$ holds only when the singleton $\{1\}$ is a large set of $f$, by `Filter.le_one_iff`, and for a proper filter it forces $f = 1$ (`Filter.NeBot.le_one_iff`).

### Exercises

1. (A) Prove `Filter.pure_mul_pure` from `Filter.mem_mul` and `Filter.mem_pure`.
2. (A) Show that $\mathrm{pure}(a) \cdot f$ is the image of $f$ under $x \mapsto a x$.
3. (B) On $\mathbb{R}$, show that `atTop + atTop = atTop` and that `atTop - atTop` is the top filter `⊤`.
4. (B) Give a proper filter $f$ on $\mathbb{Z}$ such that $f - f \ne 0$, and conclude that the filters on $\mathbb{Z}$ do not form an additive group.
5. (B) Prove `Filter.nonneg_sub_iff` in an additive group.
6. (C) Let $G$ be a topological group. Prove that the continuity of the multiplication at $(x, y)$ is equivalent to $\mathcal{N}(x)\,\mathcal{N}(y) \le \mathcal{N}(x y)$.
