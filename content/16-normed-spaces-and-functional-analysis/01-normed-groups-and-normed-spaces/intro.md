# Normed groups and normed spaces

This chapter defines the basic objects of the volume. Section 1 defines a seminorm and a norm on an abelian group, the metric that a norm gives, and the continuity of the norm and of the group operations. It explains why Mathlib makes a normed group a metric space with an extra field, and not a group with a norm from which a metric is computed. Section 2 adds the scalars: normed fields, the class `RCLike` for $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$, and normed spaces. It compares the norms $\|\cdot\|_1$, $\|\cdot\|_2$ and $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ and shows how Mathlib puts a $p$-norm on a product with the type synonym `WithLp`. Section 3 studies series in a normed group. It proves that a normed group is complete if and only if each absolutely convergent series converges, and it builds the spaces $\ell^p$ of sequences, with the space of finitely supported sequences as the first normed space that is not complete. Section 4 collects the consequences of the invariance of the metric under translation: uniform continuity of the operations, isometries, sums of balls and bounded sets. Section 5 gives the norms of the integers, the rationals, the reals and the circle, and the norms on subgroups, submodules and quotients. Section 6 studies the class `RCLike` with its real part, imaginary part and conjugation. Section 7 completes the $\ell^p$ constructions with products of two spaces, finitely supported functions and the inequality of Hölder for `lp`. Section 8 studies the geometry of balls, spheres and rays in a real normed space. Section 9 defines normed torsors, the metric affine spaces over a normed group, and the affine isometries between them.

The chapter needs Volume 7 for vector spaces and linear maps, Volume 13 for metric spaces, completeness and limits with filters, and Volume 15 for series of real numbers and absolute convergence. Chapter 2 studies the linear maps between normed spaces, Chapter 3 the spaces of finite dimension, and Chapter 4 the complete ones. Every later chapter uses the definitions of this one.
