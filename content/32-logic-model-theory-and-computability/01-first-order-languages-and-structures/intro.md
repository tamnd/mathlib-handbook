# First-order languages and structures

This chapter sets up the syntax and the semantics of first-order logic as Mathlib has them. Section 1 defines a language as a family of function symbols and a family of relation symbols, indexed by arity. It builds terms over a type of variables, bounded formulas with de Bruijn variables for the quantifiers, sentences and theories. It studies substitution and relabeling, maps of languages and the expansion by constants, the prenex normal form, and the encoding of terms and formulas as lists, which gives a bound on the number of formulas. Section 2 defines structures, with homomorphisms, embeddings and equivalences between them. It defines the realization of terms and formulas, models of a theory, elementary equivalence and bundled models. It then treats substructures, the substructure that a set generates and its size, finitely generated structures, and quotients of structures by a congruence.

The chapter needs the language of Mathlib from Volume 1: types, propositions, structures with fields, and type classes. It uses lists, finite types and cardinality from Volume 3, and from Volume 4 the complete lattices and closure operators that organize the substructures. No earlier logic is needed, but a reader who knows the informal definitions of formula and truth will read faster.

Chapter 2 uses this material to prove the compactness theorem by ultraproducts and the Löwenheim-Skolem theorems, and to study elementary embeddings and complete types. Chapter 3 uses formulas with parameters to define the definable sets, and it uses substructures and finitely generated structures for ages and Fraïssé limits. Chapter 4 applies the language of rings and the language of Presburger arithmetic, both built with the tools of this chapter.
