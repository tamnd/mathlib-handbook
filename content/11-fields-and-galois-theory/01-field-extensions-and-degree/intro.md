# Field extensions and degree

Galois theory studies a field $L$ together with a subfield $K$. This chapter sets up that object and its first invariant. A field extension $L/K$ is a field $L$ with a homomorphism $K \to L$, and $L$ is then a vector space over $K$. The dimension of that vector space is the degree $[L:K]$. Most results of the volume are statements about degrees.

Section 1 defines extensions and intermediate fields. An intermediate field of $L/K$ is a subfield of $L$ that contains the image of $K$. The intermediate fields form a complete lattice with the bottom $K$ and the top $L$. The field $K(S)$ generated by a subset $S$ is the smallest intermediate field that contains $S$. The section also finds the prime field of each field: it is $\mathbb{Q}$ or $\mathbb{F}_p$. Section 2 defines the degree and proves the tower law $[M:K] = [M:L]\,[L:K]$ with the product basis. The corollaries limit the intermediate fields: their degrees divide $[L:K]$, and an extension of prime degree has no intermediate field other than $K$ and $L$.

The chapter needs fields, subfields and the field of fractions from Volume 6, Chapter 3, and algebras, subalgebras and `Algebra.adjoin` from Volume 6, Chapter 8. Section 2 needs bases and dimension from Volume 7, Chapter 2. The chapter does not yet ask whether an element satisfies a polynomial equation. That question is the subject of Chapter 2, which proves that $[K(\alpha):K]$ is the degree of the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$. With that result the tower law becomes a tool to compute: Chapter 3 bounds the degree of a splitting field, and Chapter 5 compares the degree of a Galois extension with the order of its Galois group.

In Mathlib, an extension is a pair of types with an `Algebra K L` instance, an intermediate field is a term of `IntermediateField K L`, and the degree is `Module.finrank K L`. The two sections explain why the library chose these forms and how to work with them.
