# Covering spaces

This chapter studies covering maps and uses them to compute the first nontrivial fundamental group. Section 1 defines evenly covered points and covering maps, and proves that a covering map is a local homeomorphism with unique lifts on connected spaces. It proves the lifting of paths and of homotopies, the injectivity of a covering map on fundamental groups, and the lifting criterion for maps from a simply connected, locally path connected space. It shows that the quotient of a space by a free group action with small disjoint translates is a covering map, defines deck transformations, and identifies the fundamental group of the base with the group when the total space is simply connected. Section 2 applies this to the covering of the circle $\mathbb{R}/p\mathbb{Z}$ by the real line. It defines the winding number of a loop through its lift and proves that the fundamental group of the circle is the group of integers, with checks by hand on explicit loops.

The chapter needs the fundamental group, the fundamental groupoid, simply connected spaces and the straight-line homotopy of Chapter 1. It uses compactness, connectedness and quotient topologies from Volume 13, topological groups from Volume 14, and groups, quotient groups and group actions from Volume 5.

The circle and its fundamental group return throughout the volume. Chapter 5 defines singular homology; for the circle its first group is again the integers, the abelianization of the fundamental group. The homotopy lifting property of Section 1 is the model for the fibrations of Chapter 6, and the cell complexes of that chapter build spaces such as the torus from the circle and disks.
