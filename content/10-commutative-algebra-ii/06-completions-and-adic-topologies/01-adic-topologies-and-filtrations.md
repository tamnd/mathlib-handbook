## Adic topologies and filtrations

### Overview

An ideal $I$ of a ring $R$ defines a topology on every $R$-module $M$: the submodules $I^nM$ are a base of neighbourhoods of $0$. Two elements are close when their difference lies in a high power $I^nM$. For $R = \mathbb{Z}$ and $I = (p)$ this is the $p$-adic topology; for $R = k[X]$ and $I = (X)$ two polynomials are close when they agree to high order at $0$.

Two theorems make the $I$-adic topology work well for Noetherian rings and finite modules. The Artin-Rees lemma says that the topology of a submodule $N \subseteq M$ induced from $M$ is the $I$-adic topology of $N$. The Krull intersection theorem says that $\bigcap_n I^nM = 0$ when $I$ is in the Jacobson radical, so the topology is Hausdorff. Both are statements about filtrations, and Mathlib proves them through the notion of a stable filtration.

### Definitions

**Definition (Filtration).** Let $I$ be an ideal of $R$ and $M$ an $R$-module. An $I$-filtration of $M$ is a decreasing sequence of submodules $M = F_0 \supseteq F_1 \supseteq F_2 \supseteq \cdots$ with $IF_n \subseteq F_{n+1}$ for all $n$. The $I$-adic filtration is $F_n = I^nM$.

**Definition (Stable filtration).** An $I$-filtration $(F_n)$ is stable if $IF_n = F_{n+1}$ for all large $n$. Equivalently, there is $n_0$ with $F_{n_0 + k} = I^kF_{n_0}$ for all $k \ge 0$.

The $I$-adic filtration is stable with $n_0 = 0$. If $(F_n)$ is stable, with $F_{n_0 + k} = I^kF_{n_0}$, then $I^{n}M \subseteq F_n$ (by induction from $IF_n \subseteq F_{n+1}$) and $F_{n_0 + k} \subseteq I^kM$. So a stable filtration and the $I$-adic filtration differ by a bounded shift, and they define the same topology.

**Definition ($I$-adic topology).** The $I$-adic topology on $M$ is the topology in which a set $U$ is open if for every $x \in U$ there is $n$ with $x + I^nM \subseteq U$. It makes $M$ a topological group, and $R$ a topological ring when $M = R$.

### Theorems and proofs

The proofs use the Rees algebra. For an ideal $I$, let $R^\ast = \bigoplus_{n \ge 0} I^n t^n \subseteq R[t]$. For an $I$-filtration $(F_n)$ of $M$, let $M^\ast = \bigoplus_n F_n t^n$, a module over $R^\ast$ because $I^aF_b \subseteq F_{a+b}$.

**Lemma (Stability and finite generation).** Let $R$ be Noetherian and $M$ finite. An $I$-filtration $(F_n)$ by submodules of $M$ is stable if and only if $M^\ast$ is a finitely generated $R^\ast$-module.

*Proof.* Let $M^\ast_{\le n}$ be the $R^\ast$-submodule generated by $F_0, F_1 t, \dots, F_n t^n$. Its component of degree $n + k$ is $I^kF_n$ for $k \ge 0$, so
$$M^\ast_{\le n} = F_0 \oplus F_1 t \oplus \cdots \oplus F_n t^n \oplus IF_n t^{n+1} \oplus I^2F_n t^{n+2} \oplus \cdots.$$
The filtration is stable at $n$ exactly when $M^\ast_{\le n} = M^\ast$. If $M^\ast$ is finitely generated, the generators lie in some $M^\ast_{\le n}$, so $M^\ast = M^\ast_{\le n}$. Conversely, if $M^\ast = M^\ast_{\le n}$, then $M^\ast$ is generated by generators of the finite modules $F_0, \dots, F_n$. $\square$

**Theorem (Artin-Rees lemma).** Let $R$ be Noetherian, $I$ an ideal, $M$ a finite $R$-module and $N \subseteq M$ a submodule. There is $k$ with
$$I^nM \cap N = I^{n-k}(I^kM \cap N) \quad \text{for all } n \ge k.$$

*Proof.* If $I = (a_1, \dots, a_r)$, then $R^\ast = R[a_1t, \dots, a_rt]$ is a quotient of a polynomial ring over $R$, so it is Noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem (Volume 9). The filtration $I^nM$ is stable, so $\bigoplus I^nM t^n$ is a finite $R^\ast$-module by the lemma, hence a Noetherian module. The filtration $F_n = I^nM \cap N$ of $N$ is an $I$-filtration, and $\bigoplus F_n t^n$ is an $R^\ast$-submodule of $\bigoplus I^nM t^n$, so it is finitely generated. By the lemma $(F_n)$ is stable: there is $k$ with $F_{k + j} = I^jF_k$ for all $j$. $\square$

**Theorem (Krull intersection theorem).** Let $R$ be Noetherian, $I$ an ideal and $M$ a finite $R$-module, and let $N = \bigcap_n I^nM$. Then $IN = N$, and an element $x \in M$ lies in $N$ if and only if $(1 - a)x = 0$ for some $a \in I$. If $I$ is contained in the Jacobson radical of $R$, then $N = 0$.

*Proof.* By Artin-Rees, there is $k$ with $I^{k+1}M \cap N = I(I^kM \cap N)$. Since $N \subseteq I^nM$ for all $n$, both intersections are $N$, so $N = IN$. The module $N$ is finite because $R$ is Noetherian. By Nakayama's lemma in the form of Chapter 2, Section 2, there is $a \in I$ with $(1 - a)N = 0$. This gives one direction of the criterion. Conversely, if $(1 - a)x = 0$ with $a \in I$, then $x = ax = a^2x = \cdots = a^nx \in I^nM$ for every $n$. If $I$ is in the Jacobson radical, $1 - a$ is a unit, so $(1 - a)N = 0$ gives $N = 0$. $\square$

**Corollary (Noetherian domains).** Let $R$ be a Noetherian domain and $I \ne R$ an ideal. Then $\bigcap_n I^n = 0$.

*Proof.* If $x \in \bigcap I^n$, then $(1 - a)x = 0$ for some $a \in I$. Since $I \ne R$, $a \ne 1$, so $1 - a \ne 0$, and $x = 0$ because $R$ is a domain. $\square$

### Examples

**Example ($p$-adic topology).** On $\mathbb{Z}$ with $I = (p)$, $\bigcap p^n\mathbb{Z} = 0$, and the topology is Hausdorff. A sequence $x_n$ converges to $0$ if and only if the power of $p$ that divides $x_n$ tends to infinity. The completion is the ring $\mathbb{Z}_p$ of $p$-adic integers (Section 2).

**Example (The criterion is needed).** Let $R = \mathbb{Z}$, $I = (2)$ and $M = \mathbb{Z}/3$. Then $IM = M$, since $2$ is invertible modulo $3$, so $\bigcap I^nM = M \ne 0$. The criterion holds with $a = -2$: $(1 - a)x = 3x = 0$. Here $I$ is not in the Jacobson radical of $\mathbb{Z}$, which is $0$.

**Example (Failure without Noetherian hypothesis).** Let $R$ be the ring of germs at $0$ of smooth real functions, a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ of germs that vanish at $0$. The germ of $f(x) = e^{-1/x^2}$ (extended by $f(0) = 0$) has all derivatives $0$ at $0$, and lies in $\bigcap_n \mathfrak m^n$, by Taylor's theorem with remainder. So $\bigcap \mathfrak m^n \ne 0$, and $R$ is not Noetherian.

**Example (Artin-Rees for a principal submodule).** Let $R = k[X, Y]$, $I = (X)$, $M = R$ and $N = (Y)$. Then $I^nM \cap N = (X^n) \cap (Y) = (X^nY) = I^nN$, so $k = 0$ works. For $N = (X^2)$, $I^n \cap N = (X^{\max(n, 2)})$, which is $I^{n-2}(I^2 \cap N)$ for $n \ge 2$, so $k = 2$ works and $k = 0$ does not.

### The library around it

The file `Mathlib/RingTheory/Filtration.lean` has the filtrations, stability, the Rees module as a submodule of a module of polynomials with coefficients in `M`, the Artin-Rees lemma and the Krull intersection theorem. The topology defined by an ideal, with `IsAdic`, is in Volume 14, and the completion with respect to it is in Section 2. The graded rings and Hilbert functions that measure a filtration are in Chapter 7.

The Rees algebra of an ideal $I$ is $R[It] = \bigoplus_n I^n t^n$. In `Mathlib.RingTheory.ReesAlgebra` it is `reesAlgebra I`, the subalgebra of $R[X]$ of the polynomials whose coefficient of $X^i$ lies in $I^i$, by `mem_reesAlgebra_iff`. A monomial $rX^i$ lies in it if and only if $r \in I^i$, `reesAlgebra.monomial_mem`. It is generated by $IX$, `adjoin_monomial_eq_reesAlgebra`, so it is finitely generated when $I$ is, `reesAlgebra.fg`. Its quotient by $I$ is the associated graded ring $\bigoplus_n I^n/I^{n+1}$, as `mem_map_algebraMap_reesAlgebra_iff` shows, and `isNoetherianRing_reesAlgebra_quotient` says that this ring is Noetherian when $I$ is finitely generated and $R/I$ is Noetherian. The stable filtrations above are the filtrations whose Rees module is finitely generated over this algebra.

The modules `Mathlib.RingTheory.IdealFilter.Basic` and `Mathlib.RingTheory.IdealFilter.Topology` generalize the powers of one ideal to a filter of ideals. An `IdealFilter A` is a filter in the lattice of ideals of $A$. It is uniform, `IdealFilter.IsUniform`, when $I \in F$ gives $(I : a) \in F$ for each $a$, `IdealFilter.IsUniform.colon_mem`. It is a Gabriel filter, `IdealFilter.IsGabriel`, when it is also closed under the condition `IdealFilter.IsGabriel.gabriel_closed`, and `IdealFilter.isGabriel_iff` says that this is the same as being uniform and equal to its own composition `IdealFilter.gabrielComposition`. An element $m$ of a module is torsion for $F$, `IdealFilter.IsTorsionElem`, when some $I \in F$ kills it, and `IdealFilter.IsTorsion` asks this for every element. For a uniform filter, `IdealFilter.ringFilterBasis` is a basis of neighborhoods of $0$, and the type synonym `WithIdealFilter F` carries the ring topology in which the ideals of $F$ are the open neighborhoods of $0$, `WithIdealFilter.mem_nhds_zero_iff`. The topology is linear, by the instance `WithIdealFilter.instIsLinearTopology`. The $I$-adic topology is the case of the filter of the ideals that contain a power of $I$.

Common errors of a learner:

- In `I.Filtration M` the field `N 0` need not be `⊤`; the `I`-adic filtration of `M` is `I.stableFiltration ⊤`.
- The Krull intersection theorem in the form `⨅ i, I ^ i = ⊥` needs a hypothesis such as $I$ in the Jacobson radical, a local ring or a domain; for $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ and $I = \mathbb{Z} \times 0$, the powers are all $I$.
- The statement `I ^ n • ⊤ ⊓ N` is the intersection of submodules, read as `(I ^ n • ⊤) ⊓ N`.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that a stable $I$-filtration and the $I$-adic filtration define the same topology on $M$.
2. (A) Show that $\bigcap_n I^n = I$ for $R = \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ and $I = \mathbb{Z} \times 0$, and find $a \in I$ that satisfies the criterion for $x = (1, 0)$.
3. (A) Find the least $k$ in the Artin-Rees lemma for $R = k[X]$, $I = (X)$, $M = R$ and $N = (X^3)$.
4. (B) Use the Artin-Rees lemma to show: if $0 \to N \to M \to P \to 0$ is an exact sequence of finite modules over a Noetherian ring, then the $I$-adic topology of $M$ induces the $I$-adic topology on $N$.
5. (B) Let $R$ be a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and $x \in R$ a nonzero element. Show that $x \notin \mathfrak m^n$ for some $n$.
6. (B) Show that the Rees algebra $\bigoplus I^n t^n$ is Noetherian when $R$ is Noetherian.
7. (C) Let $M$ be a finite module over a Noetherian ring $R$ and $I$ an ideal. Show that $M$ is Hausdorff in the $I$-adic topology if and only if no element of $1 + I$ is a zero divisor on $M$.

### Notes and history

The intersection theorem is due to Krull. The Artin-Rees lemma is named after Emil Artin and David Rees, who found it independently in the 1950s.
