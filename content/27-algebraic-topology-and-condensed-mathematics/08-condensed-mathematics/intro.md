# Condensed mathematics

Condensed mathematics replaces topological abelian groups with sheaves on a site of compact spaces. A condensed set is a sheaf on compact Hausdorff spaces, or equivalently on profinite or Stonean spaces, for the coherent topology. Its value at a space $S$ is the set of families of points parametrized continuously by $S$. Condensed abelian groups and condensed modules form abelian categories with good exactness properties, and they contain the topological groups of interest as a full subcategory. This chapter gives the definitions in Mathlib and proves the basic structure theorems.

Section 1 treats condensed sets and condensed modules over `CompHaus`. It proves the explicit sheaf condition, the reduction to Stonean spaces, where a sheaf is a presheaf that preserves finite products, and the description of epimorphisms. It builds the adjunction with topological spaces and shows that compactly generated spaces embed fully faithfully. It also proves the exactness axioms AB4, AB4* and AB5 for condensed modules. Section 2 treats light condensed sets, the sheaves on light profinite sets. The site is essentially small, so the values stay in the same universe and light condensed modules form a Grothendieck abelian category. Countable products are exact, sequential spaces embed fully faithfully, and the free module on a convergent sequence is internally projective.

The chapter needs the categories of spaces of Chapter 7, the sheaves and Grothendieck topologies of Volume 25, Chapters 2 and 6, and the limits, adjunctions and abelian categories of Volume 24. Later material uses the chapter in two ways. Homological algebra on condensed modules uses its exactness results, and the solid and liquid theories of Clausen and Scholze start from the free modules on profinite sets that it defines. Volume 28 returns to sheaves in geometry, where the same sheaf methods apply.
