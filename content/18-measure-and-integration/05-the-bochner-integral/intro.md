# The Bochner integral

This chapter integrates functions that take values in a Banach space: real and complex functions, vectors of $\mathbb{R}^n$, and functions with values in a space of functions. Chapter 4 gave an integral with values in $[0, \infty]$. A real function is the difference of its positive and negative parts, but this trick does not work in a general space. The Bochner integral replaces it with a limit: it integrates simple functions by a finite sum and extends the integral by continuity. Section 1 explains the little needed about normed spaces and completeness. It defines strongly measurable and integrable functions and proves that the integrable functions form a vector space. Section 2 builds the integral: first on simple functions, then on the space $L^1$ by density, and proves its rules. It also explains why Mathlib gives the value $0$ to a function that is not integrable. Section 3 proves the dominated convergence theorem for the Bochner integral, the continuity of integrals with a parameter, the theorem of Egorov and the link between convergence in measure and convergence almost everywhere. Section 4 changes a measure by an exponential density, the tilted measure, and studies the logarithm of the ratio of two densities. Section 5 treats locally integrable functions, the strong measurability of limits and series, and the lemma of Doob and Dynkin on factorization through a measurable map. Section 6 builds measures from contents and proves the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani theorem: a positive linear functional on the continuous functions with compact support is the integral against a regular measure. Section 7 describes the general extension of a dominated additive set function to all integrable functions, of which the Bochner integral is one case. Section 8 has averages, the mean value theorem for integrals, the layer cake formula and peak functions. Section 9 collects the rules for continuous linear maps, real and complex parts, densities, sums of measures and vector valued codomains, and the theorem of Vitali and Carathéodory.

The chapter needs the measures of Chapter 2, the examples of Lebesgue measure of Chapter 3 and the whole of Chapter 4. Its dominated convergence theorem rests on the one for $[0, \infty]$ in Chapter 4. It uses the metric spaces and the completeness of Volume 13 and the sequences and series of Volume 15. The normed spaces of Volume 16 are introduced here only as far as the chapter needs them. Chapter 6 uses the integral for the theorem of Fubini, Chapter 7 studies the spaces $L^p$ of which $L^1$ is the first case, and Chapter 8 defines the integral over an interval as a Bochner integral.
