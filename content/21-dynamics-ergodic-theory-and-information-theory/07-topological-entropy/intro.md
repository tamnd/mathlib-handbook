# Topological entropy

The invariants of Chapters 1 to 4, such as periodic points and the rotation number, describe single orbits. Topological entropy describes the system as a whole. It is the exponential rate at which the number of distinguishable orbit segments grows with their length. An isometry has entropy $0$, the doubling map of the circle has entropy $\log 2$, and the full shift on $k$ symbols has entropy $\log k$. Entropy does not increase under a factor map, so it distinguishes systems that are not conjugate.

Section 1 defines the dynamical entourages of a map of a uniform space, the dynamical covers and the entropy at a fixed scale, as a limsup and as a liminf. It proves that the minimal cover size is submultiplicative, that the two forms agree on an invariant set, and that the entropy at each scale is finite on a compact invariant set. Section 2 counts dynamical nets in place of covers and proves that they give the same entropy. It proves that entropy is monotone in the set, takes the maximum on a finite union, does not change under closure, and does not increase under a uniformly continuous semiconjugacy. Its main example is the full shift of Chapter 3.

The chapter needs uniform spaces, entourages and compactness from Volume 13, Chapters 5 and 7, and the iterates of Chapter 1. It needs no measure theory. Mathlib keeps the subject in the namespace `Dynamics` and states it for an arbitrary subset $F$ of the space. The index of this edition has no declaration for the measure theoretic entropy or the variational principle, so the chapter does not connect entropy with the invariant measures of Chapters 5 and 6.
