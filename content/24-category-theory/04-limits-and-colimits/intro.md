# Limits and colimits

This chapter defines limits and colimits of diagrams and computes them in the main categories of algebra and topology. A limit of a diagram is a cone through which every other cone factors in exactly one way. Products, kernels, intersections, fibre products and infima are limits. Dually, coproducts, quotients, free products, tensor products of commutative rings and increasing unions are colimits.

Section 1 defines cones, limits and terminal objects, proves that limits are unique up to unique isomorphism, and shows that a limit represents the functor of cones. Section 2 treats products, equalizers and pullbacks, computes all limits in sets as compatible families, and proves that products and equalizers give all limits. Section 3 dualizes, computes colimits in sets as quotients of disjoint unions, and proves the simple description of filtered colimits in sets. Section 4 studies functors that preserve, reflect and create limits, proves that limits in functor categories are computed pointwise, and explains why the limits of groups are computed on their underlying sets.

The chapter needs categories and opposite categories (Chapter 1), functors and natural transformations (Chapter 2), and representable functors (Chapter 3). The examples use groups (Volume 5), rings, tensor products (Volume 8) and topological spaces (Volume 13). In Mathlib a cone is `Cone F`, the proof that it is a limit is the structure `IsLimit`, and a chosen limit is `limit F` under the class `HasLimit F`. Chapter 5 uses these results: right adjoints preserve limits, and the adjoint functor theorems build adjoints from limits.
