## Products, powers and lifts of filters

### Overview

The product topology of Chapter 1 has a filter form. If $f$ is a filter on $\alpha$ and $g$ is a filter on $\beta$, the product filter $f \times g$ on $\alpha \times \beta$ is generated by the rectangles $s \times t$ with $s \in f$ and $t \in g$. The neighbourhood filter of a point of a product space is the product of the neighbourhood filters, and a function of two variables has a joint limit when it converges along a product filter. In the same way, the filter `Filter.pi` on a product $\prod_i \alpha_i$ is the filter form of the product topology of infinitely many spaces.

This section describes these constructions and a few others that build new filters from old ones. The filter `f.curry g` is the filter of iterated limits. The operation `Filter.map₂` is the image of a product under a map of two variables. The operations `Filter.lift` and `Filter.lift'` build a filter from a filter and a map on sets; uniform spaces in Chapter 7 use them all the time. The filter `Filter.smallSets` describes the sets that become small along a filter. The section ends with three small modules: filters that live on a single point, the image of a filter under a relation or a partial map, and filters on lists.

### The library around it

#### The product filter

The product filter is written `f ×ˢ g`, the same notation as the product of sets, through the instance `Filter.instSProd`. The basic membership rule is `Filter.mem_prod_iff`:

$$S \in f \times g \iff \exists s \in f,\ \exists t \in g,\ s \times t \subseteq S.$$

So a property holds eventually along $f \times g$ if and only if it holds on a product of a large set of $f$ and a large set of $g$. The library states this with predicates in `Filter.eventually_prod_iff`, and `Filter.Eventually.prod_mk` gives the easy direction: if $p$ holds eventually along $f$ and $q$ holds eventually along $g$, then $(x, y) \mapsto p(x) \wedge q(y)$ holds eventually along $f \times g$. The product is proper if and only if both factors are proper (`Filter.prod_neBot`), and it is $\bot$ if and only if one factor is $\bot$ (`Filter.prod_eq_bot`).

Limits into a product are pairs of limits.

**Proposition.** A map $s = (s_1, s_2) : \gamma \to \beta_1 \times \beta_2$ tends to $g_1 \times g_2$ along $f$ if and only if $s_1$ tends to $g_1$ and $s_2$ tends to $g_2$.

*Proof.* The projections tend to the factors, by `Filter.tendsto_fst` and its mirror, so the direct implication is a composition. Conversely, let $S \in g_1 \times g_2$ and choose $t_1 \times t_2 \subseteq S$. Eventually $s_1(x) \in t_1$ and eventually $s_2(x) \in t_2$. The intersection of two large sets of $f$ is large, so eventually $s(x) \in t_1 \times t_2 \subseteq S$. $\square$

This is `Filter.tendsto_prod_iff'`, and `Filter.Tendsto.prodMk` is the converse direction as a rule. The rule `Filter.Tendsto.prodMap` treats a map of the form $(x, y) \mapsto (f(x), g(y))$. The table gives the computation rules of the product.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Filter.prod_principal_principal` | $\mathcal{P}(s) \times \mathcal{P}(t) = \mathcal{P}(s \times t)$ |
| `Filter.prod_pure_pure` | the product of two point filters is the point filter of the pair |
| `Filter.prod_inf_prod` | $(f_1 \times g_1) \sqcap (f_2 \times g_2) = (f_1 \sqcap f_2) \times (g_1 \sqcap g_2)$ |
| `Filter.prod_iInf_left` | the product commutes with an infimum in the first factor |
| `Filter.prod_comm` | $f \times g$ is the image of $g \times f$ under the swap |
| `Filter.prod_assoc` | the product is associative up to `Equiv.prodAssoc` |
| `Filter.mem_prod_principal` | $S \in f \times \mathcal{P}(t)$ if and only if $\{a : \forall b \in t,\ (a, b) \in S\} \in f$ |

The diagonal is special. If $p$ holds eventually along $f \times f$, then $x \mapsto p(x, x)$ holds eventually along $f$ (`Filter.Eventually.diag_of_prod`). This is the form in which a property of pairs of close points gives a property of single points.

The coproduct `f.coprod g` is the dual construction. A set is large for it when it contains $s \times \beta$ for a large $s$ of $f$ and $\alpha \times t$ for a large $t$ of $g$ (`Filter.mem_coprod_iff`). The coproduct of `cofinite` with itself is the filter `cofinite` on $\alpha \times \beta$, and the coproduct is the filter form of "one of the two coordinates goes to infinity".

#### Iterated limits

The filter `f.curry g` contains $S$ if, for eventually all $x$ along $f$, eventually all $y$ along $g$ satisfy $(x, y) \in S$. This is `Filter.mem_curry_iff`, and the module `Mathlib.Order.Filter.Curry` calls it the filter of iterated limits. It is coarser than the product: `Filter.curry_le_prod` says $\mathrm{curry}(f, g) \le f \times g$. The inequality is strict in general. For $f = g = \mathrm{atTop}$ on $\mathbb{N}$, the set $\{(m, n) : n > m\}$ is in the curried filter, because for each $m$ all $n > m$ qualify, but it contains no product of two tails. The rule `Filter.Tendsto.curry` says that a family of maps that converge for eventually all values of the first variable converges along the curried filter.

#### Products of a family

For a family of filters $f_i$ on spaces $\alpha_i$, the filter `Filter.pi f` on $\prod_i \alpha_i$ is generated by the cylinders $\{x : x_i \in s\}$ with $s \in f_i$. A set is large if it contains a box with finitely many proper sides.

$$S \in \textstyle\prod_i f_i \iff \exists I \text{ finite},\ \exists (t_i)_i,\ (\forall i,\ t_i \in f_i) \wedge \{x : \forall i \in I,\ x_i \in t_i\} \subseteq S.$$

This is `Filter.mem_pi`. A map into the product converges if and only if each coordinate converges, by `Filter.tendsto_pi`. The proof is the same as for two factors, with a finite intersection in place of the intersection of two sets. The product is proper if and only if each factor is proper (`Filter.pi_neBot`), and then the projection of the product is the factor (`Filter.map_eval_pi`). The family coproduct `Filter.coprodᵢ` generalises `Filter.coprod`. The infinite product theorem of Tychonoff in Chapter 5 uses this filter through the neighbourhoods of a product space.

#### Images under maps of two variables

The filter `Filter.map₂ m f g` is the image of $f \times g$ under $(a, b) \mapsto m(a, b)$, by `Filter.map_prod_eq_map₂`. Its sets are described by images of rectangles: $u \in \mathrm{map}_2(m, f, g)$ if and only if $m(s, t) \subseteq u$ for some $s \in f$ and $t \in g$ (`Filter.mem_map₂_iff`). The module `Mathlib.Order.Filter.NAry` copies the API of `Set.image2`. Each algebraic law of $m$ gives a law of `Filter.map₂`: commutativity gives `Filter.map₂_comm`, associativity gives `Filter.map₂_assoc`, and so on with the names `Filter.map₂_left_comm` and `Filter.map_map₂_distrib`. The pointwise operations of the next section, such as $f + g$ and $f g$, are defined by `Filter.map₂`.

#### Lifts

Let $f$ be a filter on $\alpha$ and let $g$ map each set of $\alpha$ to a filter on $\beta$. The lift `f.lift g` is the infimum $\bigsqcap_{s \in f} g(s)$. When $g$ is monotone, its sets are easy to describe: $S \in f.\mathrm{lift}\ g$ if and only if $S \in g(s)$ for some $s \in f$ (`Filter.mem_lift_sets`). The variant `f.lift' h` takes a map $h$ on sets and lifts the principal filters $\mathcal{P}(h(s))$. A basis of $f$ gives a basis of the lift: if $(s_i)$ is a basis of $f$ and $h$ is monotone, then $(h(s_i))$ is a basis of `f.lift' h`, by `Filter.HasBasis.lift'`.

Lifts appear when a construction acts on sets. For example, the product filter $f \times f$ equals the lift of $s \mapsto s \times s$, by `Filter.prod_same_eq`. In Chapter 7, the filter of the closures of the entourages of a uniform space is a lift, and the composition of entourages gives the lift of $V \mapsto V \circ V$. The limit rules are `Filter.tendsto_lift` and `Filter.tendsto_lift'`. The lemma `Filter.tendsto_prod_self_iff` is the form used for uniform limits: a map of two variables tends to $y$ along $x \times x$ if and only if for each $W \in y$ there is $U \in x$ with $f(a, b) \in W$ for all $a, b \in U$.

#### Small sets

For a filter $l$ on $\alpha$, the filter `l.smallSets` on `Set α` contains the families of sets that contain all subsets of some large set. So a property holds eventually along `l.smallSets` if it holds for all subsets of a large set of $l$ (`Filter.eventually_smallSets`). A family of sets $f(x)$ tends to `lb.smallSets` if and only if $f(x)$ is eventually inside each large set of $lb$:

$$\mathrm{Tendsto}\ f\ la\ (lb.\mathrm{smallSets}) \iff \forall t \in lb,\ \forall^{f} x \in la,\ f(x) \subseteq t.$$

This is `Filter.tendsto_smallSets_iff`. The filter expresses statements such as "the ball $B(x, r)$ shrinks to $x$ as $r \to 0$", and it is the natural target for the limit of a family of sets. A basis of $l$ gives the basis of powersets (`Filter.HasBasis.smallSets`), and a smaller family also converges (`Filter.Tendsto.smallSets_mono`). Measure theory uses this filter for differentiation of integrals.

#### Filters on one point, relations and lists

A filter is subsingleton if it contains a set with at most one point. The module `Mathlib.Order.Filter.Subsingleton` defines `Filter.Subsingleton` and proves that such a filter is $\bot$ or a point filter `pure a` (`Filter.subsingleton_iff_bot_or_pure`). Subsingleton filters are closed under images, products and smaller filters (`Filter.Subsingleton.map`, `Filter.Subsingleton.prod`, `Filter.Subsingleton.anti`), and each of them is countably generated.

The module `Mathlib.Order.Filter.Partial` extends `map` and `comap` to relations and partial functions. For a relation $r \subseteq \alpha \times \beta$, `Filter.rmap r l` contains $s$ when the core $\{a : \forall b,\ r(a, b) \to b \in s\}$ is in $l$ (`Filter.mem_rmap`), and `Filter.RTendsto` is the matching limit. For a partial function $f : \alpha \rightharpoonup \beta$, the operations are `Filter.pmap` and `Filter.PTendsto`. An ordinary limit is a limit of the graph: `Filter.tendsto_iff_rtendsto`. These forms are used in the theory of computable functions more than in analysis.

The module `Mathlib.Order.Filter.ListTraverse` describes `traverse` and `sequence` for the monad structure of filters on lists. Its main lemma, `Filter.mem_traverse_iff`, describes the large sets of `traverse f l` by lists of large sets.

#### Common errors

1. The product filter `atTop ×ˢ atTop` gives a joint limit, and `atTop.curry atTop` gives an iterated limit. A limit along the second filter need not be a limit along the first: the joint limit needs the uniform statement.
2. `Filter.mem_lift_sets` needs a monotone map $g$. For a map that is not monotone, the sets of the lift are only described as sets of the infimum.
3. `Filter.mem_pi` allows only finitely many proper sides. The box $\prod_i t_i$ with infinitely many proper $t_i$ need not be a large set of `Filter.pi f`.

### Exercises

1. (A) Prove `Filter.prod_principal_principal` from `Filter.mem_prod_iff`.
2. (A) Show that $(m, n) \mapsto m + n$ tends to `atTop` along `atTop ×ˢ atTop` on $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$.
3. (B) Show that the set $\{(m, n) : n > m\}$ is in `atTop.curry atTop` on $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$ and is not in `atTop ×ˢ atTop`.
4. (B) Prove that `Filter.map₂ (· + ·) (pure a) g` is the image of $g$ under $x \mapsto a + x$.
5. (B) Show that the closed balls $\overline{B}(x, r)$ tend to `(𝓝 x).smallSets` as $r \to 0^{+}$ in a metric space.
6. (C) Prove `Filter.subsingleton_iff_bot_or_pure`.
