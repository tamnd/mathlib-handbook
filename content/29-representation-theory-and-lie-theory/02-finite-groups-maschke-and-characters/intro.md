# Finite groups, Maschke's theorem and characters

This chapter studies representations of a finite group whose order is invertible in the field. Section 1 proves the theorem of Maschke: an equivariant projection, made by averaging a linear projection over the group, gives each subrepresentation a complement. It follows that each finite dimensional representation is a direct sum of irreducible ones, that the group algebra is a semisimple ring, and that each object of `Rep k G` and `FDRep k G` is projective and injective. The section then proves the lemma of Schur and its consequences: over an algebraically closed field the endomorphisms of an irreducible representation are scalars, and the irreducible representations of an abelian group are one dimensional. Section 2 defines characters, proves that the average of a character is the dimension of the invariants and that the inner product of two characters is the dimension of the space of intertwining maps, and deduces the orthonormality of irreducible characters, with the character table of $S_3$ as the running example. It ends with Tannaka duality: a finite group is the group of monoidal automorphisms of the forgetful functor on its finite dimensional representations.

The chapter needs Chapter 1 of this volume, traces and eigenvalues from Volume 7, tensor products from Volume 8, projective and injective modules from Volume 25, and monoidal categories and natural transformations from Volume 24.

Chapter 3 builds induced representations and Frobenius reciprocity on top of these results, and characters give a direct way to compute the induced representations of finite groups. The ideas of simple and semisimple objects return in Chapter 5, where semisimple Lie algebras are defined through their ideals.
