# Measurable spaces and measurable functions

A measure assigns a size to a set. It cannot assign a size to every subset of $\mathbb{R}$ in a way that is countably additive and invariant under translation, so measure theory first fixes a family of sets that receive a size. This family is a σ-algebra, and a set with a σ-algebra is a measurable space. This chapter builds the language of measurable spaces. It has no measures yet. Chapter 2 adds them.

Section 1 defines σ-algebras, the σ-algebra generated by a family of sets, and the complete lattice of σ-algebras on a type. It defines the Borel σ-algebra of a topological space, the classes `OpensMeasurableSpace` and `BorelSpace` of Mathlib, and proves that the Borel sets of $\mathbb{R}$ are generated by the open intervals with rational end points and by the rays $(-\infty, q)$ with $q$ rational. Section 2 proves the π-λ theorem of Dynkin. This theorem is the main tool of the volume to show that a property holds for all measurable sets, and Chapter 2 uses it to prove that a measure is determined by its values on a π-system. Section 3 defines measurable functions, the pull back and the push forward of a σ-algebra, and the product σ-algebra. It proves that continuous functions are Borel measurable and that sums, products, suprema and pointwise limits of measurable real functions are measurable. Section 4 states the same rules once for every type with a measurable operation, through classes such as `MeasurableMul₂`, and lists the measurability of the elementary functions. Section 5 collects the measurable structures around these definitions: the instances on discrete types, the measurably generated filters, the generators of product σ-algebras, the invariant σ-algebra of a map, measurability modulo a filter, the cardinality of a generated σ-algebra and the measurability of the lattice operations. Section 6 studies the Borel sets of Polish spaces: analytic sets, the theorems of Suslin and of Lusin and Souslin, and the classification of standard Borel spaces.

The chapter needs sets and countability from Volume 3, complete lattices and Galois connections from Volume 4, and open sets, bases, second countability and metric spaces from Volume 13. The Mathlib statements use filters for limits, also from Volume 13. Chapter 2 defines measures on the measurable spaces of this chapter, and Chapter 4 integrates the measurable functions of Section 3.
