## The neighbourhood filter and Tendsto

### Overview

This section is the centre of the volume. It defines one filter for each point of a space, the filter of its neighbourhoods, and one predicate for limits, `Tendsto`. It then shows, statement by statement, that the classical definitions of limit are special cases.

The key idea is a change of view. The classical definition of $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = b$ is a game with two moves: given an $\varepsilon$, find a $\delta$. The filter definition hides the game in two filters. The filter $\mathcal{N}(a)$ of neighbourhoods of $a$ describes "$x$ near $a$". The filter $\mathcal{N}(b)$ describes "$y$ near $b$". The limit statement is that $f$ sends the first filter into the second: for each neighbourhood $V$ of $b$, the set $f^{-1}(V)$ is a neighbourhood of $a$. The $\varepsilon$ chooses $V$, the $\delta$ shows that $f^{-1}(V)$ is a neighbourhood. Nothing is lost, and one definition now covers each kind of limit.

### Definitions

**Definition (Neighbourhood filter).** Let $X$ be a topological space and $x \in X$. The neighbourhood filter $\mathcal{N}(x)$ is the family of sets $N \subseteq X$ that contain an open set $U$ with $x \in U$.

The family is a proper filter. It contains $X$. A superset of a neighbourhood is a neighbourhood. If $U \subseteq N$ and $U' \subseteq N'$ are open and contain $x$, then $U \cap U'$ is open, contains $x$ and lies in $N \cap N'$. Each neighbourhood contains $x$, so $\emptyset$ is not a neighbourhood.

**Definition (Tendsto).** Let $f : A \to B$ be a map, $F$ a filter on $A$ and $G$ a filter on $B$. Then $f$ tends to $G$ along $F$ if $f_{*}F \le G$, that is, if $f^{-1}(V) \in F$ for each $V \in G$.

Read $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ f\ F\ G$ as: "for each large set $V$ of $G$, $f(x) \in V$ eventually along $F$".

::: mathlib
`Filter.Tendsto` · `Mathlib.Order.Filter.Defs`

`Tendsto f l₁ l₂` is defined as `map f l₁ ≤ l₂`. The three arguments are the map, the source filter and the target filter. A limit of a sequence `u` at `a` is `Tendsto u atTop (𝓝 a)`. A limit of a function at a point is `Tendsto f (𝓝 a) (𝓝 b)`. The limit $f(x) \to \infty$ as $x \to \infty$ is `Tendsto f atTop atTop`.

**Definition (Continuity at a point, filter of a punctured or one-sided neighbourhood).** A map $f : X \to Y$ is continuous at $a$ if $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ f\ \mathcal{N}(a)\ \mathcal{N}(f(a))$. For $s \subseteq X$, the filter $\mathcal{N}_s(a) = \mathcal{N}(a) \sqcap \mathcal{P}(s)$ is the filter of neighbourhoods of $a$ within $s$. Its members are the sets that contain $N \cap s$ for some neighbourhood $N$ of $a$.

### Theorems and proofs

**Theorem (Tendsto to a neighbourhood filter).** Let $F$ be a filter on $A$, $f : A \to X$ and $x \in X$. Then $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ f\ F\ \mathcal{N}(x)$ if and only if $f^{-1}(U) \in F$ for each open $U$ with $x \in U$.

*Proof.* The open sets that contain $x$ are neighbourhoods, so the condition is necessary. Conversely, assume the condition and let $N \in \mathcal{N}(x)$. Choose an open $U$ with $x \in U \subseteq N$. Then $f^{-1}(U) \in F$ and $f^{-1}(U) \subseteq f^{-1}(N)$, so $f^{-1}(N) \in F$. $\square$

**Theorem (Limits of sequences).** Let $(a_n)$ be a sequence of real numbers and $L \in \mathbb{R}$. Then $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ a\ \mathrm{atTop}\ \mathcal{N}(L)$ if and only if for each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is an $N$ with $|a_n - L| < \varepsilon$ for all $n \ge N$.

*Proof.* Assume the filter statement and let $\varepsilon > 0$. The interval $(L - \varepsilon, L + \varepsilon)$ is open and contains $L$, so it is in $\mathcal{N}(L)$. So $\{n : a_n \in (L - \varepsilon, L + \varepsilon)\} \in \mathrm{atTop}$. It contains a tail $\{N, N+1, \dots\}$, and $|a_n - L| < \varepsilon$ for each $n \ge N$.

Conversely, assume the $\varepsilon$-$N$ condition and let $V \in \mathcal{N}(L)$. Then $V$ contains an open set $U$ with $L \in U$. The open intervals form a basis of the topology of $\mathbb{R}$ (Chapter 1), so some interval $(c, d)$ with $c < L < d$ lies in $U$. Take $\varepsilon = \min(L - c, d - L) > 0$. Choose $N$ for this $\varepsilon$. For $n \ge N$, $a_n \in (L - \varepsilon, L + \varepsilon) \subseteq V$. So $\{n : a_n \in V\}$ contains a tail and is in $\mathrm{atTop}$. $\square$

::: mathlib
`Metric.tendsto_atTop` · `Mathlib.Topology.MetricSpace.Pseudo.Defs`

The theorem for a sequence in any pseudometric space (Chapter 3), with any nonempty directed index type: `Tendsto u atTop (𝓝 a) ↔ ∀ ε > 0, ∃ N, ∀ n ≥ N, dist (u n) a < ε`. This is the bridge from the filter form to the classical form, and a proof uses it in both directions.

<?decl Metric.tendsto_atTop?>
:::

**Theorem (Limits of functions).** Let $f : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ and $a, b \in \mathbb{R}$. Then $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ f\ \mathcal{N}(a)\ \mathcal{N}(b)$ if and only if for each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ such that $|x - a| < \delta$ implies $|f(x) - b| < \varepsilon$.

*Proof.* The intervals $(a - \delta, a + \delta)$ with $\delta > 0$ form a basis of the filter $\mathcal{N}(a)$: each is a neighbourhood, and each neighbourhood contains one, by the argument of the previous proof. In the same way the intervals $(b - \varepsilon, b + \varepsilon)$ form a basis of $\mathcal{N}(b)$. By the basis criterion for `Tendsto`, the filter statement says: for each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ with $f((a - \delta, a + \delta)) \subseteq (b - \varepsilon, b + \varepsilon)$. This is the $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ statement. $\square$

*Remarks.* The filter $\mathcal{N}(a)$ contains the point $a$ itself, so this limit uses $f(a)$. It is the statement of continuity at $a$ when $b = f(a)$, and for $b \ne f(a)$ it is false. The classical limit, which ignores the value at $a$, uses the punctured filter $\mathcal{N}_{\ne}(a) = \mathcal{N}_{X \setminus \{a\}}(a)$. The same proof gives: $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ f\ \mathcal{N}_{\ne}(a)\ \mathcal{N}(b)$ if and only if for each $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a $\delta > 0$ such that $0 < |x - a| < \delta$ implies $|f(x) - b| < \varepsilon$.

::: mathlib
`Metric.tendsto_nhds_nhds` · `Mathlib.Topology.MetricSpace.Pseudo.Defs`

<?decl Metric.tendsto_nhds_nhds?>

**Theorem (Composition of limits).** If $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ f\ F\ G$ and $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ g\ G\ H$, then $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ (g \circ f)\ F\ H$.

*Proof.* Let $W \in H$. Then $g^{-1}(W) \in G$, so $f^{-1}(g^{-1}(W)) \in F$. This set is $(g \circ f)^{-1}(W)$. $\square$

This one line proof replaces several classical theorems: the limit of $g(a_n)$ for a continuous $g$, the chain rule for limits, and the change of variable in a limit.

**Theorem (Continuity is continuity at each point).** A map $f : X \to Y$ is continuous if and only if it is continuous at each point.

*Proof.* Assume $f$ is continuous, let $a \in X$ and let $V$ be an open set with $f(a) \in V$. Then $f^{-1}(V)$ is open and contains $a$, so it is in $\mathcal{N}(a)$. By the first theorem, $f$ is continuous at $a$.

Conversely, assume that $f$ is continuous at each point and let $V \subseteq Y$ be open. For $a \in f^{-1}(V)$, the set $V$ is a neighbourhood of $f(a)$, so $f^{-1}(V)$ is a neighbourhood of $a$. A set that is a neighbourhood of each of its points is open. So $f^{-1}(V)$ is open. $\square$

### Examples

**Example (A table of translations).** Each line gives a classical statement and its filter form. Here $u$ is a sequence and $f$ a real function.

- $u_n \to L$: `Tendsto u atTop (𝓝 L)`.
- $u_n \to \infty$: `Tendsto u atTop atTop`, which unfolds by `Filter.tendsto_atTop_atTop` to "for each $b$ there is an $N$ with $b \le u_n$ for $n \ge N$".
- $f(x) \to b$ as $x \to a$, $x \ne a$: `Tendsto f (𝓝[≠] a) (𝓝 b)`.
- $f(x) \to b$ as $x \to a^{+}$: `Tendsto f (𝓝[>] a) (𝓝 b)`.
- $f(x) \to b$ as $x \to \infty$: `Tendsto f atTop (𝓝 b)`.
- $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to 0^{+}$: `Tendsto f (𝓝[>] 0) atBot`.
- $f$ is continuous at $a$: `Tendsto f (𝓝 a) (𝓝 (f a))`.
- $u_n$ is eventually positive: `∀ᶠ n in atTop, 0 < u n`.

The theorems on limits are then stated once. The composition theorem gives each "limit of a composite" statement in the list.

**Example (The limit of $1/n$).** The filter proof that $1/n \to 0$ in $\mathbb{R}$ goes as follows. By the first theorem it suffices to treat an open $U$ that contains $0$. It contains an interval $(-\varepsilon, \varepsilon)$. By the Archimedean property choose $N$ with $N \varepsilon > 1$. For $n \ge N$, $0 < 1/n \le 1/N < \varepsilon$. So $\{n : 1/n \in U\}$ contains the tail from $N$, and it is in $\mathrm{atTop}$. This is the classical proof; the filter form only names its parts.

**Counterexample (Limits need not be unique).** Give $X = \{p, q\}$ the indiscrete topology. Then $\mathcal{N}(p) = \mathcal{N}(q) = \{X\}$. Each map into $X$ tends to $\mathcal{N}(p)$ and to $\mathcal{N}(q)$ along each filter, since the only member $X$ has preimage the whole domain. So the constant sequence $p$ tends to $q$. Limits are unique in a Hausdorff space (Chapter 4), and this is why Mathlib has no function "the limit" without a Hausdorff hypothesis.

**Example (Eventual equality).** If $f = g$ on a neighbourhood of $a$, then $f$ and $g$ have the same limits along $\mathcal{N}(a)$. In filter language, $f =^{f} g$ along $\mathcal{N}(a)$ and $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ f\ \mathcal{N}(a)\ G$ give $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ g\ \mathcal{N}(a)\ G$. The proof: for $V \in G$, $g^{-1}(V) \supseteq f^{-1}(V) \cap \{x : f(x) = g(x)\}$, an intersection of two members.

### The library around it

The definitions of `𝓝`, `𝓝[s]`, `ContinuousAt` and `ContinuousWithinAt` are in `Mathlib.Topology.Defs.Filter`. The basic lemmas on `𝓝` are in `Mathlib.Topology.Neighborhoods`, and the general lemmas on `Tendsto` are in `Mathlib.Order.Filter.Tendsto`. The lemmas below appear in almost each proof about limits.

- `tendsto_const_nhds`: a constant map tends to `𝓝 c`.
- `Filter.tendsto_id`: the identity tends to `l` along `l`.
- `Filter.Tendsto.mono_left`: a limit along `l₁` gives a limit along a finer `l₁' ≤ l₁`. For example, a limit along `𝓝 a` gives a limit along `𝓝[s] a`.
- `Filter.Tendsto.eventually`: a limit transfers an eventual property of the target to the source.
- `Filter.tendsto_atTop`: `Tendsto m f atTop` means that `b ≤ m a` eventually, for each `b`.

The algebra of limits, such as `Filter.Tendsto.add` and `Filter.Tendsto.mul`, needs a topological group or ring and is in Volume 14.

### Exercises

1. (A) Write the filter form of: $f(x) \to 3$ as $x \to 2^{-}$; $u_n \to -\infty$; $f(x) \to \infty$ as $x \to 0$, $x \ne 0$.
2. (A) Prove from the definitions that if $u_n \to L$ in $\mathbb{R}$ and $L > 0$, then $u_n > 0$ for all large $n$. Name each filter lemma that the proof uses.
3. (A) Show that in a discrete space $\mathcal{N}(x) = \mathcal{P}(\{x\})$, and that a sequence tends to $x$ if and only if it is eventually equal to $x$.
4. (B) Show that $\mathrm{Tendsto}\ f\ F\ \mathcal{P}(B)$ holds if and only if $f(x) \in B$ eventually along $F$.
5. (B) Let $X$ have the cofinite topology and be infinite. Show that a sequence of distinct points tends to each point of $X$.
6. (B) Prove the punctured form of the limit theorem stated in the remarks.
7. (B) Show that $f : X \to Y$ is continuous at $a$ if and only if $f_{*}\mathcal{N}(a) \le \mathcal{N}(f(a))$, and deduce that the composite of maps continuous at $a$ and at $f(a)$ is continuous at $a$.
8. (C) Let $X$ be a set with a family $(\mathcal{N}(x))_{x \in X}$ of filters such that each member of $\mathcal{N}(x)$ contains $x$, and each $N \in \mathcal{N}(x)$ contains some $M \in \mathcal{N}(x)$ with $N \in \mathcal{N}(y)$ for all $y \in M$. Show that there is exactly one topology on $X$ whose neighbourhood filters are the $\mathcal{N}(x)$.

### Notes and history

The neighbourhood axioms of Exercise 8 are the original axioms of Hausdorff (1914). Cartan defined convergence of a filter to a point as $F \le \mathcal{N}(x)$ in 1937, and Bourbaki stated each limit as the image of a filter. The predicate `Tendsto` with three arguments, a map and two filters, is the form of Bourbaki, *Topologie générale* I, §7. In Mathlib, Patrick Massot and Johannes Hölzl built the large library of `Tendsto` lemmas that makes the classical theorems on limits short consequences of a few general ones.
