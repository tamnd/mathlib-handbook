## Star subalgebras and the star closure

### Overview

A star subalgebra of a star algebra $A$ is a subalgebra $S$ with $x^* \in S$ for each $x \in S$. The bounded operators on a Hilbert space, the continuous functions on a compact space and the matrices give the main examples, and their star subalgebras are the objects of operator theory. A von Neumann algebra is a star subalgebra of the bounded operators that is equal to its double centralizer. The star subalgebra that a normal element generates is commutative, and this fact is the base of the continuous functional calculus.

The six modules of this section give the substructures closed under the star, at four levels: star subsemigroups, star subsemirings, star subalgebras and their non-unital forms. They also give the star subalgebra that a set generates, the centre and the centralizer, and the pointwise star of a set. The substructures use the morphisms of Section 1: a star algebra homomorphism has a range, an equalizer and a restriction to a star subalgebra.

### The library around it

#### The structures

Each substructure extends the plain substructure with one condition: the carrier is closed under the star. The table gives the structures.

| Lean name | Extends | Ambient type |
|---|---|---|
| `SubStarSemigroup` | `Subsemigroup` | a multiplication and a star |
| `NonUnitalStarSubsemiring` | `NonUnitalSubsemiring` | a non-unital semiring with a star |
| `StarSubsemiring` | `Subsemiring` | a semiring with a star |
| `NonUnitalStarSubalgebra` | `NonUnitalSubalgebra` | a non-unital algebra with a star |
| `StarSubalgebra` | `Subalgebra` | an algebra with a star |

Each structure has a `SetLike` instance, so an element of `StarSubalgebra R A` is a set of $A$, and `x ∈ S` is membership in the carrier. The star condition is the class `StarMemClass`, from `Mathlib.Algebra.Star.Basic`, and its lemma `StarMemClass.star_mem` says that $x \in S$ implies $x^* \in S$. The instances `StarSubsemiring.starMemClass` and `StarSubalgebra.starMemClass` register the structures in this class.

A star subalgebra is a star algebra in its own right. The instances `StarSubalgebra.starRing`, `StarSubalgebra.algebra` and `StarSubalgebra.starModule` give the structure on the subtype. For a general type with `StarMemClass`, `StarMemClass.instStarRing` does the same. The inclusion of a star subalgebra is `StarSubalgebra.subtype`, a star algebra homomorphism, and `StarSubalgebra.inclusion h` is the map $S_1 \to S_2$ for $S_1 \le S_2$.

The forgetful maps are `StarSubalgebra.toSubalgebra` and `StarSubsemiring.toSubsemiring`, with the same pattern at the other levels. Each is injective, and the lemmas `StarSubsemiring.toSubsemiring_injective`, `StarSubsemiring.toSubsemiring_le_iff` and `StarSubalgebra.mem_toSubalgebra` give the usual bridge. A non-unital star subalgebra that contains $1$ becomes a unital one with `NonUnitalStarSubalgebra.toStarSubalgebra S h`, and `StarSubalgebra.toNonUnitalStarSubalgebra` goes the other way. Each structure has the constructions copy and ofClass with the same meaning as in Section 1, for example `StarSubsemiring.copy` and `StarSubsemiring.ofClass`.

The star subalgebras of $A$ form a complete lattice, `StarSubalgebra.completeLattice`. The infimum is the intersection, and the supremum is the star subalgebra that the union generates. For a directed family, the supremum is the union: `StarSubalgebra.coe_iSup_of_directed` says $\bigvee_i S_i = \bigcup_i S_i$ as sets when the index type is not empty.

#### The star subalgebra generated by a set

`StarAlgebra.adjoin R s` is the smallest star subalgebra of $A$ that contains $s$. It is the subalgebra that the set $s \cup s^*$ generates, where $s^* = \{x^* : x \in s\}$.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `StarAlgebra.subset_adjoin` | $s \subseteq \mathrm{adjoin}_R(s)$ |
| `StarAlgebra.adjoin_le` | $s \subseteq S$ implies $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s) \le S$ |
| `StarAlgebra.adjoin_le_iff` | $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s) \le S \iff s \subseteq S$ |
| `StarAlgebra.gc` | adjoin and the coercion to sets form a Galois connection |
| `StarAlgebra.gi` | the Galois connection is a Galois insertion |
| `StarAlgebra.adjoin_toSubalgebra` | the subalgebra of $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s)$ is $\mathrm{Alg.adjoin}_R(s \cup s^*)$ |
| `StarAlgebra.adjoin_eq_starClosure_adjoin` | $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s)$ is the star closure of $\mathrm{Alg.adjoin}_R(s)$ |
| `StarAlgebra.adjoin_eq_span` | as a module, $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s)$ is the span of the monoid generated by $s \cup s^*$ |
| `StarAlgebra.adjoin_induction` | induction over the elements of $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s)$ |

The proof of `StarAlgebra.adjoin_toSubalgebra` shows the main idea. Let $T = \mathrm{Alg.adjoin}_R(s \cup s^*)$. The set $s \cup s^*$ is closed under the star, so its star is equal to itself. The star of a subalgebra is a subalgebra, and `Subalgebra.star_adjoin_comm` says $\mathrm{Alg.adjoin}_R(s)^* = \mathrm{Alg.adjoin}_R(s^*)$. So $T^* = T$, and $T$ is a star subalgebra. It contains $s$, and each star subalgebra that contains $s$ contains $s \cup s^*$ and so contains $T$. The lemma `Subalgebra.star_adjoin_comm` itself needs `StarModule R A`, because the star of $r \cdot x$ must be $r^* \cdot x^*$.

The star closure of a subalgebra $S$ is `Subalgebra.starClosure S`, the smallest star subalgebra that contains $S$. The lemma `Subalgebra.coe_starClosure` says that as a set it is $S \sqcup S^*$, the subalgebra that $S \cup S^*$ generates. The non-unital forms are `NonUnitalStarAlgebra.adjoin` and `NonUnitalSubalgebra.starClosure`, with `NonUnitalSubalgebra.starClosure_eq_adjoin`. The two constructions agree after one more step: `StarAlgebra.adjoin_nonUnitalStarSubalgebra` says that the unital star subalgebra generated by the non-unital one is the unital star subalgebra generated by $s$.

A star algebra homomorphism is determined by its values on a generating set. `StarAlgHom.adjoin_le_equalizer` says: if $f = g$ on $s$, then $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s)$ lies in the equalizer `StarAlgHom.equalizer f g`. The proof is one line: the equalizer is a star subalgebra, because $f(x^*) = f(x)^* = g(x)^* = g(x^*)$ for $x$ in it, so `StarAlgebra.adjoin_le` applies. Then `StarAlgHom.ext_adjoin` gives $f = g$ on the whole of $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s)$, and `StarAlgHom.ext_of_adjoin_eq_top` gives $f = g$ when $s$ generates $A$. Images behave well: `StarAlgHom.map_adjoin` says $f(\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s)) = \mathrm{adjoin}_R(f(s))$.

#### Ranges, maps and comaps

The operations on subalgebras have star forms with the same names. `StarSubalgebra.map f S` is the image of $S$ under a star algebra homomorphism $f$, and `StarSubalgebra.comap f S` is the preimage. `StarAlgHom.range f` is the image of the whole algebra. `StarAlgHom.codRestrict` restricts the codomain of $f$ to a star subalgebra that contains its image, and `StarAlgHom.rangeRestrict` restricts it to the range. An injective star algebra homomorphism gives an equivalence with its range, `StarAlgEquiv.ofInjective`. The lemma `StarSubalgebra.algebraMap_mem` says that each star subalgebra contains the image of $R$.

#### The centre and the centralizer

The centre of a star monoid is closed under the star. The lemma `Set.star_mem_center` says that $a$ central implies $a^*$ central: for each $b$, $a^* b = (b^* a)^* = (a b^*)^* = b a^*$. So the centre is a substructure at each level: `SubStarSemigroup.center`, `StarSubsemiring.center`, `NonUnitalStarSubsemiring.center` and `NonUnitalStarSubalgebra.center`. In a commutative algebra the centre is everything: `NonUnitalStarSubalgebra.center_eq_top`.

The centralizer of a set needs more care. The centralizer of $s$ need not be closed under the star, because $a$ can commute with $s$ while $a^*$ does not. The lemma `Set.star_centralizer` gives the exact rule.

$$\left(C(s)\right)^* = C(s^*).$$

Proof: $a \in C(s)^*$ means that $a^*$ commutes with each $x \in s$. Apply the star to $a^* x = x a^*$: this gives $x^* a = a x^*$. So $a$ commutes with each element of $s^*$. Each step reverses. So the centralizer of a set that is closed under the star is closed under the star, and `Set.star_mem_centralizer'` states this. For a general set, the library uses $s \cup s^*$: `Set.star_mem_centralizer` says that $C(s \cup s^*)$ is closed under the star.

The star subalgebra form is `StarSubalgebra.centralizer R s`. By `StarSubalgebra.coe_centralizer`, its carrier is $C(s \cup s^*)$, not $C(s)$. The centralizer is antitone, `StarSubalgebra.centralizer_le`.

The double centralizer contains the generated star subalgebra: `StarAlgebra.adjoin_le_centralizer_centralizer` says $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s) \le C(C(s \cup s^*))$. Proof: each $x \in s$ commutes with each element of $C(s \cup s^*)$, so $s$ lies in the double centralizer, and the double centralizer is a star subalgebra. Then `StarAlgebra.adjoin_le` applies.

This gives a test for commutativity. `StarAlgebra.isMulCommutative_adjoin` says that $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s)$ is commutative when each element of $s$ is normal, the elements of $s$ commute in pairs, and each $x \in s$ commutes with $y^*$ for each $y \in s$. Proof: by `Set.union_star_self_comm`, the set $t = s \cup s^*$ then commutes in pairs. So $t \subseteq C(t)$, and $C(C(t)) \subseteq C(t)$. Two elements $x, y$ of $C(C(t))$ commute, because $y \in C(t)$ and $x$ commutes with each element of $C(t)$. The star subalgebra $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(s)$ lies in $C(C(t))$, so it is commutative. For one element, the instance `StarAlgebra.isMulCommutative_adjoin_singleton` says that a normal element generates a commutative star subalgebra. This is the start of the continuous functional calculus.

#### The pointwise star of a set

In the scope *Pointwise*, the definition `Set.star` makes `Set α` a type with a star: $s^* = \{x : x^* \in s\}$. For an involutive star this is the image of $s$ under the star, by `Set.image_star`. The instance `Set.instInvolutiveStar` makes the operation involutive.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Set.mem_star` | $a \in s^* \iff a^* \in s$ |
| `Set.star_mem_star` | $a^* \in s^* \iff a \in s$ |
| `Set.union_star` | $(s \cup t)^* = s^* \cup t^*$ |
| `Set.inter_star` | $(s \cap t)^* = s^* \cap t^*$ |
| `Set.star_subset_star` | $s^* \subseteq t^* \iff s \subseteq t$ |
| `Set.star_singleton` | $\{x\}^* = \{x^*\}$ |
| `Set.star_add` | $(s + t)^* = s^* + t^*$ |
| `Set.star_mul` | $(s t)^* = t^* s^*$ |
| `Set.Pairwise.commute_star_iff` | $s^*$ commutes in pairs if and only if $s$ does |

The proof of `Set.star_mul` follows the law $(x y)^* = y^* x^*$: $z \in (s t)^*$ means $z^* = x y$ with $x \in s$ and $y \in t$, so $z = y^* x^*$ with $y^* \in t^*$ and $x^* \in s^*$. The order of the factors reverses, as for elements.

For a substructure $S$ with `StarMemClass`, `StarMemClass.star_coe_eq` says $S^* = S$ as a set.

#### Common errors

1. `StarSubalgebra.centralizer R s` is the centralizer of $s \cup s^*$, not of $s$. A proof that an element lies in it must check commutation with $s^*$ as well.
2. A pairwise commuting set of normal elements need not generate a commutative star subalgebra. `StarAlgebra.isMulCommutative_adjoin` also needs $x y^* = y^* x$ for $x, y \in s$. In a C*-algebra this condition follows from the others, by the Fuglede theorem, but the lemma works in a general star algebra and so it asks for the condition.
3. The pointwise star `Set.star` is only active after `open Pointwise`. Without it, `star s` for a set $s$ does not elaborate.

### Exercises

1. (A) Prove `Set.star_mem_center` from the law $(x y)^* = y^* x^*$ and `star_star`.
2. (A) Show that the range of a star algebra homomorphism is closed under the star, and compare the proof with the definition of `StarAlgHom.range`.
3. (B) Prove that the centralizer of a self-adjoint element is a star subalgebra, with `Set.star_mem_centralizer'`.
4. (B) Find a star algebra $A$ and an element $a$ such that the centralizer of $\{a\}$ is not closed under the star. (Hint: use $2 \times 2$ matrices over $\mathbb{C}$ with the conjugate transpose and a nilpotent matrix.)
5. (B) Prove that $\mathrm{adjoin}_R(\{a\})$ is commutative for a normal element $a$, with `StarAlgebra.isMulCommutative_adjoin`.
6. (C) Prove that $C(C(C(s))) = C(s)$ for each set $s$ of a monoid. Deduce that, for a set $s$ of a star algebra that is closed under the star, `StarSubalgebra.centralizer` applied twice gives a star subalgebra that is equal to its own double centralizer.
