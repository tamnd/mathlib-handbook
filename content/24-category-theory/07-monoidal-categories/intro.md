# Monoidal categories

A monoidal category is a category with a product of objects that is associative and unital up to coherent isomorphism. The cartesian product of sets, the tensor product of modules and the composition of endofunctors are the main examples. The structure lets the axioms of a monoid, a ring or a monad be stated inside a category, and it is the setting for enriched categories, for representation theory and for the tensor products of homological algebra.

Section 1 defines monoidal categories in the textbook form and in Mathlib's whiskered form, derives the first consequences of the pentagon and the triangle, and explains Mac Lane's coherence theorem through the free monoidal category and its normal forms. Section 2 adds braidings and symmetries with the hexagon axioms, lax and strong monoidal functors, closed monoidal categories with their internal homs, and monoid objects. It proves that lax monoidal functors carry monoid objects to monoid objects and that monads are the monoid objects among endofunctors; monoid objects in types are monoids and in abelian groups they are rings.

The chapter needs functors and natural transformations from Chapter 2, products from Chapter 4, adjunctions from Chapter 5 and monads from Chapter 6. The examples use modules and linear maps from Volume 7 and tensor products from Volume 8. Chapter 8 turns from tensor products to sums: zero objects, biproducts and abelian categories. Volume 25 uses monoidal structures on triangulated and derived settings, and the tensor product of chain complexes in Volume 26 is a monoidal structure of this kind.
