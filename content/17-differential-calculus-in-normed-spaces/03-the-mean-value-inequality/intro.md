# The mean value inequality

The mean value theorem of Volume 15 gives a point $c$ with $f(b) - f(a) = f'(c)(b - a)$. For a map with values in a normed space of dimension at least $2$ no such point need exist: the curve $t \mapsto (\cos t, \sin t)$ returns to its start, and its velocity never vanishes. This chapter proves the inequality that replaces the theorem.

Section 1 proves the mean value inequality $\|\gamma(b) - \gamma(a)\| \le M(b - a)$ for a curve with $\|\gamma'\| \le M$. With the chain rule on segments, it gives $\|f(y) - f(x)\| \le M\|y - x\|$ on a convex set where $\|Df\| \le M$, and a version with a linear term. The consequences are Lipschitz bounds, and the theorem that a map with zero derivative on a connected open set is constant. The section ends with the derivative of the norm.

Section 2 gives two applications. The first is the strict derivative, which compares $f(y)$ and $f(z)$ for two points near $x$. The second is the exchange of a limit and a derivative: when the derivatives converge uniformly, the limit is differentiable, and a series can be differentiated term by term under a summable bound.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 and 2, and Volume 15, Chapters 1 and 3. The inequality with a linear term and the strict derivative are the tools of the inverse function theorem in Chapter 6, and the inequality for curves is the base of Grönwall's inequality in Chapter 8.
