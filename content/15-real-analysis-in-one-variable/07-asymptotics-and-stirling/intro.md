# Asymptotics and Stirling's formula

Analysis often needs to compare two functions near a point or at infinity, not to compute them. The statements "$f$ is bounded by a constant times $g$", "$f$ is small compared with $g$" and "$f$ and $g$ have ratio $1$" have the names $f = O(g)$, $f = o(g)$ and $f \sim g$. This chapter makes the three notions precise and proves the rules to compute with them. Then it applies them to the factorial.

Section 1 defines $O$, $o$, $\Theta$ and $\sim$ along a filter, as Mathlib does, so that one definition covers the limits $n \to \infty$, $x \to \infty$, $x \to x_0$ and $x \to x_0^+$. It proves the rules of transitivity, sums and products, the characterization of $o$ and $\sim$ by quotients, and the scale of growth: a power of $\log x$ is small compared with a power of $x$, and a power of $x$ is small compared with $e^x$. Section 2 proves Stirling's formula $n! \sim \sqrt{2\pi n}\,(n/e)^n$. The proof has two parts. The first part shows that $n!/(\sqrt{2n}\,(n/e)^n)$ decreases to a positive limit, with the series of the logarithm. The second part identifies the limit as $\sqrt{\pi}$ with Wallis's product for $\pi/2$. Section 3 defines the linear and the exponential growth rates of a sequence, as the lower and upper limits of $u_n/n$ and of $\frac{1}{n}\log u_n$, and superpolynomial decay, and it compares complex powers with exponentials along suitable filters. Section 4 extends $O$, $o$ and $\Theta$ to products of normed spaces, to product filters and to completions, and defines these relations through gauges for topological vector spaces with no norm.

The chapter uses the limits of sequences of Chapter 1 and the limits of functions of Chapter 2, the mean value theorem of Chapter 3, the Peano form of Taylor's theorem of Chapter 4, the exponential, the logarithm and the real powers of Chapter 5, and the sine and $\pi$ of Chapter 6. The notation $=O[l]$ and $=o[l]$ appears again in the later volumes: in the definition of the Fréchet derivative in several variables, in the estimates of measure theory and in analytic number theory.
