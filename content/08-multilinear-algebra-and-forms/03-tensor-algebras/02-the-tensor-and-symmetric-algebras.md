## The tensor and symmetric algebras

### Overview

This section builds the two most general $R$-algebras that contain an $R$-module $M$, for a commutative ring $R$. The tensor algebra $T(M)$ is the most general associative algebra with a linear map from $M$. The symmetric algebra $S(M)$ is the most general commutative one. When $M$ is free with basis $(e_k)_{k \in \kappa}$, the first is the algebra of noncommutative polynomials in the variables $e_k$, and the second is the polynomial ring $R[X_k : k \in \kappa]$.

The section uses the tensor powers, the direct sums and the gradings of Section 1 of this chapter. The polynomial rings are those of Volume 6, Chapter 5.

### Definitions

**Definition (Tensor algebra).** The tensor algebra of $M$ is the graded algebra $T(M) = \bigoplus_{n \ge 0} M^{\otimes n}$ of Section 1. The map $\iota : M \to T(M)$ is the inclusion of $M = M^{\otimes 1}$ as the degree $1$ part.

In $T(M)$ the product of $\iota(x_1), \dots, \iota(x_n)$ is the pure tensor $x_1 \otimes \dots \otimes x_n$. So $T(M)$ is generated as an $R$-algebra by $\iota(M)$.

**Definition (Free algebra).** For a set $X$, the free algebra $R\langle X \rangle$ is the algebra of noncommutative polynomials in $X$. As a module it is free with basis the words $x_1 x_2 \cdots x_n$ in the letters of $X$, the empty word being $1$. The product of two words is their concatenation.

**Definition (Symmetric algebra).** Let $I \subseteq T(M)$ be the two-sided ideal generated by the elements $x \otimes y - y \otimes x$ for $x, y \in M$. The symmetric algebra is $S(M) = T(M) / I$. The map $\iota : M \to S(M)$ is $\iota$ of $T(M)$ followed by the quotient map $\pi$.

### Theorems and proofs

**Theorem (Universal property of the tensor algebra).** Let $A$ be an $R$-algebra, not necessarily commutative, and $f : M \to A$ a linear map. There is a unique algebra homomorphism $\bar f : T(M) \to A$ with $\bar f \circ \iota = f$.

*Proof.* For each $n$ the map $(x_1, \dots, x_n) \mapsto f(x_1) \cdots f(x_n)$ is multilinear, since the product of $A$ is bilinear. For $n = 0$ it is the constant $1$. By the universal property of Section 1 it gives a linear map $f_n : M^{\otimes n} \to A$. By the universal property of the direct sum, the $f_n$ give a linear map $\bar f : T(M) \to A$. On pure tensors,
$$\bar f\bigl((x_1 \otimes \dots \otimes x_n)(y_1 \otimes \dots \otimes y_m)\bigr) = f(x_1) \cdots f(x_n) f(y_1) \cdots f(y_m) = \bar f(x_1 \otimes \dots \otimes x_n) \, \bar f(y_1 \otimes \dots \otimes y_m).$$
Both sides are bilinear in the two factors, and the pure tensors span. So $\bar f$ is multiplicative, and $\bar f(1) = 1$. For uniqueness, an algebra homomorphism is fixed by its values on a set of generators, and $\iota(M)$ generates $T(M)$. $\square$

**Theorem (Tensor algebra of a free module).** Let $M$ have a basis $(e_k)_{k \in \kappa}$. Then $T(M) \cong R\langle \kappa \rangle$, with $\iota(e_k) \mapsto k$. So the products $\iota(e_{k_1}) \cdots \iota(e_{k_n})$, over all words $k_1 \cdots k_n$, form a basis of $T(M)$.

*Proof.* Let $\varphi : T(M) \to R\langle \kappa \rangle$ be the lift of the linear map with $e_k \mapsto k$. Let $\psi : R\langle \kappa \rangle \to T(M)$ be the homomorphism with $k \mapsto \iota(e_k)$, from the universal property of the free algebra. The homomorphism $\psi \varphi$ fixes each $\iota(e_k)$, so it agrees with the identity on $\iota(M)$ by linearity. By uniqueness it is the identity. In the same way $\varphi \psi$ fixes each letter $k$, so it is the identity. $\square$

When $\kappa$ is finite with $d$ elements, the degree $n$ part of $T(M)$ has rank $d^n$, as Section 1 also shows.

**Theorem (Universal property of the symmetric algebra).** The algebra $S(M)$ is commutative. For each commutative $R$-algebra $A$ and each linear map $f : M \to A$ there is a unique algebra homomorphism $\bar f : S(M) \to A$ with $\bar f \circ \iota = f$.

*Proof.* The elements $\iota(x)$ of $S(M)$ commute in pairs, since $\iota(x)\iota(y) - \iota(y)\iota(x) = \pi(x \otimes y - y \otimes x) = 0$. They generate $S(M)$. The set of elements that commute with all $\iota(x)$ is a subalgebra, so it is all of $S(M)$. Then the set of elements that commute with all of $S(M)$ is a subalgebra that contains each $\iota(x)$, so it is $S(M)$. This proves commutativity. For the lift, let $g : T(M) \to A$ be the lift of $f$ to the tensor algebra. Then $g(x \otimes y - y \otimes x) = f(x) f(y) - f(y) f(x) = 0$, as $A$ is commutative. So the ideal $I$ lies in $\ker g$, and $g$ factors as $\bar f \circ \pi$. Uniqueness holds since $\iota(M)$ generates $S(M)$. $\square$

**Theorem (Symmetric algebra of a free module).** Let $M$ have a basis $(e_k)_{k \in \kappa}$. Then $S(M) \cong R[X_k : k \in \kappa]$ with $\iota(e_k) \mapsto X_k$. So the monomials $\prod_k \iota(e_k)^{a_k}$, for $a : \kappa \to \mathbb{N}$ of finite support, form a basis of $S(M)$.

*Proof.* The proof is the proof of the theorem on $T(M)$, with the universal property of the polynomial ring in place of that of the free algebra. The map $\varphi : S(M) \to R[X_k]$ lifts $e_k \mapsto X_k$. The map $\psi : R[X_k] \to S(M)$ evaluates $X_k$ at $\iota(e_k)$, which is possible since $S(M)$ is commutative. Both compositions fix the generators, so they are the identities. $\square$

The ideal $I$ is generated by elements of degree $2$, so it is the direct sum of its intersections with the $M^{\otimes n}$. Hence $S(M) = \bigoplus_n S^n(M)$ with $S^n(M) = M^{\otimes n} / (I \cap M^{\otimes n})$. The index of this edition has no declaration for this grading of `SymmetricAlgebra`.

### Examples

**Example (One generator).** For $M = R$ with basis $e$, both $T(R)$ and $S(R)$ are the polynomial ring $R[X]$. Indeed, the words in one letter are the powers $X^n$. Here the tensor algebra is commutative, although $T(M)$ is not commutative for a free $M$ of rank $2$ or more.

**Example (Ranks in two variables).** Let $M = \mathbb{Q}^2$ with basis $x, y$. The degree $3$ part of $T(M)$ has the $8$ basis words $xxx, xxy, xyx, yxx, xyy, yxy, yyx, yyy$. In $S(M)$ the words with the same number of $x$ become equal, so the degree $3$ part has the $4$ monomials $x^3, x^2 y, x y^2, y^3$. For $M = \mathbb{Q}^d$ the degree $n$ part of $S(M)$ has rank $\binom{n + d - 1}{d - 1}$, the number of $a \in \mathbb{N}^d$ with $a_1 + \dots + a_d = n$.

**Counterexample (The target of the lift must be commutative).** Let $M = \mathbb{Q}^2$ and $A = M_2(\mathbb{Q})$. Let $f(x) = E_{12}$ and $f(y) = E_{21}$. Then $f(x) f(y) = E_{11}$ and $f(y) f(x) = E_{22}$. An algebra homomorphism $S(M) \to A$ that extends $f$ would send $xy = yx$ to both $E_{11}$ and $E_{22}$. So no lift exists. The lift to $T(M)$ exists and sends $x \otimes y$ to $E_{11}$.

### The library around it

The maps `TensorAlgebra.toExterior` and `TensorAlgebra.toClifford` of Chapters 4 and 7 are lifts in the sense of this section. The file `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.TensorPower.Symmetric` defines `SymmetricPower`, the quotient of a tensor power by the permutations of the factors, which is a second model of $S^n(M)$.

The file `Mathlib.Algebra.FreeAlgebra.Cardinality` bounds the size of a free algebra: `FreeAlgebra.cardinalMk_le_max` states $\#\,\mathrm{FreeAlgebra}(R, X) \le \max(\#R, \#X, \aleph_0)$, and `FreeAlgebra.cardinalMk_eq_max` gives equality when $X$ is nonempty and $R$ is nontrivial. For empty $X$ the free algebra is $R$ (`FreeAlgebra.cardinalMk_eq`). The same bound for the subalgebra generated by a set is `Algebra.cardinalMk_adjoin_le`.

Without the laws of associativity and of a unit, the free object is `FreeNonUnitalNonAssocAlgebra R X`, the monoid algebra of the free magma on $X$. The map `FreeNonUnitalNonAssocAlgebra.of` sends $X$ into it, and `FreeNonUnitalNonAssocAlgebra.lift` is the universal property: each function from $X$ to a non-unital non-associative $R$-algebra $A$ extends to exactly one homomorphism of non-unital algebras (`FreeNonUnitalNonAssocAlgebra.lift_unique`, `FreeNonUnitalNonAssocAlgebra.hom_ext`).

### Exercises

1. (A) Write $(x + y)^2$ in $T(\mathbb{Q}^2)$ and in $S(\mathbb{Q}^2)$ in the bases of this section. Hint: in $T$ the product does not commute.
2. (A) Show that $T(0) = R$ and $S(0) = R$. Hint: use the universal properties, or the direct sum.
3. (A) Let $f : M \to N$ be linear. Show that there is a unique algebra homomorphism $T(f) : T(M) \to T(N)$ with $T(f)(\iota x) = \iota(f x)$, and that $T(g \circ f) = T(g) \circ T(f)$. Hint: lift $\iota \circ f$.
4. (B) Show that $S(M \oplus N) \cong S(M) \otimes_R S(N)$. Hint: check the universal property for $S(M) \otimes_R S(N)$.
5. (B) Show that the rank of $S^n(R^d)$ is $\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}$. Hint: count the ways to put $n$ balls in $d$ boxes.
6. (B) Show that the center of $T(R^2)$ is $R$ when $R$ is a field. Hint: compare the coefficients of words in $z x$ and $x z$.
7. (B) Show that a linear map $f : M \to A$ with $f(x)^2 = 0$ for all $x$ satisfies $f(x) f(y) = -f(y) f(x)$. Hint: expand $f(x + y)^2$. This is the starting point of Chapter 4.
8. (C) Show that $T(M)$ has no zero divisors when $R$ is a field. Hint: compare the components of the highest degree of $a$, $b$ and $ab$, and use a basis of words.

### Notes and history

In Mathlib the tensor algebra is the quotient of the free algebra, since a quotient gives the universal property with no reference to tensor powers. The grading and the direct sum form come later as theorems.
