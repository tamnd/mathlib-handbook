## The field of rational functions

### Overview

For a field $K$, the field $K(X)$ of rational functions in one variable is the field of fractions of the polynomial ring $K[X]$. It is the first example of a transcendental extension: the element $X$ is transcendental over $K$, and each field generated by one transcendental element is isomorphic to $K(X)$. In Mathlib this field is `RatFunc K`.

This section describes the library of `RatFunc`: the construction, the numerator and the denominator in lowest terms, evaluation, the degree $\deg p - \deg q$ of a fraction $p/q$, the valuation at infinity, and the substitution $X \mapsto X + r$. It ends with two theorems on the intermediate fields of $K(X)/K$. For a nonconstant $f = p/q$ in lowest terms, the degree $[K(X) : K(f)]$ is $\max(\deg p, \deg q)$. The theorem of Lüroth says that each intermediate field $E \neq K$ is again generated by one element, so $E \cong K(X)$.

The type `RatFunc K` exists for each commutative ring $K$ that is a domain, not only for a field. Most of the statements on numerators, evaluation and degree need a field.

### The library around it

#### The construction

The module `Mathlib.FieldTheory.RatFunc.Defs` defines `RatFunc K` as a structure with one field, an element of `FractionRing (Polynomial K)`. The constructor is `RatFunc.ofFractionRing` and the projection is `RatFunc.toFractionRing`. The structure is a wrapper: the type `FractionRing (Polynomial K)` has many instances that come from the general theory of localization, and some of them do not have good definitional behavior for polynomials. The wrapper gives a type with only the instances that the library defines for it.

The function `RatFunc.mk p q` makes the fraction $p/q$ from two polynomials, with the junk value $0$ if $q = 0$. The lemma `RatFunc.mk_eq_div` says that it is the quotient of the images of $p$ and $q$. To define a function on `RatFunc K`, use `RatFunc.liftOn`, which asks for a function on pairs $(p, q)$ with $q$ a nonzero divisor that is constant on equivalent pairs. To prove a statement for all rational functions, use `RatFunc.induction_on`, which reduces it to the fractions $p/q$ with $q \neq 0$.

The module `Mathlib.FieldTheory.RatFunc.Basic` gives the field structure and the universal property. The instance `RatFunc.instIsFractionRingPolynomial` says that `RatFunc K` is a field of fractions of `Polynomial K` in the sense of `IsFractionRing`, so the general API of fraction fields applies. The isomorphism with the wrapped type is `RatFunc.toFractionRingAlgEquiv`. The universal property is `RatFunc.liftAlgHom`: an algebra map $\varphi$ from $K[X]$ to a field $L$ that sends nonzero divisors to nonzero divisors extends to an algebra map from $K(X)$ to $L$. The variants are `RatFunc.liftRingHom` and `RatFunc.liftMonoidWithZeroHom`. In the other direction, a map of polynomial rings with the same condition induces `RatFunc.map`, and `RatFunc.mapAlgHom` is the version for algebra maps. For an algebraic extension $K \subseteq L$, `RatFunc.finrank_ratFunc_ratFunc` gives $[L(X) : K(X)] = [L : K]$.

#### Numerator and denominator

Over a field, each rational function has a unique representation $f = p/q$ with $p, q$ coprime and $q$ monic. The functions are `RatFunc.num` and `RatFunc.denom`. The basic lemmas are:

- `RatFunc.num_div_denom`: the quotient of `RatFunc.num f` by `RatFunc.denom f` is $f$;
- `RatFunc.isCoprime_num_denom` and `RatFunc.monic_denom`: the two polynomials are coprime and the denominator is monic;
- `RatFunc.denom_ne_zero` and `RatFunc.num_eq_zero_iff`: the denominator is never $0$, and the numerator is $0$ exactly for $f = 0$.

For a quotient of polynomials, `RatFunc.num_div` and `RatFunc.denom_div` compute the reduced form: divide $p$ and $q$ by their greatest common divisor, and then make the denominator monic. The lemma `RatFunc.num_dvd` says that if $f = p/q$, then `RatFunc.num f` divides $p$. The values of the functions on sums and products are not simple, but the lemmas `RatFunc.num_denom_add` and `RatFunc.num_denom_mul` give the relation $\operatorname{num}(f + g) \cdot \operatorname{denom}(f) \operatorname{denom}(g) = (\operatorname{num} f \operatorname{denom} g + \operatorname{num} g \operatorname{denom} f) \cdot \operatorname{denom}(f + g)$ and its analogue for products.

#### The variable, constants and evaluation

The module `Mathlib.FieldTheory.RatFunc.AsPolynomial` treats polynomials as rational functions. The element `RatFunc.X` is the image of the variable, and `RatFunc.C` is the ring map from constants. The lemmas `RatFunc.algebraMap_X` and `RatFunc.algebraMap_C` say that the map from `Polynomial K` sends $X$ and the constants to these elements, and `RatFunc.smul_eq_C_mul` turns scalar multiplication into multiplication by a constant. The reduced form of $X$ is $X/1$: `RatFunc.num_X` and `RatFunc.denom_X`.

Evaluation is `RatFunc.eval f a x`, for a ring map $f \colon K \to L$ into a field, a point $a \in L$ and $x \in K(X)$. It evaluates numerator and denominator at $a$ and divides, so it has the junk value $0$ at the poles of $x$. Thus evaluation is not a ring map. The lemmas `RatFunc.eval_add` and `RatFunc.eval_mul` are true only under the hypothesis that the denominators of the two terms do not vanish at $a$. The lemmas `RatFunc.eval_X` and `RatFunc.eval_C` have no hypothesis.

The element $X$ is transcendental over $K$ (`RatFunc.transcendental_X`). Conversely:

**Theorem.** Let $L/K$ be a field extension and $t \in L$ transcendental over $K$. Then $K(X) \cong K(t)$ by an isomorphism of $K$-algebras with $X \mapsto t$.

*Proof.* Since $t$ is transcendental, the evaluation map $K[X] \to L$, $p \mapsto p(t)$, is injective. So it sends nonzero polynomials to nonzero elements of the field $L$, and it extends to a map $K(X) \to L$ by the universal property. The image is a field that contains $K$ and $t$, and each element of the image is a quotient of polynomials in $t$, so the image is $K(t)$. A ring map from a field is injective. $\square$

This is `RatFunc.algEquivOfTranscendental`, and `RatFunc.algEquivOfTranscendental_X` says that it sends $X$ to $t$.

#### The degree and the valuation at infinity

The module `Mathlib.FieldTheory.RatFunc.Degree` defines `RatFunc.intDegree f`, the integer $\deg(\operatorname{num} f) - \deg(\operatorname{denom} f)$, with the value $0$ at $f = 0$. It is additive on products, so it is a group homomorphism on $K(X)^\times$: `RatFunc.intDegree_mul` and `RatFunc.intDegree_div` for nonzero factors, and `RatFunc.intDegree_inv`. For polynomials it is the degree (`RatFunc.intDegree_polynomial`), with `RatFunc.intDegree_X` equal to $1$ and `RatFunc.intDegree_C` equal to $0$. The lemma `RatFunc.intDegree_add_le` gives $\deg(f + g) \le \max(\deg f, \deg g)$ for $f + g \neq 0$.

These two properties make $f \mapsto \deg f$ the negative of a discrete valuation, the valuation at the point $\infty$. The module `Mathlib.FieldTheory.RatFunc.Valuation` defines `RatFunc.inftyValuation F` as a multiplicative valuation with values in `WithZero (Multiplicative ℤ)`. By `RatFunc.inftyValuation_of_ne_zero`, its value at $f \neq 0$ is the exponential of `RatFunc.intDegree f`. So the value of $X$ is the exponential of $1$ (`RatFunc.inftyValuation.X`), a nonzero constant has the value $1$ in the multiplicative group (`RatFunc.inftyValuation.C`), and the value of a polynomial is the exponential of its degree (`RatFunc.inftyValuation.polynomial`). In this multiplicative convention, a large value means a large absolute value, as for the absolute value on $\mathbb{Q}$. The completion of $K(X)$ for this valuation is `RatFunc.CompletionAtInfty`.

The valuation at the point $0$ comes from the prime ideal $(X)$ of $K[X]$, through the general theory of valuations at the primes of a Dedekind domain. The prime is `Polynomial.idealX`, and `Polynomial.valuation_X_eq_neg_one` gives the value of $X$ at it. The instance `RatFunc.valuedRatFunc` puts this valuation on $K(X)$.

#### The substitution $X \mapsto X + r$

The module `Mathlib.FieldTheory.Laurent` defines `RatFunc.laurent r`, the $R$-algebra endomorphism of $R(X)$ that substitutes $X + r$ for $X$. On polynomials it is the Taylor substitution `Polynomial.taylor r`, which sends nonzero divisors to nonzero divisors (`RatFunc.taylor_mem_nonZeroDivisors`), so it extends to fractions. The lemmas are `RatFunc.laurent_X`, `RatFunc.laurent_C`, `RatFunc.laurent_at_zero` (the identity for $r = 0$) and `RatFunc.laurent_laurent`, which composes two substitutions to the substitution by $r + s$. From the last two, `RatFunc.laurent_injective` follows: `RatFunc.laurent (-r)` is an inverse. The substitution moves the point $r$ to the point $0$, so it reduces the expansion of a rational function at $r$ to the expansion at $0$.

#### Intermediate fields and the theorem of Lüroth

Let $f = p/q \in K(X)$ be in lowest terms and not constant. Then $X$ is a root of the polynomial
$$\mu_f(T) = p(T) - f \cdot q(T) \in K(f)[T].$$
This is `RatFunc.minpolyX` in the module `Mathlib.FieldTheory.RatFunc.IntermediateField`, with `RatFunc.minpolyX_aeval_X` for the root. Its degree is $\max(\deg p, \deg q)$ (`RatFunc.natDegree_minpolyX`). Thus $X$ is algebraic over $K(f)$ (`RatFunc.isAlgebraic_adjoin_simple_X`), and $f$ is transcendental over $K$ (`RatFunc.transcendental_of_ne_C`), since $K(X)$ has transcendence degree $1$.

**Theorem.** For a nonconstant $f = p/q$ in lowest terms, $[K(X) : K(f)] = \max(\deg p, \deg q)$.

*Proof sketch.* It is sufficient to show that $\mu_f$ is irreducible over $K(f)$. The element $f$ is transcendental, so $K[f]$ is a polynomial ring, and by the lemma of Gauss it is sufficient to show irreducibility in $K[f][T] = K[f, T]$. There $\mu_f = p(T) - f q(T)$ has degree $1$ in $f$, and a factorization would give a factor that depends only on $T$ and divides both $p$ and $q$. Such a factor is a constant, since $p$ and $q$ are coprime. $\square$

This is `RatFunc.finrank_eq_max_natDegree`, with irreducibility as `RatFunc.irreducible_minpolyX`. Since `Module.finrank` is $0$ for an infinite extension, the formula is also true for a constant $f$, when both sides are $0$.

The theorem of Lüroth is in the module `Mathlib.FieldTheory.RatFunc.Luroth`. Let $E$ be an intermediate field of $K(X)/K$ with $E \neq K$. Then $X$ is algebraic over $E$ (`RatFunc.IntermediateField.isAlgebraic_X`). Let $\varphi$ be its minimal polynomial over $E$. Some coefficient $g$ of $\varphi$ is not in $K$, since otherwise $X$ is algebraic over $K$. This coefficient is `RatFunc.Luroth.generator E`, an element of $E$ (`RatFunc.Luroth.generator_mem`) that is not a constant (`RatFunc.Luroth.generator_spec`). The proof compares $\varphi$ with $\mu_g$. Both have $X$ as a root, and an argument with the lemma of Gauss in $K[X][T]$ shows that they have the same degree. So $[K(X) : E] = [K(X) : K(g)]$, and from $K(g) \subseteq E$ it follows that $E = K(g)$.

**Theorem (Lüroth).** Each intermediate field $E$ of $K(X)/K$ with $E \neq K$ is isomorphic to $K(X)$ as a $K$-algebra.

In Lean, `RatFunc.Luroth.algEquiv` takes a proof of `E ≠ ⊥` and gives an isomorphism from `RatFunc K` to $E$. It is `RatFunc.algEquivOfTranscendental` for the generator, and `RatFunc.Luroth.algEquiv_X` says that it sends $X$ to `RatFunc.Luroth.generator E`.

#### Common errors

1. `RatFunc K` is not definitionally `FractionRing (Polynomial K)`. Use `RatFunc.toFractionRingAlgEquiv` or the `IsFractionRing` instance to pass between them, not a proof by definitional unfolding.
2. `RatFunc.eval` has the junk value $0$ at a pole, and `RatFunc.eval_add` needs hypotheses on the denominators. A rewrite with this lemma makes side goals of the form `Polynomial.eval₂ f a x.denom ≠ 0`.
3. `RatFunc.inftyValuation` is multiplicative, with values in `WithZero (Multiplicative ℤ)`. Its value at $X$ is the exponential of $1$, not of $-1$, so it grows with the degree.

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute `RatFunc.num` and `RatFunc.denom` of the quotient of $2X^2 - 2$ by $2X + 2$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
2. (A) Show that `RatFunc.intDegree` of $X^3/(X^2 + 1)$ is $1$, and find its value at infinity.
3. (A) Show that $[K(X) : K(X^2)] = 2$ and $[K(X) : K(X + 1/X)] = 2$.
4. (B) Show that each $K$-algebra automorphism of $K(X)$ sends $X$ to $(aX + b)/(cX + d)$ with $ad - bc \neq 0$. Hint: the degree formula.
5. (B) Let $s = X^2 + X^{-2}$ and $t = X + X^{-1}$. Show that $K(s, t) = K(t)$ and compute $[K(X) : K(s)]$.
6. (C) Prove the degree formula $[K(X) : K(f)] = \max(\deg p, \deg q)$ in full, with the lemma of Gauss for $K[f][T]$.
