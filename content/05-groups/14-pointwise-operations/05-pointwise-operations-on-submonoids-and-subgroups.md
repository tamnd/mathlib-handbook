## Pointwise operations on submonoids and subgroups

### Overview

A subgroup $H$ of a group $G$ is also a set, so the pointwise operations of the sections before apply to it. The product $H K$ of two subgroups is a set, the inverse $H^{-1}$ is a set, and the translate $a H$ is a set. The first two modules of this section compare these sets with the lattice operations of Chapter 3. The main results are that the join $H \sqcup K$ is the subgroup generated by $H K$, that $H K$ is already a subgroup when one factor is normal, and the modular law of Dedekind.

The same modules give a second construction. When a monoid $\alpha$ acts on $G$ by group automorphisms, it also acts on the set of subgroups of $G$: the element $a$ sends $H$ to its image $a \cdot H$. The conjugation action of $G$ on its subgroups is the main case, and it expresses normality, normalizers and normal cores in the language of Chapter 6. The last three modules go in the other direction: they restrict an action of $M$ on a type to an action of a submonoid or a subgroup of $M$.

### The library around it

#### Subgroups as sets

A subgroup is closed under the product and the inverse, so as a set it is equal to its own product and its own inverse. The library states this for all classes of substructures at once, with lemmas in the root namespace. The lemma `coe_mul_coe` states $H H = H$ for every `SubmonoidClass`, and `coe_set_pow` states $H^n = H$ for $n \ne 0$. The lemma `inv_coe_set` states $H^{-1} = H$ for every `InvMemClass`. The criterion `coe_set_eq_one` states that the set of $H$ is the set $\{1\}$ if and only if $H = \bot$. For a submonoid, the form with a name in the namespace is `Submonoid.coe_mul_self_eq`. Two small lemmas test membership of products: `Subgroup.mul_subset` states that $s t \subseteq H$ when $s, t \subseteq H$, and `Subgroup.singleton_mul_subgroup` states $\{h\} H = H$ for $h \in H$.

#### Joins and products of subgroups

**Theorem.** For subgroups $H$ and $K$ of $G$, $H \sqcup K = \langle H K \rangle$.

*Proof.* The set $H K$ is in $H \sqcup K$, because both factors are in it and it is closed under the product. So $\langle H K \rangle \le H \sqcup K$. Conversely, $H = H \cdot 1 \subseteq H K$ and $K = 1 \cdot K \subseteq H K$, so $H$ and $K$ are both in $\langle H K \rangle$, and so is their join. $\square$

The Lean name is `Subgroup.sup_eq_closure_mul`, and `Submonoid.sup_eq_closure_mul` is the form for submonoids. For sets, `Submonoid.closure_mul_le` gives the inclusion $\langle S T \rangle \le \langle S \rangle \sqcup \langle T \rangle$. In general $H K$ is not a subgroup. In the symmetric group $S_3$, two subgroups of order $2$ give a set $H K$ with $4$ elements, and $4$ does not divide $6$. The product is a subgroup when one factor is normal.

**Theorem.** If $N$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ and $H$ is a subgroup, then the set of $N \sqcup H$ is the set $N H$.

*Proof.* By the theorem above, it is enough to show that $N H$ is a subgroup. It contains $1 = 1 \cdot 1$. For a product, $n_1 h_1 n_2 h_2 = n_1 (h_1 n_2 h_1^{-1}) h_1 h_2$, and $h_1 n_2 h_1^{-1} \in N$ because $N$ is normal. For an inverse, $(n h)^{-1} = h^{-1} n^{-1} = (h^{-1} n^{-1} h) h^{-1}$, which is again in $N H$. $\square$

This is `Subgroup.normal_mul`. The proof only uses the fact that $H$ normalizes $N$, and `Subgroup.coe_mul_of_left_le_normalizer_right` states this sharper form: if $H \le N_G(N)$, then the set of $H \sqcup N$ is $H N$. For a normal subgroup $N$ and any set $S$, `Subgroup.set_mul_normal_comm` states $S N = N S$. The join of normal subgroups is normal, by the instances `Subgroup.sup_normal` and `Subgroup.iSup_normal`.

The modular law of Dedekind is a rule for the meet of a product. It holds for subgroups that are not normal.

**Theorem.** Let $A$, $B$, $C$ be subgroups of $G$ with $C \le A$. Then $(A \sqcap B) C = A \cap (B C)$ as sets.

*Proof.* An element of $(A \sqcap B) C$ has the form $x c$ with $x \in A \cap B$ and $c \in C$. It is in $B C$, and it is in $A$ because $c \in C \le A$. Conversely, let $a = b c$ with $a \in A$, $b \in B$ and $c \in C$. Then $b = a c^{-1} \in A$, because $C \le A$. So $b \in A \sqcap B$ and $a = b c \in (A \sqcap B) C$. $\square$

The Lean name is `Subgroup.inf_mul_assoc`, and `Subgroup.mul_inf_assoc` is the mirror form $A (B \sqcap C) = A B \cap C$ for $A \le C$.

#### Inverses and closures

For a submonoid $S$ of a group, the set $S^{-1}$ is again a submonoid, but it is not $S$ in general. Mathlib gives this submonoid with the definition `Submonoid.inv` of the inverse, and the membership lemma `Submonoid.mem_inv`: $g \in S^{-1}$ if and only if $g^{-1} \in S$. The map $S \mapsto S^{-1}$ is an order isomorphism of the lattice of submonoids, `Submonoid.invOrderIso`, with `Submonoid.inv_le_inv`. It commutes with closures: `Submonoid.closure_inv` states $\langle s^{-1} \rangle = \langle s \rangle^{-1}$ for submonoids. For subgroups the inverse disappears: `Subgroup.closure_inv` states $\langle s^{-1} \rangle = \langle s \rangle$, with the special case `Subgroup.closure_singleton_inv`, and `Subgroup.inv_subset_closure` states $s^{-1} \subseteq \langle s \rangle$.

**Theorem.** As a submonoid, the subgroup generated by a set $S$ is the submonoid generated by $S \cup S^{-1}$.

*Proof.* The submonoid generated by $S \cup S^{-1}$ is in the subgroup $\langle S \rangle$, because $\langle S \rangle$ contains $S$ and $S^{-1}$. For the other inclusion, it is enough to show that this submonoid is closed under inverses. The inverse of a product $s_1 \cdots s_k$ of elements of $S \cup S^{-1}$ is $s_k^{-1} \cdots s_1^{-1}$, and each $s_i^{-1}$ is again in $S \cup S^{-1}$. $\square$

This is `Subgroup.closure_toSubmonoid`. The special case of one element is `Subgroup.toSubmonoid_zpowers`: the integer powers of $g$ are the join of the natural powers of $g$ and of $g^{-1}$, with `Submonoid.powers_le_zpowers` for one inclusion. The theorem gives an induction principle that adds one generator at a time from the left, `Subgroup.closure_induction_left`. For a join of a family, `Subgroup.iSup_induction` reduces a property of all elements of $\bigsqcup_i S_i$ to the elements of each $S_i$, to $1$ and to products. The lemma `Subgroup.coe_iSup_eq_iUnion_finset_coe_biSup` writes the join of a family as the union of its joins over finite subfamilies. For a set $s$ that contains $1$, `Subgroup.closure_pow` states $\langle s^n \rangle = \langle s \rangle$ for $n \ne 0$.

#### The action on subgroups

Let a monoid $\alpha$ act on $G$ by group endomorphisms, which is the class `MulDistribMulAction α G`. Then $\alpha$ acts on `Subgroup G` by images: `Subgroup.pointwise_smul_def` states that $a \cdot S$ is the image of $S$ under the endomorphism of $a$. This action is the definition `Subgroup.pointwiseMulAction`. It is a scoped instance, so it is available only after `open Pointwise`. The same holds for submonoids with `Submonoid.pointwiseMulAction`. The coercion lemma `Subgroup.coe_pointwise_smul` states that the set of $a \cdot S$ is the pointwise translate $a \cdot S$ of sets. Membership is `Subgroup.mem_smul_pointwise_iff_exists`: $m \in a \cdot S$ if and only if $m = a \cdot s$ for some $s \in S$.

The action commutes with closures and joins, by `Subgroup.smul_closure` and `Subgroup.smul_sup`, and `Submonoid.smul_closure` is the form for submonoids. When $\alpha$ is a group, each $a$ acts by an automorphism, and more holds. The cancellation `Subgroup.smul_mem_pointwise_smul_iff` states $a x \in a S$ if and only if $x \in S$. The definition `Subgroup.equivSMul` is the group isomorphism $S \cong a \cdot S$.

**Theorem.** If $\alpha$ is a group, then $a \cdot (S \sqcap T) = a \cdot S \sqcap a \cdot T$.

*Proof.* The inclusion from left to right holds for every map. For the other inclusion, let $m \in a \cdot S \sqcap a \cdot T$. Then $a^{-1} m \in S$ and $a^{-1} m \in T$ by the cancellation lemma, so $a^{-1} m \in S \sqcap T$ and $m = a (a^{-1} m) \in a \cdot (S \sqcap T)$. $\square$

This is `Subgroup.smul_inf`. For a monoid that is not a group, the image of a meet can be smaller than the meet of the images, so the hypothesis is necessary.

#### Conjugation and normality

The group $G$ acts on itself by inner automorphisms through `MulAut.conj`, and so it acts on its subgroups by conjugation: `MulAut.conj g • H` is $g H g^{-1}$. A normal subgroup is fixed by each conjugation, by `Subgroup.Normal.conj_smul_eq_self`, and each subgroup is fixed by conjugation with its own elements, by `Subgroup.conj_smul_eq_self_of_mem`. The lemma `Subgroup.conj_smul_le_of_le` states that for $P \le H$ and $h \in H$, the conjugate $h P h^{-1}$ is again in $H$.

**Theorem.** If $g H g^{-1} = H$ for all $g \in G$, then $H$ is normal.

*Proof.* Let $h \in H$ and $g \in G$. Then $g h g^{-1} \in g H g^{-1} = H$. This is the definition of a normal subgroup. $\square$

The Lean name is `Subgroup.Normal.of_conjugate_fixed`. With the conjugation action `ConjAct G` of Chapter 6, the normalizer is the stabilizer of $H$: `Subgroup.conjAct_pointwise_smul_iff` states that `ConjAct.toConjAct g • H = H` if and only if $g \in N_G(H)$.

**Theorem.** The normal core of $H$ is the meet of all conjugates of $H$: $\mathrm{core}(H) = \bigsqcap_{g} g H g^{-1}$.

*Proof.* The meet $D = \bigsqcap_g g H g^{-1}$ is in $H$, because $H$ is the conjugate for $g = 1$. It is normal, because conjugation by $x$ permutes the conjugates of $H$: $x (g H g^{-1}) x^{-1} = (x g) H (x g)^{-1}$. If $N \le H$ is normal, then $N = g N g^{-1} \le g H g^{-1}$ for each $g$, so $N \le D$. So $D$ is the largest normal subgroup in $H$, which is the normal core. $\square$

This is `Subgroup.normalCore_eq_iInf_conjAct`, where $g$ runs over `ConjAct G`. The normal core is the subject of Chapter 5. With `Subgroup.normalizer_le_normalizer_sup_normal`, the normalizer of $H$ is in the normalizer of $H \sqcup N$ for a normal subgroup $N$.

#### Actions of submonoids and subgroups

An action of a monoid $M'$ on a type $\alpha$ restricts to each submonoid $S$: an element $g \in S$ acts as its image in $M'$. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Submonoid.MulAction` gives this restriction with `Submonoid.smul` and `Submonoid.mulAction`. The definition is `Submonoid.smul_def`, $g \cdot a = (g : M') \cdot a$, and `Submonoid.mk_smul` is the form for an element given with its proof of membership. The instances keep the properties of the action: `Submonoid.isScalarTower`, `Submonoid.smulCommClass_left` and `Submonoid.smulCommClass_right`. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Submonoid.DistribMulAction` adds the same restriction for an action on an additive monoid, `Submonoid.distribMulAction`, and for an action by monoid endomorphisms, `Submonoid.mulDistribMulAction`.

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Subgroup.Actions` gives the forms for a subgroup, `Subgroup.smul_def` and `Subgroup.mk_smul`, and `Subgroup.smulCommClass_left` and `Subgroup.smulCommClass_right`.

**Theorem.** If the action of $G$ on $\alpha$ is faithful, then the restricted action of each subgroup $S$ is faithful.

*Proof.* Let $g_1, g_2 \in S$ act in the same way on each $a \in \alpha$. Their images in $G$ act in the same way, so they are equal in $G$ because the action of $G$ is faithful. The map from $S$ to $G$ is injective, so $g_1 = g_2$. $\square$

This is the instance `Subgroup.instFaithfulSMulSubtypeMem`. The instance `Subgroup.center.smulCommClass_left` states that the action of the center of $G$ on $G$ by left multiplication commutes with left multiplication by $G$. The proof is the definition of the center.

#### Common errors

1. A learner writes `a • H` for a subgroup $H$ and gets an error: no scalar action instance is found. The action on subgroups is a scoped instance, so the file needs `open Pointwise`.
2. A learner uses `Subgroup.normal_mul` with two subgroups that are not normal. Then $H K$ is not a subgroup in general, and only `Subgroup.sup_eq_closure_mul` is true.
3. A learner applies `Subgroup.smul_inf` with an action of a monoid that is not a group. The lemma needs `Group α`, because the proof uses the inverse of $a$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Use `Subgroup.sup_eq_closure_mul` and `Subgroup.closure_toSubmonoid` to describe the elements of $H \sqcup K$ as finite products of elements of $H$ and $K$.
2. (A) Prove `Subgroup.mul_inf_assoc` from `Subgroup.inf_mul_assoc` with the map $x \mapsto x^{-1}$.
3. (B) Prove that if $H$ and $K$ are finite subgroups with $H K = K H$ as sets, then $H K$ is a subgroup of order $|H| |K| / |H \cap K|$.
4. (B) Let $H$ be a subgroup of index $n$ in $G$. Use `Subgroup.normalCore_eq_iInf_conjAct` to show that the normal core of $H$ is the meet of at most $n$ conjugates of $H$.
5. (B) Prove that for a group $\alpha$ that acts on $G$ by automorphisms, the stabilizer of a subgroup $H$ under the pointwise action is a subgroup of $\alpha$, and identify it for the conjugation action.
6. (C) Give a monoid $\alpha$ that acts on a group $G$ by endomorphisms and two subgroups $S$, $T$ with $a \cdot (S \sqcap T) \ne a \cdot S \sqcap a \cdot T$ for some $a$.
