# Finite maps and search trees

A finite map sends finitely many keys to values. This chapter builds finite maps in two ways. The first section stores the pairs in a list. It defines lookup, erase, insert and union on lists of dependent pairs, puts the condition of distinct keys into the type `AList`, and then forgets the order of the entries to get `Finmap`. Two finite maps with the same lookups are equal, and this extensionality makes the algebra of `Finmap` simple. The second section stores ordered keys in a binary tree. It starts with plain binary trees, their node counts and the path by which `BinaryTree.get` finds a node. It then studies `Ordnode`, the size balanced trees of Adams, with the three invariants of order, size and balance. It proves that a rotation keeps the order of the keys and the balance of the sizes, that an insertion keeps a valid tree valid, and that the height of a valid tree grows only as the logarithm of its size. The type `Ordset` packs a valid tree with its proof.

The chapter needs lists, permutations and the relation `List.Perm` from the first two chapters of this volume, the finite sets and multisets of Volume 3, and the preorders and linear orders of Volume 4. The proofs use induction and some arithmetic with natural numbers.

The next chapters change the form of the data but keep the method: give a type a precise invariant, then prove that each operation keeps it. Chapter 4 fixes the length of a list in its type and then lets the length be infinite. Chapter 5 adds sequences that may stop and computations that may not stop. Chapter 6 returns to trees and lists as instances of the class `Traversable`.
