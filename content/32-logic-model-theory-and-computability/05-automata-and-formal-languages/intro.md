# Automata and formal languages

This chapter studies languages, that is, sets of words over an alphabet, and the finite devices that describe them. The first section defines the operations on languages, shows that the languages form a Kleene algebra, and proves Arden's lemma. It then defines regular expressions, their languages, and the Brzozowski derivative, which gives a matcher that the library proves correct. The second section defines deterministic automata, nondeterministic automata and automata with silent moves, and proves that the three models recognize the same languages. It proves the Boolean closure and the closure under reversal of the regular languages, the pumping lemma and the Myhill-Nerode theorem, and it shows that $\{a^n b^n\}$ is not regular. The third section defines context-free grammars and derivations, computes the languages of two grammars, and proves closure under reversal.

The chapter needs lists and their operations, sets and the order on sets, finite types with the pigeonhole principle, and induction on lists and on inductive relations. The algebra of the first section uses semirings and the star of a Kleene algebra. No model theory is needed, and the chapter does not depend on the earlier chapters of this volume.

The automata of this chapter have a finite memory. The next chapters remove this limit. Chapter 6 defines the partial recursive functions, and Chapter 7 defines Turing machines, which add an unbounded tape to a finite control. Chapter 8 turns to a different side of computation, the running time of divide and conquer algorithms. The text points out at each place where the index of this edition has no declaration for a classical result, such as Kleene's theorem.
