# Local rings and Nakayama's lemma

A local ring is a commutative ring with exactly one maximal ideal. The local rings are the rings of "functions near a point": the ring $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ of fractions with denominator prime to $p$ looks at the integers near the prime $p$, and the ring of power series $k[[X]]$ looks at the line near the origin. Localization at a prime ideal turns any ring into a local ring, so many questions about a ring reduce to questions about its local rings.

Section 1 defines local rings, gives the criteria by units, defines the residue field and the local homomorphisms, and proves that the localization $R_{\mathfrak p}$ at a prime is local. Section 2 proves Nakayama's lemma, first in the form of the determinant trick and then for the Jacobson radical, and derives its consequences for a finitely generated module $M$ over a local ring $(R, \mathfrak m, k)$: a set generates $M$ if and only if its image generates the vector space $M/\mathfrak m M$, and the minimal number of generators of $M$ is $\dim_k M/\mathfrak m M$. For $M = \mathfrak m$ this number is the embedding dimension, the dimension of the cotangent space $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$.

The chapter needs Chapter 1 for the determinant trick, the ideals and quotients of Volume 6, and the modules of Volume 7. The construction of the localization and its universal property are in Volume 9, Commutative algebra I, which this chapter uses without proof. Chapter 3 uses local rings for valuation rings and discrete valuation rings, Chapter 4 uses localization at the primes of a Dedekind domain, and Chapter 7 compares the embedding dimension with the Krull dimension.

In Mathlib a local ring is a ring `R` with the class `[IsLocalRing R]`, its maximal ideal is `IsLocalRing.maximalIdeal R`, and its residue field is `IsLocalRing.ResidueField R`.
