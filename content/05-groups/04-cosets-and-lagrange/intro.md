# Cosets and the theorem of Lagrange

The cosets of a subgroup cut a group into parts of the same size. Section 1 defines the cosets and the index, proves the theorem of Lagrange and the tower law for indices, and compares left and right cosets. Section 2 studies the order of an element, proves that it divides the order of the group, and derives the theorems of Fermat and Euler. It shows that $A_4$ has no subgroup of order $6$, so the converse of Lagrange fails. Section 3 describes the exponent of a group, the least common multiple of the orders, with the theorem that a commutative group has an element whose order is the exponent, and the minimum order and the groups of units modulo $n$-th powers. Section 4 studies complements and transversals of a subgroup: their cardinality, their existence, the decomposition $g = st$, and the criterion by coprime orders. Section 5 gives finitely generated groups and their rank, the lemma of Schreier on subgroups of finite index, residually finite and virtually cyclic groups, and a descent criterion for finite generation. Section 6 studies torsion-free monoids and groups with unique roots, the unique product property of ordered groups, free monoids and free abelian groups, and the affine monoids, which embed in $\mathbb{Z}^n$ and are generated by their finitely many irreducible elements.

The chapter needs Chapter 3 and the divisibility of the integers from Volume 2. The quotient groups of Chapter 5 are sets of cosets, and the counting arguments of Chapters 6 and 8 rest on Lagrange.
