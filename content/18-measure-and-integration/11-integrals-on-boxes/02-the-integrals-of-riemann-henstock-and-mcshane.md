## The integrals of Riemann, Henstock and McShane

### Overview

A Riemann sum of $f$ over a tagged partition $\pi$ of a box is
$$\sum_{J \in \pi} \operatorname{vol}(J) \, f(\text{tag } J).$$
The integral of Riemann is the limit of these sums as the boxes become small. The integral of Henstock and Kurzweil allows the size of each box to depend on its tag, through a gauge. More functions have this integral, and every derivative on an interval has it. The integral of McShane uses the same gauges but lets the tag lie outside its box. In $\mathbb{R}^n$ it gives the Lebesgue integral.

Mathlib defines all these integrals with one definition. The filter on tagged partitions changes, and a structure of three Boolean values selects it. This section explains the parameters, the definition, the main theorems and the relation with the Bochner integral of Chapter 5. It ends with the divergence theorem on a box, which is the reason for one of the parameters.

The section uses the boxes and partitions of Section 1 of this chapter, the topology of Volume 13 and the measures and integrals of Chapters 2 and 5.

### The library around it

#### Integration parameters

The structure `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams` has three Boolean fields.

1. `bRiemann`: the gauge is constant, so the condition is on the size of the boxes only.
2. `bHenstock`: each tag lies in the closed box of its own box.
3. `bDistortion`: the gauge may depend on a bound on the distortion of the boxes.

The predicate `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.RCond l r` says that $r$ is constant when `l.bRiemann = true`. Four values have names.

| Lean name | `bRiemann` | `bHenstock` | `bDistortion` | Integral |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.Riemann` | true | true | false | Riemann |
| `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.Henstock` | false | true | false | Henstock and Kurzweil |
| `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.McShane` | false | false | false | McShane |
| `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.GP` | false | true | true | a form of Henstock for the divergence theorem |

The parameters form a bounded order, and a larger value gives a larger filter. A larger filter has fewer convergent sums, so fewer functions are integrable. The value `GP` is the bottom element, by `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.gp_le`. The lemmas `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.henstock_le_riemann` and `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.henstock_le_mcShane` place the integral of Henstock below the two others.

#### The filters

For a box `I` and parameters `l`, the predicate `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.MemBaseSet l I c r π` collects the conditions on a tagged prepartition: it is subordinate to $r$, it is Henstock if `l.bHenstock = true`, and its distortion is at most $c$ if `l.bDistortion = true`. The filter `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.toFilteriUnion I π₀` on tagged prepartitions of `I` is generated by the sets of such prepartitions with the same union as $\pi_0$. Its form as a basis is `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.hasBasis_toFilteriUnion`. The filter is not trivial, by `BoxIntegral.IntegrationParams.toFilteriUnion_neBot`. The proof of this fact uses the lemma of Cousin of Section 1.

#### The definition of the integral

The integral sum is `BoxIntegral.integralSum f vol π`. It takes a box additive map `vol : ι →ᵇᵃ (E →L[ℝ] F)` and applies `vol J` to `f (tag J)`. For a measure, the volume is `BoxIntegral.BoxAdditiveMap.toSMul (MeasureTheory.Measure.toBoxAdditive μ)`, and the sum is the usual Riemann sum. The bilinear form lets one definition cover integrals of vector valued functions against vector valued volumes.

| Lean name | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `BoxIntegral.HasIntegral I l f vol y` | the integral sums tend to $y$ along `l.toFilteriUnion I ⊤` |
| `BoxIntegral.Integrable I l f vol` | some $y$ satisfies `HasIntegral` |
| `BoxIntegral.integral I l f vol` | such a $y$ if it exists, and $0$ otherwise |

The design follows `HasSum` and `tsum`. The lemma `BoxIntegral.HasIntegral.unique` says that the limit is unique, and `BoxIntegral.Integrable.hasIntegral` says that the integral of an integrable function satisfies `HasIntegral`. The lemmas `BoxIntegral.HasIntegral.mono` and `BoxIntegral.Integrable.mono` pass to smaller parameters: a function integrable in the sense of Riemann is integrable in the sense of Henstock, with the same integral.

#### Rules of computation

The integral is linear: `BoxIntegral.integral_add`, `BoxIntegral.integral_neg`, `BoxIntegral.integral_sub` and `BoxIntegral.integral_smul`, with integrability hypotheses where needed. The lemma `BoxIntegral.HasIntegral.sum` treats finite sums. The integral of a constant $c$ is `vol I c`, by `BoxIntegral.integral_const`. For a measure, `BoxIntegral.norm_integral_le_of_le_const` gives
$$\Big\| \int_I f \Big\| \le \mu(I) \, C \quad \text{when } \|f\| \le C \text{ on } [I],$$
and `BoxIntegral.integral_nonneg` gives positivity.

The criterion of Cauchy is `BoxIntegral.integrable_iff_cauchy`, and `BoxIntegral.integrable_iff_cauchy_basis` is its form with gauges. An integrable function is integrable on every subbox, by `BoxIntegral.Integrable.to_subbox`. The map $J \mapsto \int_J f$ is box additive, by `BoxIntegral.Integrable.toBoxAdditive`. The lemma `BoxIntegral.Integrable.dist_integralSum_le_of_memBaseSet` is the inequality of Henstock and Sacks: it bounds the distance between the integral sums of two prepartitions that are subordinate to the gauge of the definition.

#### Which functions are integrable

The file `Mathlib.Analysis.BoxIntegral.Basic` and the file `Mathlib.Analysis.BoxIntegral.Integrability` give the main criteria.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `BoxIntegral.integrable_of_continuousOn` | a function continuous on $[I]$ is integrable for every `l` |
| `BoxIntegral.integrable_of_bounded_and_ae_continuous` | a bounded function that is continuous almost everywhere is integrable for every `l` |
| `MeasureTheory.ContinuousOn.hasBoxIntegral` | for a continuous function, the box integral is the Bochner integral |
| `MeasureTheory.IntegrableOn.hasBoxIntegral` | a Bochner integrable function has the Bochner integral as its box integral, when `l.bRiemann = false` |
| `MeasureTheory.SimpleFunc.hasBoxIntegral` | the same for simple functions |
| `BoxIntegral.HasIntegral.congr_ae` | changing $f$ on a null set does not change the integral, when `l.bRiemann = false` |

The second criterion is the criterion of Lebesgue for the integral of Riemann. The fourth one says that the integral of McShane extends the Bochner integral on a box. It fails for the integral of Riemann, since an unbounded function, or the indicator of the rational points, is not Riemann integrable.

The lemmas `BoxIntegral.HasIntegral.of_bRiemann_eq_false_of_forall_isLittleO` and `BoxIntegral.HasIntegral.mcShane_of_forall_isLittleO` give a test for `HasIntegral`. They take a box additive map $g$ and compare it with $f$ on small boxes near each point. The divergence theorem uses this test.

#### The divergence theorem

The file `Mathlib.Analysis.BoxIntegral.DivergenceTheorem` proves the divergence theorem for the parameters `GP`. Let $I$ be a box in $\mathbb{R}^{n+1}$ and $f : \mathbb{R}^{n+1} \to E^{n+1}$. Assume that $f$ is continuous on $[I]$ at the points of a countable set $s$ and has a derivative $f'$ within $[I]$ at the other points of $[I]$. The lemma `BoxIntegral.hasIntegral_GP_divergence_of_forall_hasDerivWithinAt` says that the divergence $\sum_i \partial_i f_i$ has the integral
$$\sum_i \Big( \int_{F_i^+} f_i - \int_{F_i^-} f_i \Big),$$
where $F_i^+$ and $F_i^-$ are the upper and lower faces of $I$ normal to the coordinate $i$. The theorem needs no integrability of the divergence. This is the reason for the parameter `GP`. The lemma `BoxIntegral.hasIntegral_GP_pderiv` is the form for one partial derivative. The lemma `BoxIntegral.norm_volume_sub_integral_face_upper_sub_lower_smul_le` is the local estimate behind the proof.

Chapter 8 states the divergence theorem for the Bochner integral. Its proof applies this theorem and then `MeasureTheory.IntegrableOn.hasBoxIntegral` to identify the two integrals when the divergence is integrable.

#### Common errors

1. Using `BoxIntegral.integral` with a measure in place of a box additive map. The volume argument is `BoxIntegral.BoxAdditiveMap.toSMul (MeasureTheory.Measure.toBoxAdditive μ)`, of type `ι →ᵇᵃ (E →L[ℝ] E)`.
2. Applying `MeasureTheory.IntegrableOn.hasBoxIntegral` to the integral of Riemann. The lemma needs `l.bRiemann = false`. For the integral of Riemann, use `BoxIntegral.integrable_of_bounded_and_ae_continuous`.
3. Reading the order of parameters backwards. `Riemann` is larger than `Henstock`, so the integral of Riemann is the stronger condition. `BoxIntegral.HasIntegral.mono` goes from the larger value to the smaller one.

### Exercises

1. (A) Show that a constant function on a box $I$ has the integral $c \operatorname{vol}(I)$ for every parameter `l`.
2. (A) Show that a function integrable in the sense of Riemann is integrable in the sense of McShane, with `BoxIntegral.Integrable.mono`.
3. (B) Show that the indicator of the rational points in $(0, 1]$ is integrable in the sense of McShane with integral $0$ and not integrable in the sense of Riemann.
4. (B) Show that $\big| \int_I f \big| \le \operatorname{vol}(I) \sup_{[I]} |f|$ for a continuous real $f$.
5. (B) Show that $\int_I (\partial_1 g) = \int_{F_1^+} g - \int_{F_1^-} g$ for a $C^1$ function $g$ on a box in $\mathbb{R}^2$.
6. (C) Prove the inequality of Henstock and Sacks in dimension one.
