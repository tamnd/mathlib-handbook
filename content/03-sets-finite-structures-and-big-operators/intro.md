# Sets, finite structures and big operators

This volume gives the collections of Mathlib and the sums and products over them. Its subject is the mathematics of finite things: finite sets, their cardinality, the counting of subsets, maps and permutations, the pigeonhole principle, inclusion and exclusion, double counting, and the sums and products over finite sets with their algebra and their standard identities. Each section gives the definitions, the theorems with complete proofs, worked examples with computed values, and the names of Mathlib that state the same results.

The volume needs the sets, the subtypes, the functions and the classes of Volume 1, and the natural numbers and induction of Volume 2. It uses the binomial coefficient `Nat.choose` of Volume 2, and it proves its counting meaning here. A reader who knows the language of sets from a first course in proofs can read the mathematics with no Lean. The `::: mathlib` boxes and the (L) exercises are for the reader who also writes Lean.

Chapter 1 gives the operations on sets: images, preimages, indexed unions and intersections, and partitions, and the API of the library around them: functions on sets, products of sets and the semiring of sets. Chapter 2 builds the multisets as lists up to order, with their sums and counts, their lattice operations, folds and powersets, and the symmetric powers and unordered pairs. Chapter 3 builds the finsets on the multisets with no repeated element, and it does the first counting: the card of a finset, the subsets of a given size and the binomial coefficients, and the rules of sum, product and pigeonhole, and it gives the library of finsets: the lattice operations, filters, images, folds, maxima, sorting and the binary operations on finsets. Chapter 4 gives the finite types, where the finset of all elements is `Finset.univ`, and it counts maps, injections and permutations. Chapter 5 gives the predicate `Set.Finite`, the cardinality `Nat.card` and the infinite sets, and it connects the three ways to say that a collection is finite. Chapter 6 gives the big operators `Finset.sum` and `Finset.prod`, their algebra (linearity, reindexing, Fubini, splitting and telescoping) and the standard identities: the sums of powers, the binomial theorem, inclusion and exclusion and double counting. It also gives the products of lists and multisets, the sums over $\mathrm{Fin}\ n$ and over intervals, the products in rings and fields, the averages, the sums `finsum` over a type with finite support and the antidiagonals. Chapter 7 gives the finitely supported functions, the base of the free modules and the polynomials, and the sums over their supports, and the dependent version `DFinsupp` with its order, and the orders on finitely supported functions with the monomial orders. Chapter 8 gives the countable and the uncountable types, the pairing of $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$ with $\mathbb{N}$, and the diagonal argument of Cantor.

Each chapter uses the chapters before it, but Chapter 8 needs only Chapters 1 to 5. Volume 4 on order and lattices, Volume 5 on groups and the later volume on combinatorics use the counting of this volume.
