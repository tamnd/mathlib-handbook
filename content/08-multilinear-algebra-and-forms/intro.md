# Multilinear algebra and forms

This volume continues Volume 7. Volume 7 studies one module at a time and the linear maps between modules. This volume studies functions of several vectors: bilinear and multilinear maps, the tensor product that turns them into linear maps, the algebras that collect all tensor powers of a module, and the bilinear and quadratic forms that measure lengths and angles. Each main theorem has a complete proof on paper, and each section computes with explicit modules, mostly $\mathbb{R}^n$, $\mathbb{Q}^n$, $\mathbb{Z}/n$ and $\mathbb{F}_p^n$.

Mathlib works over a commutative ring from the start, and most constructions of this volume make sense there. The tensor product $M \otimes_R N$, the exterior algebra and the Clifford algebra are defined for modules over any commutative ring. The volume states each definition at this level. It says where a theorem needs a field, where it needs $2$ to be invertible, and where it needs the real numbers. The condition on $2$ is a theme of the volume: a quadratic form and its symmetric bilinear form carry the same information only when $2$ is a unit, and many textbook proofs fail over $\mathbb{F}_2$.

Chapter 1 defines bilinear, multilinear and alternating maps, and shows the determinant of Volume 7 as the unique alternating $n$-form on $R^n$ up to a scalar. Chapter 2 constructs the tensor product of two modules by its universal property, studies its functoriality and its right exactness, computes bases and Kronecker products, and treats the base change of a module and the tensor product of algebras. Chapter 3 builds the tensor powers, the direct sums and graded algebras, the tensor algebra and the symmetric algebra. Chapter 4 studies the exterior algebra and the exterior powers, with the basis of $\bigwedge^k R^n$ of size $\binom{n}{k}$, the top exterior power and the determinant, and orientations. Chapter 5 studies bilinear and sesquilinear forms: symmetry, nondegeneracy, orthogonal complements, orthogonal bases, adjoints, isometries and perfect pairings. Chapter 6 studies quadratic forms and the polar form, the diagonalization of a quadratic form, and the law of inertia of Sylvester with the signature. Chapter 7 constructs the Clifford algebra of a quadratic form, computes it in small cases (the complex numbers, the quaternions, the dual numbers), and studies its grading, its involutions and the spin group. Chapter 8 studies the projective space of a vector space, its subspaces, its points over a finite field, and the action of the linear groups.

Read Volume 7 first. It gives the modules, the bases and the dimension, the matrices, the determinant by the formula of Leibniz, and the dual space, and this volume does not prove these results again. Volume 5 gives the sign of a permutation, and Volume 6 gives the rings, the ideals and the polynomial rings that Chapters 2, 3 and 7 use. Inner product spaces and the spectral theorem are in Volume 16, and the volume gives only pointers to them. Coalgebras, bialgebras and Hopf algebras have their home in this volume in the map of Mathlib, but this edition does not treat them. The volume has no Lean companion yet.
