# The class group

In a Dedekind domain every nonzero ideal is a product of primes, but the primes need not be principal. The class group measures the difference: it is the group of nonzero fractional ideals modulo the principal ones. It is trivial exactly when the ring is a PID, which for a Dedekind domain is the same as being a UFD. So the class group of the ring of integers of a number field measures the failure of unique factorization of elements.

Section 1 defines the class group of a domain as Mathlib does, gives the exact sequence that relates units, principal ideals and classes, proves that the class group of a Dedekind domain is trivial if and only if the ring is a PID, and shows how ring maps act on class groups. Section 2 computes the class group of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$: it has two elements, generated by the class of $(2, 1 + \sqrt{-5})$. The computation uses a lattice argument that bounds the norms of ideals in each class, an elementary form of the Minkowski bound.

The chapter needs the fractional ideals and the factorization of ideals of Chapter 4. The finiteness of the class group of a number field, the Minkowski bound and the class number are number theory and belong to Volume 12. The Picard group of invertible modules, which agrees with the class group for a Dedekind domain, is mentioned in Section 1. Section 3 describes the Picard group in the library: invertible modules, the group they form under the tensor product, and its isomorphism with the class group of a domain.

In Mathlib the class group is `ClassGroup R` for a domain `R`, with the class of a nonzero ideal given by `ClassGroup.mk0`.
