# Properties of schemes

This chapter studies properties of a scheme read from its rings, and constructions of new schemes. Section 1 defines reduced, integral, locally Noetherian, Artinian and quasi-affine schemes. It proves that a scheme is integral exactly when it is irreducible and reduced, constructs the function field at the generic point, and shows that the Noetherian property is local. It treats the properties that hold after each change of the base field, the closed points of a scheme of finite type over an algebraically closed field, and the relative normalization. Section 2 builds the projective spectrum of a graded ring from homogeneous primes and fractions of degree $0$. It proves that the basic opens are affine, computes the projective line, and constructs the separated morphism to the spectrum of the degree $0$ part. It ends with affine space over a scheme. Section 3 defines ideal sheaves, their support, the closed subscheme they cut out, and the scheme-theoretic image of a morphism. It then introduces sheaves of modules and the sheaf of a module on an affine scheme, the model of a quasi-coherent sheaf.

The chapter needs schemes, affine schemes and the structure sheaf of Chapter 3, with the sheaves and locally ringed spaces of Chapters 1 and 2. It uses localization, Noetherian and Artinian rings and the Nullstellensatz from Volume 9, integral extensions from Volume 10, and adjunctions and abelian categories from Volume 24.

Chapter 5 studies morphisms of schemes, and it uses the closed immersions, the scheme-theoretic image and the properness of the projective spectrum from this chapter. Chapter 6 puts Grothendieck topologies on schemes, where affine covers and basic opens return. Chapter 7 uses integral schemes and function fields for rational maps, cycles and group schemes. Chapter 8 places elliptic curves in the projective plane.
