# Rings, fields and ordered algebra

This volume teaches the theory of rings and fields and its form in Mathlib. A ring has two operations, an addition and a multiplication, tied by the distributive laws. The integers, the integers modulo $n$, the rational, real and complex numbers, the polynomials and the square matrices are rings. The volume studies these examples with one set of definitions, and it proves the first structure theorems: the isomorphism theorems for rings, the fact that $\mathbb{Z}/p$ is a field, the existence of the field of fractions, and the chain of implications from Euclidean domain to principal ideal domain to unique factorization domain. Each main theorem has a complete proof on paper, the Mathlib name of the result, and examples that compute.

Chapter 1 defines rings, ring homomorphisms and subrings. Chapter 2 defines ideals and quotient rings, proves the isomorphism theorems and the Chinese remainder theorem, and introduces prime and maximal ideals. Chapter 3 studies units, zero divisors, integral domains, fields and the field of fractions. Chapter 4 defines the characteristic of a ring, with the class `CharP`, and studies $\mathbb{Z}/n$ as a ring and $\mathbb{Z}/p$ as a field. Chapter 5 gives a first view of the polynomial ring $R[X]$: degree, evaluation, division with remainder and roots. Chapter 6 studies divisibility in an integral domain: Euclidean domains, principal ideal domains and unique factorization domains, with the Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ as the main example. Chapter 7 studies ordered rings and ordered fields and the archimedean property, and then the library around them: floor and ceiling, archimedean classes, absolute values and linearly ordered groups with zero. Chapter 8 defines algebras over a commutative ring, gives a short account of star rings, and ends with continued fractions, quadratic algebras, quaternion algebras and trivial square-zero extensions. It then gives the library of algebras: algebra isomorphisms, towers and restriction of scalars, subalgebra constructions, non-unital algebras with their unitization, and the spectrum of an element. Chapter 9 builds on the star rings of Chapter 8: it covers star homomorphisms and star subalgebras, unitary elements, star projections and star orthogonality with the CHSH inequality, and the star structures on modules, linear maps, products, free algebras, quotients and tensor products.

The volume needs Volume 5 on groups: the reader must know groups, homomorphisms, normal subgroups, quotient groups and the first isomorphism theorem. The integers, their divisibility, and $\mathbb{Z}/n$ as a type are in Volume 2, and the order theory of Chapter 7 uses Volume 4. Read the chapters in order. The first sections of Chapters 7 and 8 need only Chapters 1 to 3, so a reader in a hurry can go to them after Chapter 3.

Later volumes build on this one. Volume 7 on linear algebra uses rings as scalars and algebras as the home of matrices. Volume 9 on commutative algebra takes up ideals, polynomials and factorization in full generality, and Volume 11 on fields and Galois theory starts from the fields, the characteristic and the polynomial rings of Chapters 3 to 5. Volume 15 on real analysis uses the ordered fields and the archimedean property of Chapter 7.

The Mathlib form of rings is a hierarchy of classes. A type `R` with `[CommRing R]` is a commutative ring, and every lemma about commutative rings applies to it by instance search. Fields, integral domains, ordered rings and algebras are further classes, often small mixins such as `IsDomain` or `IsStrictOrderedRing` that add one property to the structure already present. Each chapter explains the classes it uses and why Mathlib chose that form. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the examples are on paper, and the Mathlib boxes give the names and the signatures of the library.
