# Morphisms of schemes

This chapter studies the properties of a morphism of schemes $f : X \to Y$. Section 1 sets up the frame: a property of morphisms is a `MorphismProperty Scheme`, and most properties are local on the target, on the source, or come from a property of ring homomorphisms on affine opens. It then treats isomorphisms, open immersions, affine, quasi-compact and quasi-separated morphisms, preimmersions, immersions and closed immersions, and scheme-theoretically dominant morphisms. Section 2 defines morphisms locally of finite type and of finite presentation, finite, integral and quasi-finite morphisms, and flat morphisms. It treats faithfully flat descent and the rank of a finite flat morphism. Section 3 defines separated, universally closed and proper morphisms, proves that the affine line with a doubled origin is not separated, states the valuative criteria and checks them on the projective line, and gives the form of Zariski's main theorem in Mathlib. Section 4 defines formally unramified, smooth, étale and weakly étale morphisms and computes them on the squaring map of the affine line.

The chapter needs the schemes, open covers, fiber products and fibers of Chapter 3, and the reduced schemes, affine space and ideal sheaves of Chapter 4. On the algebra side it uses localization and Noetherian rings from Volume 9, and integral extensions, local rings, valuation rings and flat, étale and smooth algebras from Volume 10. Pullbacks and the diagonal come from the limits of Volume 24.

Chapter 6 uses the classes of this chapter to build Grothendieck topologies: open immersions give the Zariski topology, étale morphisms the étale site, and flat quasi-compact surjections the fpqc topology. Chapter 7 uses dominant and separated morphisms for rational maps, and smooth and proper morphisms for group schemes and abelian varieties.
