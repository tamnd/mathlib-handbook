# The derived category

This chapter constructs the derived category of an abelian category and the Ext groups in it. Section 1 defines the derived category $D(A)$ as the localization of the category of cochain complexes at the quasi-isomorphisms. It shows that $D(A)$ is also a localization of the homotopy category. It proves that a morphism of the homotopy category is a quasi-isomorphism exactly when its cone is acyclic, so that $D(A)$ is a Verdier quotient and is triangulated. A short exact sequence of complexes gives a distinguished triangle, the cohomology functors detect isomorphisms, and K-injective complexes compute morphisms. Section 2 defines $\operatorname{Ext}^n(X, Y)$ as the group of morphisms $X \to Y[n]$ in $D(A)$. It proves that $\operatorname{Ext}^0$ is $\operatorname{Hom}$, that projective resolutions compute the groups, that projective and injective objects make them vanish, and the two long exact sequences. For modules it proves Baer's criterion in Ext form and a bound on the injective dimension.

The chapter needs the localization of categories and the calculus of fractions of Volume 25, Chapter 4, and the triangulated categories and the Verdier quotient of Volume 25, Chapter 5. It uses the homology of complexes of Chapter 2, the mapping cone and the homotopy category of Chapter 3, and the resolutions of Chapter 4. The examples use cyclic groups and polynomial rings.

The derived category is the setting of derived functors in later volumes. The cohomology of sheaves in Volume 28 and the cohomology of spaces in Volume 27 are morphisms in derived categories, and the Ext groups of group representations in Volume 29 are the Ext groups of this chapter. Chapter 6 builds spectral sequences from spectral objects, which are families of long exact sequences of the kind that the triangles of this chapter give.
