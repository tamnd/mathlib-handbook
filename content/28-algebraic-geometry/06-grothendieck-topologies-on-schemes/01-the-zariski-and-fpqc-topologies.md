## The Zariski and fpqc topologies

### Overview

A Grothendieck topology on the category of schemes says which families of morphisms $\{f_i : X_i \to X\}$ count as covers of $X$. The Zariski topology takes families of open immersions whose images cover $X$. The fppf and fpqc topologies take flat families, so that a single morphism such as $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[i] \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$ is a cover. A topology with more covers has fewer sheaves, and the first test is whether each functor $\operatorname{Hom}(-, Z)$ is a sheaf.

Mathlib builds all these topologies from one recipe. A property $P$ of morphisms gives the precoverage of jointly surjective families of morphisms with property $P$. A quasi-compactness condition is added for fpqc. This section gives the recipe, proves that the covers are stable under base change, compares the topologies, proves that the Zariski and fpqc topologies are subcanonical, and treats the small affine Zariski site and the gluing of a functor from local pieces.

### Definitions

**Definition (Precoverage of a property).** Let $P$ be a property of morphisms of schemes. A family $\{f_i : X_i \to X\}_{i \in I}$ is a *$P$-cover* when each $f_i$ has property $P$ and the family is *jointly surjective*: for each $x \in X$ some $i$ and some $x' \in X_i$ satisfy $f_i(x') = x$.

When $P$ is stable under base change and composition and holds for isomorphisms, the $P$-covers form a pretopology, and the topology generated by it is the *$P$-topology*. A sieve $S$ on $X$ covers in this topology when it contains all members of some $P$-cover of $X$.

**Definition (Big Zariski topology).** The *Zariski topology* on the category of schemes is the $P$-topology for $P$ the class of open immersions. A family of open immersions is a Zariski cover when the open images $f_i(X_i)$ cover $X$.

**Definition (Small and over sites).** Let $Q$ be a property of morphisms and $S$ a scheme. The category of $S$-schemes $X \to S$ whose structure morphism has $Q$ carries the topology induced by the $P$-topology: a family over $S$ covers when its underlying family of schemes is a $P$-cover. With $Q = P$ this is the *small $P$-site* of $S$.

**Definition (Quasi-compact cover).** A family $\{f_i : X_i \to X\}$ is *quasi-compact* when for each affine open $U \subseteq X$ there are finitely many indices $i_1, \dots, i_n$ and quasi-compact opens $V_k \subseteq X_{i_k}$ with $f_{i_1}(V_1) \cup \dots \cup f_{i_n}(V_n) = U$.

**Definition (fppf and fpqc).** The *fppf topology* is the $P$-topology for $P$ = flat and locally of finite presentation. The *fpqc topology* has as covers the jointly surjective quasi-compact families of flat morphisms.

The quasi-compactness is a condition on the whole family, not on each morphism.

**Definition (Affine Zariski site).** Let $X$ be a scheme. The *affine Zariski site* of $X$ has as objects the affine opens of $X$, with $U \le V$ when $U = D(f) \subseteq V$ for some $f \in \Gamma(X, V)$. A family $\{D(f_i) \le U\}$ covers when the $D(f_i)$ cover $U$.

This order is not the inclusion of opens: an affine open of an affine open $V$ need not be a basic open of $V$.

### Theorems and proofs

**Lemma (Covers and base change).** Let $\{f_i : X_i \to X\}$ be jointly surjective and $g : Y \to X$ a morphism. Then the family $\{X_i \times_X Y \to Y\}$ is jointly surjective.

*Proof.* Let $y \in Y$ and $x = g(y)$. Choose $i$ and $x' \in X_i$ with $f_i(x') = x$. The residue fields give field extensions $\kappa(x) \to \kappa(x')$ and $\kappa(x) \to \kappa(y)$. The ring $A = \kappa(x') \otimes_{\kappa(x)} \kappa(y)$ is nonzero, because a tensor product of two nonzero vector spaces over a field is nonzero. So $A$ has a prime ideal and $\operatorname{Spec} A$ has a point $q$. The morphisms $\operatorname{Spec} A \to \operatorname{Spec} \kappa(x') \to X_i$ and $\operatorname{Spec} A \to \operatorname{Spec} \kappa(y) \to Y$ agree over $X$, so they give a morphism $\operatorname{Spec} A \to X_i \times_X Y$. The image $z$ of $q$ maps to $y$ in $Y$. $\square$

So $P$-covers are stable under base change when $P$ is, which is the pullback axiom of a pretopology. The other axioms hold because isomorphisms are surjective and composites of jointly surjective families are jointly surjective.

**Theorem (Comparison of topologies).** The Zariski topology is contained in the fppf topology, and the fppf topology is contained in the fpqc topology.

*Proof.* Step 1. An open immersion is flat, because its local rings are local rings of the target. It is locally of finite presentation, because locally it is the inclusion $\operatorname{Spec} A_f \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ and $A_f = A[t]/(ft - 1)$. So each Zariski cover is an fppf cover.

Step 2. A flat morphism locally of finite presentation is an open map. Let $\{f_i\}$ be an fppf cover of $X$ and $U \subseteq X$ an affine open. For each $x \in U$ choose $i$ and $x' \in X_i$ over $x$, and an affine open $V_x \ni x'$ inside $f_i^{-1}(U)$. The set $f_i(V_x)$ is open and contains $x$. These sets cover $U$, and $U$ is quasi-compact, so finitely many of them cover $U$. Affine opens are quasi-compact, so the family is quasi-compact. Each $f_i$ is flat, so the family is an fpqc cover.

Step 3. The inclusions of precoverages pass to the generated topologies. $\square$

**Theorem (Zariski topology is subcanonical).** For each scheme $Z$ the functor $\operatorname{Hom}(-, Z)$ is a sheaf for the Zariski topology.

*Proof.* Let $\{f_i : X_i \to X\}$ be a Zariski cover and $g_i : X_i \to Z$ morphisms that agree on each $X_i \times_X X_j$. Each $f_i$ is an isomorphism onto the open $U_i = f_i(X_i)$, and $X_i \times_X X_j$ is isomorphic to $U_i \cap U_j$. So the $g_i$ give morphisms $h_i : U_i \to Z$ that agree on $U_i \cap U_j$. The continuous maps glue to $h : X \to Z$ because the $U_i$ cover $X$. The maps of structure sheaves glue because $\mathcal{O}_X$ is a sheaf, and being a map of locally ringed spaces is a local condition. So a unique $h$ restricts to $h_i$ on $U_i$. Uniqueness gives the separation part of the sheaf condition. $\square$

**Theorem (Affine covers in the affine Zariski site).** Let $U = \operatorname{Spec} A$ be an affine open of $X$ and $s \subseteq A$. The opens $D(f)$, $f \in s$, cover $U$ if and only if the ideal generated by $s$ is $A$.

*Proof.* The $D(f)$ cover $U$ if and only if no prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of $A$ contains all of $s$. A proper ideal lies in a maximal ideal, which is prime. So the condition is that the ideal generated by $s$ is not proper. $\square$

**Lemma (Faithfully flat equalizer).** Let $\varphi : R \to S$ be a faithfully flat ring map. Then the sequence $R \to S \rightrightarrows S \otimes_R S$, with the maps $s \mapsto s \otimes 1$ and $s \mapsto 1 \otimes s$, is an equalizer.

*Proof.* Step 1. Write $d(s) = s \otimes 1 - 1 \otimes s$. The claim is that $0 \to R \to S \xrightarrow{d} S \otimes_R S$ is exact. Because $S$ is faithfully flat over $R$, it suffices to prove exactness after applying $S \otimes_R -$.

Step 2. After base change the sequence is $0 \to S \to S \otimes_R S \to S \otimes_R S \otimes_R S$, with first map $b \mapsto b \otimes 1$ and second map $b \otimes s \mapsto b \otimes s \otimes 1 - b \otimes 1 \otimes s$. Define $h_0 : S \otimes_R S \to S$ by $b \otimes s \mapsto bs$ and $h_1 : S \otimes_R S \otimes_R S \to S \otimes_R S$ by $b \otimes s \otimes t \mapsto bs \otimes t$.

Step 3. The composite $S \to S \otimes_R S \to S$ is the identity, so the first map is injective. If $\xi = \sum b_k \otimes s_k$ lies in the kernel of the second map, then applying $h_1$ gives $\sum b_k s_k \otimes 1 - \sum b_k \otimes s_k = 0$. So $\xi = h_0(\xi) \otimes 1$, which lies in the image of the first map. $\square$

**Theorem (Sheaves for quasi-compact topologies).** Let $P$ be a property that is local on the source, stable under composition and base change, and holds for isomorphisms. A presheaf of sets $F$ on schemes is a sheaf for the quasi-compact $P$-topology if and only if $F$ is a Zariski sheaf and $F$ satisfies the sheaf condition for each surjective morphism $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ with property $P$.

*Proof.* The forward direction holds because Zariski covers and single surjective $P$-morphisms are covers of the $P$-topology. For the converse let $\{f_i : X_i \to T\}$ be a cover.

Step 1. Cover $T$ by affine opens $T_j$. The base change of the cover to each $T_j$ is again a cover. Since $F$ is a Zariski sheaf, the sheaf condition for the cover of $T$ follows from the sheaf condition and the separation of the base changes over the $T_j$ and over the affine opens of the $T_j \cap T_k$. So assume $T = \operatorname{Spec} A$.

Step 2. By quasi-compactness there are finitely many affine opens $V_k \subseteq X_{i_k}$ whose images cover $T$. The family $\{V_k \to T\}$ refines the given cover. A refinement argument, which again uses the Zariski sheaf property on the $X_i$, reduces the sheaf condition to the finite affine family $\{V_k \to T\}$.

Step 3. The disjoint union $V = \coprod_k V_k$ is affine, equal to $\operatorname{Spec} B$ with $B = \prod_k \Gamma(V_k)$. A Zariski sheaf sends finite disjoint unions to products, so the sheaf condition for $\{V_k \to T\}$ is the sheaf condition for the single morphism $V \to T$. This morphism is surjective and has property $P$ because $P$ is local on the source. So the hypothesis applies. $\square$

**Corollary (fpqc topology is subcanonical).** For each scheme $Z$ the functor $\operatorname{Hom}(-, Z)$ is an fpqc sheaf.

*Proof.* By the Zariski theorem and the criterion, it suffices to treat a faithfully flat $\operatorname{Spec} B \to \operatorname{Spec} A$. For affine $Z = \operatorname{Spec} C$, morphisms $\operatorname{Spec} D \to Z$ are ring maps $C \to D$, and the lemma says that a ring map $C \to B$ whose two images in $B \otimes_A B$ agree factors uniquely through $A$. The case of general $Z$ needs a further argument on the underlying spaces. Mathlib gives a general proof: it shows that a faithfully flat morphism to an affine scheme is a regular epimorphism, so it is an effective epimorphism, which is the sheaf condition for a single morphism. $\square$

**Theorem (Local representability).** Let $F$ be a Zariski sheaf of sets on schemes. Assume that there are schemes $X_i$ and morphisms $\operatorname{Hom}(-, X_i) \to F$ that are relatively representable open immersions and jointly surjective as maps of Zariski sheaves. Then $F$ is representable.

*Proof.* Step 1. For each $i, j$ the fiber product $\operatorname{Hom}(-, X_i) \times_F \operatorname{Hom}(-, X_j)$ is represented by an open subscheme $X_{ij} \subseteq X_i$, by the assumption on open immersions. The canonical isomorphisms $X_{ij} \cong X_{ji}$ satisfy the cocycle condition, because they come from the fiber products over $F$.

Step 2. Glue the $X_i$ along the $X_{ij}$ to a scheme $X$. The maps $\operatorname{Hom}(-, X_i) \to F$ agree on overlaps, and $F$ is a Zariski sheaf, so they glue to a map $\operatorname{Hom}(-, X) \to F$.

Step 3. This map is injective and locally surjective as a map of Zariski sheaves, by the construction of $X$ and the joint surjectivity. A locally bijective map of sheaves is an isomorphism. $\square$

### Examples

**Example (Two basic opens of the integers).** The opens $D(2)$ and $D(3)$ cover $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$, because $3 - 2 = 1$ lies in the ideal $(2, 3)$. The opens $D(2)$ and $D(4)$ do not cover: $D(4) = D(2)$ and the prime $(2)$ lies in neither.

**Example (The projective line).** The two standard affine lines $\operatorname{Spec} k[t]$ and $\operatorname{Spec} k[t^{-1}]$ form a Zariski cover of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$. Both lines are objects of the affine Zariski site of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$. Their intersection $\operatorname{Spec} k[t, t^{-1}]$ is the basic open $D(t)$ of the first line and $D(t^{-1})$ of the second, so it lies below both in the order of that site. The site has no largest object, because $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ is not affine.

**Example (A finite flat cover).** The ring $\mathbb{Z}[i] = \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^2 + 1)$ is free of rank $2$ over $\mathbb{Z}$, so $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}[i] \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$ is flat and of finite presentation. It is surjective because $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}[i]$ is injective and integral, so each prime lies under a prime. So it is an fppf cover and an fpqc cover. It is not a Zariski cover. An open immersion is injective on points, but $5 = (2+i)(2-i)$ and the fiber over $(5)$ has the two points $(2+i)$ and $(2-i)$.

**Counterexample (Flat and jointly surjective but not fpqc).** Let $p$ run through the primes and take the family $\{\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}_{(p)} \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}\}$. Each map is flat, since localization is flat. The image of $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$ is $\{(0), (p)\}$, so the family is jointly surjective. Take $U = \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$. Finitely many images contain only finitely many primes $(p)$, so they never equal $U$. The family is not an fpqc cover.

**Counterexample (A constant presheaf).** Let $T$ be a set with two elements and $F$ the presheaf with $F(X) = T$ for all $X$ and identity restriction maps. The empty scheme is covered by the empty family. The sheaf condition for this family asks $F(\emptyset)$ to have one element, so $F$ is not a Zariski sheaf.

Common errors of a learner: the fpqc condition asks for finitely many quasi-compact opens whose images equal each affine open, and flatness with joint surjectivity is not enough. The affine Zariski site uses basic opens, so its order is not the inclusion of affine opens. `Scheme.zariskiTopology` is a topology on the big category of schemes, not the topology of the space of a scheme.

### Exercises

1. (A) Decide whether $D(6)$ and $D(35)$ cover $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}$. *Hint:* Find integers $a, b$ with $6a + 35b = 1$.
2. (A) Show that $\operatorname{Spec} \overline{\mathbb{Q}} \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Q}$ is an fpqc cover but not an fppf cover. *Hint:* A finitely generated algebraic field extension is finite.
3. (B) Show that a Zariski sheaf $F$ satisfies $F(X \sqcup Y) \cong F(X) \times F(Y)$. *Hint:* Use the cover by the two open pieces, whose fiber product is empty.
4. (B) Let $k$ be a field and $L/k$ a finite extension. Use the equalizer lemma to compute the equalizer of the two maps $L \to L \otimes_k L$. *Hint:* Faithful flatness is automatic over a field.
5. (C) Prove the faithfully flat equalizer lemma for $R$-modules: if $M$ is an $R$-module, then $M \to M \otimes_R S \rightrightarrows M \otimes_R S \otimes_R S$ is an equalizer. *Hint:* Tensor the proof with $M$ and keep the same homotopy.
6. (C) Show that the functor $X \mapsto \Gamma(X, \mathcal{O}_X)$ is an fpqc sheaf. *Hint:* It is $\operatorname{Hom}(-, \mathbb{A}^1_{\mathbb{Z}})$.

### Notes and history

Grothendieck developed faithfully flat descent and Grothendieck topologies. SGA 1 treats descent along faithfully flat morphisms, and SGA 4 treats sites and topoi. The criterion for fpqc sheaves and the local representability theorem follow the Stacks project, which the sources cite as tags 022H and 01JJ.
