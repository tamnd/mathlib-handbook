# Trigonometric functions and pi

The sine and the cosine are the second family of special functions of the volume, after the exponential and the logarithm. This chapter defines them from the exponential series, as Mathlib does, and proves all their properties from that definition. No geometry is assumed: the angle, the circle and the number $\pi$ come out of the analysis.

Section 1 defines $\sin$ and $\cos$ as the real parts of the complex functions built from $\exp$, derives the Pythagorean identity and the addition formulas, and computes the derivatives. It then defines $\pi$ as twice the unique zero of the cosine in $[1, 2]$, with the estimates $\cos 1 > 0$ and $\cos 2 < 0$ from the series, and proves the periodicity of $\sin$ and $\cos$, the description of their zeros and the limit $\sin x / x \to 1$. Section 2 defines the tangent and the inverse functions $\arcsin$, $\arccos$ and $\arctan$ with their derivatives. It computes $3.14 < \pi < 3.15$ from nested square roots, as Mathlib proves `Real.pi_gt_d2`, proves the series of Leibniz for $\pi/4$ and the inequality of Jordan. Section 3 proves that $e$ and $\pi$ are irrational, the second by the method of Niven, and describes the topology of the irrational numbers.

The chapter needs the series of Chapter 1, the intermediate value theorem of Chapter 2, the derivative rules, the mean value theorem and the inverse function rule of Chapter 3, and the exponential series of Chapter 5. It uses no integral: each proof that is usually written with an integral is written here with the mean value theorem. Chapter 7 uses $\pi$ in Stirling's formula. The complex form of the trigonometric functions and their power series belong to Volume 19, and the integral proofs to Volume 16.

In Mathlib the definitions of `Real.sin`, `Real.cos` and `Real.tan` and their algebraic identities sit in `Mathlib.Analysis.Complex.Trigonometric`. The facts that need $\pi$, derivatives or inequalities sit in the files `Mathlib.Analysis.SpecialFunctions.Trigonometric.*` and `Mathlib.Analysis.Real.Pi.*`, which are the subject of this chapter.
