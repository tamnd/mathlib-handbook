# Topological rings, fields and modules

A topological ring is a ring with a topology in which addition, negation and multiplication are continuous. A topological field asks in addition that the inversion is continuous at each nonzero point. A topological module over a topological ring asks that the scalar multiplication is continuous. Examples are $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$, the $p$-adic numbers and normed spaces.

Section 1 defines topological rings and division rings, with the classes `IsTopologicalRing`, `ContinuousInv₀` and `IsTopologicalDivisionRing`. It proves that closures of subrings and ideals are subrings and ideals, that polynomials and the determinant are continuous, and that a compact Hausdorff division ring is finite. Section 2 defines topological modules and the continuous linear maps `M →L[R] N` and gives a linear functional that is not continuous. Section 3 proves the main theorem of the chapter: over a complete nontrivially normed field, a finite-dimensional vector space has only one Hausdorff topology that makes it a topological vector space, and each linear map on it is continuous.

The chapter needs Chapter 1 of this volume, rings from Volume 6, linear algebra from Volume 7 and the topology of Volume 13. Chapter 3 completes topological rings and fields. Chapter 8 studies the topologies that absolute values and valuations define on a field.
