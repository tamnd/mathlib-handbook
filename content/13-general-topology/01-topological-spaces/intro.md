# Topological spaces

A topology on a set says which subsets are open. From the open sets come the closed sets, the closure and the interior of a set, the neighbourhoods of a point and the continuous maps. This chapter gives these notions and the first examples.

Section 1 defines a topological space and its open and closed sets, the closure, the interior and the frontier, and dense sets. It studies the discrete, the indiscrete and the cofinite topologies, and the order on the topologies of a set, which Mathlib reverses. Section 2 defines neighbourhoods, a basis of a topology, the topology generated by a family of sets, and the countability axioms. It builds the topology of $\mathbb{R}$ from the open intervals. Section 3 defines continuous maps and homeomorphisms, and builds the induced, coinduced, subspace, product and quotient topologies by their universal properties. Section 4 describes the library of derived sets, discrete subsets and the codiscrete filter, locally finite families and functions with locally finite support. Section 5 glues spaces along open subsets and describes the topology generated by a family of test spaces, with the convenient categories of such spaces. Section 6 describes the library of inducing maps, embeddings, open and closed maps, quotient maps, strict maps and local homeomorphisms, and the properties of maps that are local on the target. Section 7 describes partial homeomorphisms, the local charts of manifolds, with their restriction, composition and gluing. Section 8 describes the criteria for a continuous bijection to be a homeomorphism and the catalogue of standard homeomorphisms between products, subtypes and quotients. Section 9 describes the lattices of open, closed, clopen and irreducible closed sets as types of their own.

The chapter needs the set operations of Volume 1 and the complete lattices of Volume 4. Chapter 2 rewrites the neighbourhoods of Section 2 as a filter, and the rest of the volume rests on the definitions of this chapter.
