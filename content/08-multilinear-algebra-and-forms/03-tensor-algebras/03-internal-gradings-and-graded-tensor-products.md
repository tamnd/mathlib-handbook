## Internal gradings, graded maps and graded tensor products

### Overview

Section 1 of this chapter defines a graded algebra in two forms. The external form is a family of types $A_i$ with products $A_i \times A_j \to A_{i+j}$. The internal form is a family of submodules $A_i$ of one algebra $A$ with $A_i A_j \subseteq A_{i+j}$. This section collects the library that makes the internal form usable. It computes the components of a product in $\bigoplus_i A_i$, it defines graded actions and maps that preserve a grading, and it applies direct sum decompositions to idempotents, to characters and to the trace of a linear map.

The second half of the section treats the graded tensor product $A \mathbin{\hat\otimes} B$ of two graded algebras. As a module it is $A \otimes_R B$. Its product has a sign: $(a \otimes b)(a' \otimes b') = (-1)^{|b| |a'|} \, a a' \otimes b b'$ for homogeneous $b$ and $a'$. This is the product that makes the exterior algebra of $M \oplus N$ the tensor product of the exterior algebras, and the Clifford algebra of an orthogonal sum the tensor product of the Clifford algebras. Chapters 4 and 7 use it in this way.

### The library around it

#### Internal gradings as graded rings

Let $R$ be a semiring and let $A : \iota \to \sigma$ be a family of additive submonoids (or submodules, or subgroups) of $R$ with `SetLike.GradedMonoid A`. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.DirectSum.Internal` turns this internal grading into the external data of Section 1. The instances `SetLike.gnonUnitalNonAssocSemiring`, `SetLike.gsemiring`, `SetLike.gcommSemiring`, `SetLike.gring` and `SetLike.gcommRing` give the family `fun i ↦ A i` a structure of `DirectSum.GSemiring` and its variants, and `Submodule.galgebra` gives `DirectSum.GAlgebra` for submodules of an algebra. So `⨁ i, A i` is a semiring, and an algebra when the $A_i$ are submodules.

The sum map $\bigoplus_i A_i \to R$, $(a_i) \mapsto \sum_i a_i$, is then multiplicative. The library has it as a ring map `DirectSum.coeRingHom A` and as an algebra map `DirectSum.coeAlgHom A`. These are the bundled forms of `DirectSum.coeAddMonoidHom` and `DirectSum.coeLinearMap`. The rules `DirectSum.coeRingHom_of` and `DirectSum.coeAlgHom_of` say that `DirectSum.of _ i x` goes to the element `↑x` of $R$. The image of `DirectSum.coeAlgHom A` is the subalgebra generated by the $A_i$: `Submodule.iSup_eq_toSubmodule_range` states $\bigvee_i A_i = \operatorname{range}$. The map is bijective exactly when the grading is a direct sum decomposition; this is the predicate `DirectSum.IsInternal`, and `GradedRing` packages it with a `DirectSum.Decomposition`.

The degree $0$ part is a subring. The library calls it `SetLike.GradeZero.subsemiring A`, `SetLike.GradeZero.subring A` and `SetLike.GradeZero.subalgebra A`, and gives the type `A 0` the instances `SetLike.GradeZero.instSemiring`, `SetLike.GradeZero.instRing`, `SetLike.GradeZero.instCommSemiring` and `SetLike.GradeZero.instAlgebra`. The lemmas `SetLike.natCast_mem_graded`, `SetLike.intCast_mem_graded` and `SetLike.algebraMap_mem_graded` place the numerals and the scalars in degree $0$. A standard source of examples is `Submodule.nat_power_gradedMonoid`: for a submodule $p$ of an algebra, the powers $p^0, p^1, p^2, \dots$ form an $\mathbb{N}$-graded monoid, since $p^i p^j = p^{i+j}$.

#### Components of a product

An element $r$ of $\bigoplus_i A_i$ has components $r_i \in A_i$. A product has the components of a convolution.

**Theorem.** Let $\iota$ be an additive monoid with a finite antidiagonal, for example $\mathbb{N}$. For $r, r' \in \bigoplus_i A_i$ and $n \in \iota$,
$$(r r')_n = \sum_{i + j = n} r_i \, r'_j .$$

*Proof.* Both sides are biadditive in $(r, r')$, so it suffices to take $r = \operatorname{of}_k(x)$ and $r' = \operatorname{of}_l(y)$. The product is $\operatorname{of}_{k+l}(x y)$ by the definition of the graded product. Its component $n$ is $x y$ when $k + l = n$ and $0$ otherwise. On the right side, only the pair $(i, j) = (k, l)$ can give a nonzero term, and it lies in the antidiagonal of $n$ exactly when $k + l = n$. $\square$

This is `DirectSum.coe_mul_apply_eq_sum_antidiagonal`. The general form `DirectSum.coe_mul_apply` sums over the pairs of the supports with $i + j = n$, and needs no finite antidiagonal. For a canonically ordered index such as $\mathbb{N}$, the product with a homogeneous element has a closed form: `DirectSum.coe_of_mul_apply` states
$$(\operatorname{of}_i(x) \, r')_n = \begin{cases} x \, r'_{n - i} & i \le n, \\ 0 & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases}$$
and `DirectSum.coe_mul_of_apply` is the mirror rule. Over a cancellative index such as $\mathbb{Z}$ the forms `DirectSum.coe_of_mul_apply_add` and `DirectSum.coe_mul_of_apply_add` state $(\operatorname{of}_i(x) \, r')_{i+j} = x \, r'_j$. The proof of `DirectSum.coe_of_mul_apply` is the theorem above with one factor homogeneous: only the term $(i, n - i)$ survives.

For a linearly ordered index, the lemma `mul_apply_eq_zero` says that orders add. If $r_k = 0$ for $k < m$ and $r'_k = 0$ for $k < n$, then $(r r')_k = 0$ for $k < m + n$. The lemmas `listProd_apply_eq_zero`, `multisetProd_apply_eq_zero` and `finsetProd_apply_eq_zero` extend it to products of many factors. Their primed versions take a different hypothesis on each factor.

#### Graded actions

A graded module over a graded ring is a family $M_j$ with actions $A_i \times M_j \to M_{i + j}$. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.GradedMulAction` has the classes for it. In the external form, `GradedMonoid.GSMul A M` is the operation `A i → M j → M (i +ᵥ j)`, and `GradedMonoid.GMulAction A M` adds the laws `GradedMonoid.GMulAction.one_smul` and `GradedMonoid.GMulAction.mul_smul`. As for `GradedMonoid.GMonoid`, the laws are stated in the sigma types `GradedMonoid A` and `GradedMonoid M`, where `GradedMonoid.mk_smul_mk` computes the action. The index of $M$ is a type with an additive action `VAdd ιA ιM`, so a $\mathbb{Z}$-graded module over an $\mathbb{N}$-graded ring is allowed. In the internal form, `SetLike.GradedSMul A B` says that $a \in A_i$ and $b \in B_j$ give $a \cdot b \in B_{i +ᵥ j}$ (`SetLike.GradedSMul.smul_mem`). The instances `GradedMonoid.GMul.toGSMul` and `SetLike.GradedMul.toGradedSMul` let a graded ring act on itself. The module structure on a direct sum of graded pieces, `DirectSum.Gmodule.module`, is built from these classes.

#### Maps that preserve a grading

For gradings $\mathcal{A}$ of $A$ and $\mathcal{B}$ of $B$ by the same index, the class `GradedFunLike F 𝒜 ℬ` says that each `f : F` maps $\mathcal{A}_i$ into $\mathcal{B}_i$ (`Graded.map_mem`). The class `GradedEquivLike E 𝒜 ℬ` asks more: $x \in \mathcal{A}_i$ if and only if $e(x) \in \mathcal{B}_i$ (`GradedEquivLike.map_mem_iff`). The classes follow the pattern of `FunLike` and `EquivLike` of Volume 1, so they apply to any bundled map type. A graded map restricts to each grade as `Graded.subtypeMap f i : 𝒜 i → ℬ i`, and a graded equivalence as `Graded.equiv e i : 𝒜 i ≃ ℬ i`.

#### Three applications of decompositions

*Idempotents.* Let $R = \bigoplus_i V_i$ be a decomposition of a semiring into ideals, with `DirectSum.Decomposition V`. The element `DirectSum.idempotent V i` is $e_i$, the component of $1$ in $V_i$.

**Theorem.** The $e_i$ are idempotent, $e_i e_j = 0$ for $i \ne j$, and $\sum_i e_i = 1$ when the index is finite. Each $x \in R$ has the components $x_i = x e_i$.

*Proof.* Since $V_i$ is an ideal, $x e_i \in V_i$. The sum $\sum_i x e_i = x \cdot 1 = x$ is a decomposition of $x$, and the decomposition is unique, so $x_i = x e_i$. Apply this to $x = e_j$. Since $e_j \in V_j$, its component in $V_i$ is $e_j$ for $i = j$ and $0$ otherwise. So $e_j e_j = e_j$ and $e_j e_i = 0$ for $i \ne j$. The sum of the components of $1$ is $1$. $\square$

The library states the parts as `DirectSum.decompose_eq_mul_idempotent`, `DirectSum.isIdempotentElem_idempotent` and `DirectSum.completeOrthogonalIdempotents_idempotent`, the last with the predicate `CompleteOrthogonalIdempotents`.

*Characters.* For additive characters $\psi_i : G_i \to R$ into a commutative monoid, `AddChar.directSum ψ` is the character $a \mapsto \prod_i \psi_i(a_i)$ of $\bigoplus_i G_i$. The rule `AddChar.directSum_apply` writes it with `DirectSum.toAddMonoid`, and `AddChar.directSum_injective` says that the family $\psi$ is determined by the result.

*Traces.* Let $M = \bigoplus_i N_i$ be an internal direct sum of finite free $R$-modules over a commutative ring, and let $f$ be an endomorphism of $M$.

**Theorem.** If $f(N_i) \subseteq N_i$ for each $i$, then $\operatorname{tr} f = \sum_i \operatorname{tr}(f|_{N_i})$. If instead $f(N_i) \subseteq N_{\sigma(i)}$ for a map $\sigma$ with no fixed point, then $\operatorname{tr} f = 0$.

*Proof.* Join bases of the $N_i$ to a basis of $M$. In the first case the matrix of $f$ is block diagonal, with the matrix of $f|_{N_i}$ as block $i$, and the trace of a block diagonal matrix is the sum of the traces of the blocks. In the second case each basis vector of $N_i$ goes into $N_{\sigma(i)}$ with $\sigma(i) \ne i$, so each diagonal entry of the matrix is $0$. $\square$

The two parts are `LinearMap.trace_eq_sum_trace_restrict` and `LinearMap.trace_eq_zero_of_mapsTo_ne`; the second asks also for `IsNoetherian R M`. The joined basis is `DirectSum.IsInternal.collectedBasis`, and the block form is `LinearMap.toMatrix_directSum_collectedBasis_eq_blockDiagonal'`, with `Matrix.blockDiagonal'`. The variant `LinearMap.trace_eq_sum_trace_restrict_of_eq_biSup` treats a finite independent family whose supremum is invariant. The lemma `LinearMap.trace_comp_eq_zero_of_commute_of_trace_restrict_eq_zero` applies this to the generalized eigenspaces `Module.End.maxGenEigenspace` of $f$ over a principal ideal domain: if $g$ commutes with $f$ and has trace $0$ on each of them, then $\operatorname{tr}(g f) = 0$. Lie theory uses this result.

#### The graded tensor product

Let $\iota$ be a commutative semiring of degrees with a sign map $\iota \to \{\pm 1\}$, given by an instance `Module ι (Additive ℤˣ)`. This covers $\mathbb{N}$, $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/2$, where $(-1)^i$ has its usual meaning.

The external form is in `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.TensorProduct.Graded.External`, for families `𝒜 ℬ : ι → Type*` of modules. The braiding `TensorProduct.gradedComm R 𝒜 ℬ` is the linear isomorphism $(\bigoplus \mathcal{A}) \otimes (\bigoplus \mathcal{B}) \to (\bigoplus \mathcal{B}) \otimes (\bigoplus \mathcal{A})$ with the Koszul sign: `TensorProduct.gradedComm_of_tmul_of` states $a \otimes b \mapsto (-1)^{j i} \, b \otimes a$ for $a$ of degree $i$ and $b$ of degree $j$. It is its own inverse up to the exchange of the factors, `TensorProduct.gradedComm_symm`. The product `TensorProduct.gradedMul R 𝒜 ℬ` is a bilinear map on $(\bigoplus \mathcal{A}) \otimes (\bigoplus \mathcal{B})$, defined as the composite of the braiding of the middle factors with the two products. The rule `TensorProduct.tmul_of_gradedMul_of_tmul` is the sign rule of the Overview. The laws are `TensorProduct.gradedMul_assoc`, `TensorProduct.one_gradedMul` and `TensorProduct.gradedMul_one`, and `TensorProduct.gradedComm_gradedMul` says that the braiding is multiplicative.

The internal form is in `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.TensorProduct.Graded.Internal`. For graded algebras `𝒜 : ι → Submodule R A` and `ℬ : ι → Submodule R B` with `GradedAlgebra` instances, the type `GradedTensorProduct R 𝒜 ℬ`, with the notation `𝒜 ᵍ⊗[R] ℬ`, is a copy of `A ⊗[R] B`. The pure tensors are written `a ᵍ⊗ₜ[R] b` (the abbreviation `GradedTensorProduct.tmul`). The linear equivalence `GradedTensorProduct.of R 𝒜 ℬ` passes between the copy and `A ⊗[R] B`, and `GradedTensorProduct.auxEquiv` passes to the tensor product of the direct sums, where the external product lives. The instances `GradedTensorProduct.instRing` and `GradedTensorProduct.instAlgebra` carry the product.

**Theorem.** For $b_1 \in \mathcal{B}_{j}$ and $a_2 \in \mathcal{A}_{i}$,
$$(a_1 \otimes b_1)(a_2 \otimes b_2) = (-1)^{j i} \, (a_1 a_2) \otimes (b_1 b_2).$$

*Proof.* Under `GradedTensorProduct.auxEquiv` the product is `TensorProduct.gradedMul`, which first exchanges $b_1$ and $a_2$ by the braiding. The braiding gives the factor $(-1)^{j i}$, and the products in $A$ and in $B$ give $a_1 a_2$ and $b_1 b_2$. The other factors $a_1$ and $b_2$ need not be homogeneous, because both sides are additive in them. $\square$

This is `GradedTensorProduct.tmul_coe_mul_coe_tmul`. The special cases with no sign are `GradedTensorProduct.tmul_zero_coe_mul_coe_tmul` ($b_1$ of degree $0$), `GradedTensorProduct.tmul_coe_mul_zero_coe_tmul` ($a_2$ of degree $0$) and `GradedTensorProduct.tmul_one_mul_one_tmul`, which says $(a \otimes 1)(1 \otimes b) = a \otimes b$. The inclusions `GradedTensorProduct.includeLeft` and `GradedTensorProduct.includeRight` are algebra maps $a \mapsto a \otimes 1$ and $b \mapsto 1 \otimes b$, and the scalars are `GradedTensorProduct.algebraMap_def`.

**Theorem (Universal property).** Let $f : A \to C$ and $g : B \to C$ be algebra maps such that $f(a) g(b) = (-1)^{j i} g(b) f(a)$ for $a \in \mathcal{A}_i$ and $b \in \mathcal{B}_j$. Then $h(a \otimes b) = f(a) g(b)$ defines an algebra map $h : A \mathbin{\hat\otimes} B \to C$.

*Proof.* The formula is bilinear, so $h$ is linear, and $h(1 \otimes 1) = 1$. For homogeneous $b_1 \in \mathcal{B}_j$ and $a_2 \in \mathcal{A}_i$,
$$h(a_1 \otimes b_1) \, h(a_2 \otimes b_2) = f(a_1) \, g(b_1) f(a_2) \, g(b_2) = (-1)^{j i} f(a_1) f(a_2) \, g(b_1) g(b_2),$$
by the hypothesis and $((-1)^{j i})^2 = 1$. The right side is $h$ of the product of the theorem above. Both sides of $h(x y) = h(x) h(y)$ are biadditive, and the homogeneous pure tensors span, so $h$ is multiplicative. $\square$

This is `GradedTensorProduct.lift`, with `GradedTensorProduct.lift_tmul`. The bijection `GradedTensorProduct.liftEquiv` between such pairs $(f, g)$ and algebra maps out of $A \mathbin{\hat\otimes} B$ is the full universal property, and `GradedTensorProduct.algHom_ext` is its uniqueness part. The graded commutativity `GradedTensorProduct.comm` is an algebra isomorphism $\mathcal{A} \mathbin{\hat\otimes} \mathcal{B} \cong \mathcal{B} \mathbin{\hat\otimes} \mathcal{A}$ with `GradedTensorProduct.comm_coe_tmul_coe`: $a \otimes b \mapsto (-1)^{j i} \, b \otimes a$. The ungraded product `Algebra.TensorProduct.comm` would not be multiplicative here. The main user is `CliffordAlgebra.prodEquiv` of Chapter 7, the isomorphism $\mathrm{Cl}(Q_1 \oplus Q_2) \cong \mathrm{Cl}(Q_1) \mathbin{\hat\otimes} \mathrm{Cl}(Q_2)$ for the $\mathbb{Z}/2$-gradings `CliffordAlgebra.evenOdd`.

#### Common errors

1. Writing `a ⊗ₜ[R] b` for an element of `𝒜 ᵍ⊗[R] ℬ`. The ordinary pure tensor lives in `A ⊗[R] B`, which carries the product of Chapter 2 with no sign. Use `a ᵍ⊗ₜ[R] b`, or pass through `GradedTensorProduct.of`.
2. Using `DirectSum.coe_of_mul_apply` over $\mathbb{Z}$. It needs a canonically ordered index with subtraction, such as $\mathbb{N}$. Over a group of degrees, use `DirectSum.coe_of_mul_apply_add`.
3. Forgetting that a graded tensor product needs the sign instance `Module ι (Additive ℤˣ)`. The module structure exists without it, but the ring structure does not, and Lean then finds no multiplication on the type.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $R = \mathbb{Q}[x]$ with the grading by degree. Use the product formula to compute the component of degree $3$ of $(1 + x + x^2)(2 x + x^3)$.
2. (A) Let $R = \mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ with the ideals $V_1 = \mathbb{Z} \times 0$ and $V_2 = 0 \times \mathbb{Z}$. Compute the idempotents $e_1, e_2$ and check the theorem on idempotents.
3. (B) Show that $f(x, y) = (y, x)$ on $\mathbb{Q}^2 = \mathbb{Q} e_1 \oplus \mathbb{Q} e_2$ has trace $0$ by the second trace theorem, and compute its trace from the matrix.
4. (B) Check that the product of the graded tensor product is associative on homogeneous pure tensors. Hint: compare the two signs $(-1)^{|b_1| |a_2| + |b_1 b_2| |a_3|}$ and $(-1)^{|b_2| |a_3| + |b_1| |a_2 a_3|}$.
5. (B) Let $A = B = \bigwedge \mathbb{Q}$, graded by $\mathbb{N}$ with $e$ of degree $1$. Show that $e \otimes 1$ and $1 \otimes e$ anticommute in $A \mathbin{\hat\otimes} B$, and that $(e \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes e)^2 = 0$.
6. (C) Show that $\bigwedge(\mathbb{Q}^2) \cong \bigwedge \mathbb{Q} \mathbin{\hat\otimes} \bigwedge \mathbb{Q}$ as graded algebras. Hint: use the universal property of the graded tensor product in one direction and that of the exterior algebra in the other.
