## Resultants, Vieta's formulas and universal factorizations

### Overview

Two polynomials $f$ and $g$ over a field have a common factor exactly when a single element, their resultant, is zero. The resultant is the determinant of the Sylvester matrix, a square matrix built from the coefficients of $f$ and $g$. When both polynomials split, it is the product of the differences of their roots. The discriminant of $f$ is, up to a sign and a power of the leading coefficient, the resultant of $f$ and $f'$, and it is zero exactly when $f$ has a multiple root. For $f = a X^2 + b X + c$ the discriminant is $b^2 - 4ac$.

Vieta's formulas express the coefficients of a split polynomial through the elementary symmetric functions of its roots. The universal factorization ring is the ring over which a monic polynomial has a factorization into monic factors of given degrees, in the most general way. This section is a guide to these modules, including the module on the space of polynomials of degree less than $n$ on which the Sylvester map acts.

### The library around it

**Polynomials of bounded degree.** The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Polynomial.DegreeLT` studies the submodule `Polynomial.degreeLT R n` of polynomials of degree less than $n$. It has the basis $1, X, \ldots, X^{n-1}$, `Polynomial.degreeLT.basis`, with `Polynomial.degreeLT.basis_val`, so it is free and finite. The equivalence `Polynomial.degreeLT.addLinearEquiv R m n` splits a polynomial of degree less than $m + n$ into its low part and its high part. The Taylor shift $X \mapsto X + r$ keeps the degree, `Polynomial.map_taylorEquiv_degreeLT`, so it restricts to `Polynomial.taylorLinearEquiv r n`, and its determinant is $1$, `Polynomial.det_taylorLinearEquiv`.

**The Sylvester matrix and the resultant.** The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Polynomial.Resultant.Basic` defines the Sylvester matrix `Polynomial.sylvester f g m n`, a square matrix of size $m + n$. It is the matrix of the linear map
$$(p, q) \mapsto f p + g q, \qquad \deg p < n,\ \deg q < m,$$
which is `Polynomial.sylvesterMap`, in the bases of `Polynomial.degreeLT`, `Polynomial.toMatrix_sylvesterMap`. The resultant `Polynomial.resultant f g m n` is its determinant. The numbers $m$ and $n$ are optional arguments whose default values are the natural degrees of $f$ and $g$, so `f.resultant g` is the usual resultant. With the adjugate `Polynomial.adjSylvester`, Cramer's rule gives the main identity:
$$f p + g q = \operatorname{Res}(f, g), \qquad \deg p < n,\ \deg q < m,$$
which is `Polynomial.exists_mul_add_mul_eq_C_resultant`. So the resultant lies in the ideal generated by $f$ and $g$. Over a field it follows that
$$\operatorname{Res}(f, g) = 0 \iff (f \neq 0 \text{ or } g \neq 0) \text{ and } f, g \text{ are not coprime},$$
which is `Polynomial.resultant_eq_zero_iff`. For a monic $f$ over any commutative ring, the resultant is a unit exactly when $f$ and $g$ are coprime, `Polynomial.isUnit_resultant_iff_isCoprime`.

**Computation rules.** The resultant changes by a sign under exchange of the arguments, `Polynomial.resultant_comm`, $\operatorname{Res}(f, g) = (-1)^{mn} \operatorname{Res}(g, f)$. It is multiplicative in each argument, `Polynomial.resultant_mul_left` and `Polynomial.resultant_mul_right`, with versions for powers and finite products. The resultant with a linear polynomial is an evaluation, `Polynomial.resultant_X_sub_C_right`: $\operatorname{Res}(f, X - r) = (-1)^m f(r)$. Adding a multiple of $f$ to $g$ does not change the resultant, `Polynomial.resultant_add_mul_left`, and this lets one compute resultants by a Euclidean algorithm. The resultant commutes with ring homomorphisms, `Polynomial.resultant_map_map`, and with the Taylor shift, `Polynomial.resultant_taylor`. When $f$ splits, the resultant is a product of values:
$$\operatorname{Res}(f, g) = a^n \prod_{f(\alpha) = 0} g(\alpha),$$
where $a$ is the leading coefficient of $f$, which is `Polynomial.resultant_eq_prod_eval`. When $f$ and $g$ are monic and split over a field, `Polynomial.resultant_eq_prod_roots_sub` gives $\prod_{i, j} (\alpha_i - \beta_j)$. The proofs of these formulas use `Polynomial.induction_of_Splits_of_injective_of_surjective`, which reduces a statement to the case of split polynomials.

**The discriminant.** The discriminant `Polynomial.discr f` is defined from the Sylvester matrix of $f$ and $f'$. The relation with the resultant is
$$\operatorname{Res}(f, f') = (-1)^{n(n-1)/2} \, a \, \operatorname{disc}(f),$$
for $f$ of degree $n > 0$ with leading coefficient $a$, which is `Polynomial.resultant_deriv`. The small cases are explicit: `Polynomial.discr_of_degree_eq_one` gives $1$, `Polynomial.discr_of_degree_eq_two` gives $b^2 - 4ac$, and `Polynomial.discr_of_degree_eq_three` gives the formula for cubics.

**Vieta's formulas.** The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Polynomial.Vieta` expands products of linear factors. For a multiset $s$ of elements of $R$,
$$\prod_{r \in s} (X + r) = \sum_{j=0}^{|s|} e_j(s) X^{|s| - j},$$
which is `Multiset.prod_X_add_C_eq_sum_esymm`, where $e_j$ is the elementary symmetric function `Multiset.esymm`. The version with $X - r$ is `Multiset.prod_X_sub_X_eq_sum_esymm`, and the version in which the $r$ are variables is `MvPolynomial.prod_C_add_X_eq_sum_esymm`. Applied to the roots of a polynomial $p$ that splits, this gives
$$p_k = a \, (-1)^{n - k} \, e_{n-k}(\text{roots of } p),$$
which is `Polynomial.coeff_eq_esymm_roots_of_splits` over a field and `Polynomial.coeff_eq_esymm_roots_of_card` over a domain when the number of roots equals the degree. The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Polynomial.SmallDegreeVieta` states the case of degree two: the roots of $a X^2 + b X + c$ are $x_1, x_2$ exactly when $b = -a(x_1 + x_2)$ and $c = a x_1 x_2$, `Polynomial.roots_quadratic_eq_pair_iff_of_ne_zero`.

**Symmetric functions of the roots.** The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.MvPolynomial.Symmetric.Eval` evaluates a symmetric polynomial at a multiset of elements. For a multiset $m$ in an $R$-algebra $S$ whose size is the number of variables, `MvPolynomial.symmetricSubalgebra.aevalMultiset σ R m` is the algebra map from the symmetric polynomials to $S$ that substitutes the elements of $m$ for the variables. It sends $e_{i+1}$ to the elementary symmetric function of $m$, `MvPolynomial.symmetricSubalgebra.aevalMultiset_esymm`. With Vieta's formulas this gives the classical consequence: a symmetric expression in the roots of a polynomial over $R$ lies in the image of $R$ after a multiplication by a power of the leading coefficient. The precise form is `Polynomial.leadingCoeff_pow_natDegree_smul_sum_map_aroots_aeval_mem_range_algebraMap`, which treats the sum $\sum_\alpha q(\alpha)$ over the roots $\alpha$ of a split polynomial $p$. The sum $\sum_i q(x_i)$ as a symmetric polynomial is `MvPolynomial.symmetricSubalgebra.sumPolynomial`.

**The universal factorization ring.** The module `Mathlib.RingTheory.Polynomial.UniversalFactorizationRing` starts with the free monic polynomial `Polynomial.freeMonic R n`, whose lower coefficients are variables. For a monic $p$ of degree $n = m + k$ over $R$, the ring `Polynomial.UniversalFactorizationRing m k hn p` is an $R$-algebra over which $p$ factors as a product of monic polynomials of degrees $m$ and $k$, `Polynomial.UniversalFactorizationRing.factor₁_mul_factor₂`. It is universal: an $R$-algebra map from it to $S$ is the same as such a factorization of $p$ in $S[X]$, `Polynomial.UniversalFactorizationRing.homEquiv`. The ring is finite and finitely presented over $R$, by instances. The variant `Polynomial.UniversalCoprimeFactorizationRing` asks for coprime factors. Its main application is `Algebra.exists_etale_bijective_residueFieldMap_and_map_eq_mul_and_isCoprime`: if a monic $p$ factors into coprime monic factors over the residue field of a prime $P$, then the factorization lifts to an étale algebra over $R$ with a prime over $P$ and the same residue field. This is an algebraic form of Hensel's lemma.

#### Common errors

1. `Polynomial.resultant f g` uses the natural degrees of $f$ and $g$ as default sizes. A lemma stated with explicit sizes $m$ and $n$ applies to the default only after a proof that the sizes agree. For the zero polynomial the default size is $0$, and the resultant behaves differently.
2. `Polynomial.resultant_eq_zero_iff` holds over a field. Over a general commutative ring the useful statement is `Polynomial.isUnit_resultant_iff_isCoprime`, which needs $f$ monic and compares a unit resultant with coprimality.
3. The sign conventions differ between sources. In the library $\operatorname{Res}(f, X - r) = (-1)^m f(r)$, not $f(r)$, and the discriminant is related to $\operatorname{Res}(f, f')$ by the sign in `Polynomial.resultant_deriv`.

### Exercises

1. (A) Write the Sylvester matrix of $f = X^2 + 1$ and $g = X - 1$, compute its determinant, and compare with `Polynomial.resultant_X_sub_C_right`.
2. (A) Use `Polynomial.discr_of_degree_eq_two` to decide for which $c \in \mathbb{Q}$ the polynomial $X^2 + X + c$ has a multiple root.
3. (B) Use `Polynomial.coeff_eq_esymm_roots_of_splits` to prove that the sum of the roots of a monic split polynomial of degree $n$ is minus its coefficient of $X^{n-1}$.
4. (B) Let $f, g \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$ and let $p$ be a prime number. Use `Polynomial.resultant_map_map` and `Polynomial.resultant_eq_zero_iff` to prove that the reductions of $f$ and $g$ modulo $p$ have a common factor when $p$ divides the resultant, provided the leading coefficients are not divisible by $p$.
5. (B) Prove from `Polynomial.exists_mul_add_mul_eq_C_resultant` that the resultant of $f$ and $g$ lies in the ideal $(f, g)$ of $R[X]$.
6. (C) Describe `Polynomial.UniversalFactorizationRing 1 1 rfl p` for $p = X^2 + bX + c$ over $R$ as a quotient of $R[Y]$, and prove that it is free of rank $2$ over $R$.
