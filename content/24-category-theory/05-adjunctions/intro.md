# Adjunctions

This chapter studies adjunctions, the pairs of functors $F$ and $G$ with a natural bijection between maps $F X \to Y$ and maps $X \to G Y$. Free groups, abelianization, products with a fixed set, Galois connections and the inclusion of a full subcategory with a reflector all give adjunctions. An adjunction determines each of its functors up to unique isomorphism.

Section 1 gives the definition by a natural bijection of Hom sets and the definition by a unit and a counit with the triangle identities, and proves that the two definitions are equivalent. Section 2 proves that right adjoints preserve limits and left adjoints preserve colimits, proves the uniqueness of adjoints, and characterizes fully faithful adjoints and reflective subcategories by the unit and the counit. Section 3 introduces comma categories, proves that a functor has a left adjoint exactly when each category of structured arrows has an initial object, and proves Freyd's general adjoint functor theorem.

The chapter needs categories, functors and natural transformations (Chapters 1 and 2), representable functors (Chapter 3), and limits, colimits and their preservation (Chapter 4). The examples use groups (Volume 5), sets and orders (Volume 4) and topological spaces (Volume 13). In Mathlib an adjunction is `Adjunction F G`, with notation `F ⊣ G`, and the classes `Functor.IsLeftAdjoint` and `Functor.IsRightAdjoint` record that an adjoint exists. Chapter 6 builds on this chapter: each adjunction gives a monad $G F$, and the comparison with the category of algebras of this monad leads to the monadicity theorems.
