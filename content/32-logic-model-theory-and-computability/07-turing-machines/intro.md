# Turing machines

This chapter gives the machine model of computation and connects it with the partial recursive functions of Chapter 6. Section 1 sets up state transition systems, their evaluation and the notion of one system respecting another, and proves that a simulation preserves the results of runs. It defines tapes as pairs of half-tapes with blanks at the ends, then three machine models: TM0, the classical Turing machine; TM1, a machine with a small program language and a local store; and TM2, a machine with several stacks. It proves that the models simulate each other, that a binary alphabet suffices, and that a machine that adds one to a binary number is correct, with an exact count of its steps. The section ends with a basis of partial recursive functions on lists of numbers and its sequential evaluator. Section 2 defines encodings of types as words over an alphabet, Turing computable functions, and computability in a given time and in polynomial time. It then compiles each program of the list basis into a TM2 machine with four stacks and proves that each partial recursive function is computed by a Turing machine.

The chapter needs lists and countable types from Volume 3, the natural numbers from Volume 2, and decidable propositions from Volume 1. From Chapter 6 it uses the partial recursive functions, the basis $\mathrm{Nat.Partrec'}$ on vectors and the equivalence of the two bases.

Chapter 8 studies the running time of divide-and-conquer algorithms, and the time bounds of Section 2 give the machine meaning of such bounds. Volume 33 on data structures and computation returns to programs and their cost, with Lean functions in place of machines.
