# Determinants

The determinant attaches one scalar to each square matrix. It is zero exactly when the matrix is singular, it measures the change of volume under a linear map, and it is the source of the characteristic polynomial of Chapter 5. This chapter develops the determinant over a commutative ring. Section 1 defines it by the formula of Leibniz, a sum over the permutations, and proves its properties as a function of the rows: it is multilinear and alternating, it is invariant under the transpose, and it has a Laplace expansion. Section 2 proves the multiplicativity $\det(A B) = \det A \det B$, defines the adjugate and proves $A \operatorname{adj} A = \det A \cdot I$. From these two results it characterizes the invertible matrices by their determinant, derives the rule of Cramer for linear systems, and defines the determinant of a linear endomorphism. Section 3 describes the permutation matrices, the elementary matrices and the transvections, and reduces each invertible matrix to a diagonal matrix by row operations. Section 4 treats the inverses with the class `Invertible`, the integer powers of a matrix, and the inverses over a commutative semiring, where Mathlib splits the determinant into two halves. Section 5 describes classes of matrices from combinatorics and probability: totally unimodular and totally nonnegative matrices, the permanent, Hadamard matrices, stochastic matrices and irreducible matrices. Section 6 defines rays in a module over an ordered ring, the base of orientations, and the standard orientation of the complex plane. Section 7 proves the interpolation theorem of Lagrange, the inverse of the evaluation map whose matrix is the Vandermonde matrix.

The chapter needs the matrices of Chapter 3 and the sign of a permutation of Volume 5, Chapter 7. All the theorems hold over a commutative ring and not only over a field. This generality is necessary for Chapter 5, where the entries of the matrix $X I - A$ are polynomials.
