## Products of additive subgroups and opposite subrings

### Overview

Two sets $s$ and $t$ in a ring have a pointwise product $s t = \{x y : x \in s, y \in t\}$ and a pointwise sum $s + t$. Volume 5 sets up these operations for sets in a monoid. In a ring, the two operations interact through the distributive law, but only up to inclusion: $(s + t) u \subseteq s u + t u$, and the two sides differ in general.

For additive subgroups the product is defined differently. The product $M N$ of two additive submonoids is the additive submonoid generated by the products $m n$, so it holds all finite sums $\sum m_i n_i$. This is the product that ideals and submodules use, and it makes the additive submonoids of a semiring into a semiring of their own. This section covers that product, the action of a group on the subrings of a ring, and the opposite of a subring. These topics use the subrings of Section 3 and the ring actions of Section 6.

### The library around it

#### Pointwise operations on sets

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Pointwise.Set` gives the ring facts for sets. The pointwise product and sum satisfy two inclusions, `Set.add_mul_subset` for $(s + t) u \subseteq s u + t u$ and `Set.mul_add_subset` for $s (t + u) \subseteq s t + s u$. The inclusions are strict for many sets. Take $s = t = \{1\}$ and $u = \{0, 1\}$ in $\mathbb{Z}$. The left side is $\{2\} \cdot \{0, 1\} = \{0, 2\}$, and the right side is $\{0, 1\} + \{0, 1\} = \{0, 1, 2\}$. The element $1$ on the right comes from $1 \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot 1$, where the two factors of $u$ are different. That is the reason for the inclusion: the pointwise operations choose each element independently.

Negation also acts pointwise. The instance `Set.hasDistribNeg` makes $(-s) t = -(s t) = s (-t)$ hold for sets in a ring. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Pointwise.Finset` gives the same results for finite sets, with `Finset.add_mul_subset`, `Finset.mul_add_subset` and the instance `Finset.distribNeg`.

For scalar multiplication of sets, `Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Action.Pointwise.Set` has the distributive laws `Set.add_smul_subset`, `Set.neg_smul` and `Set.smul_set_neg`. It also has a criterion for zero: `Set.zero_mem_smul_iff` says that $0 \in s \cdot t$ if and only if $0 \in s$ and $t$ is not empty, or $0 \in t$ and $s$ is not empty, for a module over a domain without torsion. The finite set forms are `Finset.zero_mem_smul_iff` and `Finset.zero_mem_smul_finset_iff` in `Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Action.Pointwise.Finset`.

#### The product of additive submonoids

Let $R$ be a semiring and $M, N$ additive submonoids of $R$. The product $M N$ is the smallest additive submonoid that holds each product $m n$ with $m \in M$ and $n \in N$. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Submonoid.Pointwise` defines it as the instance `AddSubmonoid.mul`. The pointwise product of the underlying sets is not closed under addition in general, so the two products differ; `AddSubmonoid.mul_eq_closure_mul_set` states that $M N$ is the closure of the set product.

Three lemmas describe the product. `AddSubmonoid.mul_mem_mul` gives $m n \in M N$. `AddSubmonoid.mul_le` is the universal property: $M N \le P$ if and only if $m n \in P$ for all $m \in M$ and $n \in N$. `AddSubmonoid.mul_induction_on` is the induction principle: a property of the elements of $M N$ holds if it holds for each product $m n$ and is closed under addition. Most proofs about $M N$ use one of these three, not the definition.

**Proposition.** For subsets $S$ and $T$ of $R$, the product of the generated submonoids is the submonoid generated by $S T$.

*Proof.* Write $\langle S \rangle$ for the generated additive submonoid. Each product $s t$ with $s \in S$ and $t \in T$ is in $\langle S \rangle \langle T \rangle$, so $\langle S T \rangle \le \langle S \rangle \langle T \rangle$. For the other inclusion, use `AddSubmonoid.mul_le`. Each element of $\langle S \rangle$ is a finite sum $\sum s_i$, and each element of $\langle T \rangle$ is a finite sum $\sum t_j$. Their product is $\sum_{i,j} s_i t_j$ by distributivity, and this is in $\langle S T \rangle$. $\square$

The lemma is `AddSubmonoid.closure_mul_closure`. The product is monotone in each factor (`AddSubmonoid.mul_le_mul`, `AddSubmonoid.mul_le_mul_left`, `AddSubmonoid.mul_le_mul_right`) and distributes over joins (`AddSubmonoid.mul_sup`, `AddSubmonoid.sup_mul`, `AddSubmonoid.iSup_mul`, `AddSubmonoid.mul_iSup`). The bottom submonoid is absorbing, by `AddSubmonoid.mul_bot` and `AddSubmonoid.bot_mul`.

The unit for this product is the image of $\mathbb{N}$: `AddSubmonoid.one_eq_closure` states $1 = \langle 1 \rangle$, and `AddSubmonoid.mem_one` says that $x \in 1$ if and only if $x$ is a natural number cast. With the join as addition, the additive submonoids of a semiring form a monoid, by `AddSubmonoid.monoid`, and powers satisfy `AddSubmonoid.pow_eq_closure_pow_set`. If each element of $M$ commutes with each element of $N$, then $M N = N M$ by `AddSubmonoid.mul_comm_of_commute`.

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Subgroup` gives the same product for additive subgroups of a ring, `AddSubgroup.mul`, with `AddSubgroup.mul_toAddSubmonoid` to compare it with the submonoid product. The scalar version `AddSubmonoid.smul` multiplies an additive submonoid of $R$ with an additive submonoid of a module, with `AddSubmonoid.smul_mem_smul`, `AddSubmonoid.smul_le` and `AddSubmonoid.smul_induction_on`. Chapter 2 uses the same pattern for the product of ideals.

#### Group actions on subrings

When a monoid $M$ acts on a ring $R$ by ring endomorphisms, it also acts on the subrings of $R$: $m \cdot S$ is the image of $S$ under $x \mapsto m \cdot x$. The modules `Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Subring.Pointwise` and `Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Subsemiring.Pointwise` define the actions `Subring.pointwiseMulAction` and `Subsemiring.pointwiseMulAction`. These are scoped instances in the namespace `Pointwise`, so a file must open that namespace to use them. The definition `Subring.pointwise_smul_def` writes $m \cdot S$ as `Subring.map` of the ring homomorphism `MulSemiringAction.toRingHom`.

Membership in $m \cdot S$ has two forms. `Subring.mem_smul_pointwise_iff_exists` says that $r \in m \cdot S$ if and only if $r = m \cdot s$ for some $s \in S$. For a group, `Subring.mem_pointwise_smul_iff_inv_smul_mem` gives the more useful form $x \in g \cdot S \iff g^{-1} \cdot x \in S$. The action commutes with the lattice operations: `Subring.smul_bot`, `Subring.smul_sup` and `Subring.smul_closure`, which says $a \cdot \langle s \rangle = \langle a \cdot s \rangle$. The variants with a subscript zero, such as `Subring.mem_pointwise_smul_iff_inv_smul_mem₀`, work for a group with zero and a nonzero scalar. The subsemiring forms have the same names in the namespace `Subsemiring`.

#### Opposite subrings

The opposite ring $R^{\mathrm{op}}$ has the same elements as $R$ with the product reversed. A subring $S$ of $R$ gives a subring of $R^{\mathrm{op}}$ with the same elements, since the subring conditions do not depend on the order of the factors. The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Subring.MulOpposite` defines `Subring.op` and `Subring.unop`, with `Subring.mem_op` for membership. They are inverse order isomorphisms, packaged as `Subring.opEquiv`, so they preserve the bottom and top subrings, meets, joins and closures: `Subring.op_bot`, `Subring.op_sup` and `Subring.op_closure`.

The subring $S^{\mathrm{op}}$ and the ring $S$ with reversed product are the same ring in two forms. `Subring.mopRingEquivOp` is the ring isomorphism from the opposite of $S$ to $S^{\mathrm{op}}$, and `Subring.ringEquivOpMop` goes from $S$ to the opposite of $S^{\mathrm{op}}$. The additive part `Subring.addEquivOp` is simpler, since the additive structures agree. `Mathlib.Algebra.Ring.Subsemiring.MulOpposite` gives the same API for subsemirings, with `Subsemiring.op`, `Subsemiring.opEquiv` and `Subsemiring.mopRingEquivOp`.

#### Common errors

1. The product $M N$ of two additive submonoids is not the set of products. An element of $M N$ is a finite sum $\sum m_i n_i$. To prove a property of all elements, use `AddSubmonoid.mul_induction_on`, not a case split on $x = m n$.
2. The distributive law for sets holds only as an inclusion. The lemma `Set.add_mul_subset` cannot be turned into an equation without further hypotheses.
3. The action of a group on subrings is a scoped instance. A proof that writes `g • S` for a subring `S` fails to elaborate until `open Pointwise` is in force.

### Exercises

1. (A) In $\mathbb{Z}$, compute the product of the additive subgroups $2\mathbb{Z}$ and $3\mathbb{Z}$.
2. (A) Find sets $s, t, u \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ with $s (t + u) \ne s t + s u$.
3. (B) Prove that the product of additive submonoids is associative, using `AddSubmonoid.mul_le` and `AddSubmonoid.mul_induction_on`.
4. (B) Let a group $G$ act on $R$ by ring automorphisms. Show that $g \cdot S = S$ for all $g$ if and only if $S$ is an invariant subring.
5. (B) Show that the center of $R$ and the center of $R^{\mathrm{op}}$ have the same elements.
6. (C) Let $R$ be a commutative ring. Show that the additive subgroups of $R$ form a commutative monoid under the product of this section.
