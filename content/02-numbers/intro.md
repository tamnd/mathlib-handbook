# Numbers

This volume builds the number systems of mathematics in the order of their construction, $\mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{Z} \subset \mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{R}$, and proves the standard theorems of elementary number theory and of the real line. Each section gives the mathematics with complete proofs and then its form in Mathlib: the definitions, the junk values and the names of the main lemmas.

Chapter 1 starts from the axioms of Peano. It proves the laws of arithmetic and the forms of induction, the well ordering principle, and the binomial theorem. Chapter 2 proves the division algorithm, Euclid's algorithm, Bézout's identity, Euclid's lemma, the infinitude of primes and the fundamental theorem of arithmetic. Chapter 3 builds `ℤ`, treats congruences, and proves the Chinese remainder theorem and Fermat's little theorem. Chapter 4 builds `ℚ` and proves that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational and that `ℚ` does not have the least upper bound property. Chapter 5 explains the casts between the number types, which appear in each statement that mixes two of them. Chapter 6 makes the classes modulo $n$ into the rings `Fin n` and `ZMod n` and finds their units. Chapter 7 builds `ℝ` from Cauchy sequences and proves that it is a complete ordered field. Chapter 8 adds the infinite values in `NNReal`, `ENNReal`, `ENat` and `EReal`. Chapter 9 gives the binary forms of the natural numbers: the bits of a number, the binary numerals `PosNum`, `Num` and `ZNum`, and the positive natural numbers `ℕ+`. Chapter 10 builds `ℂ` as the pairs of real numbers and proves that it is a field with a conjugation.

Read Volume 1 first, in particular its chapter on junk values. Chapters 1 to 4 go in order. Chapter 5 uses Chapters 1, 3 and 4, and Chapters 6 to 8 use all of the chapters before them. Chapter 9 uses Chapters 1, 2 and 6. Chapter 10 uses Chapters 5 and 7.
