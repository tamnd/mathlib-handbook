# Taylor's theorem

A differentiable function is close to its tangent line near a point. Taylor's theorem says how close, and it replaces the tangent line by a polynomial of any degree. The error of the approximation has an exact form, the remainder, and the remainder comes from the mean value theorem. This chapter proves the theorem and uses it.

Section 1 defines the higher derivatives, the classes $C^n$ and the Taylor polynomial. It proves Taylor's theorem with the Lagrange remainder and with the Cauchy remainder, both from the Cauchy mean value theorem, and it proves the Peano form $f(x) - P_n(x) = o((x - x_0)^n)$. Section 2 applies the theorem: the second derivative test and the higher derivative test for extrema, convexity from $f'' \ge 0$, numerical bounds for $\sin$, $\exp$ and $\log$, the series of $e^x$, and a smooth function whose Taylor series does not converge to it.

The chapter needs the derivative, Rolle's theorem and the mean value theorem of Chapter 3, and the limits of sequences of Chapter 1. Chapter 5 uses the estimates of Section 2 for the exponential and the logarithm, Chapter 6 uses them for the sine and the cosine, and Chapter 7 restates the Peano form in the language of $o$.
