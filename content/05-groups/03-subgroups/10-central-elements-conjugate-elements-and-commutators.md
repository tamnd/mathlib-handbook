## Central elements, conjugate elements and commutators

### Overview

Three notions measure how far a monoid is from being commutative. An element is central when it commutes with every element. Two elements $a$ and $b$ are conjugate when $c a c^{-1} = b$ for some unit $c$. The commutator of two elements of a group is $[g, h] = g h g^{-1} h^{-1}$, and it is $1$ exactly when $g$ and $h$ commute. In a commutative monoid each element is central, each element is conjugate only to itself, and each commutator is $1$.

The four modules of this section hold these notions at the level of sets and elements, with no subgroup structure. The center and the centralizers as sets come from `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Center`, and the earlier section on centers and centralizers turns them into subsemigroups, submonoids and subgroups. The relation of conjugacy and the type of conjugacy classes come from `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Conj` and `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.ConjFinite`. The commutator notation comes from `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Commutator`. The class equation of Chapter 6 counts conjugacy classes, and the commutator subgroups of Chapter 5 are generated by commutators.

### The library around it

#### The center of a magma

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Center` defines `Set.center M` for any type with a multiplication. The definition does not assume associativity, so it asks for more than commutation. An element $z$ is in the center when `IsMulCentral z` holds, by `Set.mem_center_iff`. The structure `IsMulCentral` has four fields: `IsMulCentral.comm` says that $z$ commutes with each element, and `IsMulCentral.left_assoc`, `IsMulCentral.mid_assoc` and `IsMulCentral.right_assoc` say that each product of three elements with $z$ in one position is associative. In a magma that is not associative, commutation alone does not make the set closed under products. These conditions make it closed.

In a semigroup the three associativity conditions hold for every element. Then `Semigroup.mem_center_iff` gives the usual definition:

$$z \in Z(M) \iff \forall g,\ g z = z g.$$

The center is closed under the operations, at the level of sets. The lemmas `Set.one_mem_center`, `Set.mul_mem_center` and `Set.npow_mem_center` give $1$, products and powers. The lemmas `Set.inv_mem_center` and `Set.div_mem_center` give inverses and quotients in a division monoid. The proof of `Set.mul_mem_center` in a semigroup is short: if $z_1$ and $z_2$ commute with $g$, then

$$g (z_1 z_2) = (g z_1) z_2 = (z_1 g) z_2 = z_1 (g z_2) = z_1 (z_2 g) = (z_1 z_2) g.$$

In a commutative semigroup the center is everything, by `Set.center_eq_univ`.

The center behaves well with products and isomorphisms. The center of $M \times N$ is the product of the centers, by `Set.center_prod`, and the center of a product of a family is the product of the centers, by `Set.center_pi`. Proof sketch for two factors: $(a, b)$ commutes with every $(x, y)$ if and only if $a$ commutes with every $x$ and $b$ commutes with every $y$, because the operations are componentwise. An isomorphism maps the center onto the center, by `Set.image_center_eq`. A surjective homomorphism maps central elements to central elements, by `IsMulCentral.map`.

#### Centralizers as sets

The centralizer `Set.centralizer S` of a set $S$ is the set of elements that commute with every element of $S$, by `Set.mem_centralizer_iff`. The centralizer of all of $M$ is the center, by `Set.centralizer_univ`, in a semigroup. Proof: the definitions agree after `Semigroup.mem_center_iff`. A larger set has a smaller centralizer, so the centralizer of a union is the intersection, by `Set.centralizer_union`. The centralizer of the empty set is everything, by `Set.centralizer_empty`. The centralizer is closed under products in a semigroup, by `Set.mul_mem_centralizer`, and under inverses in a group, by `Set.inv_mem_centralizer`.

The operation $S \mapsto C(S)$ reverses inclusion, and $S \subseteq C(C(S))$. These two facts give `Set.centralizer_centralizer_centralizer`: three centralizers are one centralizer. When the elements of $S$ commute with each other, the double centralizer $C(C(S))$ is commutative, by `Set.centralizer_centralizer_pairwise_commute`. This is the usual way to put a set of commuting elements inside a commutative substructure.

#### Conjugate elements

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Conj` defines `IsConj a b` in a monoid: there is a unit $c$ with $c a = b c$. In a group this is the familiar condition, by `isConj_iff`:

$$\operatorname{IsConj}(a, b) \iff \exists c,\ c a c^{-1} = b.$$

Conjugacy is an equivalence relation: `IsConj.refl`, `IsConj.symm` and `IsConj.trans`. The proof of transitivity in a group: if $c a c^{-1} = b$ and $d b d^{-1} = e$, then $(d c) a (d c)^{-1} = d (c a c^{-1}) d^{-1} = e$. In a monoid the same proof uses the product of the two units. The relation as a setoid is `IsConj.setoid`. In a commutative monoid conjugacy is equality, by `isConj_iff_eq`, because $c a = b c$ and $c a = a c$ give $a = b$ after cancelling the unit $c$. The element $1$ is conjugate only to itself, by `isConj_one_right` and `isConj_one_left`.

Homomorphisms preserve conjugacy, by `MonoidHom.map_isConj`, and conjugate elements have conjugate powers, by `IsConj.pow`. The conjugation map $g \mapsto x g x^{-1}$ is a homomorphism of groups, and the lemmas `conj_mul`, `conj_inv`, `conj_pow` and `conj_zpow` state its compatibility with the operations. It is injective, by `conj_injective`.

The set of the conjugates of $a$ is `conjugatesOf a`, and `mem_conjugatesOf_self` says that it contains $a$. Two elements are conjugate if and only if they have the same set of conjugates, by `isConj_iff_conjugatesOf_eq`.

#### Conjugacy classes

The type `ConjClasses α` is the quotient of $\alpha$ by conjugacy. The map `ConjClasses.mk` sends an element to its class, and `ConjClasses.mk_eq_mk_iff_isConj` states

$$[a] = [b] \iff \operatorname{IsConj}(a, b).$$

This is the defining property of a quotient by an equivalence relation. The map `ConjClasses.mk` is surjective, by `ConjClasses.mk_surjective`, and each class has a representative, by `ConjClasses.exists_rep`. The members of a class form the set `ConjClasses.carrier`. A homomorphism $f$ induces `ConjClasses.map f` on the classes, and a surjective $f$ induces a surjective map, by `ConjClasses.map_surjective`. In a commutative monoid each class has one element, so `ConjClasses.mkEquiv` is an equivalence between $\alpha$ and its conjugacy classes.

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.ConjFinite` holds only instances. A finite monoid has finitely many conjugacy classes (`instFiniteConjClasses`). With decidable conjugacy, the classes and the set of conjugates of an element form finite types (`instFintypeConjClassesOfDecidableRelIsConj`, `conjugatesOf.fintype`). Conjugacy is decidable in a finite monoid with decidable equality, by `instDecidableRelIsConjOfDecidableEqOfFintype`, because the search over all units is finite. These instances let `Fintype.card (ConjClasses G)` appear in the class equation of Chapter 6.

#### The commutator of two elements

The module `Mathlib.Algebra.Group.Commutator` gives a group the bracket notation `⁅g₁, g₂⁆` through the instance `commutatorElement`. The definition is `commutatorElement_def`:

$$[g_1, g_2] = g_1 g_2 g_1^{-1} g_2^{-1}.$$

This is the convention of the library: the inverse factors come second. With this convention $[g_1, g_2] = 1$ if and only if $g_1 g_2 = g_2 g_1$. The lemmas about commutator elements and the commutator subgroup are in the section on commutators and the abelianization in Chapter 5.

#### Common errors

1. In a magma, `Set.center` is not the set of elements that commute with everything. It also has the three associativity conditions of `IsMulCentral`. Use `Semigroup.mem_center_iff` to get the simple form, which needs a semigroup.
2. `IsConj` in a monoid uses a unit $c$ and the equation $c a = b c$, not $c a c^{-1} = b$. In a monoid with few units, two elements can be conjugate in a larger monoid but not in the original one. In a group, rewrite with `isConj_iff` to get the familiar form.
3. The bracket `⁅g₁, g₂⁆` is the commutator of the library. The square brackets `[g₁, g₂]` in Lean are a list. Other texts use the convention $g_1^{-1} g_2^{-1} g_1 g_2$, so compare the definition before a computation.

### Exercises

1. (A) Prove `Set.mul_mem_center` for a semigroup from `Semigroup.mem_center_iff`, and `Set.inv_mem_center` for a group.
2. (A) Use `Set.center_prod` to compute the center of $S_3 \times \mathbb{Z}/4$, given that the center of $S_3$ is trivial.
3. (A) Prove that $\operatorname{IsConj}(a, b)$ implies $\operatorname{IsConj}(a^n, b^n)$ in a group, by the formula $(c a c^{-1})^n = c a^n c^{-1}$ of `conj_pow`.
4. (B) Prove $S \subseteq C(C(S))$ and $S \subseteq T \Rightarrow C(T) \subseteq C(S)$. Deduce `Set.centralizer_centralizer_centralizer`.
5. (B) List the conjugacy classes of $S_3$. Then show that `ConjClasses.map f` is not injective for the sign map $f : S_3 \to \{\pm 1\}$.
6. (C) Let $G$ be a group. Prove that $[g, h] = 1$ if and only if $g h = h g$, and that $[g, h]^{-1} = [h, g]$. Then show that the conjugate $x [g, h] x^{-1}$ is the commutator $[x g x^{-1}, x h x^{-1}]$.
