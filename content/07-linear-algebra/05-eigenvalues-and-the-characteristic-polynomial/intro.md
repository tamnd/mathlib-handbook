# Eigenvalues and the characteristic polynomial

An eigenvector of a linear map is a nonzero vector that the map only stretches. The scalar of the stretch is an eigenvalue. In a basis of eigenvectors, the matrix of the map is diagonal, and its powers and its polynomials are easy to compute. This chapter finds the eigenvalues as the roots of a polynomial and studies the polynomials that a map satisfies. Section 1 defines the eigenvalues and the eigenspaces, proves that eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are independent, and defines the characteristic polynomial $\det(X I - A)$. Section 2 proves the theorem of Cayley and Hamilton, compares the characteristic polynomial with the minimal polynomial, and states the decomposition of Jordan and Chevalley as Mathlib has it.

The chapter needs the determinant of Chapter 4 over the ring of polynomials, and the polynomials of Volume 6.
