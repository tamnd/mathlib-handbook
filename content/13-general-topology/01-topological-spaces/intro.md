# Topological spaces

A topology on a set says which subsets are open. From the open sets come the closed sets, the closure and the interior of a set, the neighbourhoods of a point and the continuous maps. This chapter gives these notions and the first examples.

Section 1 defines a topological space and its open and closed sets, the closure, the interior and the frontier, and dense sets. It studies the discrete, the indiscrete and the cofinite topologies, and the order on the topologies of a set, which Mathlib reverses. Section 2 defines neighbourhoods, a basis of a topology, the topology generated by a family of sets, and the countability axioms. It builds the topology of $\mathbb{R}$ from the open intervals. Section 3 defines continuous maps and homeomorphisms, and builds the induced, coinduced, subspace, product and quotient topologies by their universal properties.

The chapter needs the set operations of Volume 1 and the complete lattices of Volume 4. Chapter 2 rewrites the neighbourhoods of Section 2 as a filter, and the rest of the volume rests on the definitions of this chapter.
