# Alephs and infinite cardinal arithmetic

This chapter joins the two scales of the volume. Chapter 1 gave the cardinals and Chapter 2 gave the ordinals. Each cardinal has a least ordinal of that size, its initial ordinal. The infinite initial ordinals form a closed unbounded class, and their enumeration gives the alephs $\aleph_\alpha$ and the ordinals $\omega_\alpha$. The power set gives a second scale, the beths $\beth_\alpha$. Section 1 builds the three scales and proves the basic facts: $\aleph_{\alpha+1}$ is the successor of $\aleph_\alpha$, the alephs are a normal function, and each infinite cardinal is an aleph.

Section 2 proves the theorem that makes infinite cardinal arithmetic simple: $\kappa \cdot \kappa = \kappa$ for each infinite cardinal $\kappa$. The proof uses the initial ordinals of Section 1 and the order of Gödel on pairs. From this theorem, the sum and the product of two cardinals, one of them infinite, are their maximum, and $\kappa^\kappa = 2^\kappa$. The section also counts lists and finite sets, shows that each infinite cardinal is prime in the semiring of cardinals, and gives the small tools of Mathlib for direct limits, countable covers and the predicate `HasCardinalLT`. Section 3 applies the results to the continuum $\mathfrak{c} = 2^{\aleph_0}$ and to the structures of algebra: free groups and rings, finitely supported functions, the rationals, the subfield generated by a set, and the countable set of algebraic numbers.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 to 4 of this volume, the Galois connections of Volume 4, Chapter 6, and the countable sets of Volume 3, Chapter 8. Chapter 6 uses the alephs and the arithmetic of this chapter to define the cofinality, to separate the regular cardinals from the singular cardinals, and to study the closed unbounded sets.
