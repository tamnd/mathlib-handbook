# Group homology and cohomology

This chapter applies the derived functors of the earlier chapters to the category of representations of a group. Section 1 describes representations in Mathlib, builds the standard resolution of the trivial module $k$ over the group algebra $k[G]$ with its contracting homotopy, and defines group cohomology and group homology in two ways: as Ext and Tor, and as the homology of the inhomogeneous complexes. Section 2 interprets the low degrees: invariants and coinvariants, crossed homomorphisms, $2$-cocycles and group extensions, the abelianization as $H_1$, and Hilbert's Theorem 90 in the form of Noether. Section 3 treats the maps between cohomology groups of different pairs, the inflation and restriction sequence, the long exact sequences, Shapiro's lemma, the periodic resolution of a finite cyclic group with its complete computation, and Tate cohomology as Mathlib defines it.

The chapter needs abelian categories and projective objects from Volume 25, Chapter 2. It uses homotopies of complexes (Chapter 3), projective resolutions, derived functors, Ext and Tor (Chapter 4), the long exact sequence of homology (Chapter 2), and the category of modules over a ring from Chapter 7. The examples use groups and their actions from Volume 5 and Galois theory from Volume 11.

The chapter closes the volume with a full family of computations that use each tool of the earlier chapters on concrete groups such as $\mathbb{Z}/n$ and the symmetric group $S_3$. Volume 29 on representation theory takes up the representations themselves, with induction, coinduction and characters. Group cohomology of Galois groups, with Tate cohomology and the Herbrand quotient, is the language of class field theory, and the computations of Section 3 for cyclic extensions are its first step.
