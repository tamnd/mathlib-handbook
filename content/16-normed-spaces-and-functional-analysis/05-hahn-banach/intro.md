# The theorem of Hahn-Banach

The dual space $E^*$ of a normed space was defined in Chapter 2. Without more work it could be the zero space. The theorem of Hahn-Banach shows that it is large: each bounded functional on a subspace extends to the whole space with the same norm. Section 1 proves the analytic form. A real linear functional dominated by a sublinear functional on a subspace extends to the whole space with the same domination, by Zorn's lemma and a one dimensional extension step. The complex form follows from the real form by the formula $f(x) = u(x) - i\,u(ix)$, which recovers a complex functional from its real part. Section 2 derives the consequences: each vector has a norming functional, the dual separates the points, the norm of a vector is a supremum over the unit ball of the dual, and the canonical map into the double dual is an isometry. Section 3 proves the geometric forms, which separate disjoint convex sets by closed hyperplanes, through the gauge of a convex open set.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 and 2, Zorn's lemma from Volume 4, Chapter 1, and the linear algebra of Volume 7. Section 2 uses the uniform boundedness principle of Chapter 4 once, for weakly bounded sets. Section 3 defines the convex sets that it needs. Chapter 7 proves the theorem of Riesz on the dual of a Hilbert space, which gives a direct proof of the theorem of Hahn-Banach in that case.
