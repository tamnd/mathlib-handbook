# Algebras, star rings and continued fractions

This chapter collects several topics that later volumes use. Section 1 defines an algebra over a commutative ring $R$: a ring $A$ with a ring homomorphism $R \to A$ whose image is central. It proves that this agrees with the definition through a module structure, shows that every ring is a $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra and every division ring of characteristic zero a $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra, proves the universal property of $R[X]$ as an algebra, and describes the subalgebra generated by one element. It explains why Mathlib's class `Algebra` holds the scalar multiplication as data. Section 2 defines star rings, with complex conjugation, the conjugate transpose and quaternion conjugation as examples, and it studies self-adjoint and normal elements and star-ordered rings. Section 3 develops simple continued fractions: the algorithm, the recurrences for the convergents, the determinant identity, the finite expansions of rational numbers, and the error bound that makes the convergents tend to the number. Section 4 treats the quadratic algebras $R[\omega]$ with $\omega^2 = a + b\omega$: their conjugation, norm and trace, their classification by the discriminant $b^2 + 4a$, their integral closures over $\mathbb{Z}$, and the quadratic formula for $a x^2 + b x + c$ in a field. Section 5 describes the quaternion algebras with general constants and the Hamilton quaternions, with the norm as a sum of four squares, the division ring over an ordered field, and the universal property through a basis $i, j, k$. Section 6 builds the trivial square-zero extension $R \oplus M$ of a ring by a bimodule, with its units, its powers, its universal property and the ideal $M$ of square zero. Sections 7 to 12 describe the library of algebras around Section 1. Section 7 gives the algebra isomorphisms and the basic lemmas on algebra maps. Section 8 treats products of algebras, towers of algebras and the restriction of scalars. Section 9 gives the constructions on subalgebras and the products of submodules of an algebra. Section 10 describes central algebras, non-associative algebras and the dual numbers. Section 11 defines non-unital algebras and the unitization that adds a unit. Section 12 defines the spectrum of an element of an algebra and its non-unital form, the quasispectrum.

The chapter needs rings, homomorphisms, subrings and division rings from Chapters 1 to 3, the polynomial ring of Chapter 5, and the ordered fields and the floor function of Chapter 7. Section 1 uses modules only through the definition of a scalar multiplication. Sections 2 and 3 can be read in either order after Section 1. Sections 4 and 5 use the star rings of Section 2, and Section 6 needs only Section 1. Sections 7, 8 and 10 need only Section 1, Section 9 also uses Section 2, Section 11 uses Section 8, and Section 12 uses Section 11.

Volume 7 on linear algebra uses algebras for matrices and endomorphisms, and the conjugate transpose of Section 2 for inner product spaces. Volume 9 on commutative algebra takes up algebras, adjoining elements and finitely generated algebras in full. Volume 16 builds C*-algebras on the star rings of Section 2. Continued fractions return in number theory, in the study of Pell's equation and of quadratic irrationals.
