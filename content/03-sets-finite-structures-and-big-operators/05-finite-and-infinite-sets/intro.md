# Finite and infinite sets

A statement often needs only the fact that a set is finite, not a finset that a program can use. The class `Finite` and the predicate `Set.Finite` give this fact as a proposition, and `Infinite` and `Set.Infinite` are their negations. This chapter shows how to prove that a set is finite or infinite, and how to count a set with no instance.

Section 1 gives the definitions, proves that a finite set has a unique finset with the same elements, and proves the closure rules for subsets, unions, intersections, images, products, preimages and power sets. It gives the three counts `Nat.card`, `Set.ncard` and `Set.encard`, and shows where the junk value $0$ of `ncard` on an infinite set needs care. Section 2 turns the hypothesis "infinite" into positive statements: an element outside each finset, finsets of each size, and an injection from $\mathbb{N}$. It proves that a set of natural numbers is infinite exactly when it is not bounded above, the theorem of Euclid on the primes, and the criterion of Dedekind by an injective map that is not surjective. Section 3 gives the next results on finite sets: finite ranges, maxima on a finite set, induction on a finite set, the finite power sets, the counts of unions, the exhaustions of a countable set by finite sets and the subsets of a given size.

The chapter needs Chapters 3 and 4. Chapter 7 uses finite sets for the support of a function, and Chapter 8 goes from the finite sets to the countable sets.
