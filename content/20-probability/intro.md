# Probability

This volume teaches probability theory on the foundation of measure theory, and its form in Mathlib. A probability space is a measure space of total mass $1$, a random variable is a measurable function, and the expectation is an integral. Kolmogorov fixed this frame in 1933, and Mathlib follows it without change. So the volume can use each theorem of Volume 18, Measure and integration: the monotone and dominated convergence theorems, Fubini's theorem, the $L^p$ spaces and the Radon-Nikodym theorem. It does not prove them again. Its own subject is what probability adds to measure theory: independence, the laws of the random variables, the limit theorems, conditioning, martingales and kernels.

Chapter 1 defines probability spaces, events, conditional probability and Bayes' formula, then random variables, their laws and their distribution functions. Chapter 2 defines independence for events, for σ-algebras and for random variables, builds infinite sequences of independent variables with the product measure, and proves the two Borel-Cantelli lemmas and Kolmogorov's zero-one law. Chapter 3 studies the expectation, the variance, the covariance, the moments and the moment generating function, with the inequalities of Markov, Chebyshev and Chernoff, and computes the variance of a binomial variable. Chapter 4 presents the main distributions: Bernoulli, binomial, geometric and Poisson in the discrete case, the uniform, exponential, gamma and Cauchy laws with a density, and the Gaussian law, also on Banach spaces and for processes. Chapter 5 proves the weak and the strong law of large numbers, with the strong law for coin tosses, and the central limit theorem through characteristic functions. These five chapters are the course of a first year of probability, and the undergraduate path U of the handbook ends with them.

Chapter 6 defines the conditional expectation with respect to a σ-algebra and conditional independence. Chapter 7 studies filtrations, stopping times and martingales: the optional stopping theorem with the gambler's ruin as its first application, Doob's maximal and upcrossing inequalities and the convergence theorems. Chapter 8 studies Markov kernels, their composition and products, the disintegration of a measure into conditional distributions, the Ionescu-Tulcea theorem for Markov chains on paths, Gaussian processes and Brownian motion as Mathlib has them, and the Radon-Nikodym derivative of kernels. Chapter 9 states statistical decision theory with kernels: the risk of an estimator, the Bayes and minimax risks, the data processing inequality and Bayes estimators.

The volume needs Volume 18 for measure and integration, Volume 15 for real analysis (the exponential function, limits and series), Volume 13 for the topology of limits and Volume 3 for finite sums. Mathlib puts probability in the namespace `ProbabilityTheory` and keeps the measure theory in `MeasureTheory`. A probability measure is a measure `μ` with the class `IsProbabilityMeasure μ`, not a new type, so each lemma about measures applies at once. The notation `ℙ` and `𝔼[X]` of `Mathlib.Probability.Notation` stands for the measure `volume` of a `MeasureSpace` and its integral. Mathlib states many theorems for kernels first and gets the results for measures as the case of a constant kernel. The text explains this choice where it appears. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
