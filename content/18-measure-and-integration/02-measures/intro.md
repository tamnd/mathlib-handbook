# Measures

A measure on a measurable space assigns to each measurable set a size in $[0, \infty]$, and it is countably additive: the size of a countable disjoint union is the sum of the sizes. This chapter defines measures, builds them, and gives the language of null sets in which the rest of the volume is written.

Section 1 defines measures and the type `ℝ≥0∞` of their values. It proves monotonicity, countable subadditivity, continuity from below and from above, and the first Borel-Cantelli lemma. It defines the Dirac and the counting measures, and the classes of finite, probability, s-finite and σ-finite measures. Section 2 constructs measures. An outer measure is defined on all subsets and is only subadditive; the theorem of Carathéodory says that its Carathéodory measurable sets form a σ-algebra on which it is a measure. With it, a countably additive function on a ring of sets extends to a measure. In Mathlib this construction is also the definition: a `Measure` is an outer measure with extra properties. Section 3 defines null sets, the filter `ae μ` of almost everywhere statements, null measurable sets and the completion of a measure, and almost everywhere measurable functions. It proves with the π-λ theorem of Chapter 1 that two finite measures that agree on a generating π-system are equal, and it defines the image and the restriction of a measure.

The chapter needs the σ-algebras and measurable functions of Chapter 1, the sets and countability of Volume 3, the complete lattices of Volume 4, and the filters of Volume 13. Chapter 3 uses Section 2 to construct the Lebesgue measure and Section 3 to prove that it is unique. Chapter 4 integrates against the measures of this chapter.
