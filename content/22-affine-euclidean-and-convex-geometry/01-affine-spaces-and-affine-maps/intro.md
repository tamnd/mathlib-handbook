# Affine spaces and affine maps

An affine space is a set of points on which a vector space acts by translation, freely and transitively. Two points determine the vector from one to the other, but no point is an origin. This chapter sets up this structure as Mathlib gives it and studies the maps that respect it.

Section 1 defines torsors and affine spaces, proves the rules of Chasles, and studies midpoints, including the fact that a map between groups that keeps midpoints is additive. It also treats ordered torsors. Section 2 defines affine maps through their linear part, the line maps, the homotheties and the affine equivalences. It proves that an affine map is injective, surjective or bijective exactly when its linear part is, and it describes the affine group. Section 3 adds a topology: continuous affine maps and equivalences, and closed affine subspaces.

The chapter needs linear algebra over a ring (Volume 7) and group actions (Volume 5). Section 3 also needs normed spaces (Volumes 13 and 16). Chapter 2 builds affine subspaces and affine independence on these definitions, and Chapters 3 and 6 to 8 use them for Euclidean and convex geometry.
