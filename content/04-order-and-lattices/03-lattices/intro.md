# Lattices

A lattice is a partial order in which any two elements have a least upper bound $a \sqcup b$ and a greatest lower bound $a \sqcap b$. The subsets of a set, the subspaces of a vector space, the subgroups of a group, the propositions and each linear order are lattices. The first three sections of this chapter give the theory, and the later sections are guides to more of the library.

Section 1 defines semilattices, lattices and bounded lattices, and it proves that a lattice is the same thing as a set with two operations that are commutative, associative and absorptive. It gives the standard examples, among them divisibility on $\mathbb{N}$ and the two small lattices $N_5$ and $M_3$. Section 2 studies distributive lattices, complements and Boolean algebras. It proves that complements are unique in a distributive lattice, derives the laws of De Morgan, and introduces Heyting algebras. Section 3 treats ideals and filters of an order, prime and maximal filters, and the ultrafilter lemma. It then explains the type `Filter α` of Mathlib as a lattice of families of sets with the reversed order. Section 4 describes the maps that preserve $\sqcup$, $\sqcap$, $\top$ and $\bot$, from `SupHom` to `BoundedLatticeHom`, and their extension to `WithTop` and `WithBot`. Section 5 describes atoms and coatoms, the atomic and atomistic lattices and the simple orders, which have exactly two elements. Section 6 treats the Boolean subalgebras of a Boolean algebra, the Booleanisation of a generalized Boolean algebra, and the theorem of Birkhoff that a finite distributive lattice is a lattice of lower sets. Section 7 describes Heyting, co-Heyting and bi-Heyting algebras, the boundary of an element, the regular elements and the maps that preserve the Heyting operations. Section 8 describes sup-closed sets, the bundled sublattices with their images and preimages, the lattice structure of intervals and the congruences of a lattice. Section 9 treats modular lattices and the diamond isomorphism, independent families of elements, sup-irreducible and sup-prime elements and the radical of a complete lattice. Section 10 describes the complete lattice of equivalence relations on a type, its isomorphism with the lattice of partitions, and the finite partitions and equipartitions of a finite set.

The chapter needs the partial orders of Chapter 1, and Section 3 uses Zorn's lemma from Section 4 of Chapter 1. Chapter 4 extends the lattices of this chapter to complete lattices, in which each set has a supremum. Chapter 6 uses the lattice operations for Galois connections, and Volume 13 builds limits and topology on the filters of Section 3.
