# Automation

This chapter is about the tactics that search for a proof. The tactics `exact?` and `apply?` search the library for one lemma that fits the goal. The tactic `aesop` applies rules from a rule set again and again, in a search tree. The tactics `fun_prop` and `continuity` prove the properties of a function, such as continuity, from the structure of the function. The tactic `grind` combines congruence closure, case splits and procedures for arithmetic.

A search tactic can fail on a true goal, and it can take a long time. Use it to find a proof, and then write the proof that it found into the file: `exact?` and `aesop?` print a script for this. The sections explain the method of each search, so that the reader can tell from the goal whether a search can succeed, and can read the message of a failure. The first section proves the soundness of `apply` and the theorem that safe rules lose no proof, which is the reason for the order of the search of `aesop`. The second section proves the continuity of a sum and of a quotient, the two kinds of steps of `fun_prop`, and the soundness of congruence closure.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 to 3, because `aesop` calls `simp_all`, and `grind` has procedures of the kind of `ring` and `linarith`. Chapter 5 gives the tactics that a search does not replace: the introduction of variables, the case splits on a hypothesis and the induction.
