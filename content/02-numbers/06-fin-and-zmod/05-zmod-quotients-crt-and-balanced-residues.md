## ZMod as a quotient, the Chinese remainder isomorphism and balanced residues

### Overview

The first section of this chapter defines `ZMod n` by cases on $n$, and not as a quotient of `ℤ`. This section collects the files that connect `ZMod n` to the other descriptions of $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$, and the files that give more structure to it.

The file `Mathlib.Data.ZMod.QuotientRing` gives the ring isomorphism between the quotient ring $\mathbb{Z}/(n)$ and `ZMod n`, and the Chinese remainder theorem as a ring isomorphism for a finite family of pairwise coprime moduli. The file `Mathlib.Data.ZMod.QuotientGroup` gives the same isomorphism for the quotient group of `ℤ` by the subgroup $n\mathbb{Z}$, and uses `ZMod` to describe the orbit of a point under the powers of one group element.

The file `Mathlib.Data.ZMod.Units` studies the map $(\mathbb{Z}/m)^\times \to (\mathbb{Z}/n)^\times$ for $n \mid m$ and proves that it is surjective when $m \neq 0$. The file `Mathlib.Data.ZMod.Aut` identifies the automorphisms of the additive group of `ZMod n` with the units of `ZMod n`.

The file `Mathlib.Data.ZMod.ValMinAbs` defines the balanced residue: the representative of a class in the interval $(-n/2, n/2]$. The file `Mathlib.Data.ZMod.IntUnitsPower` lets the elements of `ZMod 2` act as exponents on the signs $\pm 1$.

### The library around it

#### ZMod n as a quotient of ℤ

The ring isomorphism `Int.quotientSpanNatEquivZMod n : ℤ ⧸ Ideal.span {↑n} ≃+* ZMod n` identifies the quotient by the ideal $(n)$ with `ZMod n`. For an integer $a$, `Int.quotientSpanEquivZMod a` has the target `ZMod a.natAbs`, because $(a) = (-a)$. The lemma `Int.quotientSpanNatEquivZMod_comp_Quotient_mk` says that the isomorphism, after the quotient map, is the cast `Int.castRingHom (ZMod n)`. So an argument that works in a general quotient ring can move to `ZMod n` without a change in the elements. The instance `Int.instFiniteQuotientIdealSpanSingletonSetOfNeZero` says that the quotient is finite when $a \neq 0$.

*Proof.* The cast $\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/n$ is a surjective ring map. Its kernel is the set of $k$ with $n \mid k$, which is the ideal $(n)$. The first isomorphism theorem gives the isomorphism.

The additive form is `Int.quotientZMultiplesNatEquivZMod n : ℤ ⧸ AddSubgroup.zmultiples ↑n ≃+ ZMod n`, with `Int.quotientZMultiplesEquivZMod` for an integer. The lemma `Int.index_zmultiples` says that the index of $a\mathbb{Z}$ in `ℤ` is $|a|$. The lemma `Int.relIndex_zmultiples_mul` gives the index of $a\mathbb{Z} \cap b\mathbb{Z}$ in $b\mathbb{Z}$ for natural numbers $a$ and $b$: it is $a / \gcd(a, b)$, in the form of a product equal to $a$.

#### Orbits of a cyclic group

Let a group act on a set, and let $g$ be an element and $b$ a point. The orbit of $b$ under the powers of $g$ is the set $\{g^k b : k \in \mathbb{Z}\}$. The equivalence `MulAction.orbitZPowersEquiv g b` identifies this orbit with `ZMod` of the minimal period of $b$ under the map $x \mapsto g \cdot x$. The lemma `MulAction.orbitZPowersEquiv_symm_apply` says that the class of $k$ goes to $g^k b$. If the orbit is finite, `MulAction.minimalPeriod_eq_card` says that the minimal period is the number of points in the orbit, and `MulAction.minimalPeriod_pos` says that it is not zero.

*Proof sketch.* The map $k \mapsto g^k b$ from `ℤ` onto the orbit is surjective. Two integers $k$ and $l$ give the same point if and only if $g^{k-l}$ fixes $b$, and the set of these exponents is the subgroup $d\mathbb{Z}$, where $d$ is the minimal period. So the orbit is in bijection with $\mathbb{Z}/d$.

The same file gives `MulAction.zpowersQuotientStabilizerEquiv`, `Nat.card_zpowers` (the cyclic subgroup generated by $g$ has `orderOf g` elements), `finite_zpowers` and `infinite_zpowers`. The equivalence `Subgroup.quotientEquivSigmaZMod` splits $G / H$ into the orbits of the powers of $g$, and `Subgroup.index_eq_sum_minimalPeriod` writes the index of $H$ as the sum of the minimal periods. Each name has an additive form with `AddAction` and the word zmultiples in place of zpowers, for example `AddAction.orbitZMultiplesEquiv` and `Nat.card_zmultiples`.

#### The Chinese remainder isomorphism for many moduli

Chapter 3 gives the Chinese remainder theorem for two coprime moduli, and the ring isomorphism `ZMod.chineseRemainder` gives it in `ZMod`. The isomorphism `ZMod.prodEquivPi` extends it to a finite family. For a `Fintype` index type $\iota$, a function `a : ι → ℕ` and a proof that the values are pairwise coprime, it is a ring isomorphism from `ZMod (∏ i, a i)` to the product of the rings `ZMod (a i)`. The lemma `ZMod.prodEquivPi_apply` says that the component $i$ of the image of $x$ is the cast of $x$ to `ZMod (a i)`.

*Proof for positive moduli.* Let $N = \prod_i a_i$ with all $a_i > 0$. The map $x \mapsto (x \bmod a_i)_i$ is a ring map from $\mathbb{Z}/N$, because each $a_i$ divides $N$. If $x$ goes to $0$, then each $a_i$ divides $x$. The $a_i$ are pairwise coprime, so their product divides $x$. This is an induction on the family: at each step the new factor is coprime to the product of the others, by `Nat.Coprime.prod_right`, and the product of two coprime divisors of $x$ divides $x$. So $x = 0$ in $\mathbb{Z}/N$, and the map is injective. The two rings have $N$ elements, so the map is bijective. If one $a_i$ is $0$, every other $a_j$ is coprime to $0$, so $a_j = 1$ and `ZMod 1` has one element. The isomorphism is then $\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}$ times a product of zero rings.

The isomorphism `ZMod.equivPi n h`, for $n \neq 0$, applies this to the prime power factors. It is a ring isomorphism from `ZMod n` to the product over $p$ in `n.primeFactors` of `ZMod (p ^ n.factorization p)`. With this isomorphism, a question about $\mathbb{Z}/n$ becomes a question about the rings $\mathbb{Z}/p^k$.

#### Units and the reduction map

For $n \mid m$, the reduction map on units is `ZMod.unitsMap h : (ZMod m)ˣ →* (ZMod n)ˣ`. The lemma `ZMod.unitsMap_val` says that it is the cast on the underlying elements. The lemmas `ZMod.unitsMap_self` and `ZMod.unitsMap_comp` say that it is the identity for $n = m$ and that two reductions in sequence are one reduction. The theorem `ZMod.unitsMap_surjective` says that every unit mod $n$ lifts to a unit mod $m$, under `NeZero m`.

*Proof.* Let $x$ be a natural number coprime to $n$. Let $t$ be the product of the primes $p \mid m$ with $p \nmid x$. Put $y = x + t n$. Let $p$ be a prime that divides $m$. If $p \mid x$, then $p \nmid n$, because $\gcd(x, n) = 1$, and $p \nmid t$, because $t$ is a product of other primes. So $p \nmid tn$ and $p \nmid y$. If $p \nmid x$, then $p \mid t$, so $p \mid tn$ and again $p \nmid y$. So $y$ is coprime to $m$, and $y \equiv x \pmod n$. The class of $y$ is a unit mod $m$ that reduces to the class of $x$.

The same file works with integers and `IsCoprime`. The unit `ZMod.unitOfIsCoprime a h` is the class of an integer $a$ with `IsCoprime a ↑m`, and `ZMod.coe_unitOfIsCoprime` gives its value. The lemma `ZMod.isUnit_intCast_iff_isCoprime` says that the class of $a$ is a unit if and only if `IsCoprime (↑m) a`. The lemmas `ZMod.intCast_mul_inv_eq_one` and `ZMod.intCast_inv_mul_eq_one` say that the inverse of `ZMod` is the true inverse on these classes. The lemma `ZMod.not_isUnit_of_mem_primeFactors` says that a prime factor of $n$ is not a unit mod $n$.

#### Automorphisms of the additive group

The equivalence `ZMod.AddAutEquivUnits n : AddAut (ZMod n) ≃+ Additive (ZMod n)ˣ` sends an additive automorphism $f$ to the unit $f(1)$. Its inverse sends a unit $u$ to the map $x \mapsto u x$. Composition of automorphisms goes to multiplication of units.

*Proof.* An additive map $f$ satisfies $f(k \cdot 1) = k f(1)$ for all integers $k$, so $f(x) = f(1) x$ for every $x$. So $f$ is multiplication by $f(1)$. This map is bijective if and only if $f(1)$ is a unit, and $(f \circ g)(1) = f(1) g(1)$.

#### Balanced residues

For $x$ in `ZMod n`, `ZMod.val x` is the representative in $[0, n)$. The integer `ZMod.valMinAbs x` is the representative in $(-n/2, n/2]$. The lemma `ZMod.valMinAbs_spec` characterizes it, under `NeZero n`: `x.valMinAbs = y` if and only if `x = ↑y` and $2y \in (-n, n]$. The lemma `ZMod.valMinAbs_def_pos` gives the formula: the value is `x.val` if `x.val ≤ n / 2`, and `x.val - n` in the other case. The lemma `ZMod.coe_valMinAbs` says that the cast of `x.valMinAbs` back to `ZMod n` is $x$, so `ZMod.valMinAbs_inj` and `ZMod.injective_valMinAbs` hold.

The absolute value is the distance to the nearest multiple of $n$. The lemma `ZMod.valMinAbs_natAbs_eq_min` says that `x.valMinAbs.natAbs = min x.val (n - x.val)`, and `ZMod.natAbs_valMinAbs_le` gives the bound $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$. The lemmas `ZMod.natAbs_valMinAbs_neg` and `ZMod.natAbs_valMinAbs_eq_natAbs_valMinAbs` say that $x$ and $-x$ have the same absolute value, and that only they do. The lemma `ZMod.valMinAbs_neg_of_ne_half` says that the balanced residue of $-x$ is the negative of the residue of $x$, except when $2 \cdot$ `x.val` $= n$. For casts of natural numbers, `ZMod.valMinAbs_natCast_of_le_half` and `ZMod.valMinAbs_natCast_of_half_lt` give the two cases.

For example, in `ZMod 7` the class of $5$ has the value $5$ under `ZMod.val` and the value $-2$ under `ZMod.valMinAbs`. In `ZMod 6` the class of $3$ has the balanced residue to $3$, not $-3$, because the interval is closed on the right.

#### ZMod 2 as exponents of signs

The units of `ℤ` are $\pm 1$, and $s^2 = 1$ for each of them. So the power $s^k$ only depends on $k \bmod 2$. The file `Mathlib.Data.ZMod.IntUnitsPower` makes `Additive ℤˣ` a module over `ZMod 2`, with the instance `instModuleZModOfNatNatAdditiveUnitsInt`. From any such module structure over a commutative semiring $R$, the instance `Int.instUnitsPow` defines a power $s^r$ for $s : ℤˣ$ and $r : R$. The usual laws hold: `uzpow_add` gives $s^{x+y} = s^x s^y$, `uzpow_mul` gives $s^{xy} = (s^x)^y$, and `mul_uzpow`, `uzpow_neg` and `uzpow_sub` give the others. The lemmas `uzpow_natCast` and `uzpow_intCast` say that the new power agrees with the usual power at casts of natural numbers and integers. The lemma `ZMod.smul_units_def` says that $z \cdot u$ is `z.val • u` for $z$ in `ZMod 2`. A sign of the form $(-1)^{k}$ with $k$ in `ZMod 2` then has a meaning, without a choice of representative.

#### Common errors

1. `ZMod.unitsMap_surjective` needs `NeZero m`. For $m = 0$ the source is the units of `ℤ`, which are $\pm 1$, and the map to the units of `ZMod 5` is not surjective, because the class of $2$ is not $\pm 1$.
2. The argument order in `ZMod.isUnit_intCast_iff_isCoprime` is `IsCoprime (↑m) a`, with the modulus first, but `ZMod.unitOfIsCoprime` takes `IsCoprime a ↑m`. Use `IsCoprime.symm` to change one into the other.
3. `ZMod.valMinAbs` is not an odd function in all cases. When $n$ is even and $2 \cdot$ `x.val` $= n$, both $x$ and $-x$ have the balanced residue $n/2$. So the hypothesis of `ZMod.valMinAbs_neg_of_ne_half` is necessary.

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute `ZMod.valMinAbs` for each class of `ZMod 8`, and check the bound of `ZMod.natAbs_valMinAbs_le` for each value.
2. (A) Use `ZMod.prodEquivPi` with the family $3, 4, 5$ to find the element of `ZMod 60` that goes to $(1, 2, 3)$.
3. (B) Prove `ZMod.valMinAbs_natAbs_eq_min` from `ZMod.valMinAbs_def_pos`, with a case split on `x.val ≤ n / 2`.
4. (B) Find a unit of `ZMod 12` that lifts the class of $5$ in `ZMod 4`, by the method of the proof of `ZMod.unitsMap_surjective`, and check the result.
5. (B) Use `ZMod.AddAutEquivUnits` and `ZMod.card_units_eq_totient` to count the additive automorphisms of `ZMod 12`, and list them as maps $x \mapsto ux$.
6. (C) Let a group $G$ act on a finite set $X$, and let $g \in G$. Use `MulAction.orbitZPowersEquiv` to prove that the order of the permutation $x \mapsto g \cdot x$ of $X$ is the lcm of the sizes of the orbits of the powers of $g$.
