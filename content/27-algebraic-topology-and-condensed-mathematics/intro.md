# Algebraic topology and condensed mathematics

Algebraic topology assigns algebraic objects to spaces. A continuous map gives a homomorphism of groups, and two spaces with different groups cannot be homeomorphic. The circle and the plane are both connected, but the fundamental group of the circle is ℤ and the fundamental group of the plane is trivial, so the two spaces are different. This volume develops the tools of the subject in their form in Mathlib: homotopy and the fundamental group, covering spaces, simplicial sets, homology, and the abstract homotopy theory of model categories. The last two chapters treat the categories of spaces and the condensed sets of Clausen and Scholze, a new foundation for the algebra of topological objects.

Chapter 1 defines homotopies, homotopy equivalences, the fundamental groupoid and the higher homotopy groups. Chapter 2 treats covering maps and the lifting of paths, and it proves that the fundamental group of the circle is ℤ. Chapter 3 gives the simplex category, simplicial objects and simplicial sets, with the standard simplices, horns and nondegenerate simplices. Chapter 4 treats nerves, the homotopy category of a simplicial set, quasicategories, Kan complexes and anodyne extensions. Chapter 5 defines the chain complexes of simplicial objects, singular homology and the axioms of Eilenberg and Steenrod, and it proves the Dold-Kan correspondence between simplicial objects and chain complexes.

Chapter 6 gives model categories, cylinder and path objects, the homotopy category of bifibrant objects, and the cell complexes of Whitehead. Chapter 7 studies the categories of spaces in Mathlib: topological spaces, compact Hausdorff spaces, profinite and Stonean spaces, and the theorem of Nöbeling that the locally constant functions with values in ℤ on a profinite set form a free abelian group. Chapter 8 defines condensed sets and condensed modules as sheaves on compact Hausdorff spaces, compares them with topological spaces, and treats the light condensed sets.

The volume needs Volume 13 for topological spaces, Volume 24 for categories, limits and adjunctions, Volume 25 for abelian categories and sheaves, and Volume 26 for chain complexes and homology. Chapter 7 uses ordinals from Volume 31. Chapters 1 and 2 form one path, Chapters 3 to 6 form a second path, and Chapters 7 and 8 form a third. Chapter 5 uses the topological simplex and the singular set, which join the first two paths. The level is that of a graduate course that leads to research. The references are Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, Goerss and Jardine, *Simplicial Homotopy Theory*, Hovey, *Model Categories*, and the lecture notes of Scholze on condensed mathematics.

The conventions are those of Mathlib. A homotopy is a map from the product of the unit interval I with the space, with the interval as the first factor. A simplicial object is a functor from the opposite of the simplex category. Many objects of this volume are categories, so the boxes give the names of the categories and of their functors. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises.
