# Inner product spaces

An inner product gives a normed space a geometry with angles and right angles. This chapter studies the spaces whose norm comes from an inner product. Section 1 defines the inner product over $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ in the convention of Mathlib, which is conjugate linear in the first argument, and proves the inequality of Cauchy-Schwarz, the parallelogram law and the polarization identities. It proves the theorem of Jordan and von Neumann in the real case: a norm comes from an inner product if and only if it satisfies the parallelogram law. Section 2 studies orthogonality: the theorem of Pythagoras, orthonormal families, the inequality of Bessel, the process of Gram-Schmidt and orthonormal bases in finite dimension. Section 3 adds completeness. A Hilbert space has a Hilbert basis by Zorn's lemma, each vector is the sum of its expansion in the basis, and the identity of Parseval makes each Hilbert space isometric to a space $\ell^2(\iota)$. Section 4 collects the constructions of new inner product spaces from old ones: subspaces, products, quotients, completions, tensor products and exterior powers, together with the Gram matrix. Section 5 studies calculus with the inner product: the derivatives of the norm, the Laplacian, harmonic functions and the Bessel potential spaces. Section 6 defines the volume form of an oriented inner product space, the area form and the rotation of an oriented plane, and the norm determinant of a linear map.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 to 3 and the linear algebra of Volume 7. Section 3 also uses the series of Chapter 1, Section 3, and Zorn's lemma from Volume 4. Chapter 7 uses all three sections for the orthogonal projection and the theorem of Riesz. Section 5 also uses the derivatives of Volume 17, and Section 6 uses the alternating forms of Volume 8.
