# The exponential, the logarithm and real powers

The exponential function is the base of the elementary functions. This chapter defines it by its power series, defines the logarithm as its inverse, and defines the real power $x^y$ as $\exp(y \log x)$. Every property of the three functions comes from a few facts about the series: the functional equation $\exp(x + y) = \exp(x)\exp(y)$, the inequality $1 + x \le \exp(x)$, and the derivative $\exp' = \exp$.

Section 1 defines $\exp$ and proves the functional equation with the Cauchy product of series. It proves that $\exp$ is positive, strictly increasing and its own derivative, and that it is a bijection from $\mathbb{R}$ onto $(0, \infty)$. It computes $e$ to eight decimal places and proves $(1 + 1/n)^n \to e$. Section 2 defines $\log$, proves $\log(xy) = \log x + \log y$, the derivative $1/x$, the inequality $\log x \le x - 1$ and the slow growth $\log x / x \to 0$, and applies them to the harmonic numbers and to the function $x \log x$. Section 3 defines real powers, proves the laws of exponents with their hypotheses, computes the derivatives of $x^a$ and $a^x$, finds the minimum of $x^x$, and proves the inequality of arithmetic and geometric means. Section 4 extends real powers to `NNReal`, `ENNReal` and the complex numbers, with the exact hypotheses of the laws of exponents in each type, and adds the integer root `Nat.nthRoot`, the derivative of the square root and the inequalities between power means. Section 5 treats functions built from the logarithm: $-x \log x$ and the entropy functions, the positive part $\log^+$, the functions $1/\log x$ and $\log \log x$, sums of logarithms, infinite products through series of logarithms, and the logarithm on $[0, \infty]$.

In Mathlib the three functions are total: `Real.log` and `Real.rpow` have values at $0$ and at negative numbers, and these values are junk values or conventions. Each section lists these values and shows how they decide the hypotheses of the lemmas.

The chapter needs the series and the comparison tests of Chapter 1, the limits, continuity and the intermediate value theorem of Chapter 2, and the derivative, the chain rule and the mean value theorem of Chapter 3. It uses the real numbers of Volume 2 and the language of filters of Volume 13 for the limits in Mathlib. Chapter 4 gives sharper bounds for $\exp$ and $\log$ with Taylor's theorem. Chapter 6 defines the sine and the cosine from the complex exponential and proves that $e$ is irrational. Chapter 7 compares the growth of $\exp$, of powers and of $\log$ in the language of $o$ and $O$, and proves Stirling's formula for $n!$.
