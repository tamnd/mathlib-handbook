# Ordered groups and the absolute value

An ordered group is a group with an order that addition keeps: from $a \le b$ follows $c + a \le c + b$. The integers, the rationals and the reals are ordered groups, and most computations with inequalities use only this axiom.

The first section defines the ordered monoids and the ordered groups through the Mathlib mixins `IsOrderedAddMonoid` and `IsOrderedCancelAddMonoid`. It proves from the axioms that inequalities add, that $a \le b$ exactly when $0 \le b - a$, and that negation reverses the order, and it shows that the positive cone determines the order. The second section defines the absolute value $|a| = a \sqcup (-a)$ and the positive and negative parts, and it proves the triangle inequality, the reverse triangle inequality and the decomposition $a = a^+ - a^-$.

The third section explains the covariant and contravariant classes, such as `MulLeftMono`, from which most lemmas about inequalities take their hypotheses, and how to read their names. The fourth section collects the inequalities of ordered groups: moving terms across an inequality, bounds, densely ordered groups, lattice-ordered groups and cones. The fifth section treats the canonically ordered monoids, where $a \le b$ means $b = a + c$, and the truncated subtraction of $\mathbb{N}$ and $[0, \infty]$. The sixth section extends the addition and the order to `WithTop α` and `WithBot α`, the types of the extended reals and of the degree of a polynomial. The seventh section gives the ordered monoid structure of products, function types, order duals, lexicographic orders, opposites, units and submonoids. The eighth section applies the theory to the integers, the natural numbers, multisets, finite suprema, indicator functions and partial suprema.

The chapter needs the orders of Chapter 1, the monotone maps of Chapter 2, the lattices of Chapter 3, and the class hierarchy of Volume 1. The last section also uses the partial suprema of Chapter 4. The absolute value and the distance $|a - b|$ are the start of the metric spaces and of the estimates of analysis in later volumes.
