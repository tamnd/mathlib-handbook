## Modules over a polynomial ring

### Overview

A module over $R[X]$ is an $R$-module $M$ together with one $R$-linear endomorphism, the action of $X$. Conversely each endomorphism $\varphi$ of an $R$-module makes $M$ an $R[X]$-module, with $f \cdot m = f(\varphi)(m)$. This change of view turns questions about one linear map into questions about modules over a ring with good properties. Over a field $k$, the ring $k[X]$ is a principal ideal domain, and the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over it gives the rational canonical form and the Jordan form.

Mathlib makes this construction a type, because the module structure depends on $\varphi$ and a type can carry only one instance. The file `Mathlib.Algebra.Polynomial.Module.AEval` defines the type `Module.AEval R M a` for an element $a$ of an $R$-algebra acting on $M$. The file `Mathlib.Algebra.Polynomial.Module.FiniteDimensional` proves that it is a torsion module in finite dimension. The file `Mathlib.Algebra.Polynomial.Module.Basic` defines a second $R[X]$-module, the module $M[X]$ of polynomials with coefficients in $M$, and the file `Mathlib.Algebra.Polynomial.Module.TensorProduct` identifies it with $R[X] \otimes_R M$.

### The library around it

#### The module of an endomorphism

Let $A$ be an $R$-algebra, $M$ an $A$-module that is also an $R$-module with compatible scalars, and $a \in A$. The type `Module.AEval R M a` is a copy of $M$. The linear equivalence `Module.AEval.of` $R$ $M$ $a$ from $M$ to the copy marks the passage between the two views. The instance `Module.AEval.instModulePolynomial` gives the copy the $R[X]$-module structure with $f \cdot m = f(a) \cdot m$, where $f(a)$ is `Polynomial.aeval` $a$ $f$. The defining rule is `Module.AEval.of_aeval_smul`: the image of $f(a) \cdot m$ is $f$ times the image of $m$. The special cases are `Module.AEval.X_smul_of`, which says that $X$ acts as $a$, and `Module.AEval.C_smul`, which says that a constant $C\,t$ acts as the scalar $t$. Powers of $X$ act as powers of $a$ by `Module.AEval.X_pow_smul_of`.

The most common case is $A = $ `Module.End R M` and $a = \varphi$. The abbreviation `Module.AEval'` $\varphi$ is `Module.AEval R M φ`, with the equivalence `Module.AEval'.of` $\varphi$. Then `Module.AEval'.X_smul_of` says $X \cdot m = \varphi(m)$. So a statement about the $R[X]$-module is a statement about $\varphi$, and the equivalence moves it back.

Proof of `Module.AEval.X_smul_of`: by the definition of the action, $X \cdot m$ is the image of $X(a) \cdot m$, and $X(a) = a$ by `Polynomial.aeval_X`. The same argument with `Polynomial.aeval_C` gives `Module.AEval.C_smul`.

#### Annihilators and torsion

The annihilator of the $R[X]$-module is the ideal of polynomials that kill $a$. This is `Module.AEval.annihilator_eq_ker_aeval`: if $A$ acts faithfully on $M$, then `Module.annihilator` of the module is `RingHom.ker` of `Polynomial.aeval` $a$. Proof: $f$ is in the annihilator when $f(a) \cdot m = 0$ for all $m$. Faithfulness says that an element of $A$ that kills all of $M$ is zero, so this holds exactly when $f(a) = 0$. For $A = $ `Module.End R M` the action is faithful, so the annihilator is the ideal of polynomials $f$ with $f(\varphi) = 0$. Over a field this ideal is generated by the minimal polynomial of $\varphi$.

The torsion results are in `Mathlib.Algebra.Polynomial.Module.FiniteDimensional`. The lemma `Module.AEval.isTorsion_of_aeval_eq_zero` says that the module is torsion over $R[X]$ when a nonzero polynomial $p$ with $p(a) = 0$ exists and $R$ has no zero divisors. Proof: over such an $R$, the nonzero $p$ is not a zero divisor of $R[X]$, and $p \cdot m = p(a) \cdot m = 0$ for every $m$. The lemma `Module.AEval.isTorsion_of_finiteDimensional` applies this when $A$ is finite dimensional over a field $K$: the element $a$ has a nonzero minimal polynomial `minpoly` $K$ $a$ by `minpoly.ne_zero_of_finite`, and it kills $a$ by `minpoly.aeval`. So the $K[X]$-module of an endomorphism of a finite dimensional space is a finitely generated torsion module, and the structure theorem writes it as a sum of cyclic modules $K[X]/(f_i)$. This is the module theoretic form of the rational canonical form.

#### Finiteness, submodules and maps

If $M$ is finite over $R$, it is finite over $R[X]$: the instances `Module.AEval.instFinitePolynomial` and `Module.instFinitePolynomialAEval'` give this. Proof: generators over $R$ are also generators over $R[X]$, because $R$ acts through the constants.

The $R[X]$-submodules are the $a$-invariant $R$-submodules. The order isomorphism `Module.AEval.mapSubmodule` goes from the invariant submodules of the action of $a$, the lattice `Module.End.invtSubmodule`, to the $R[X]$-submodules of `Module.AEval R M a`. Proof: an $R[X]$-submodule is an $R$-submodule closed under the action of $X$, which is $a$. Conversely an $R$-submodule closed under $a$ is closed under every $f(a)$, because $f(a)$ is a linear combination of powers of $a$. The membership rule is `Module.AEval.mem_mapSubmodule_apply`. The restriction of $a$ to an invariant submodule gives a module of the same kind, and `Module.AEval.restrict_equiv_mapSubmodule` identifies it with the corresponding $R[X]$-submodule.

Maps out of the module have a simple test. For an $R[X]$-module $N$ and an $R$-linear $f \colon M \to N$ with $f(a \cdot m) = X \cdot f(m)$ for all $m$, `LinearMap.ofAEval` is $f$ as an $R[X]$-linear map. Proof: the condition says that $f$ commutes with the action of $X$. Then $f$ commutes with the action of $X^n$ by induction, with the constants by $R$-linearity, and with every polynomial by additivity. The bijective version is `LinearEquiv.ofAEval`. In particular two endomorphisms $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are conjugate exactly when their $R[X]$-modules are isomorphic, since a linear isomorphism $g$ with $g \varphi = \psi g$ satisfies the condition.

#### The module of polynomials with coefficients in a module

The type `PolynomialModule R M` is $M[X]$, the finitely supported sequences in $M$ indexed by $\mathbb{N}$. It is a one field structure with the constructor `PolynomialModule.ofCoeff` and the field `PolynomialModule.coeff`, and the linear equivalence with `ℕ →₀ M` is `PolynomialModule.coeffLinearEquiv`. The element `PolynomialModule.single` $R$ $n$ $m$ is $m X^n$, with the linear form `PolynomialModule.lsingle` and the coefficients `PolynomialModule.coeff_single`. The induction principle `PolynomialModule.induction_linear` reduces a statement to $0$, sums and single terms, and `PolynomialModule.hom_ext` says that a linear map out of $M[X]$ is fixed by its composites with each `PolynomialModule.lsingle`.

The module structure over $R[X]$ is `PolynomialModule.polynomialModule`. A polynomial acts by the convolution of coefficients, `PolynomialModule.smul_apply`: the coefficient of $X^n$ in $f \cdot g$ is $\sum_{i + j = n} f_i \cdot g_j$. On single terms this is `PolynomialModule.monomial_smul_single`: $(r X^i) \cdot (m X^j) = (r m) X^{i+j}$. In the library the structure is the module of the shift endomorphism $m X^n \mapsto m X^{n+1}$, in the sense of the previous part; `PolynomialModule.smul_def` states this. Proof of `PolynomialModule.monomial_smul_single`: the polynomial $r X^i$ acts as $r$ times the $i$th power of the shift, and the $i$th power of the shift sends $m X^j$ to $m X^{i+j}$.

Evaluation `PolynomialModule.eval` $r$ sends $\sum m_n X^n$ to $\sum r^n m_n \in M$, so `PolynomialModule.eval_single` gives $r^i m$. It is compatible with the action: `PolynomialModule.eval_smul` says that $(f \cdot g)(r) = f(r) \cdot g(r)$. Proof: both sides are additive in $f$ and in $g$, so it is enough to take $f = s X^i$ and $g = m X^j$. Then the left side is $r^{i+j} s m$ by `PolynomialModule.monomial_smul_single` and `PolynomialModule.eval_single`, and the right side is $s r^i \cdot r^j m$. Composition `PolynomialModule.comp` $p$ substitutes $p$ for $X$, with `PolynomialModule.comp_single` on single terms, and `PolynomialModule.comp_eval` says that $(q \circ p)(r) = q(p(r))$. A linear map $M \to M'$ gives the map `PolynomialModule.map` on coefficients.

When $M$ is an $R$-algebra $S$, the module $S[X]$ in this sense is the polynomial ring over $S$. The linear equivalence `PolynomialModule.equivPolynomial` identifies `PolynomialModule R S` with `Polynomial S` and sends $x X^n$ to the monomial $x X^n$ (`PolynomialModule.equivPolynomial_single`). For $S = R$, the equivalence `PolynomialModule.equivPolynomialSelf` is linear over $R[X]$. Evaluation matches through it: `PolynomialModule.aeval_equivPolynomial` compares `Polynomial.aeval` with `PolynomialModule.eval`.

#### The tensor product

The file `Mathlib.Algebra.Polynomial.Module.TensorProduct` has one definition. The equivalence `PolynomialModule.polynomialTensorProductLEquivPolynomialModule` is an isomorphism of $R[X]$-modules between `TensorProduct R (Polynomial R) M` and `PolynomialModule R M`. It sends $X^n \otimes m$ to $m X^n$. Proof: $R[X]$ is free over $R$ with basis $X^n$, so $R[X] \otimes_R M$ is the direct sum of copies $X^n \otimes M$, which is $M[X]$ as an $R$-module. The two actions of $X$ shift the index on both sides, so the map is $R[X]$-linear. So $M[X]$ is the extension of scalars of $M$ from $R$ to $R[X]$, and base change results for tensor products apply to it.

#### Common errors

1. Putting the $R[X]$-module structure on $M$ itself. Two endomorphisms would give two instances on one type. Use `Module.AEval'` $\varphi$, and move elements with `Module.AEval'.of` $\varphi$ and its inverse.
2. Forgetting the faithfulness hypothesis of `Module.AEval.annihilator_eq_ker_aeval`. For a general algebra element only the inclusion of the kernel in the annihilator holds; for an endomorphism the action of `Module.End R M` on $M$ is faithful and the hypothesis is found by instance search.
3. Confusing `PolynomialModule R M` with `Polynomial M`. The first one needs only a module $M$ and carries the action of $R[X]$; the second needs a semiring and has a product. For an algebra the two agree through `PolynomialModule.equivPolynomial`.

### Exercises

1. (A) For $\varphi = 0$ on $M$, describe the action of a polynomial $f$ on `Module.AEval'` $\varphi$ with `Module.AEval.of_aeval_smul`.
2. (A) Compute $(X^2 + 1) \cdot (m X)$ in `PolynomialModule R M` with `PolynomialModule.monomial_smul_single`.
3. (B) Show that the annihilator of `Module.AEval'` $\varphi$ for a projection $\varphi^2 = \varphi$ on a nonzero space $V$ with $\varphi \ne 0, 1$ is the ideal $(X^2 - X)$.
4. (B) Use `Module.AEval.mapSubmodule` to show that the kernel of $\varphi$ is an $R[X]$-submodule of `Module.AEval'` $\varphi$.
5. (B) Prove with `LinearEquiv.ofAEval` that conjugate endomorphisms give isomorphic $R[X]$-modules.
6. (C) Deduce from `Module.AEval.isTorsion_of_finiteDimensional` and the structure theorem over a principal ideal domain that every endomorphism of a nonzero finite dimensional space has an invariant subspace of dimension $1$ or $2$ over $\mathbb{R}$.
