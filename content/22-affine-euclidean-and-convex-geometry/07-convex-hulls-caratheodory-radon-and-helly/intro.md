# Convex hulls and the theorems of Caratheodory, Radon and Helly

The convex hull of a set is the smallest convex set that contains it. From outside, it is the intersection of all convex sets that contain the set. From inside, it is the set of finite convex combinations of its points. The chapter proves that the two descriptions agree in every convex space, with the free convex space of Chapter 6.

Section 1 defines the hull, proves its rules, and studies the standard simplex and convex independence. Section 2 proves the three classical theorems of finite-dimensional convexity. Caratheodory bounds the number of points in a convex combination by $d + 1$. Radon splits $d + 2$ points into two groups whose hulls meet. Helly gets a common point of a family of convex sets from common points of each $d + 1$ members. A planar example of Helly is worked step by step.

The chapter needs Chapter 6, affine independence from Chapter 2, and Volumes 7 and 13. Chapter 8 uses the hull for extreme points, cones and simplicial complexes.
