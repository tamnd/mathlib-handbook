# Banach spaces and the Baire category theorem

A Banach space is a complete normed space. This chapter studies the Banach spaces and proves the three classical theorems that need completeness through the Baire category theorem. Section 1 collects the ways to build Banach spaces: closed subspaces, quotients by closed subspaces with the quotient norm, and the completion of a normed space. It shows that Mathlib has no separate class for Banach spaces and writes `[NormedAddCommGroup E] [NormedSpace 𝕜 E] [CompleteSpace E]`. Section 2 proves the uniform boundedness principle of Banach and Steinhaus: a family of bounded operators on a Banach space that is bounded at each point is bounded in norm. A consequence is that a pointwise limit of bounded operators on a Banach space is bounded. Section 3 proves the open mapping theorem with its two steps, the Baire step and the step of successive approximation, and derives from it the bounded inverse theorem and the closed graph theorem. Section 4 studies complemented subspaces, compact operators and Fredholm operators. Section 5 studies Schauder bases, whose partial sum projections are uniformly bounded by Section 2, and the L-projections.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 to 3, and the Baire category theorem of Volume 13, Chapter 3, Section 2, which it uses without a new proof. Each counterexample of the chapter shows that the hypothesis of completeness is necessary. Chapter 5 combines the uniform boundedness principle with the theorem of Hahn-Banach to show that weakly bounded sets are bounded.
