# ZFC inside Lean

The earlier chapters of this volume work with types. A cardinal is a class of types up to bijection, and an ordinal is a class of well-ordered types up to isomorphism. A textbook of set theory works instead with one universe of sets, in which each object is a set and the only primitive relation is membership. This chapter builds that universe inside Lean and compares the two foundations.

Section 1 defines the pre-sets of Mathlib, a family of pre-sets indexed by a type, and the relation of extensional equivalence between them. The ZFC sets are the classes of pre-sets up to this relation. The section proves that membership is well-founded and that the axioms of Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory with choice hold for these sets. Section 2 defines classes, transitive sets and von Neumann ordinals, proves the Burali-Forti paradox for them, and identifies the von Neumann ordinals with the ordinals of Chapter 2. It then defines the rank of a set and the von Neumann hierarchy $V_\alpha$, and computes the cardinality of $V_\alpha$ with the beth numbers of Chapter 5. Section 3 compares the type theory of Lean with ZFC: universes, small types, the inaccessible cardinal of a universe, and two smaller models of set theory in the library, the hereditarily finite lists and the trees of descriptive set theory. It also states what the index of this edition does not have, in particular any proof of the independence of the continuum hypothesis.

The chapter needs the quotients of Volume 1, Chapter 3, well-founded relations from Volume 4, Chapter 1, the ordinals and the rank of Chapter 2, and the alephs and beths of Chapter 5. Section 3 uses the inaccessible cardinals of Chapter 6. No later chapter needs this one.
