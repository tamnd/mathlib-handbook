# Representations of groups

This chapter sets up the basic language of representation theory. Section 1 defines a representation of a monoid $G$ on a module over a commutative ring $k$ as a monoid homomorphism into the linear endomorphisms, and it proves that such a representation is the same as a module over the group algebra $k[G]$. It defines intertwining maps, equivalences, subrepresentations and invariants, builds direct sums, tensor products, duals and spaces of linear maps, and proves that the intertwining maps are the invariants of the space of linear maps. It closes with the averaging projection onto the invariants for a finite group whose order is invertible in $k$. Section 2 forms the category `Rep k G`, proves that it is equivalent to the category of $k[G]$-modules and to an action category, studies its monoidal closed structure and its projectives, proves that restriction along a group homomorphism is faithful and exact, and introduces the category `FDRep k G` of finite dimensional representations with the dimension form of the lemma of Schur.

The chapter needs groups and group actions from Volume 5, modules, linear maps and tensor products from Volumes 6 to 8, and, for Section 2, the language of categories, functors, equivalences, adjunctions and monoidal and abelian categories from Volumes 24 and 25.

Chapter 2 uses the averaging projection to prove the theorem of Maschke and the lemma of Schur, and it studies characters and Tannaka duality in `FDRep`. Chapter 3 uses the restriction functor and the adjunction with the internal Hom to build induced and coinduced representations. The category `Rep k G` and its projectives are also the base of group cohomology in Volume 26, Chapter 8.
