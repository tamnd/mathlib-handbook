# Ideals and quotient rings

This chapter studies the ideals of a ring, which play for rings the part that normal subgroups play for groups. Section 1 defines ideals, the ideal generated by a set, and the sum, the product and the intersection of ideals. It proves that the kernel of a homomorphism is an ideal, that the ideals of $\mathbb{Z}$ are the sets $n \mathbb{Z}$, and that a commutative ring is a field if and only if its only ideals are $0$ and the whole ring. Section 2 builds the quotient ring $R/I$, proves its universal property and the three isomorphism theorems, the correspondence theorem and the Chinese remainder theorem, and computes quotients such as $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(2 + i) \cong \mathbb{Z}/5$. Section 3 defines prime and maximal ideals, proves that $R/P$ is an integral domain exactly when $P$ is prime and that $R/M$ is a field exactly when $M$ is maximal, and proves with Zorn's lemma that each proper ideal is contained in a maximal ideal.

The chapter needs Chapter 1, and quotient groups and the first isomorphism theorem for groups from Chapter 5 of Volume 5. In Mathlib the ideals live in the library of commutative algebra, so most names of the chapter are treated in full in Volume 9. This chapter gives the theory that a first course needs and the names to use it.

Chapter 3 uses the quotients by prime and maximal ideals to produce integral domains and fields, and Chapter 4 studies the quotients $\mathbb{Z}/n$ in detail. The principal ideals of Section 1 are the starting point of Chapter 6 on principal ideal domains.
