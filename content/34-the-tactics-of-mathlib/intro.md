# Introduction

This volume is about the tactics of Lean and Mathlib, for a mathematician who writes proofs in Lean. A tactic is a step of a proof: it takes the goal state, a list of hypotheses and a target, and it makes a new goal state, with a part of the proof term. Each section takes one tactic or a small group of tactics. It tells what the tactic does, which goals it closes, and how it works inside, at the level that a user needs to predict its result. It shows the tactic on worked goals of real mathematics, with the goal state step by step. It gives the common failures, with the messages of Lean and the fixes, and graded exercises.

Most sections prove a theorem behind the tactic. The theorem tells the exact class of goals that the tactic closes. For `ring` it is the normal form of polynomials. For `linarith` it is the theorem of Fourier and Motzkin on linear inequalities. For `omega` it is the tightening of integer inequalities. For `aesop` it is the theorem that safe rules lose no proof. For `induction` it is the principle of induction, proved from the well-ordering of $\mathbb{N}$. With the theorem, a failure of the tactic has a meaning: often the message shows a point that satisfies all the facts that the tactic sees.

The volume has five chapters. Chapter 1 is about the tactics that rewrite: `simp`, `norm_num`, the cast tactics, `rw` and `conv`. Chapter 2 is about algebra: `ring`, `linear_combination`, `field_simp`, `positivity`, `gcongr` and `calc`. Chapter 3 is about the decision procedures of arithmetic: `linarith`, `nlinarith`, `omega` and `decide`. Chapter 4 is about search: `exact?`, `apply?`, `aesop`, `fun_prop`, `continuity` and `grind`. Chapter 5 is about the structure of a proof: `intro`, `exact`, `apply`, `refine`, `rcases`, `obtain`, `choose`, `lift`, `filter_upwards`, `induction` and `cases`.

Read this volume with any other volume. It needs only Volume 0. A reader who is new to Lean can start with Chapter 5, Section 3, which explains the goal state and the first tactics, and then read the chapters in order. The examples with an anchor come from the companion `tamnd/mathlib-lean`, and each one builds against the Mathlib commit of the edition. The other blocks of Lean in the text were checked against the same commit.
