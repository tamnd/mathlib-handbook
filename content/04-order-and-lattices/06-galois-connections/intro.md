# Galois connections

A Galois connection is a pair of monotone maps $l : \alpha \to \beta$ and $u : \beta \to \alpha$ with $l(a) \le b \iff a \le u(b)$ for all $a$ and $b$. The condition is short, but it occurs in many parts of mathematics: the image and the preimage of a function, the cast from `ℤ` to `ℝ` and the floor function, multiplication and division with rounding, a set and the subgroup it generates, and the fields and groups of Galois theory. The first section defines the connection and proves its consequences: each map fixes the other, the lower map keeps all suprema and the upper map keeps all infima, and a map on a complete lattice that keeps all suprema has an upper adjoint. The second section studies the connections in which one composite is the identity, the Galois insertions and coinsertions, and the closure operators that the other composite gives. It proves that the closed elements of a closure operator on a complete lattice form a complete lattice, and that a Galois insertion carries a complete lattice structure to its target. The third section treats the nuclei of a frame, the closure operators that keep binary infima, and their correspondence with sublocales, the subsets of a frame that play the role of subspaces in point-free topology.

The chapter needs the chapter on monotone maps and the chapter on lattices. The theorems on suprema and infima use the complete lattices of the fourth chapter, and the examples use the floor function and the subgroups of `ℤ`.

Mathlib uses the results of this chapter to build the lattice of subgroups, submodules, ideals, closed sets and filters. Each of these lattices comes from a Galois insertion between sets and the substructures, with the closure or the span as the lower map.
