# The natural numbers and induction

The type `ℕ` is the first number type of Mathlib, and induction is its first method of proof. This chapter starts from the axioms of Peano and builds the arithmetic of $\mathbb{N}$ with complete proofs.

The first section proves the recursion theorem, defines addition and multiplication, and proves their laws. It then derives induction from a start value, strong induction and induction in two steps from ordinary induction, and it shows the Mathlib names `Nat.le_induction`, `Nat.strong_induction_on` and `Nat.twoStepInduction`. The second section defines the order and the truncated subtraction of `ℕ`, proves the well ordering principle and infinite descent, and shows `Nat.find`, the least number with a property. The third section defines the factorial and the binomial coefficient by recursion, proves the factorial formula, and proves the binomial theorem `add_pow`. The fourth section shows the library around factorials and binomial coefficients: double factorials, bounds, Vandermonde's identity, the central binomial coefficient, the theorem of Lucas and the multinomial coefficients. The fifth section shows the digits of a number in a base, the divisibility tests by the sum of the digits, and the logarithms and square roots with values in `ℕ`. The sixth section shows the counting and the enumeration of the numbers with a property, the Fibonacci numbers with the theorem of Zeckendorf, divisibility sequences and the hyperoperations. The seventh section shows the pairing function `Nat.pair`, the distance `Nat.dist`, partial subtraction, recursion on the binary digits and the type `WithBot ℕ`.

The chapter needs Volume 1, Chapter 5 for the junk values. Chapter 2 uses the well ordering principle for the division algorithm and the factorial for the infinitude of primes, and Chapter 3 uses the binomial theorem for Fermat's little theorem.
