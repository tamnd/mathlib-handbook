# Dedekind domains

The ring $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ does not have unique factorization of elements: $6 = 2 \cdot 3 = (1 + \sqrt{-5})(1 - \sqrt{-5})$, and the four factors are irreducible and not associate. Kummer and Dedekind repaired this failure by factoring ideals instead of elements. In $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ the ideal $(6)$ is a product of four prime ideals, in a unique way, and the two factorizations of the element $6$ are two ways of grouping these four primes. The rings in which every nonzero ideal is a unique product of prime ideals are the Dedekind domains.

Section 1 defines Dedekind domains as Noetherian, integrally closed domains of dimension at most $1$, proves that they are exactly the Noetherian domains whose localizations at nonzero primes are discrete valuation rings, and shows that the integral closure of a Dedekind domain in a finite separable extension is again Dedekind. This covers the rings of integers of number fields and the coordinate rings of smooth affine curves. Section 2 introduces fractional ideals and proves that in a Dedekind domain every nonzero fractional ideal is invertible. Section 3 proves the unique factorization of ideals, describes the valuations at the primes, and works out the factorization of $(6)$ in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$. Section 4 builds the completions $K_v$ at the primes, the ring of finite adeles, the $S$-integers, the $S$-units and the Selmer groups, and states Gauss's lemma with the $v$-adic absolute values. Section 5 treats the discriminant, the different and the conductor of an extension, the extension and the norm of fractional ideals, and the Frobenius elements of primes with finite residue fields.

The chapter uses the integral closure and the going up theorem of Chapter 1, the local rings of Chapter 2, and the discrete valuation rings of Chapter 3. Noetherian rings, localization and unique factorization domains are in Volume 9, Commutative algebra I. Chapter 5 studies the group of fractional ideals modulo principal ones, the class group, which measures how far a Dedekind domain is from a PID. The number theory of rings of integers is in Volume 12.

In Mathlib a Dedekind domain is a ring `A` with `[IsDedekindDomain A]`, a fractional ideal is a term of `FractionalIdeal (nonZeroDivisors A) K` for a fraction field `K`, and the nonzero primes form the type `IsDedekindDomain.HeightOneSpectrum A`.
