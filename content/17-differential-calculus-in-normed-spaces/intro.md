# Differential calculus in normed spaces

This volume teaches the differential calculus of maps between normed spaces and its form in Mathlib. The derivative of a map $f : E \to F$ at a point is a continuous linear map $Df(x) : E \to F$, the best linear approximation of $f$ near $x$. With this one definition, the calculus of one variable, of several variables and on Banach spaces of functions become one theory. Each main theorem has a complete proof on paper: the chain rule, the mean value inequality, the symmetry of the second derivative, Taylor's formula, the inverse and implicit function theorems, the smoothness of analytic functions, Grönwall's inequality and the theorem of Picard-Lindelöf. Each chapter also computes: the derivative of the determinant at the identity, the inverse of $(x, y) \mapsto (x + y^2, y)$, the second derivative test for $x^2 + xy + y^2 - 3x$, and the Picard iterates of $x' = x$.

Chapter 1 defines the Fréchet derivative and compares it with the directional and the partial derivatives. Chapter 2 proves the rules of calculus: sums, the chain rule, maps into products, bilinear maps, and the inverse in a Banach algebra. Chapter 3 replaces the mean value theorem, which fails for vector values, by the mean value inequality, and deduces strict differentiability and the theorems on limits of derivatives. Chapter 4 studies the higher derivatives as multilinear maps, proves Schwarz's theorem, and defines the classes $C^n$ as Mathlib does with `ContDiff`. Chapter 5 proves Taylor's formula in normed spaces and the second derivative test. Chapter 6 proves the inverse function theorem from the contraction principle and deduces the implicit function theorem and the rule of Lagrange multipliers. Chapter 7 studies power series with values in Banach spaces and analytic functions. Chapter 8 proves the existence and the uniqueness of the solutions of ordinary differential equations.

The volume needs Volume 15 for the derivative of a function of one real variable, the mean value theorem, Taylor's theorem in one variable and the elementary functions, and Volume 7 for linear maps and determinants. It needs Volume 16 for normed spaces, Banach spaces, the operator norm and continuous multilinear maps, which are the spaces where the derivatives live. Volume 13 supplies the filters and the metric spaces of the Mathlib statements. The proofs of Chapter 8 use the integral of a continuous curve, which Volume 18 treats.

Mathlib states the theorems of this volume over a nontrivially normed field `𝕜`, so that one statement covers the real and the complex case. The derivative is a relation `HasFDerivAt f f' x` together with a total function `fderiv 𝕜 f x`, which is $0$ where $f$ is not differentiable. The smoothness `ContDiff 𝕜 n f` takes `n` in `WithTop ℕ∞`, where the top value $\omega$ means analytic. The volume has no Lean companion yet, so the sections show the Mathlib statements and give no Lean exercises. Volume 19 continues with complex analysis, and Volume 23 with manifolds, where the derivative becomes the tangent map `mfderiv`.
