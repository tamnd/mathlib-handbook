# Concrete categories of algebraic structures

The earlier chapters of this volume work in an abstract abelian category. This chapter describes the concrete categories in which the examples live, and the form that Mathlib gives them. Section 1 treats monoids, groups, abelian groups and rings: the bundled objects and the wrapped morphisms of Mathlib, the forgetful functors, the free and forgetful adjunctions, abelianization and units, the computation of limits of rings on the underlying sets, the proof that an epimorphism of groups is surjective, and the proof that abelian groups form an abelian category. Section 2 treats the category of modules over a ring: the abelian structure with a full proof, free modules, the three functors of change of rings and their adjunctions, enough projectives and enough injectives, and the tensor product. Section 3 treats algebras, commutative algebras, coalgebras, bialgebras and Hopf algebras, with the equivalence between coalgebras and comonoids and the group algebra as a Hopf algebra.

The chapter needs categories, functors, limits, adjunctions and monoidal categories from Volume 24, and abelian categories with projective and injective objects from Volume 25, Chapter 2. It uses the algebra of groups, modules and tensor products from Volumes 5, 7 and 8. It does not use the homology of the earlier chapters, except in a few examples.

Chapter 8 uses this chapter directly. A representation of a group $G$ over $k$ is a module over the group algebra $k[G]$, so Section 2 gives the abelian structure and the projective resolutions that define group cohomology. The Hopf structure of $k[G]$ from Section 3 makes tensor products and duals of representations into representations. The change of rings adjunctions of Section 2 reappear as restriction, induction and coinduction of representations in Shapiro's lemma.
