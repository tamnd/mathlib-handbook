## Sieves and Grothendieck topologies

### Overview

A sheaf on a topological space $T$ is a presheaf on the open sets whose sections glue along open covers. The definition uses the open sets only through two facts: an open set $V$ can be inside an open set $U$, and a family of open sets can cover $U$. The first fact is a morphism $V \to U$ in the poset category of open sets. Grothendieck saw that the second fact can also be given in terms of morphisms. A cover of $U$ is then a family of morphisms with target $U$, and the category does not need to be a poset. This idea gives the étale topology of a scheme, where an "open set" of $X$ is an étale morphism $Y \to X$.

This section defines the notion of a cover in a category. There are three forms. A Grothendieck topology chooses covering sieves, which are families of morphisms closed under composition on the right. A pretopology chooses covering families, and it needs pullbacks. A coverage is the weakest form and needs no limits. Each form generates a Grothendieck topology, and Mathlib uses the Grothendieck topology as the main notion. The sheaf condition is in Section 2.

In the text $g \circ f$ is the textbook composite, and Mathlib writes `f ≫ g`.

### Definitions

Let $C$ be a category and let $X$ be an object of $C$.

**Definition (Presieve).** A *presieve* on $X$ is a set $R$ of morphisms with target $X$. In Mathlib, `Presieve X` is the type `∀ ⦃Y⦄, Set (Y ⟶ X)`, and `R f` is the statement that $f$ is in $R$. The presieve `Presieve.singleton f` contains only $f$, and `Presieve.ofArrows Y f` contains the morphisms $f_i : Y_i \to X$ of a family.

**Definition (Sieve).** A *sieve* on $X$ is a presieve $S$ on $X$ with this property: if $f : Y \to X$ is in $S$ and $g : Z \to Y$ is any morphism, then $f \circ g$ is in $S$. The *maximal sieve* $\top$ contains all morphisms with target $X$. The sieves on $X$ form a complete lattice under inclusion, and the meet and join are the intersection and union.

When $C$ is the poset of open sets of $T$, a sieve on $U$ is a set $S$ of open subsets of $U$ such that each open subset of a member of $S$ is also in $S$.

**Definition (Generated sieve).** Let $R$ be a presieve on $X$. The *sieve generated by* $R$ is the set of morphisms $h : Z \to X$ such that $h = e \circ g$ for some $e$ in $R$ and some $g$. It is the smallest sieve that contains $R$. For a sieve $S$ the statement "the sieve generated by $R$ is in $S$" is equivalent to "$R$ is in $S$", and the sieve generated by a sieve is the same sieve. So generation and the forgetful map from sieves to presieves form a Galois insertion, in the sense of Volume 4.

**Definition (Pullback of a sieve).** Let $S$ be a sieve on $X$ and let $h : Y \to X$. The *pullback* $h^* S$ is the sieve on $Y$ of all $g : Z \to Y$ with $h \circ g$ in $S$. Then $1_X^* S = S$ and $(h \circ k)^* S = k^* (h^* S)$. The pullback of a sieve is not a pullback in the sense of limits, and it does not need limits in $C$.

**Definition (Grothendieck topology).** A *Grothendieck topology* $J$ on $C$ gives, for each object $X$, a set $J(X)$ of sieves on $X$, the *covering sieves*. These sets must satisfy three axioms.

1. (Maximality) The maximal sieve $\top$ is in $J(X)$.
2. (Stability) If $S \in J(X)$ and $h : Y \to X$, then $h^* S \in J(Y)$.
3. (Transitivity) Let $S \in J(X)$ and let $R$ be a sieve on $X$. If $f^* R \in J(Y)$ for each $f : Y \to X$ in $S$, then $R \in J(X)$.

A category with a Grothendieck topology is a *site*. A sieve $S$ on $X$ *covers* a morphism $f : Y \to X$ if $f^* S \in J(Y)$. Axiom 3 says that a sieve that covers each morphism of a covering sieve is also a covering sieve.

**Definition (Pretopology).** Let $C$ have pullbacks. A *pretopology* $K$ on $C$ gives, for each $X$, a set $K(X)$ of presieves on $X$, the *covering families*, with these axioms.

1. If $f : Y \to X$ is an isomorphism, then the presieve $\{f\}$ is in $K(X)$.
2. If $R = \{ r_i : X_i \to X \}$ is in $K(X)$ and $h : Y \to X$, then the family of projections $X_i \times_X Y \to Y$ is in $K(Y)$.
3. If $R \in K(X)$ and if for each $r : X_r \to X$ in $R$ there is $T_r \in K(X_r)$, then the family of all composites $r \circ t$ with $t \in T_r$ is in $K(X)$.

The Stacks project calls a category with a pretopology a site, and Mac Lane and Moerdijk call a pretopology a basis for a topology.

**Definition (Coverage).** A *coverage* $K$ on $C$ gives, for each $X$, a set $K(X)$ of presieves on $X$ with one axiom. For each $R \in K(X)$ and each $f : Y \to X$ there is $T \in K(Y)$ that *factors through $R$ along $f$*. This means that for each $g : Z \to Y$ in $T$ there are $e : W \to X$ in $R$ and $i : Z \to W$ with $e \circ i = f \circ g$.

The axiom of a coverage is the weak form of the pullback axiom of a pretopology. It does not need pullbacks in $C$, and it does not ask that the family $T$ is built from $R$ in a fixed way.

### Theorems and proofs

Let $J$ be a Grothendieck topology on $C$. The first lemma is a small fact about sieves, and the proofs below use it many times.

**Lemma.** Let $S$ be a sieve on $X$ and let $f : Y \to X$. Then $f$ is in $S$ if and only if $f^* S = \top$.

*Proof.* If $f$ is in $S$, then $f \circ g$ is in $S$ for each $g$ with target $Y$, because $S$ is a sieve. So each $g$ is in $f^* S$. Conversely, if $f^* S = \top$, then $1_Y$ is in $f^* S$, and so $f = f \circ 1_Y$ is in $S$. $\square$

**Theorem (Supersets of covering sieves).** Let $S \subseteq R$ be sieves on $X$. If $S \in J(X)$, then $R \in J(X)$.

*Proof.* Use axiom 3 with the covering sieve $S$. Let $f : Y \to X$ be in $S$. By the lemma, $f^* S = \top$. Since $S \subseteq R$, also $f^* S \subseteq f^* R$, so $f^* R = \top$. By axiom 1, $f^* R \in J(Y)$. This is true for each $f$ in $S$, and axiom 3 gives $R \in J(X)$. $\square$

**Theorem (Intersections of covering sieves).** Let $R$ and $S$ be sieves on $X$. Then $R \cap S \in J(X)$ if and only if $R \in J(X)$ and $S \in J(X)$.

*Proof.* If $R \cap S$ is covering, then $R$ and $S$ are covering by the previous theorem. Conversely, let $R$ and $S$ be covering. Use axiom 3 with the covering sieve $R$. Let $f : Y \to X$ be in $R$. Pullback commutes with intersection, so $f^*(R \cap S) = f^* R \cap f^* S$. By the lemma, $f^* R = \top$, and so $f^*(R \cap S) = f^* S$. This sieve is in $J(Y)$ by axiom 2. Axiom 3 gives $R \cap S \in J(X)$. $\square$

So $J(X)$ is a filter in the lattice of sieves on $X$. The three axioms say more than this, because they also link the filters at different objects.

**Theorem (Composite covers).** Let $S \in J(X)$, and for each $f : Y \to X$ in $S$ let $R_f \in J(Y)$. Let $B$ be the set of all composites $f \circ g$ with $f$ in $S$ and $g$ in $R_f$. Then $B$ is a covering sieve on $X$.

*Proof.* First, $B$ is a sieve. If $f \circ g$ is in $B$ and $k$ is a morphism into the source of $g$, then $f \circ g \circ k = f \circ (g \circ k)$, and $g \circ k$ is in $R_f$ because $R_f$ is a sieve. Next, let $f$ be in $S$. Each $g$ in $R_f$ is in $f^* B$, because $f \circ g$ is in $B$. So $R_f \subseteq f^* B$, and $f^* B \in J(Y)$ by the theorem on supersets. Axiom 3 gives $B \in J(X)$. $\square$

**Theorem (The topology of a pretopology).** Let $C$ have pullbacks and let $K$ be a pretopology on $C$. For each $X$, let $J_K(X)$ be the set of sieves $S$ on $X$ that contain some $R \in K(X)$. Then $J_K$ is a Grothendieck topology. It is the smallest Grothendieck topology $J$ such that the sieve generated by each covering family is in $J$.

*Proof.* Axiom 1. The family $\{1_X\}$ is in $K(X)$ by the first axiom of a pretopology, and it is in $\top$.

Axiom 2. Let $R \subseteq S$ with $R \in K(X)$, and let $h : Y \to X$. The family $P$ of projections $p_i : X_i \times_X Y \to Y$ is in $K(Y)$. Let $q_i : X_i \times_X Y \to X_i$ be the other projection, so that $h \circ p_i = r_i \circ q_i$. Since $r_i$ is in $S$ and $S$ is a sieve, $r_i \circ q_i$ is in $S$. So $h \circ p_i$ is in $S$, and $p_i$ is in $h^* S$. Thus $P \subseteq h^* S$, and $h^* S \in J_K(Y)$.

Axiom 3. Let $R' \subseteq S$ with $R' \in K(X)$, and let $R$ be a sieve with $f^* R \in J_K(Y)$ for each $f$ in $S$. For each $r$ in $R'$, the morphism $r$ is in $S$, so there is $T_r \in K(X_r)$ with $T_r \subseteq r^* R$. By the third axiom of a pretopology, the family $B$ of all $r \circ t$ with $t \in T_r$ is in $K(X)$. Each $t$ in $T_r$ is in $r^* R$, so $r \circ t$ is in $R$. Thus $B \subseteq R$, and $R \in J_K(X)$.

Last, let $J$ be a Grothendieck topology that contains the sieve generated by each covering family. If $S$ contains $R \in K(X)$, then $S$ contains the sieve generated by $R$, and $S \in J(X)$ by the theorem on supersets. So $J_K \subseteq J$. $\square$

A coverage $K$ also generates a Grothendieck topology. The covering sieves are given by induction. The sieve generated by each $R \in K(X)$ is covering, the maximal sieve is covering, and a sieve that covers each morphism of a covering sieve is covering. The axiom of a coverage is used to prove stability under pullback, by induction on this definition. In both cases the map from the weak notion to Grothendieck topologies is the left part of a Galois insertion. Thus each Grothendieck topology $J$ is generated by the pretopology, or the coverage, of all presieves whose generated sieve is in $J$.

**Theorem (The topology of open covers).** Let $T$ be a topological space and let $C$ be the poset of open sets of $T$. For an open set $U$, let $J(U)$ be the set of sieves $S$ on $U$ such that each point of $U$ is in some member of $S$. Then $J$ is a Grothendieck topology.

*Proof.* Axiom 1. Each point of $U$ is in $U$, and $U$ is in $\top$.

Axiom 2. Let $S \in J(U)$ and let $W \subseteq U$ be open. Let $x \in W$. Then $x$ is in some $V \in S$. The open set $V \cap W$ is inside $V$, so it is in $S$ because $S$ is a sieve. The composite $V \cap W \to W \to U$ is the inclusion $V \cap W \to U$, so $V \cap W$ is in the pullback of $S$ to $W$, and $x \in V \cap W$.

Axiom 3. Let $S \in J(U)$ and let $R$ be a sieve on $U$ whose pullback to each $V \in S$ is in $J(V)$. Let $x \in U$. Then $x$ is in some $V \in S$, and the pullback of $R$ to $V$ gives an open set $V' \subseteq V$ with $x \in V'$ and $V' \to V \to U$ in $R$. So $x$ is in the member $V'$ of $R$. $\square$

In this topology a sieve $S$ covers $U$ when the union of the members of $S$ is $U$. The same formula for presieves gives a pretopology, and the pretopology generates this topology.

### Examples

**Example (Covers by two open sets).** Let $U_1$ and $U_2$ be open subsets of $U$. The sieve generated by the two inclusions contains an open set $V$ when $V \subseteq U_1$ or $V \subseteq U_2$. It is in $J(U)$ exactly when $U_1 \cup U_2 = U$. The sieve is larger than the family $\{U_1, U_2\}$, and it contains $U_1 \cap U_2$ and all smaller open sets. Section 2 writes the sheaf condition for this sieve in detail.

**Example (The trivial and discrete topologies).** On any category, the sets $J(X) = \{\top\}$ define a Grothendieck topology, the *trivial* topology. It is the smallest topology. The sets of all sieves define the *discrete* topology, which is the largest one. In the discrete topology the empty sieve covers each object. By the theorem on supersets, a topology is discrete if and only if the empty sieve covers each object.

**Example (The empty cover).** In the topology of open covers, the empty sieve covers $U$ exactly when $U$ has no points. So the empty set of $T$ has a covering sieve with no members. In Section 2 this forces the value of a sheaf on the empty set to be a point.

**Example (The dense topology).** On any category, let $S \in J(X)$ when for each $f : Y \to X$ there is $g : Z \to Y$ with $f \circ g$ in $S$. This is a Grothendieck topology. For axiom 3, let $S$ be dense, let $R$ satisfy the hypothesis and let $f : Y \to X$. First choose $g$ with $f \circ g$ in $S$. Then the pullback of $R$ along $f \circ g$ is dense, so there is $k$ with $f \circ g \circ k$ in $R$. Exercise 7 computes this topology on the open sets of a space.

**Example (The atomic topology).** Let $C$ have the *right Ore condition*: for each pair $Y \to X \leftarrow Z$ there are $W \to Y$ and $W \to Z$ that make a commutative square. Then the nonempty sieves form a Grothendieck topology. The Ore condition gives axiom 2. Each category with pullbacks has the right Ore condition.

**Example (Coverages without pullbacks).** The file `Mathlib.CategoryTheory.Sites.Coherent.Basic` defines `coherentCoverage`, `regularCoverage` and `extensiveCoverage`. In the coherent coverage, the covering presieves are the finite families that are jointly effective epimorphic. In the regular coverage, they are the single effective epimorphisms. These are coverages and not pretopologies, because the definitions do not need pullbacks in $C$.

The topologies on $C$ form a complete lattice, and `GrothendieckTopology.Cover J X` is the type of covering sieves of $X$, ordered by inclusion. The examples above are `GrothendieckTopology.trivial`, `discrete`, `dense` and `atomic`.

Common errors of a learner:

- `Presieve X` is a function on morphisms. To state that `f` is in `R`, write `R f`, not `f ∈ R`.
- `Sieve.pullback h S` takes the morphism first. The term `S.pullback h` is the same, with dot notation.
- The pullback of a sieve does not need `HasPullbacks C`. The pullback of a covering family in a pretopology does need it.
- A covering family and the sieve it generates are different objects. Use `Sieve.generate` to pass from a presieve to a sieve before you use the axioms of a Grothendieck topology.

### Exercises

1. (A) Let $S$ be a sieve on $X$ and let $h : Y \to X$ and $k : Z \to Y$. Show that $(h \circ k)^* S = k^*(h^* S)$ and that $h^*(R \cap S) = h^* R \cap h^* S$.
2. (A) Show that the sieve generated by a presieve $R$ is a sieve, and that it is the smallest sieve that contains $R$.
3. (A) Show that the intersection of any set of Grothendieck topologies on $C$ is a Grothendieck topology. Conclude that the topologies on $C$ form a complete lattice.
4. (B) Let $J$ be a Grothendieck topology. Show that a sieve $S$ on $X$ is in $J(X)$ if and only if $S$ covers $1_X$, and that $S$ covers each morphism in $S$.
5. (B) Find all Grothendieck topologies on the category with one object and one morphism, and on the poset $\{0 < 1\}$.
6. (B) Let $f : Y \to X$ be a split epimorphism in $C$. Show that the sieve generated by $\{f\}$ is in $J(X)$ for each Grothendieck topology $J$ on $C$.
7. (C) Let $T$ be a topological space. Show that the dense topology on the poset of all open sets of $T$ is the topology of nonempty sieves, because the empty set is below each object. Then let $C$ be the poset of nonempty open sets. Show that a sieve $S$ on $U$ is dense if and only if the union of the members of $S$ is dense in $U$.

### Notes and history

Grothendieck introduced topologies on categories at the start of the 1960s, to define the étale cohomology of schemes. The theory of sites and sheaves is in the seminar SGA 4, *Théorie des topos et cohomologie étale des schémas* (1963 to 1964), by Artin, Grothendieck and Verdier. SGA 4 uses sieves (*cribles*), and the axioms in this section are the ones in that text. The book *Sheaves in Geometry and Logic* by Mac Lane and Moerdijk (1992) gives the same axioms, and the Mathlib files refer to it as [MM92]. The Stacks project starts from families of morphisms, as in a pretopology. The notion of a coverage is in the book *Sketches of an Elephant* by Johnstone (2002).

The Mathlib Authors of `Sites.Sieves.Presieve`, `Sites.Sieves.Basic` and `Sites.Grothendieck` are Bhavik Mehta and Edward Ayers. The files `Sites.Pretopology` and `Sites.Spaces` are by Bhavik Mehta, and the file `Sites.Coverage` is by Adam Topaz.
