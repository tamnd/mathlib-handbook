# Sites and sheaves

This chapter gives the language of sheaves on a category. A topological space has open sets and open covers, and a sheaf is a presheaf whose sections glue along covers. Grothendieck replaced the open sets by the objects of a category and the covers by families of morphisms. The étale topology of a scheme, the topologies on categories of profinite sets and the canonical topology of a topos all come from this idea.

Section 1 defines sieves, Grothendieck topologies, pretopologies and coverages, and it proves the first properties of covering sieves. The main example is the topology of open covers on the open sets of a space. Section 2 states the sheaf condition for a presieve and for a topology, with values in sets and in a general category. It gives the equalizer form of the condition, it works the case of an open set covered by two open sets in detail, and it defines the canonical topology. Section 3 constructs the sheafification of a presheaf by two applications of the plus construction. It proves that sheafification is a left adjoint that preserves finite limits, and that the sheaves with values in an abelian category form an abelian category.

The chapter needs Volume 13 for topological spaces, and Volume 24 for the Yoneda lemma, limits, filtered colimits and adjunctions. Section 3 uses the abelian categories of Chapter 2 of this volume.

Chapter 7 uses the categories of sheaves as examples of extensive and regular categories, and it gives the subobject classifier of a category of sheaves. Volume 26 defines sheaf cohomology as the derived functors of the global sections, and Volume 28 uses sites of schemes such as the Zariski site.
