# Analytic functions and power series

A smooth function has a Taylor series at each point, but the series need not converge to the function. An analytic function is one for which it does: near each point, the function is the sum of a convergent power series. This chapter develops them between normed spaces, as Mathlib does. The coefficients of a power series on $E$ are continuous multilinear maps, the same objects as the higher derivatives of Chapter 4, and the theory works over $\mathbb{R}$, over $\mathbb{C}$ and over any complete normed field.

Section 1 defines formal multilinear series, their radius of convergence and analytic functions. It proves absolute convergence inside the radius and the formula of Cauchy and Hadamard. Section 2 proves that a function with a power series on a ball is analytic at every point of the ball, by the change of origin. It then shows that the derivative of an analytic function is analytic, that analytic functions are $C^\infty$, and that the coefficients of the series are $D^k f(x)/k!$. The smooth function $e^{-1/x}$ is therefore not analytic. Section 3 shows that sums, products, compositions and inverses of analytic functions are analytic, proves the isolated zeros and the identity theorem, and states the analytic inverse function theorem. Section 4 treats the continuously polynomial functions, whose power series have only finitely many nonzero terms, and uses them to prove that linear, bilinear and multilinear maps are analytic.

The chapter needs the higher derivatives of Chapter 4, Taylor's formula along a segment of Chapter 5 and the inverse function theorem of Chapter 6. It uses connectedness from Volume 13. Complex analysis starts here: the theorems of Section 3 are its basic tools.
