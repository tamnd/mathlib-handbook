# Matrices

A matrix is a table of scalars, and it is the form in which linear maps compute. This chapter builds the bridge between the two. Section 1 defines matrices, their sum, product, transpose and trace, and proves the laws of the matrix algebra. Section 2 attaches to each linear map its matrix in a pair of bases, proves that the product of matrices is the composition of maps, and derives the formula for a change of basis. Section 3 defines the rank of a matrix, proves that the row rank equals the column rank, and computes ranks by row reduction.

The chapter needs the bases and the dimension of Chapter 2 and the finite sums of Volume 3. Mathlib defines a matrix as a function of two indices, so many proofs are computations with sums. Chapter 4 defines the determinant of a square matrix, and Chapter 5 studies its characteristic polynomial.
