## Topological algebras and star algebras

### Overview

A star algebra is an algebra $A$ with an involution $x \mapsto x^*$ that reverses products and is conjugate linear: $(xy)^* = y^* x^*$ and $(c x)^* = \bar{c} x^*$. The algebra of bounded operators on a Hilbert space, with the adjoint as the star, is the main example, and $C^*$-algebras are its abstract form. The analysis of these algebras needs a topology for which the star is continuous, and it needs closed subalgebras, because the closure of a subalgebra is again a subalgebra.

This section describes the class `ContinuousStar`, the closure of star subalgebras and of non-unital subalgebras, the elemental algebra generated by one element, the topological group of unitary elements, the star as a continuous semilinear map, continuous algebra equivalences, and positive continuous linear maps.

### The library around it

#### Continuous star operations

The class `ContinuousStar R` says that the map `star : R → R` is continuous. Its field is `ContinuousStar.continuous_star`. The lemmas follow the pattern of the other algebraic operations.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Continuous.star` | if $f$ is continuous, so is $x \mapsto f(x)^*$ |
| `Filter.Tendsto.star` | if $f \to a$, then $f^* \to a^*$ |
| `tendsto_star` | the star tends to $a^*$ at $a$ |
| `continuous_star_iff` | $x \mapsto f(x)^*$ is continuous if and only if $f$ is continuous |
| `continuousAt_star_iff` | the same at one point |
| `starContinuousMap` | the star as a bundled continuous map |

The forms with *iff* hold because the star is an involution: $f = (f^*)^*$. The instances give the class for products (`instContinuousStarProd`), Pi types (`instContinuousStarForall`), units (`instContinuousStarUnits`) and the multiplicative opposite (`instContinuousStarMulOpposite`). The module `Mathlib.Topology.Algebra.Star.Real` gives the class for the real numbers and for $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$, where the star is the identity: `instContinuousStarReal` and `NNReal.instContinuousStar`.

#### Unitary elements

An element $u$ is unitary if $u^* u = 1$ and $u u^* = 1$. The unitary elements form a submonoid `unitary R`, with $u^{-1} = u^*$. When multiplication and the star are continuous, `unitary R` is a topological group: the inversion is the star, so it is continuous. The lemma `isClosed_unitary` says that the unitary elements form a closed set in a $T_1$ space. The proof writes the set as the preimage of the point $(1, 1)$ under the continuous map $u \mapsto (u^* u, u u^*)$.

#### Closures of subalgebras

The closure of a subalgebra of a topological algebra is a subalgebra, because addition, multiplication and the scalar action are continuous. The library has this result for each kind of subalgebra. For a star subalgebra $S$, `StarSubalgebra.topologicalClosure S` is the closure as a star subalgebra; the star must be continuous, so that the closure is closed under the star.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `StarSubalgebra.le_topologicalClosure` | $S$ is contained in its closure |
| `StarSubalgebra.isClosed_topologicalClosure` | the closure is closed |
| `StarSubalgebra.topologicalClosure_minimal` | a closed star subalgebra that contains $S$ contains its closure |
| `StarSubalgebra.topologicalClosure_mono` | the closure is monotone |
| `StarSubalgebra.topologicalClosure_map` | a continuous closed map moves closures |
| `StarSubalgebra.isMulCommutative_topologicalClosure` | the closure of a commutative subalgebra of a Hausdorff algebra is commutative |
| `StarSubalgebra.instCompleteSpaceSubtypeMemTopologicalClosure` | the closure in a complete space is complete |
| `StarAlgHom.ext_topologicalClosure` | two continuous star homomorphisms on the closure of $S$ that agree on $S$ are equal |

The non-unital forms are `NonUnitalSubalgebra.topologicalClosure` and `NonUnitalStarSubalgebra.topologicalClosure`, with the same lemma names in their namespaces. They matter in the continuous functional calculus for non-unital algebras, where the functions must vanish at zero. The lemma `Subalgebra.topologicalClosure_star_comm` says that the closure of the star of a subalgebra is the star of its closure. The lemma `StarSubalgebra.topologicalClosure_adjoin_le_centralizer_centralizer` places the closure of the algebra generated by a set inside the double centralizer of the set.

The inclusion of a closed star subalgebra into a larger one is a closed embedding (`StarSubalgebra.isClosedEmbedding_inclusion`). This lets a proof move a topological property between the subtypes.

#### The elemental algebra

Let $x \in A$. The *elemental algebra* `StarAlgebra.elemental R x` is the closure of the star subalgebra generated by $x$. It is the smallest closed star subalgebra that contains $x$, and it is the domain of the continuous functional calculus at $x$.

1. `StarAlgebra.elemental.self_mem` and `StarAlgebra.elemental.star_self_mem` say that $x$ and $x^*$ are in it.
2. `StarAlgebra.elemental.isClosed` says that it is closed, and `StarAlgebra.elemental.isClosedEmbedding_coe` gives the inclusion as a closed embedding.
3. `StarAlgebra.elemental.le_iff_mem` says that, for a closed star subalgebra $s$, the elemental algebra lies in $s$ if and only if $x \in s$.
4. `StarAlgebra.elemental.isMulCommutative` says that it is commutative when $x$ is normal, that is when $x^* x = x x^*$, and $A$ is Hausdorff.
5. `StarAlgebra.elemental.induction_on` proves a property of each element of the elemental algebra from the cases $x$, $x^*$, scalars, sums and products, and closure.

The non-unital form is `NonUnitalStarAlgebra.elemental`, and the form without a star is `NonUnitalAlgebra.elemental`.

#### The star as a continuous map

In a topological star module over a commutative star ring $R$, the star is conjugate linear and continuous. The equivalence `starL R : A ≃L⋆[R] A` records both facts; the notation `≃L⋆[R]` is for a continuous semilinear equivalence along the star of $R$. The form `starL'` is for a ring with the trivial star, where the star is linear.

Each element of a star module over a ring with $\frac{1}{2}$ splits into a self adjoint part and a skew adjoint part: $x = \frac{1}{2}(x + x^*) + \frac{1}{2}(x - x^*)$. The maps `selfAdjointPartL` and `skewAdjointPartL` are the two parts as continuous linear maps, and `StarModule.decomposeProdAdjointL` is the continuous linear equivalence of $A$ with the product of the two parts.

The module `Mathlib.Topology.Algebra.Star.LinearMap` puts a star on continuous linear maps between star modules: $f^*(x) = f(x^*)^*$. This is `ContinuousLinearMap.intrinsicStar`, with the formula `ContinuousLinearMap.intrinsicStar_apply`. This star is not the adjoint of an operator on a Hilbert space, so the library puts it on the type copy `WithConv (E →L[R] F)`. By `ContinuousLinearMap.IntrinsicStar.isSelfAdjoint_iff_map_star`, a map is self adjoint for this star if and only if it commutes with the star.

#### Continuous algebra equivalences

A continuous algebra equivalence `A ≃A[R] B` (the type `ContinuousAlgEquiv`) is an algebra equivalence that is a homeomorphism. The projections are `ContinuousAlgEquiv.toAlgEquiv`, `ContinuousAlgEquiv.toHomeomorph` and `ContinuousAlgEquiv.toContinuousLinearEquiv`. The equivalences `ContinuousAlgEquiv.refl`, `ContinuousAlgEquiv.symm` and `ContinuousAlgEquiv.trans` give the groupoid structure. The topological lemmas of homeomorphisms have forms such as `ContinuousAlgEquiv.image_closure`, `ContinuousAlgEquiv.isOpenMap` and `ContinuousAlgEquiv.map_nhds_eq`. The class `ContinuousAlgEquivClass` covers each bundled type with the same properties.

#### Positive continuous linear maps

Let $E_1$ and $E_2$ be ordered topological modules. A positive continuous linear map `E₁ →P[R] E₂` (the type `PositiveContinuousLinearMap`) is a continuous linear map that sends nonnegative elements to nonnegative elements. States on a $C^*$-algebra are examples. The constructor `PositiveContinuousLinearMap.mk₀` takes a continuous linear map and a proof that it maps $0 \le x$ to $0 \le f(x)$. The lemma `PositiveContinuousLinearMap.map_nonneg` gives the property back, and the instance `PositiveContinuousLinearMap.instOrderHomClass` says that such a map is monotone. The maps form an additive commutative monoid, closed under `PositiveContinuousLinearMap.comp`.

#### Common errors

1. `StarSubalgebra.topologicalClosure` needs `ContinuousStar A`. Without it, the closure of a star subalgebra is only a `Subalgebra`, and `Subalgebra.topologicalClosure` gives that form.
2. `StarAlgebra.elemental.isMulCommutative` needs `IsStarNormal x`. For a non-normal element, such as a nilpotent matrix, the elemental algebra is not commutative.
3. `ContinuousLinearMap.intrinsicStar` is not the operator adjoint. For maps on a Hilbert space, the adjoint is in Volume 16, and the star on `E →L[𝕜] E` is that adjoint.

### Exercises

1. (A) Prove from `isClosed_unitary` that the unitary group of $\mathbb{C}$, the unit circle, is closed.
2. (A) Use `continuous_star_iff` to show that $z \mapsto \overline{f(z)}$ is continuous if and only if $f$ is continuous, for $f : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$.
3. (B) Prove that the elemental algebra of a self adjoint element is commutative.
4. (B) Show that the matrix transpose with complex conjugation is a continuous star on $2 \times 2$ complex matrices, and compute the self adjoint part of a matrix with `selfAdjointPartL`.
5. (C) Use `StarAlgHom.ext_topologicalClosure` to prove that two continuous star homomorphisms from $C([0, 1], \mathbb{C})$ that agree on the identity function are equal.
