# Power series

A formal power series is a polynomial that is allowed to go on forever: an expression $a_0 + a_1 X + a_2 X^2 + \cdots$ with infinitely many coefficients and no question of convergence. The power series over a commutative ring $R$ form a ring $R[[X]]$, which contains the polynomial ring $R[X]$. The new ring has more units than $R[X]$, since $1 - X$ becomes invertible with inverse $1 + X + X^2 + \cdots$, and its ideals are simpler: over a field $k$ the only nonzero ideals of $k[[X]]$ are the powers of $(X)$. So $k[[X]]$ is the first example of a local principal ideal domain, the algebraic model of the germs of functions at one point.

Section 1 defines $R[[X]]$, proves that a power series is a unit exactly when its constant coefficient is a unit, defines the order of a series, and determines all ideals of $k[[X]]$. It uses power series as generating functions, with the Fibonacci numbers as an example. Section 2 turns to power series in several variables, proves the unit criterion and the domain property for them, builds the field $k((X))$ of Laurent series as the field of fractions of $k[[X]]$, and proves that $R[[X]]$ is Noetherian when $R$ is Noetherian.

Section 3 treats Hahn series, the series with well-ordered support over an ordered monoid: their order and leading coefficient, the Cauchy product, the lexicographic order, the bounds on the size of the support, the link with power series and the order valuation. Section 4 defines infinite sums of Hahn series and uses them for the geometric series, the units and the field of Hahn series, the substitution into power series, and the binomial powers $x^r$.

Sections 5 to 10 are guides to the rest of the power series library. Section 5 puts the product topology on power series and uses it for evaluation and for Gauss norms. Section 6 treats the substitution of power series into power series. Section 7 covers derivatives, truncations and the expansion $X \mapsto X^p$. Section 8 collects the classical series, such as the exponential, the logarithm and the binomial series, and the Hilbert polynomials of rational functions $p/(1 - X)^d$. Section 9 studies the orders of a power series in several variables and the renaming of variables. Section 10 proves the Weierstrass division and preparation theorems over a complete local ring and defines the restricted power series over a normed ring.

The chapter needs the ideals of Chapter 1, the localization of Chapter 2 for the Laurent series, the Noetherian rings of Chapter 3 for the last theorem of Section 2, and the polynomial rings of Volume 6, Chapter 5 and of Chapter 6. In Mathlib a power series in one variable is a power series in several variables indexed by the type with one element, so Sections 1 and 2 use one theory.

Volume 10 uses $k[[X]]$ as the first example of a discrete valuation ring and of a complete local ring, and the Laurent series field $k((X))$ as the completion of the field of rational functions $k(X)$ at the place $X = 0$. Power series also return in the analysis volumes, where a series with a positive radius of convergence defines an analytic function.
