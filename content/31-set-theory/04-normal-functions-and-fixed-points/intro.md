# Normal functions and fixed points

Chapter 3 found that the maps $\beta \mapsto \alpha + \beta$, $\beta \mapsto \alpha \cdot \beta$ and $\beta \mapsto \alpha^\beta$ are strictly increasing and continuous at limit ordinals. Maps with these two properties are the normal functions. Each normal function has fixed points above each ordinal, and the fixed points are again the values of a normal function. This chapter uses this fact as a machine that makes large countable ordinals.

Section 1 defines the normal functions, proves the fixed point lemma and defines the derivative, which enumerates the fixed points. It also enumerates closed unbounded classes of ordinals, puts the order topology on the ordinals, and compares the transfinite iteration of a monotone map with the fixed point theorem of Knaster and Tarski of Volume 4. Section 2 asks which ordinals are closed under addition, multiplication and exponentiation. The answers are the powers $\omega^\alpha$, the powers $\omega^{\omega^\alpha}$ and the epsilon numbers. The section then iterates the derivative transfinitely, which gives the Veblen hierarchy and the ordinal $\Gamma_0$, and it defines fundamental sequences.

The chapter needs Chapters 2 and 3. Chapter 5 applies the normal functions to the alephs, and Chapter 6 applies them to cofinality.
