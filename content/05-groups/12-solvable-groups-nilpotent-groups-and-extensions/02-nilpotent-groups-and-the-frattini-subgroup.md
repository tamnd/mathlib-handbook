## Nilpotent groups and the Frattini subgroup

### Overview

A commutative group has $[G, G] = 1$, and its center is the whole group. A nilpotent group is a group that reaches this state in finitely many steps. There are two ways to count the steps. The lower central series starts at $G$ and takes commutators with $G$ until it reaches $1$. The upper central series starts at $1$ and takes the center modulo the last term until it reaches $G$. The two series need the same number of steps, and this number is the nilpotency class.

This section describes three modules. `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Nilpotent.LowerCentralSeries` defines the lower central series of a subgroup and descending central series. `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Nilpotent` defines the upper central series, nilpotent groups and the nilpotency class, and proves the standard structure results: finite $p$-groups are nilpotent, and a finite group is nilpotent if and only if it is the product of its Sylow subgroups. `Mathlib.GroupTheory.Frattini` defines the Frattini subgroup, the intersection of the maximal subgroups, and proves that it is nilpotent in a finite group.

The section uses the commutator subgroups of Chapter 5, the derived series of the section before this one, and the Sylow subgroups and $p$-groups of Chapter 8.

### The library around it

#### The lower central series

For a subgroup $H$ of $G$, `Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries H n` is defined by $\gamma_0(H) = H$ and $\gamma_{n+1}(H) = [\gamma_n(H), H]$, and the two equations are `Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_zero` and `Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_succ`. The commutators are taken in $G$, so all terms are subgroups of $G$, not of the type $H$. The lower central series of the group itself is the case $H = ⊤$, written `(⊤ : Subgroup G).lowerCentralSeries n`. Its first term is `commutator G` by `Subgroup.top_lowerCentralSeries_one`, and `Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_one_eq_bot_iff` says that this term is trivial exactly when $G$ is commutative.

The series decreases (`Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_antitone`), each term lies in $H$ (`Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_le_self`) and the construction is monotone in $H$ (`Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_mono`). A homomorphism maps the series into the series (`Subgroup.map_lowerCentralSeries`), and the series commutes with products (`Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_prod`) and with finite products (`Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_pi_of_finite`). The equation `Subgroup.top_subtype_lowerCentralSeries` relates the two points of view: the image in $G$ of the lower central series of the group $H$ is $\gamma_n(H)$.

A sequence $(H_n)$ is a descending central series, `Subgroup.IsDescendingCentralSeries`, if $H_0 = G$ and $[x, g] \in H_{n+1}$ for all $x \in H_n$ and $g \in G$. The lower central series is one (`Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_isDescendingCentralSeries`), and it is the fastest one.

**Proposition** (`Subgroup.descending_central_series_ge_lower`). If $(H_n)$ is a descending central series, then $\gamma_n(G) \le H_n$ for all $n$.

*Proof.* Induction on $n$. Both sides are $G$ at $n = 0$. If $\gamma_n(G) \le H_n$, then $\gamma_{n+1}(G)$ is generated by commutators $[x, g]$ with $x \in \gamma_n(G) \le H_n$, and each of these is in $H_{n+1}$. $\square$

The series also bounds the derived series.

**Proposition** (`Subgroup.derivedSeries_le_lowerCentralSeries_top`). $G^{(n)} \le \gamma_n(G)$ for all $n$.

*Proof.* Induction on $n$. If $G^{(n)} \le \gamma_n(G)$, then $G^{(n+1)} = [G^{(n)}, G^{(n)}] \le [\gamma_n(G), G] = \gamma_{n+1}(G)$, by monotonicity of the commutator in each argument. $\square$

#### The upper central series

The upper central series `Subgroup.upperCentralSeries G n` has $Z_0 = ⊥$ and $Z_{n+1} = \{x \mid \forall y,\ [x, y] \in Z_n\}$. The step is the subgroup `Subgroup.upperCentralSeriesStep`, and `Subgroup.mem_upperCentralSeries_succ_iff` states the membership condition. The step is the preimage of the center of $G/Z_n$ (`Subgroup.upperCentralSeriesStep_eq_comap_center`), so $Z_1$ is the center (`Subgroup.upperCentralSeries_one`). Each term is characteristic, by an instance, and the series increases (`Subgroup.upperCentralSeries_mono`).

A sequence $(H_n)$ is an ascending central series, `Subgroup.IsAscendingCentralSeries`, if $H_0 = ⊥$ and $[x, g] \in H_n$ for all $x \in H_{n+1}$ and $g \in G$. The upper central series is one (`Subgroup.upperCentralSeries_isAscendingCentralSeries`), and it is again the fastest.

**Proposition** (`Subgroup.ascending_central_series_le_upper`). If $(H_n)$ is an ascending central series, then $H_n \le Z_n$ for all $n$.

*Proof.* Induction on $n$, with $H_0 = ⊥ = Z_0$. If $H_n \le Z_n$ and $x \in H_{n+1}$, then $[x, y] \in H_n \le Z_n$ for each $y$, so $x \in Z_{n+1}$. $\square$

#### Nilpotent groups and the class

The class `Group.IsNilpotent G` says that $Z_n = ⊤$ for some $n$, and `Group.isNilpotent_iff` states it. The library gives the other forms as equivalences.

| Lean name | Statement |
|---|---|
| `Subgroup.nilpotent_iff_finite_ascending_central_series` | $G$ is nilpotent if and only if some ascending central series reaches $⊤$ |
| `Subgroup.nilpotent_iff_finite_descending_central_series` | $G$ is nilpotent if and only if some descending central series reaches $⊥$ |
| `Subgroup.nilpotent_iff_lowerCentralSeries` | $G$ is nilpotent if and only if $\gamma_n(G) = ⊥$ for some $n$ |
| `Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_eq_bot_iff_upperCentralSeries_eq_top` | $\gamma_n(G) = ⊥$ if and only if $Z_n = ⊤$ |
| `Subgroup.isNilpotent_iff_lowerCentralSeries` | a subgroup $S$ is nilpotent if and only if $\gamma_n(S) = ⊥$ for some $n$ |

**Proposition** (`Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_eq_bot_iff_upperCentralSeries_eq_top`). For each $n$, $\gamma_n(G) = ⊥$ if and only if $Z_n = ⊤$.

*Proof.* If $Z_n = ⊤$, the sequence $H_i = Z_{n-i}$ is a descending central series with $H_n = ⊥$ (`Subgroup.is_descending_rev_series_of_is_ascending`). So $\gamma_n(G) \le H_n = ⊥$. If $\gamma_n(G) = ⊥$, the sequence $H_i = \gamma_{n-i}(G)$ is an ascending central series with $H_n = ⊤$ (`Subgroup.is_ascending_rev_series_of_is_descending`), so $⊤ = H_n \le Z_n$. $\square$

The number `Group.nilpotencyClass G` is the least $n$ with $Z_n = ⊤$, and $0$ for a group that is not nilpotent (`Group.nilpotencyClass_of_not_nilpotent`). By the proposition, it is also the least $n$ with $\gamma_n(G) = ⊥$ (`Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries_length_eq_nilpotencyClass`). The class is $0$ exactly for the trivial group (`Group.nilpotencyClass_zero_iff_subsingleton`), and a class at most $1$ means commutative (`Group.IsNilpotent.nilpotencyClass_le_one_iff`). The definition `commGroupOfNilpotencyClass` builds the `CommGroup` structure from this bound. Passing to $G / Z(G)$ removes exactly one step: `Group.nilpotencyClass_quotient_center` gives the class of the quotient as $c - 1$, and `Group.nilpotencyClass_eq_quotient_center_add_one` gives $c$ as the class of the quotient plus $1$ for a nontrivial group.

The induction principle `Group.nilpotent_center_quotient_ind` uses this fact: to prove a property of all nilpotent groups, prove it for trivial groups, and prove that it passes from $G/Z(G)$ to $G$. The converse direction is `Group.of_quotient_center_nilpotent`: if $G/Z(G)$ is nilpotent, so is $G$.

#### Stability and first consequences

Instances make subgroups (`Subgroup.isNilpotent`), quotients (`Group.nilpotent_quotient_of_nilpotent`), commutative groups (`CommGroup.isNilpotent`), binary products (`Group.isNilpotent_prod`) and finite products (`Group.isNilpotent_pi`) nilpotent. An infinite product of nilpotent groups is nilpotent when the classes are bounded (`Group.isNilpotent_pi_of_bounded_class`). The class does not grow under these constructions: see `Subgroup.nilpotencyClass_le`, `Group.nilpotencyClass_quotient_le` and `Group.nilpotencyClass_prod`, which gives the maximum of the two classes. A group whose quotient by a central kernel is nilpotent is nilpotent (`Subgroup.isNilpotent_of_ker_le_center`).

**Proposition** (`Group.IsNilpotent.to_isSolvable`). A nilpotent group is solvable.

*Proof.* If $\gamma_n(G) = ⊥$, then $G^{(n)} \le \gamma_n(G) = ⊥$ by `Subgroup.derivedSeries_le_lowerCentralSeries_top`. $\square$

The same name is also an instance. A nontrivial nilpotent group has a nontrivial center (`Group.IsNilpotent.center_ne_bot`), and more strongly each nontrivial normal subgroup meets the center (`Group.IsNilpotent.inf_center_ne_bot_of_normal`). A nilpotent group satisfies the normalizer condition: each proper subgroup is properly contained in its normalizer (`Group.normalizerCondition_of_isNilpotent`, with the predicate `NormalizerCondition`). In a nilpotent group, two elements of coprime orders commute (`Group.IsNilpotent.commute_of_orderOf_coprime`), and each maximal subgroup has prime index (`Subgroup.isCoatom_iff_index_prime`).

#### Finite nilpotent groups

**Theorem** (`IsPGroup.isNilpotent`). A finite $p$-group is nilpotent.

*Proof sketch.* Use induction on the order. The trivial group is nilpotent. A nontrivial finite $p$-group has a nontrivial center, by the class equation of Chapter 6. The quotient $G / Z(G)$ is a smaller $p$-group, so it is nilpotent by induction, and `Group.of_quotient_center_nilpotent` gives that $G$ is nilpotent. $\square$

The main characterization is a list of equivalent conditions, stated with `List.TFAE`.

**Theorem** (`Group.isNilpotent_of_finite_tfae`). For a finite group $G$, these are equivalent: $G$ is nilpotent; $G$ satisfies the normalizer condition; each maximal subgroup is normal; each Sylow subgroup is normal; $G$ is isomorphic to the product of its Sylow subgroups, one for each prime divisor of $|G|$.

*Proof sketch.* Nilpotent implies the normalizer condition, as above. If $M$ is maximal and $M < N_G(M)$, then $N_G(M) = G$, so $M$ is normal. If a Sylow subgroup $P$ is not normal, its normalizer lies in some maximal subgroup $M$, which is normal; the Frattini argument then gives $G = M N_G(P) = M$, a contradiction. If all Sylow subgroups are normal, they commute pairwise and have coprime orders, so their product is direct. The product of finitely many $p$-groups is nilpotent by `IsPGroup.isNilpotent` and `Group.isNilpotent_pi`. $\square$

The last step alone is `Group.isNilpotent_of_product_of_sylow_group`. Two divisibility results follow from the structure: a finite nilpotent group has a normal subgroup of each order dividing $|G|$ (`Group.IsNilpotent.exists_normal_card_eq_of_dvd_card`) and of each index dividing $|G|$ (`Group.IsNilpotent.exists_normal_index_eq_of_dvd_card`). Each prime divisor of $|G|$ divides the order of the center (`Group.IsNilpotent.prime_dvd_card_center`). The predicate `Group.IsVirtuallyNilpotent` says that a subgroup of finite index is nilpotent, and `Group.IsNilpotent.isVirtuallyNilpotent` is the trivial case.

#### The Frattini subgroup

The Frattini subgroup `frattini G` is the intersection of all maximal subgroups. In the library, a maximal subgroup is a coatom of the lattice `Subgroup G` (the predicate `IsCoatom`), and the definition is `Order.radical` of this lattice. It is characteristic (`frattini_characteristic`), and a surjection maps it into the Frattini subgroup of the target (`frattini_le_comap_frattini_of_surjective`).

**Proposition** (`frattini_le_coatom`). The Frattini subgroup lies in each maximal subgroup.

*Proof.* It is the intersection of these subgroups. $\square$

**Proposition** (`frattini_nongenerating`). Assume that each proper subgroup lies in a maximal subgroup (`IsCoatomic (Subgroup G)`, true for finite groups). If $K \sqcup \Phi(G) = G$, then $K = G$.

*Proof.* Suppose $K \ne G$. Take a maximal $M \ge K$. Then $\Phi(G) \le M$ by the proposition before, so $G = K \sqcup \Phi(G) \le M$. This contradicts $M \ne G$. $\square$

So the elements of $\Phi(G)$ are the non-generators: they can be removed from each generating set. The last result, `frattini_nilpotent`, says that $\Phi(G)$ is nilpotent when $G$ is finite. Its proof shows that each Sylow subgroup $P$ of $\Phi(G)$ is normal in $\Phi(G)$: the Frattini argument gives $G = \Phi(G) N_G(P)$, the non-generator property gives $N_G(P) = G$, and the fourth condition of `Group.isNilpotent_of_finite_tfae` applies.

#### Common errors

1. `Subgroup.lowerCentralSeries` takes a subgroup, not a type. The lower central series of the group $G$ is `(⊤ : Subgroup G).lowerCentralSeries n`. The lemmas about this series of the group have `top` in the name, as in `Subgroup.top_lowerCentralSeries_one` and `Subgroup.derivedSeries_le_lowerCentralSeries_top`.
2. `Group.nilpotencyClass G` is $0$ for a group that is not nilpotent. A statement about the class needs `Group.IsNilpotent G` in the context, or it says nothing useful.
3. The upper central series is indexed so that $Z_1$ is the center, not $Z_0$. The lower central series has $\gamma_0 = G$ and $\gamma_1 = [G, G]$, so a group of class $c$ has $\gamma_c = ⊥$ and $Z_c = ⊤$.

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute the upper and lower central series of the dihedral group of order $8$ and of the quaternion group, and show that both have class $2$.
2. (A) Show that $S_3$ is solvable but not nilpotent, by computing its center.
3. (B) Show that the dihedral group of order $2^n$ is nilpotent of class $n - 1$ for $n \ge 2$.
4. (B) Show that in a finite nilpotent group, an element of order $m$ and an element of order $n$ with $\gcd(m, n) = 1$ commute. Prove it from the decomposition of $G$ as the product of its Sylow subgroups, and compare with `Group.IsNilpotent.commute_of_orderOf_coprime`.
5. (B) Compute the Frattini subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}/p^n$, of $\mathbb{Z}/6$ and of the quaternion group.
6. (C) Let $G$ be a finite group with $G / \Phi(G)$ nilpotent. Show that $G$ is nilpotent. Hint: show that each Sylow subgroup is normal with the Frattini argument and `frattini_nongenerating`.
