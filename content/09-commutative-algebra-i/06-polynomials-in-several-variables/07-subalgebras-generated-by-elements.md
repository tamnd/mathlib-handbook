## Subalgebras generated by elements

### Overview

Let $A$ be an $R$-algebra and $s \subseteq A$ a set. The subalgebra generated by $s$ is the smallest subalgebra of $A$ that contains $s$. In Lean it is `Algebra.adjoin R s`, and the notation `R[x]` is `Algebra.adjoin R {x}`. Its elements are the values of polynomials in the elements of $s$ with coefficients from $R$. The definition and the lattice lemmas sit in `Mathlib.Algebra.Algebra.Subalgebra.Lattice`. The modules of this section hold the next layer: the description by polynomials, finite generation, towers of algebras, and the special case of one element that is algebraic or transcendental.

These ideas carry the finiteness theory of commutative algebra. A finitely generated algebra is a quotient of a polynomial ring in finitely many variables (the previous sections of this chapter), and the Hilbert basis theorem of Chapter 3 then makes it Noetherian. The section on `AdjoinRoot` in Chapter 5 gives the model $R[X]/(f)$ that describes $R[x]$ for an algebraic $x$.

### The library around it

#### The basic lemmas

`Algebra.adjoin R` is the left adjoint of the coercion from subalgebras to sets: `Algebra.gc` is the Galois connection, and `Algebra.adjoin_le_iff` says $R[s] \le S \iff s \subseteq S$. Thus `Algebra.subset_adjoin` and `Algebra.adjoin_le` are the two directions, and `Algebra.adjoin_union` gives $R[s \cup t] = R[s] \sqcup R[t]$. The tool for a property of all elements of $R[s]$ is `Algebra.adjoin_induction`: check the property on $s$, on the image of $R$, and under sums and products. As an $R$-module, $R[s]$ is the span of the monoid generated by $s$ (`Algebra.adjoin_eq_span`).

`Mathlib.RingTheory.Adjoin.Basic` adds the lemmas for a tower $R \to S \to A$. The main one is `Algebra.adjoin_union_eq_adjoin_adjoin`:
$$R[s \cup t] = (R[s])[t],$$
where the right side is a subalgebra over the ring $R[s]$, seen as an $R$-subalgebra with `Subalgebra.restrictScalars`. Proof sketch: both sides are $R$-subalgebras that contain $s$ and $t$, and each is contained in the other because each is the smallest one with that property. The lemmas `Algebra.adjoin_algebraMap`, `Algebra.adjoin_adjoin_of_tower` and `Algebra.restrictScalars_adjoin` are of the same kind. For products, `Algebra.adjoin_prod_le` and `Algebra.adjoin_inl_union_inr_eq_prod` compare $R[s \times t]$ with $R[s] \times R[t]$. The lemma `Algebra.pow_smul_mem_adjoin_smul` is a denominator statement: if $x \in R[s]$, then $r^n x \in R[rs]$ for all large $n$.

#### One element and polynomials

For one element, `Algebra.adjoin_singleton_eq_range_aeval` says
$$R[x] = \operatorname{range}(\operatorname{aeval}_x : R[X] \to A).$$
Proof: the range of $\operatorname{aeval}_x$ is a subalgebra that contains $x = \operatorname{aeval}_x(X)$, so it contains $R[x]$. Each $p(x)$ is built from $x$ and constants by sums and products, so it lies in $R[x]$. The two directions are `Algebra.adjoin_mem_exists_aeval` and `Polynomial.aeval_mem_adjoin_singleton`. For a proof about all elements of $R[x]$, `Algebra.adjoin_singleton_induction` reduces the goal to the elements $p(x)$. Since $R[x]$ is the image of a commutative ring, the instances `Algebra.instCommSemiringAdjoinSingleton` and `Algebra.instCommRingAdjoinSingleton` make it commutative, also when $A$ is not commutative. `Polynomial.adjoin_X` says $R[X] = \top$ inside the polynomial ring. For several elements, `Algebra.exists_mvPolynomial_aeval_eq_of_mem_adjoin` writes each element of $R[s]$ as the value of a multivariate polynomial.

`Mathlib.RingTheory.Adjoin.Singleton` treats the image of $R[b]$ in a larger ring. For a tower $A \to B \to C$ and $b \in B$, `Algebra.RingHom.adjoinAlgebraMap` is the ring map $A[b] \to A[b']$, where $b'$ is the image of $b$ in $C$. It is surjective (`Algebra.RingHom.adjoinAlgebraMap_surjective`), and `Algebra.RingHom.adjoinAlgebraMapEquiv` is the isomorphism when $B \to C$ is injective, in the form `FaithfulSMul B C`.

#### Algebraic and transcendental elements

The type of $R[x]$ depends on whether $x$ satisfies a polynomial relation. Over a field $F$, `AlgEquiv.adjoinSingletonEquivAdjoinRootMinpoly` is the isomorphism
$$F[x] \cong F[X]/(\operatorname{minpoly}_F x).$$
Proof sketch: by the first isomorphism theorem $F[x] \cong F[X]/\ker(\operatorname{aeval}_x)$. The kernel is an ideal of the principal ideal domain $F[X]$, and the minimal polynomial generates it. The statement holds also when $x$ is transcendental, because then the minimal polynomial is $0$ and the quotient is $F[X]$. The inverse map is `AdjoinRoot.Minpoly.toAdjoin`. With this isomorphism, `Algebra.adjoin.liftSingleton` builds an $F$-algebra map $F[x] \to T$ from an element $y \in T$ with $(\operatorname{minpoly}_F x)(y) = 0$.

The same module has results for extensions of fields. `Polynomial.lift_of_splits` gives an $F$-algebra map $F[s] \to L$ when $s$ is a finite set of integral elements whose minimal polynomials split in $L$. `IsIntegral.mem_range_algHom_of_minpoly_splits` says that an integral $x \in L$ is in the range of each map $K \to L$ when the minimal polynomial of $x$ splits in $K$. The family `minpoly_add_algebraMap_splits`, `minpoly_sub_algebraMap_splits`, `minpoly_neg_splits` and their variants says that splitting of the minimal polynomial survives $x \mapsto x + r$, $x \mapsto x - r$ and $x \mapsto -x$ for $r \in K$. The rank bound `Subalgebra.adjoin_rank_le` says that the $E$-subalgebra generated by a free $F$-subalgebra $L$ has $E$-rank at most the $F$-rank of $L$.

When $x$ is transcendental over $R$, `Polynomial.algEquivOfTranscendental` is the isomorphism $R[X] \cong R[x]$, $X \mapsto x$. It is $\operatorname{aeval}_x$ with its range restricted, and it is injective by the definition of `Transcendental`. The simp lemmas `Polynomial.algEquivOfTranscendental_apply` and `Polynomial.algEquivOfTranscendental_symm_aeval` compute it. All properties of $R[X]$ then pass to $R[x]$: `Transcendental.uniqueFactorizationMonoid_adjoin` (over a factorial $R$) and `Transcendental.euclideanDomainAdjoin` (over a field). The evaluation map `Algebra.adjoin.evalOfTranscendental` sends $x$ to a chosen value $c$, and `Algebra.adjoin.evalOfTranscendental_eq_zero_iff` is the factor theorem: an element of $R[x]$ maps to $0$ under $x \mapsto c$ exactly when $x - c$ divides it.

The bivariate module applies this to two variables. `Polynomial.Bivariate.Transcendental.algEquivAdjoin` is the isomorphism $R[X][Y] \cong (R[x])[Y]$ for a transcendental $x$. Its main use is `IsAlgebraic.adjoin_singleton`: if $x$ and $y$ are transcendental over $R$ and $y$ is algebraic over $R[x]$, then $x$ is algebraic over $R[y]$. The proof swaps the two variables of a nonzero polynomial $P(x, Y)$ with $P(x, y) = 0$.

#### Finitely generated subalgebras

`Subalgebra.FG S` says that $S = R[t]$ for a finite set $t$. The definition uses a `Finset`, and `Subalgebra.fg_def` restates it with a finite `Set`. The table lists the closure properties.

| Lean name | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| `Subalgebra.fg_bot` | $\bot$ is finitely generated |
| `Subalgebra.fg_adjoin_finset` | $R[t]$ is finitely generated for a finite set $t$ |
| `Subalgebra.FG.sup` | $S, S'$ finitely generated $\Rightarrow S \sqcup S'$ finitely generated |
| `Subalgebra.FG.map` | the image of a finitely generated subalgebra is finitely generated |
| `Subalgebra.fg_of_fg_map` | the converse for an injective map |
| `Subalgebra.FG.prod` | $S \times T$ is finitely generated |
| `Subalgebra.fg_top` | $\top : \operatorname{Subalgebra} R\ S$ is finitely generated $\iff S$ is |
| `Subalgebra.fg_of_fg_toSubmodule` | module-finite implies algebra-finite |
| `Subalgebra.fg_of_noetherian` | each subalgebra of a Noetherian module is finitely generated |

The proof of `Subalgebra.FG.sup` is one line: if $S = R[t]$ and $S' = R[t']$, then $S \sqcup S' = R[t \cup t']$ by `Algebra.adjoin_union`. The predicate `Algebra.FiniteType R A` says that $\top$ is finitely generated, and `Subalgebra.fg_iff_finiteType` connects the two. For a proof about subalgebras of a Noetherian module, `Subalgebra.induction_on_adjoin` adds one element at a time.

The theorem `isNoetherianRing_of_fg` says that a finitely generated subalgebra of an algebra over a Noetherian ring is a Noetherian ring. Proof: $S = R[t]$ is the range of $\operatorname{aeval}$ on the polynomial ring in the variables $t$ (`Algebra.adjoin_eq_range`). That ring is Noetherian by the Hilbert basis theorem, and the range of an algebra map from a Noetherian ring is Noetherian (`AlgHom.isNoetherianRing_range`). The special case `is_noetherian_subring_closure` is for the subring generated by a finite subset of a commutative ring.

#### Towers

`Mathlib.RingTheory.Adjoin.Tower` holds the transitivity of finite generation. `Algebra.fg_trans'` says: if $S$ is a finitely generated $R$-algebra and $A$ is a finitely generated $S$-algebra, then $A$ is a finitely generated $R$-algebra. Proof sketch: take generators $s$ of $S$ over $R$ and $t$ of $A$ over $S$. Then $R[s \cup t] = (R[s])[t]$ by `Algebra.adjoin_union_eq_adjoin_adjoin`, and this is $S[t] = A$. The module version is `Algebra.fg_trans`.

The Artin-Tate lemma `fg_of_fg_of_fg` goes in the other direction. Let $A \to B \to C$ be a tower with $A$ Noetherian, $C$ a finitely generated $A$-algebra, $C$ a finite $B$-module, and $B \to C$ injective. Then $B$ is a finitely generated $A$-algebra. Proof sketch: `exists_subalgebra_of_fg` gives a finitely generated $A$-subalgebra $B_0 \subseteq B$ over which $C$ is still a finite module. It is generated by the coefficients that express the algebra generators of $C$ and the products of the module generators in terms of the module generators. Then $B_0$ is Noetherian by `isNoetherianRing_of_fg`, so the $B_0$-submodule $B$ of the finite $B_0$-module $C$ is finite. Thus $B$ is finitely generated over $B_0$, and `Algebra.fg_trans'` finishes the proof. The lemma is one route to the Nullstellensatz of this chapter.

#### Dimension and base change

`Subalgebra.rank_sup_le_of_free` and `Subalgebra.finrank_sup_le_of_free` bound the size of a compositum: for subalgebras $A$ and $B$ of a commutative $R$-algebra that are free $R$-modules,
$$\operatorname{finrank}_R(A \sqcup B) \le \operatorname{finrank}_R A \cdot \operatorname{finrank}_R B.$$
Proof sketch: the products $a_i b_j$ of basis vectors span $A \sqcup B$ as an $R$-module, because $A \sqcup B$ is the span of the products $ab$ and the span of the $a_i b_j$ is closed under multiplication. Finally, `exists_fg_and_mem_baseChange` says that each element of $A \otimes_R B$ lies in the base change of some finitely generated subalgebra of $B$. This reduces statements about tensor products to the finitely generated case.

#### Common errors

1. Confusing the two finiteness notions. `Subalgebra.FG` is finite generation as an algebra, and `(Subalgebra.toSubmodule S).FG` is finite generation as a module. The polynomial ring $R[X]$ is the first and not the second. `Subalgebra.fg_of_fg_toSubmodule` goes only from the second to the first.
2. Forgetting the base ring of an `Algebra.adjoin` term. In a tower, `Algebra.adjoin S t` is an $S$-subalgebra and `Algebra.adjoin R t` is an $R$-subalgebra, and they are different types. Use `Subalgebra.restrictScalars` to compare them, as in `Algebra.adjoin_union_eq_adjoin_adjoin`.
3. Using `Polynomial.algEquivOfTranscendental` for an algebraic element. For an algebraic $x$ over a field, use `AlgEquiv.adjoinSingletonEquivAdjoinRootMinpoly`, and the power basis of the section on `AdjoinRoot` when $x$ is integral.

### Exercises

1. (A) Use `Algebra.adjoin_singleton_eq_range_aeval` to show that $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt 2] = \{a + b\sqrt 2 : a, b \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ inside $\mathbb{R}$.
2. (A) Show that $R[s \cup t] = R[s] \sqcup R[t]$ and deduce `Subalgebra.FG.sup`.
3. (B) Show that $\mathbb{Q}$ is not a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-algebra. Hint: a finite set of fractions has only finitely many primes in its denominators.
4. (B) Let $x \in \mathbb{C}$ be transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$. Show that $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ is a unique factorization domain and that its units are the nonzero rationals.
5. (B) Give subfields $A, B$ of $\mathbb{C}$ with $[A : \mathbb{Q}] = [B : \mathbb{Q}] = 2$ and $[A \sqcup B : \mathbb{Q}] = 4$, and give an example where the bound of `Subalgebra.finrank_sup_le_of_free` is not an equality.
6. (C) Use `fg_of_fg_of_fg` to show: if a field $L$ is a finitely generated algebra over a subfield $K$, and $L$ is a finite extension of a purely transcendental field $K(x_1, \dots, x_n)$, then $n = 0$.
