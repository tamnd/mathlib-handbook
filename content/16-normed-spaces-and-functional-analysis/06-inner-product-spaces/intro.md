# Inner product spaces

An inner product gives a normed space a geometry with angles and right angles. This chapter studies the spaces whose norm comes from an inner product. Section 1 defines the inner product over $\mathbb{R}$ and $\mathbb{C}$ in the convention of Mathlib, which is conjugate linear in the first argument, and proves the inequality of Cauchy-Schwarz, the parallelogram law and the polarization identities. It proves the theorem of Jordan and von Neumann in the real case: a norm comes from an inner product if and only if it satisfies the parallelogram law. Section 2 studies orthogonality: the theorem of Pythagoras, orthonormal families, the inequality of Bessel, the process of Gram-Schmidt and orthonormal bases in finite dimension. Section 3 adds completeness. A Hilbert space has a Hilbert basis by Zorn's lemma, each vector is the sum of its expansion in the basis, and the identity of Parseval makes each Hilbert space isometric to a space $\ell^2(\iota)$.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 to 3 and the linear algebra of Volume 7. Section 3 also uses the series of Chapter 1, Section 3, and Zorn's lemma from Volume 4. Chapter 7 uses all three sections for the orthogonal projection and the theorem of Riesz.
