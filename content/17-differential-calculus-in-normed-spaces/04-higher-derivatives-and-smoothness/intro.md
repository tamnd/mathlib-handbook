# Higher derivatives and smoothness

The derivative of a map $f : E \to F$ between normed spaces is a map $Df : E \to L(E, F)$ into another normed space. It can have a derivative again. This chapter studies the derivatives of higher order, the classes $C^n$ and $C^\infty$, and the operations that preserve them.

Section 1 defines the second derivative $D^2 f(x)$ as a continuous bilinear map. It proves the theorem of Schwarz: if $Df$ is differentiable at $x$, then $D^2 f(x)$ is symmetric. Section 2 defines the iterated derivative $D^k f(x)$, a continuous $k$-multilinear map, and the class $C^n$ in two forms: recursively, through $Df$, and through a family of maps that are derivatives of each other. The section proves that the two forms agree. Section 3 proves that linear and bilinear maps are smooth, that compositions, products and the inversion in a Banach algebra preserve $C^n$, and it builds bump functions from $e^{-1/x}$.

The chapter needs the derivative and the chain rule of Chapters 1 and 2, the mean value inequality of Chapter 3 for the theorem of Schwarz, and the higher derivatives of one variable of Volume 15, Chapter 4. It uses the spaces of continuous multilinear maps of Volume 16.

Chapter 5 uses the iterated derivative for Taylor's formula in several variables and the symmetric Hessian for the second derivative test. Chapter 6 uses the class $C^k$ and the smoothness of the inversion of operators in the inverse function theorem. Chapter 7 uses the order $\omega$ for analytic maps.
