# W types and polynomial functors

Lists, trees and natural numbers are inductive types, and streams and lazy sequences are coinductive types. This chapter gives one description of all of them. The first section defines the W type of a family of arities: a tree whose nodes carry a label and one child for each position of the label. It proves the equivalence of a W type with its one step unfolding, computes the cardinal of a W type, and gives the conditions for a W type to be infinite. It then defines univariate polynomial functors, their W types and M types, and proves equalities of M type elements by bisimulation. It closes with quotients of polynomial functors (QPFs) and their initial and final fixed points. The examples are the natural numbers, binary trees with 1, 2 and 5 trees of sizes 1, 2 and 3, streams as an M type, and multisets as a QPF. The second section takes functors of several type arguments. It defines type vectors and their arrows, multivariate polynomial functors and their W and M types, and multivariate QPFs. It proves that fixed points of QPFs are again QPFs, so lists of trees and other nested types fit the method.

The chapter needs inductive types and universes from Volume 1, cardinal arithmetic from Volume 24, the streams of Chapter 4 and the sequences of Chapter 5, and the functors of Chapter 6. The cardinal results use the bounds of Volume 24.

Chapter 8 leaves the abstract types and turns to the data of a machine: fixed width integers, bit vectors, floating point numbers, characters and strings. These types are finite, so they need no fixed points, but the same method applies: give a type a precise model, then prove facts about the model.
