## Valuations

### Overview

The $p$-adic valuation $v_p$ of $\mathbb{Q}$ gives each nonzero rational $x = p^n a/b$, with $p \nmid ab$, the integer $v_p(x) = n$. It satisfies $v_p(xy) = v_p(x) + v_p(y)$ and $v_p(x + y) \ge \min(v_p(x), v_p(y))$. These two rules define an additive valuation. The function $|x|_p = p^{-v_p(x)}$ turns them into $|xy|_p = |x|_p |y|_p$ and $|x + y|_p \le \max(|x|_p, |y|_p)$, the rules of a multiplicative valuation, or an ultrametric absolute value.

Mathlib uses the multiplicative form, with values in an ordered commutative monoid with zero, so that $0$ has value $0$ and the value group can be any ordered group, not only a subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$. This section gives both forms, proves the basic rules, and attaches to a valuation on a field its ring of integers $\{x : v(x) \le 1\}$.

### Definitions

A linearly ordered commutative group with zero is a set $\Gamma_0 = \Gamma \cup \{0\}$, where $\Gamma$ is a linearly ordered commutative group, written multiplicatively, and $0$ is a new element with $0 < \gamma$ and $0 \cdot \gamma = 0$ for all $\gamma \in \Gamma$. Examples: $\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ with multiplication, and $\mathbb{Z}_{(0)} = \{0\} \cup \{t^n : n \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ with $t^m \le t^n$ when $m \le n$.

**Definition (Valuation).** Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $\Gamma_0$ a linearly ordered commutative monoid with zero. A valuation on $R$ with values in $\Gamma_0$ is a map $v : R \to \Gamma_0$ with

1. $v(0) = 0$ and $v(1) = 1$;
2. $v(xy) = v(x) v(y)$;
3. $v(x + y) \le \max(v(x), v(y))$.

The support of $v$ is the set $\operatorname{supp} v = \{x : v(x) = 0\}$. The value group of $v$ is the subgroup of $\Gamma$ generated by the nonzero values.

**Definition (Additive valuation).** An additive valuation on $R$ with values in a linearly ordered additive group $\Gamma$ is a map $w : R \to \Gamma \cup \{\infty\}$ with $w(0) = \infty$, $w(1) = 0$, $w(xy) = w(x) + w(y)$ and $w(x + y) \ge \min(w(x), w(y))$.

The two notions are the same up to reversing the order. Given $w$, put $v(x) = t^{w(x)}$ in the multiplicative group $\{t^n\}$ with reversed order, and $v(x) = 0$ when $w(x) = \infty$. A valuation on a field $K$ has support $0$, since $v(x) v(x^{-1}) = 1$ for $x \ne 0$.

::: mathlib

Its lemmas are in the namespace `AddValuation`: `AddValuation.map_mul` says `v (x * y) = v x + v y` and `AddValuation.map_add` says `min (v x) (v y) ≤ v (x + y)`. The additive valuation of a discrete valuation ring (Section 3) has this type with `Γ₀ = ℕ∞`.

**Definition (Equivalent valuations).** Two valuations $v_1$ and $v_2$ on $R$, with values in possibly different groups, are equivalent if $v_1(r) \le v_1(s) \Leftrightarrow v_2(r) \le v_2(s)$ for all $r, s \in R$.

For example, $|x|_p$ and $|x|_p^2$ are equivalent. Equivalence keeps the order and forgets the numbers.

::: mathlib

**Definition (Ring of integers of a valuation).** Let $v$ be a valuation on a field $K$. Its ring of integers is $\mathcal{O}_v = \{x \in K : v(x) \le 1\}$, and $\mathfrak m_v = \{x \in K : v(x) < 1\}$.

::: mathlib

### Theorems and proofs

**Theorem (Rules for a valuation).** Let $v$ be a valuation on $R$ with values in a linearly ordered commutative group with zero.

1. $v(-x) = v(x)$, and $v(x - y) = v(y - x)$.
2. If $v(x) \ne v(y)$, then $v(x + y) = \max(v(x), v(y))$.
3. If $R$ is a field, $v(x^{-1}) = v(x)^{-1}$ for $x \ne 0$.
4. $v(x_1 + \cdots + x_n) \le \max_i v(x_i)$.

*Proof.* (1) $v(-1)^2 = v(1) = 1$, and in an ordered group the only element with square $1$ is $1$: if $\gamma < 1$ then $\gamma^2 < \gamma < 1$, and similarly for $\gamma > 1$. So $v(-1) = 1$ and $v(-x) = v(-1) v(x) = v(x)$.

(2) Say $v(x) < v(y)$. Then $v(x + y) \le v(y)$. Also $y = (x + y) + (-x)$, so $v(y) \le \max(v(x + y), v(x))$. Since $v(y) > v(x)$, the maximum must be $v(x + y)$, and $v(y) \le v(x + y)$.

(3) $v(x) v(x^{-1}) = v(1) = 1$. (4) Induction on $n$, from axiom 3. $\square$

Rule 2 says that in an ultrametric world every triangle is isosceles: of the three values $v(x)$, $v(y)$, $v(x - y)$, the two largest are equal.

::: mathlib

**Theorem (Ring of integers).** Let $v$ be a valuation on a field $K$. Then $\mathcal{O}_v$ is a subring of $K$, and for each $x \in K^\times$, $x \in \mathcal{O}_v$ or $x^{-1} \in \mathcal{O}_v$. The ring $\mathcal{O}_v$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_v$, and its units are the elements of value $1$.

*Proof.* The axioms give $v(0), v(1) \le 1$, and closure under products, negatives and sums. If $v(x) > 1$, then $v(x^{-1}) = v(x)^{-1} < 1$. An element $x \in \mathcal{O}_v$ is a unit of $\mathcal{O}_v$ exactly when $x^{-1} \in \mathcal{O}_v$, that is when $v(x) \ge 1$, so when $v(x) = 1$. So the nonunits are the elements of value $< 1$, which form the set $\mathfrak m_v$. It is closed under sums by the ultrametric inequality and under multiplication by $\mathcal{O}_v$. By the criteria of Chapter 2, Section 1, $\mathcal{O}_v$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_v$. $\square$

**Theorem (Equivalence and integers).** Two valuations $v_1$, $v_2$ on a field $K$ are equivalent if and only if $\mathcal{O}_{v_1} = \mathcal{O}_{v_2}$.

*Proof.* If they are equivalent, take $s = 1$: $v_1(r) \le 1 \Leftrightarrow v_2(r) \le 1$. Conversely, assume $v_1(x) \le 1 \Leftrightarrow v_2(x) \le 1$ for all $x$. Let $r, s \in K$. If $s = 0$, then $v_i(r) \le v_i(s)$ means $r = 0$ for both. If $s \ne 0$, then $v_i(r) \le v_i(s)$ if and only if $v_i(r/s) \le 1$, and this condition is the same for $i = 1, 2$. $\square$

### Examples

**Example ($p$-adic valuation).** Let $p$ be a prime. Each $x \in \mathbb{Q}^\times$ is $x = p^n a/b$ with $p \nmid ab$, and $n = v_p(x)$ is unique by unique factorization. The map $v_p$, with $v_p(0) = \infty$, is an additive valuation with values in $\mathbb{Z}$. For the ultrametric inequality, let $x = p^n a/b$ and $y = p^m c/d$ with $n \le m$. Then $x + y = p^n (ad + p^{m-n} bc)/(bd)$, and $p \nmid bd$, so $v_p(x + y) \ge n$. Its ring of integers is $\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}$. In multiplicative form, $|x|_p = p^{-v_p(x)}$, so $|12|_2 = 1/4$ and $|1/12|_3 = 3$. The value $|1 + 2|_2 = 1 = \max(|1|_2, |2|_2)$ shows rule 2.

**Example (Order of vanishing).** Let $k$ be a field and $a \in k$. Each $f \in k(X)^\times$ is $(X - a)^n g/h$ with $g(a) h(a) \ne 0$, and $v_a(f) = n$ is an additive valuation, the order of vanishing of $f$ at $a$. Its ring of integers is $k[X]_{(X - a)}$, the rational functions defined at $a$. The degree also gives a valuation: $v_\infty(g/h) = \deg h - \deg g$, with ring of integers the rational functions defined at infinity. For $f = (X^2 - 1)/X^3$ the values are $v_1(f) = 1$, $v_0(f) = -3$ and $v_\infty(f) = 1$.

**Example (Trivial valuation).** On any field, $v(x) = 1$ for $x \ne 0$ and $v(0) = 0$ is a valuation, the trivial valuation. Its ring of integers is $K$. On a finite field it is the only valuation up to equivalence, since every nonzero element has finite order and the value group is a torsion subgroup of an ordered group, hence trivial.

**Example (A valuation of rank 2).** On $K = k(X, Y)$, order $\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}$ lexicographically. For a nonzero polynomial $f = \sum a_{ij} X^i Y^j$, let $w(f)$ be the least pair $(i, j)$, in lexicographic order, with $a_{ij} \ne 0$, and extend to quotients by $w(f/g) = w(f) - w(g)$. This is an additive valuation with value group $\mathbb{Z}^2$, which is not a subgroup of $\mathbb{R}$ as an ordered group. Here $w(X) = (1, 0)$ is larger than $w(Y^n) = (0, n)$ for every $n$. This is why Mathlib allows any ordered group of values.

### The library around it

The folder `Mathlib/RingTheory/Valuation/` contains the theory. The files `Basic.lean` (valuations, additive valuations, equivalence, support) and `Integers.lean` (the ring of integers) are the base. `Valuation.comap` pulls a valuation back along a ring map, and `Valuation.supp` is the support, a prime ideal when the values lie in a group with zero. The valuations of a Dedekind domain at its primes are in Chapter 4, Section 3, and the topology of a valued field, through the class `Valued`, is in Volume 14.

Common errors of a learner:

- The order is the multiplicative one: a small value means "divisible by a large power of $p$". The rule $v(x + y) \le \max$ replaces the rule $\min \le w(x + y)$ of the additive form.
- The type `Γ₀` of a valuation is part of the type `Valuation R Γ₀`. Two valuations with different `Γ₀` are compared with `IsEquiv`, not with `=`.
- The type `ℤᵐ⁰`, notation for `WithZero (Multiplicative ℤ)`, is the usual value group for discrete valuations. The element `Multiplicative.ofAdd (-1)` is less than `1` there.

### Exercises

1. (A) Compute $v_2$, $v_3$ and $v_5$ of $360/7$, and $|360/7|_2$.
2. (A) Show that $v(n \cdot 1) \le 1$ for every integer $n$ and every valuation $v$ on a ring.
3. (A) Let $v$ be a valuation and $x_1, \ldots, x_n$ with $v(x_1) > v(x_i)$ for $i \ge 2$. Show that $x_1 + \cdots + x_n \ne 0$.
4. (B) Show that $\operatorname{supp} v$ is a prime ideal, and that $v$ induces a valuation on the fraction field of $R/\operatorname{supp} v$.
5. (B) Show that every valuation on $\mathbb{Q}$ with values in $\mathbb{Z}_{(0)}$ that is not trivial is equivalent to $v_p$ for one prime $p$. Use the ring of integers and the fact that it contains $\mathbb{Z}$.
6. (B) Show that the map $w$ of the rank-2 example satisfies $w(fg) = w(f) + w(g)$ for polynomials, and that its ring of integers contains $Y^{-1} X$.
7. (C) Let $v$ be a valuation on a field $K$. Show directly that $\mathcal{O}_v$ is integrally closed in $K$: if $x \notin \mathcal{O}_v$ satisfies $x^n + a_{n-1}x^{n-1} + \cdots + a_0 = 0$ with $a_i \in \mathcal{O}_v$, compare the values of the terms.

### Notes and history

The $p$-adic absolute value is due to Kürschák (1913), who defined absolute values on fields, building on Hensel's $p$-adic numbers. Krull introduced general valuations with values in any ordered group in 1932. The multiplicative form with a group with zero follows Bourbaki, *Commutative Algebra*, Chapter VI. Mathlib writes valuations in the same multiplicative form.
