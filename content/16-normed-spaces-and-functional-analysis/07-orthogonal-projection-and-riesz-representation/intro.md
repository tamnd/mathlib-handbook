# Orthogonal projection and the Riesz representation

This chapter proves the theorems that make Hilbert spaces simpler than Banach spaces. Section 1 proves that a complete convex set has a unique point nearest to a given vector, by the parallelogram law, and uses it to construct the orthogonal projection onto a complete subspace $K$, with $E = K \oplus K^\perp$ and $K^{\perp\perp} = \overline{K}$. Section 2 proves the representation theorem of Riesz: each bounded functional on a Hilbert space is $\langle y, \cdot \rangle$ for a unique $y$, and the map $y \mapsto \langle y, \cdot \rangle$ is a conjugate linear isometry onto the dual. It also proves the theorem of Lax and Milgram. Section 3 defines the adjoint of a bounded operator, proves $\|A^*A\| = \|A\|^2$, and proves the spectral theorem for a symmetric operator on a finite dimensional inner product space. Section 4 studies positive operators and their order, singular values, the trace, commuting symmetric operators and the mean ergodic theorem. Section 5 defines the adjoint of an unbounded operator, the reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and the standard subspaces. Section 6 gives the norms on matrices as scoped instances, with the entrywise, Frobenius and operator norms, and studies Hermitian and positive semidefinite matrices, their spectrum, their order and the LDL decomposition.

The chapter needs Chapters 1, 2, 4 and 6. Section 3 also uses the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of Volume 7.
