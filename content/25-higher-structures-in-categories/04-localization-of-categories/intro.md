# Localization of categories

This chapter makes the morphisms of a class into isomorphisms in a universal way. Section 1 defines the localization of a category at a class of morphisms by its universal property. It constructs the localization from paths of morphisms and formal inverses, and it shows that the localization is unique up to equivalence. The predicate that a functor is a localization functor is the main tool of Mathlib, because it lets a user work with any model of the localization. The examples are the group completion of a monoid, a reflective subcategory, and a category with two parallel arrows where the localization is much larger than the original category. Section 2 treats the calculus of fractions of Gabriel and Zisman. Under the Ore condition and a cancellation condition, each morphism of the localization is a single fraction, and the equality of fractions has a simple test. As a consequence, the localization of a preadditive category is preadditive and the localization functor is additive. Section 3 extends the universal property to products of categories and to functors of two, three and four variables, shows that a localization functor preserves finite products, and makes the localization of a monoidal, braided or symmetric category at a monoidal class of morphisms into a category of the same kind. Section 4 compares two localizations through a functor that maps one class of morphisms into the other: it gives tests for an equivalence of the localized categories, resolutions of objects, and derivability structures that compute derived functors on resolutions. Section 5 compares the types of morphisms of two localizations of the same class, and it gives models of these types in a chosen universe, for plain morphisms and for shifted morphisms, which are the base of the Ext groups of Volume 26.

The chapter needs Volume 24, mainly the equivalences of Chapter 2 and the reflective subcategories of Chapter 5. It uses the classes of morphisms and the functors that invert them from Chapter 3 of this volume. The examples use monoids and groups from Volume 5 and commutative rings from Volume 6.

Localization is the base of the next chapters. Chapter 5 gives the Verdier quotient of a triangulated category as a localization at a class of morphisms with a calculus of fractions. Volume 26 defines the derived category as the localization of the homotopy category of complexes at the quasi-isomorphisms, and it uses the results of Section 2 to make the derived category additive.
