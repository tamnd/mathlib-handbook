# Finite dimensional normed spaces

This chapter shows that the analysis of a finite dimensional normed space is the analysis of $\mathbb{R}^n$ or $\mathbb{C}^n$, and that the closed unit ball detects finite dimension. Section 1 proves that all norms on a finite dimensional real or complex space are equivalent. The consequences are that each linear map from a finite dimensional space is continuous, that each finite dimensional normed space is complete, and that each finite dimensional subspace of a normed space is closed. The section gives a full proof for the real and complex fields, by the compactness of a sphere, and it explains the different proof that Mathlib uses, by induction on the dimension, which works over any complete nontrivially normed field. Section 2 proves the lemma of Riesz on almost orthogonal vectors, and from it the theorem that the closed unit ball of a normed space is compact if and only if the space has finite dimension. It ends with proper spaces and the theorem of Heine-Borel in finite dimension.

The chapter needs Chapters 1 and 2, the bases and the dimension of Volume 7, and the compact sets of Volume 13. Chapter 4 uses the closed finite dimensional subspaces, and Chapter 7 uses the finite dimensional subspaces as the first examples of complete subspaces for the orthogonal projection.
