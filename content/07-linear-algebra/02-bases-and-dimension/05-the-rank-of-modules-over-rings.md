## The rank of modules over rings

### Overview

Section 2 defines the rank `Module.rank R M`, a cardinal, as the supremum of the cardinalities of the linearly independent families. Section 3 defines the natural number `Module.finrank R M`, the rank when it is finite and $0$ when it is not. Over a field both are the number of vectors of a basis. Over a general ring a module need not have a basis, and some rings have $R^2 \cong R^3$. The files of this section say what the rank means over a ring, and which hypotheses on the ring make it behave well.

The hypotheses form a chain. A ring has the *invariant basis number* property when $R^n \cong R^m$ forces $n = m$. It satisfies the *rank condition* when a surjection $R^n \to R^m$ forces $m \le n$, and the *strong rank condition* when an injection $R^n \to R^m$ forces $n \le m$. Each commutative nontrivial ring satisfies all three, and Section 2 gives the instances. The modules below use them with the free modules of `Module.Free`: for a free module the rank is the cardinality of each basis, and two free modules are isomorphic if and only if they have the same rank.

The files sit in the folder `Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Dimension` and fall into four groups. The files Finite, Free and FreeAndStrongRankCondition relate rank, finiteness and bases. The files DivisionRing and ErdosKaplansky treat vector spaces, including infinite dimension. The files Domain, Localization, Torsion/Basic and Torsion/Finite treat domains, where the rank of a module equals the dimension of its base change to the field of fractions. The last files give the rank of linear maps, the Orzech property, and the matrix forms of these conditions.

### The library around it

#### Rank, finiteness and zero

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.Subsingleton` handles the trivial cases: `rank_subsingleton'`, `rank_punit` and `rank_bot` say that the rank of a zero module is $0$. The converse needs a hypothesis on the ring. The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.Finite` proves it with the right ones and links the rank to finiteness.

**Theorem (`Module.rank_lt_aleph0_iff`).** Over a ring with the strong rank condition, a free module has finite rank if and only if it is finitely generated.

*Proof sketch.* A free module has a basis $b$ indexed by $\iota$, and its rank is the cardinality of $\iota$. If $\iota$ is finite, the vectors of $b$ generate $M$. If $M$ is generated by a finite set $s$, each vector of $s$ uses only finitely many vectors of $b$, so finitely many vectors of $b$ span $M$, and a basis has no proper spanning subfamily. $\square$

The lemmas `Module.finite_of_rank_eq_nat`, `Module.finite_of_rank_eq_one` and `Module.finite_of_finrank_pos` are corollaries. The lemma `Module.finrank_of_not_finite` records the convention: a module that is not finite has `Module.finrank` equal to $0$.

The finite rank detects the zero module.

**Proposition (`Module.finrank_zero_iff`).** Let $R$ be a domain with the strong rank condition and let $M$ be a finite torsion free $R$-module. Then `Module.finrank R M = 0` if and only if $M$ is a subsingleton.

The companion `Module.finrank_pos_iff` says that the rank is positive if and only if $M$ is nontrivial, and `Module.finrank_pos_iff_exists_ne_zero` states it with a nonzero vector. For submodules, `Submodule.finrank_eq_zero` and `Submodule.rank_eq_zero` say that the rank is $0$ if and only if the submodule is $\bot$, and `Submodule.one_le_finrank_iff` is the positive form. The hypothesis on torsion cannot be dropped: `Module.finrank_eq_zero_iff` says that the rank of a finite module is $0$ if and only if each vector is killed by a nonzero scalar.

Independent families are bounded by the rank.

**Proposition (`LinearIndependent.fintype_card_le_finrank`).** In a finite module over a ring with the strong rank condition, an independent family indexed by a finite type has at most `Module.finrank R M` vectors.

The lemmas `LinearIndependent.finset_card_le_finrank` and `LinearIndependent.cardinalMk_le_finrank` are the forms for a `Finset` and for a cardinal, and `LinearIndependent.finite` says that each independent family is finite. In the other direction, `exists_linearIndependent_of_le_finrank` gives $n$ independent vectors for each $n \le$ `Module.finrank R M`. The lemma `Module.exists_nontrivial_relation_of_finrank_lt_card` is the contrapositive: more vectors than the rank satisfy a nontrivial relation.

The rank of a module of rank $1$ is studied in detail. The lemma `finrank_eq_one_iff'` says that a vector space has dimension $1$ if and only if it is the line of one nonzero vector, and `Submodule.finrank_le_one_iff_isPrincipal` says that a subspace has dimension at most $1$ if and only if it is generated by one vector. For algebras, `Subalgebra.finrank_eq_one_iff` says that a subalgebra of rank $1$ is $\bot$.

#### Free modules and bases of the right size

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.Free` uses the basis that `Module.Free` provides. The lemmas `Module.Free.rank_eq_card_chooseBasisIndex` and `Module.finrank_eq_card_chooseBasisIndex` say that the rank is the size of the chosen basis. For a finite free module the library gives a basis indexed by `Fin n`.

**Definition (`Module.finBasis`).** For a finite free module over a ring with the strong rank condition, `Module.finBasis R M` is a basis indexed by `Fin (Module.finrank R M)`.

The variant `Module.finBasisOfFinrankEq` takes a proof that the rank is $n$ and gives a basis indexed by `Fin n`, and `Module.basisUnique` gives a basis indexed by a type with one element when the rank is $1$.

Free modules are classified by their rank.

**Theorem (`LinearEquiv.ofFinrankEq`).** Two finite free modules over a ring with the strong rank condition that have the same `Module.finrank` are linearly equivalent.

*Proof.* Take the bases `Module.finBasis R M` and `Module.finBasisOfFinrankEq R M' h`, both indexed by the same `Fin n`. The equivalence sends the $i$-th vector of the first to the $i$-th vector of the second, by `Module.Basis.equiv`. $\square$

The forms with propositions are `FiniteDimensional.nonempty_linearEquiv_iff_finrank_eq` and `Module.nonempty_linearEquiv_iff_rank_eq`; the latter needs no finiteness. The lemma `Module.nonempty_linearEquiv_iff_finrank_eq_one` says that $M \cong R$ if and only if the rank is $1$, and `LinearMap.existsUnique_eq_smul_id_of_finrank_eq_one` says that each endomorphism of such a module is a scalar. The lemma `Module.Free.exists_linearMap_injective_of_rank_lt` gives an injection from a free module into a module of larger rank, and `rank_le_iff_exists_linearMap` turns the order of ranks into the existence of injections.

Ranks multiply in towers.

**Theorem (`Module.finrank_mul_finrank`).** Let $R \to S$ be a map of rings with $S$ free over $R$, and let $M$ be an $S$-module that is free over $S$. Then `Module.finrank R S * Module.finrank S M = Module.finrank R M`.

The proof takes a basis $b$ of $S$ over $R$ and a basis $c$ of $M$ over $S$; the products $b_i c_j$ form a basis of $M$ over $R$ (`Module.Basis.smulTower`). The cardinal form is `rank_mul_rank`. Consequences are `Module.finrank_dvd_finrank_left` and `Module.finrank_dvd_finrank_right`, the division forms `Module.finrank_div_finrank_cancel_left` and `Module.finrank_div_finrank_cancel_right`, and the bounds `Module.finrank_bot_le_finrank_of_isScalarTower_of_free` and `Module.finrank_top_le_finrank_of_isScalarTower_of_free`. In field theory this theorem is the tower law for degrees.

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.FreeAndStrongRankCondition` collects the results that need both a free module and the strong rank condition. A spanning family whose size equals the rank is a basis.

**Proposition (`linearIndependent_of_top_le_span_of_card_eq_finrank`).** In a finite free module over a ring with the strong rank condition, a finite family that spans $M$ and has `Module.finrank R M` vectors is linearly independent.

The basis built from it is `basisOfTopLeSpanOfCardEqFinrank`, with the variants `finsetBasisOfTopLeSpanOfCardEqFinrank` and `setBasisOfTopLeSpanOfCardEqFinrank`. The lemmas `linearIndependent_iff_card_eq_finrank_span` and `linearIndependent_iff_card_le_finrank_span` say that a finite family is independent if and only if its size equals the rank of its span. The modules `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.FreeModule.Finite.Basic` and `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.FreeModule.Finite.Matrix` add instances for hom spaces and matrices: `Module.Free.linearMap`, `Module.Finite.linearMap` and `Module.Finite.matrix`, with the rank formula `Module.finrank_linearMap`, the product of the two ranks. The instance `Module.Free.ChooseBasisIndex.fintype` makes the index of the chosen basis finite.

#### Vector spaces and infinite dimension

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.DivisionRing` proves the rank-nullity theorem over a division ring through the instance `DivisionRing.hasRankNullity`. The general consequences of `HasRankNullity` are in Section 3. The file also has `rank_quotient_add_rank_of_divisionRing`, the form for a quotient, `rank_add_rank_split` for a square of linear maps, and `cardinalMk_eq_cardinalMk_field_pow_rank`: a vector space $V$ over $K$ has $|K|^{\dim V}$ elements.

Infinite dimension brings a surprise. The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.ErdosKaplansky` proves the theorem of Erdős and Kaplansky.

**Theorem (`rank_dual_eq_card_dual_of_aleph0_le_rank`).** If $V$ has infinite dimension over $K$, the dimension of the dual space $V^*$ equals its cardinality.

The consequence for the size of the dual is this.

**Theorem (`rank_lt_rank_dual`).** If $V$ has infinite dimension over $K$, then $\dim V < \dim V^*$.

*Proof sketch.* Let $\dim V = \kappa$, infinite. Then $V^* \cong K^\kappa$ has cardinality $|K|^\kappa \geq 2^\kappa > \kappa$. By the previous theorem this cardinality is $\dim V^*$. $\square$

So an infinite dimensional space is never isomorphic to its dual, in contrast to Chapter 6. The lemma `rank_fun_infinite` says that the dimension of $K^\iota$ for infinite $\iota$ is the cardinality of $K^\iota$, and `max_aleph0_card_le_rank_fun_nat` bounds the dimension of the space of sequences from below.

#### Domains, torsion and localization

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.Domain` gives `IsDomain.hasRankNullity`: over a domain the rank satisfies the rank-nullity theorem, even for modules that are not free. The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.Localization` explains why. Localization at nonzerodivisors keeps the rank: `IsLocalizedModule.rank_eq`, `IsLocalization.rank_eq` and `IsBaseChange.rank_eq` say that a module and its base change to the field of fractions have the same rank. The tower formulas `Module.rank_mul_rank_of_isFractionRing_isLocalization` and `Module.finrank_mul_finrank_of_field_isLocalization` extend the tower law. The lemma `exists_set_linearIndependent_of_isDomain` gives an independent set whose cardinality is the rank.

The torsion submodule does not count. The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.Torsion.Basic` has `rank_quotient_eq_of_le_torsion`, and `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.Torsion.Finite` proves that a module has rank $0$ if and only if it is a torsion module (`Module.rank_eq_zero_iff_isTorsion`), with `Module.IsTorsion.finrank_eq_zero` and `finrank_quotient_torsion_eq` for abelian groups. The tower formula `Module.IsTorsionFree.finrank_mul_finrank` holds without freeness when $S$ is torsion free over $R$.

#### Linear maps, the Orzech property and matrices

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.LinearMap` defines `LinearMap.rank f`, the rank of the range of $f$. It is at most the rank of the domain and of the target (`LinearMap.rank_le_domain` and `LinearMap.rank_le_range`), it is subadditive (`LinearMap.rank_add_le`), and the rank of a composite is at most the rank of each factor (`LinearMap.rank_comp_le`). The lemma `LinearMap.le_rank_iff_exists_linearIndependent` says that the rank is at least $c$ if and only if some independent set of size $c$ has an independent image. The finite version is `LinearMap.finrank_range_le` in Section 3, and the matrix version is the topic of the rank of a matrix in Chapter 3.

A ring has the Orzech property when each surjective endomorphism of a finitely generated submodule of $R^n$ is injective. Each commutative ring has it. The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Dimension.OrzechProperty` derives the instance `instIsStablyFiniteRingOfOrzechProperty` and the strong rank condition from it, and `OrzechProperty.bijective_of_surjective_of_finrank_le` says that a surjection between free modules of the same finite rank is bijective.

The module `Mathlib.LinearAlgebra.Matrix.InvariantBasisNumber` states the ring conditions with matrices. The lemma `invariantBasisNumber_iff_matrix` says that $R$ has invariant basis number if and only if $AB = 1$ and $BA = 1$ for an $n \times m$ matrix $A$ and an $m \times n$ matrix $B$ force $n = m$. The lemma `rankCondition_iff_matrix` is the same for the rank condition with only $BA = 1$. The lemma `Matrix.square_of_invertible` says that an invertible matrix with rectangular index types has as many rows as columns.

#### Common errors

1. `Module.finrank R M` is $0$ for a module that is not finite. A hypothesis `Module.finrank R M = 0` does not say that $M$ is zero unless the module is finite, free or torsion free over a suitable ring.
2. Many results of this section need `StrongRankCondition R`. For a commutative ring Lean finds it, but over a noncommutative ring a lemma may fail to apply with a missing instance message.
3. `Module.finBasis R M` is indexed by `Fin (Module.finrank R M)`. Rewriting the rank to a numeral changes the type of the index; use `Module.finBasisOfFinrankEq` with the equation in place of a rewrite.

### Exercises

1. (A) Prove that `Module.finrank ℚ (Fin 3 → ℚ) = 3` and obtain a basis indexed by `Fin 3` with `Module.finBasisOfFinrankEq`.
2. (A) Show that a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space $V$ with `Module.finrank ℚ V = 2` is linearly equivalent to $\mathbb{Q} \times \mathbb{Q}$.
3. (B) Prove that the $\mathbb{Z}$-module $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ has rank $0$, and explain why `Module.finrank_zero_iff` does not apply to it.
4. (B) Use `Module.finrank_mul_finrank` to show that a $\mathbb{C}$-vector space of dimension $n$ has dimension $2n$ over $\mathbb{R}$.
5. (B) Show that four vectors in $\mathbb{Q}^3$ satisfy a nontrivial linear relation, with `Module.exists_nontrivial_relation_of_finrank_lt_card`.
6. (C) Prove that the space of all sequences $\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Q}$ is not isomorphic to the space of sequences with finite support, by comparing their dimensions.
