# Bases and dimension

A basis gives each vector unique coordinates, and the number of the basis vectors is the dimension. This chapter proves that the number does not depend on the basis. Section 1 defines linear independence and bases, and shows that a basis is the same as an isomorphism with a module of coordinates. Section 2 proves the exchange lemma of Steinitz, the existence of bases in vector spaces, and the invariance of dimension, and it introduces `Module.rank`. Section 3 defines the finite dimension `Module.finrank` and proves the rank-nullity theorem with its corollaries. Sections 4 to 7 describe the library around these notions. Section 4 gives tests for linear independence, new bases from old ones and flags. Section 5 studies the rank of modules over rings that are not fields, with the rank condition and the strong rank condition. Section 6 treats the complex numbers as a real vector space of dimension $2$. Section 7 defines the linearly disjoint submodules of an algebra and the free products of algebras.

The chapter needs Chapter 1, the finite sums of Volume 3 and the cardinals of Volume 1. Over a field every module has a basis. Over the integers most modules do not, and the chapter marks the theorems that need a field. Chapter 3 uses bases to turn linear maps into matrices, and Chapter 7 studies the free modules over a principal ideal domain, the modules that have a basis.
